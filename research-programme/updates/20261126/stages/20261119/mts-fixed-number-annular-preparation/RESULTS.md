# A different wave profile at the same occupation and outer force
Local MW/LMC preparation challenge · 19 November 2026 · No fitted inner forces

**Yes, the retained equations admit a changed radial wave profile without changing its particle number or the calibrated force at 17.58 kpc.** The permitted stellar redistribution does not solve the inner force discrepancy. This is a different test from scaling the satellite-driven wave.

## What selected the profiles?
Use the retained published radial living-star columns, integral living mass and half-mass radius (source and calibration conventions in ../mts-annular-mass-budget-20261119/RESULTS.md). Shift the ordinary living-star density in 4–7 and 7–8 kpc. The parent wave susceptibility and outer-force condition give a stationary donor/inner fractional-change ratio of **−3.6521** on the reference ground and **−3.6491** on the upper ground. Simply preserving stellar mass would have given −3.5010. Neither the sky bands nor the observed inner velocities enter this construction.

Three finite stationary challenges lie inside the frozen foreground limits, two on the reference ground and one inward from the allowed boundary on the upper preparation. In the reference positive case the inner ordinary column increases 3.14% and its neighbour decreases 11.48%. The nonlinear wave carries **14.17 million M☉ more inside the 6.185-kpc sphere**, and **23.56 million M☉ more inside 8.434 kpc**, at the same total occupation. These are nested masses, not quantities to sum. At the northern inner point the *combined* radial change is **−0.00734**, polar **+0.00108**: both needed directions, unlike a rescaled LMC mode. Most of that change is ordinary gravity. The induced wave alone gives −0.000537 radial and −0.0000182 polar. In the upper-ground negative case the signs reverse, not a repair.

The stationary outer-force change vanishes to better than 9e-16 in the solve; independent integration of the saved physical density recovers it to within 1.20e-10 in acceleration units. Six finite-duration trials (one or two outer orbits; 0.482 and 0.964 Gyr) produced different wave states with the same number and matched endpoint outer force. No observed band verdict changed. These are constructed test histories, not an inference of how the Galaxy formed.

## Why an unknown assembly time does not rescue these two sources
We also allowed *every* monotonic growth or depletion schedule for the two rings, with independent timing, starting from either retained ground. Within the linearized parent equations, conservation of wave excitation energy between increments bounds the endpoint force for the entire allowed two-dimensional foreground region. No timescale scan, optimal initial wave, or best-fit profile is used. The outer calibration tightens the bound. The 6-kpc radial shortfall stays at least **0.51435** (rounded; minimum 0.514345…), the 8.3-kpc radial shortfall at least **0.33039** (rounded), and the 6-kpc polar one at least **0.11467** (rounded), in units of 10⁻¹⁰ m/s². Even dropping the outer constraint and giving the final static ring changes their most favorable inward effect, the inner radial measurement alone would take **at least 241 times** the upper bound on the transient wave energy available from these monotonic changes across the tested histories. This is not a bound on all Galactic formation energy.

The same stellar changes do not rescue the historical static NFW control either: its nearest remaining inner radial gap is 0.07248 with fixed halo amplitude or 0.08050 after restoring the outer calibration. Its shortfall is smaller than the MTS one at that probe. Neither a static NFW calculation nor the MTS wave test is observational evidence for a specific stellar assembly history.

## Numerical controls and limits
The native set uses the original endpoint sky weights, checks the enlarged domain, tests two enriched time-response bases, independently integrates the physical density, and retains all north/south and frame comparisons. On the two primary grounds: 864 finite stationary sky comparisons across MTS and the two NFW conventions; 576 MTS and 1,152 matching NFW finite-duration comparisons; 576 any-rate component enclosures including both NFW controls. These are numerical comparisons of the same observations, not sample sizes for statistical inference. The 40-length-unit domain is a numerical repeat, not two extra galaxies.

The top-level trial files preserve an earlier smooth-interpolating force functional. Its demand for agreement on **every** high-frequency basis coefficient failed when tested against the archived sky operator. The authoritative native/ results instead use the original force functional on both actual sky probes; interpolation is needed at the added outer probe only. This distinction matters for a bound on arbitrary fields, even when the two readouts agree on the smooth states actually excited. No old research state, force band or tolerance was changed.
