# From the common Newtonian potential to the relative displacement

**Prepared system.** Use the same two normalized heavy-wave grounds, baryonic profiles and six stored [MW–LMC simulations](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.8015660). Work to first order in the incident perturbation, in the reflection-even planar dipole sector. Translate the ordinary density rigidly but allow the wave to deform. Rigid translation of the whole ordinary profile is a choice of reduced dynamics; neither a stationary density alone nor universal metric coupling proves rigidity. No new normalization is fitted. Internal binding forces within the translating ordinary body have zero resultant. The source sampled by a first-order displacement may be evaluated on the undisturbed density: displacement times incident tidal gradient is of second order.

**Extended-body drive.** Write the real m=1 potential as twice the real part of V_l(r) P_l(mu) exp(i phi), with the normalized associated Legendre functions used by the parent. Azimuth averaging the negative planar gradient, in x−iy convention, gives
```
<a_x−i a_y> = −Σ_l ∫ dm/M {sinθ P_l ∂r V_l
                         + [P_l/sinθ − mu sinθ P_l'] V_l/r}.
```
Evaluate the differential potential after subtracting its constant and its term linear in position. A purely uniform field gives no differential acceleration for either body. Integrals of the dipolar potential x r² and the harmonic x(4z²−R²) check both pieces of the gradient and the cubic interpolation against moments of the mass measures. The measure is 2πR Σ(R) dR times the folded vertical probability. Include the thin/thick living and remnant disks, the saved ring increment on one background only, both gas layers, the axisymmetric bar and the finite spherical cloud. Use the parent gas sech² convention and the source-defined stellar heights. The central bar is not added on top of the old spherical bulge. The cloud's radial probability is proportional to u²(1−u²)⁶ on [0,1]. Coordinate tails cut at 240 kpc and 50 gas scale heights are numerical integration limits, not a fitted environment.

**Independent source checks.** Translate the satellite in each planar direction in the even, *axisymmetric energy*, average Φ_s(r−D)−Φ_s(−D) over the ordinary mass and differentiate with respect to D. Compare this independently computed vector with the odd-degree m=1 gradient, first on the original bank and then on a denser radius grid with degrees through 65. Finally bypass Galactic angular harmonics entirely for HI/H2. Integration by parts gives the mean differential acceleration as the volume integral of the differential potential times ∇ρ/M. In these two gas profiles ∂R log ρ=R_m/R²−1/R_d. Integrate the original satellite potential times this factor and exp(−iφ), over both signs of z and the full azimuth, splitting angular integration at all satellite radial interpolation knots. A translated degree-three synthetic external tide tests normalization, sign and cancellation in this independent integration. The rapid tail decay justifies ignoring the exterior boundary term in the infinite-profile identity.

**Reciprocal translation.** In parent native units let D=diag(σ²), h read relative wave centroid X=hᵀc, N be the fixed wave number, γ=M_wave/M_b, and f be the previous point-frame modal forcing with its factor two. δ_b is the ordinary mean external acceleration minus the external acceleration at the original centre. The wave's internal acceleration in the previous fixed ordinary profile was −hᵀD c, independently checked against integration of the ordinary force on the deformed wave with the wave-wall stress separated. Treating the reciprocal force on the extended ordinary component, rather than the field at a point, gives
```
a_b − a_point = δ_b + γ hᵀD c
c'' + [I + γ N h hᵀ] D c = −f − N h δ_b .
```
The rank-one correction follows from a uniform inertial gradient in the translating frame and equal-and-opposite wave–ordinary gravity; there is no extra spring or local coupling. This continuum identity uses vanishing exterior wave stress. In the finite modal calculation we use the symmetric conservative reduction and test the difference using the independently integrated body force with wall traction kept separate; we do not assign an actual wall impulse to the Galaxy. Coordinates are in kpc, km/s or native wave units as identified in code. Archived time unit is kpc/(km/s), not exactly one Gyr.

**Stable propagation and verification.** With w=σc, v=σh, q=h/σ, the squared-frequency matrix A=D+γNvvᵀ is symmetric positive. Diagonalize it and propagate the same quartic incident-force interpolants from zero added disturbance. The two controls respectively remove both ordinary forcing and recoil (recovering the predecessor) and remove feedback alone (retaining δ_b). Neither control retunes the foreground. Directly compare the relative equation and the total COM acceleration against the two external spatial averages plus the boundary stress. For the site-minus-point sky diagnostic use the induced site's acceleration minus the induced central field; for the planar material-frame diagnostic subtract the extended ordinary centre's acceleration instead. The original direct satellite site-minus-point term is already in the prior subtotal. Static NFW is an untranslated-shape, no-wake control, not a new mobile halo simulation; the planar material subtraction for that control is a kinematic foreground correction only. Vertical matter motion and shape changes are not included.

**Reusing the calculation.** For an error e in δ_b, the centroid susceptibility at real Laplace argument s is
```
χ0(s)=N Σ h_j²/(s²+σ_j²),
χrel(s)=χ0(s)/[1+γ(K−s²χ0(s))],  K=N hᵀh .
```
If A has eigenvectors U and frequencies ω, A q=(1+γK)v. Therefore χrel has positive residues W_j=N ω_j² |(Uᵀq)_j|²/(1+γK), with ΣW=K and static compliance N qᵀq/(1+γK). The added relative displacement is minus the convolution of e with ΣW sin(ωτ)/ω. In physical units the displacement in pc is bounded by (1000 L/V)ΣW/ω times the integrated absolute error in km/s. Analogous signed sky kernels and any instantaneous frame coefficient are stored. These bounds apply to the ordinary-drive error only, with the rest of the weak-field problem fixed; a snapshot is not an all-time error bound.
