# The LMC mostly carries the ordinary matter and wave together

19 November 2026 · Two retained Galactic grounds × six archived LMC trajectories · Local only

Replacing the acceleration at a point with the mean acceleration of the ordinary stars and gas removes much of the inferred differential forcing. Letting the ordinary centre also recoil under the wave's own gravity reduces the modelled **present relative displacement to 2.72–18.68 pc**, versus **75.77–183.23 pc** with the earlier prescribed foreground. The intermediate experiment (extended ordinary pull but no reciprocal feedback) gives **39.34–102.36 pc**. These are conditional ranges across simulations and prepared grounds, not observations or uncertainty intervals. No new coupling, occupation, fit or observational input was introduced.

**What changes in the dynamics.** At the present epoch, the difference of the two extended bodies' external pulls is just **0.184–1.109%** of the external acceleration at the Galactic origin. The difference is a vector, not the difference of force magnitudes. For L2M10 on the reference ground, 99 pc in the prescribed frame becomes 58 pc in the one-way test and 3.1 pc with mutual gravity. The lowest relative mode's period is now about 362 Myr; it carries 71–72% of the dipole response weight rather than over 99% with the baryons held fixed. Recoil changes the internal dynamics, not just the coordinates used to report an old trajectory.

**What the probes say.** None of the 384 entries (both models, two force-reference choices) changes its original band decision. Both radial bands and the inner polar bands are still missed; outer polar remains inside. Adding the coupled dipole to the existing site-minus-point prediction changes a component by at most 0.000163 in units of 10⁻¹⁰ m/s². The alternative planar material-frame readout changes a component by at most 0.000107 on MTS. These are two readouts of one planar calculation, not independent sky measurements. The point-frame static NFW baseline is unchanged; its material-frame correction is kinematic and is not a live NFW response. The ordinary mass retains its prepared shape; warping, internal stellar/gas response, and vertical recoil have not been simulated.

**Checks that distinguish physics from numerical effects.**
- Recovered the two ordinary masses to 4.2 × 10⁻¹³ relative to their saved normalizations. The relative acceleration reconstructed from the two bodies' external and material forces agrees with the evolved equation within 3.18 × 10⁻⁷ of peak; the COM balance agrees within 1.55 × 10⁻⁷ on the primary runs. Switching off both ordinary corrections recovers the earlier prescribed-foreground centroid histories.
- The 8/16 substep comparison narrowly exceeds the unchanged 2 × 10⁻⁵ final-vector tolerance on **L2M11** on both backgrounds. Calculating the missing intermediate satellite fields *only for that orbit*, keeping the existing source nodes, brings 16/32 differences down to 2.37 × 10⁻⁷ and 2.79 × 10⁻⁷. Other primary 8/16 endpoint comparisons pass. This is time convergence on the original angular bank, not a statement of total accuracy.
- Integrating the differential potential energy and translating the satellite independently confirms the force direction but exposes a **source angular error**: the original projection differs from the higher-degree energy derivative by up to 0.0444% of the ordinary differential acceleration. The disagreement is concentrated in the extended HI disk; a denser radial grid alone does not cure it. Host degrees through 65 and a virtual derivative through 60 still differ by up to 7.39 × 10⁻⁵ relative.
- A direct full-azimuth integration of the archived LMC potential over both gas profiles bypasses this angular projection altogether. On the six present-day snapshots, changing the direct quadrature alters the mass-weighted gas correction by at most 1.81 × 10⁻⁷ of the ordinary differential pull. The physical correction to the original projected result is larger: **0.035–0.374% of the wave-minus-ordinary external force**. These corrected present external forces are recorded, not substituted for the entire time history. The quoted offsets and force-band decisions are for the original archived angular bank; the complete direct-gas correction to the evolved offsets is not yet known.

**A quantitative next test.** Saved the impulse response of the *coupled* system. A missing ordinary acceleration with integrated absolute magnitude 1 km/s can change the final centroid by at most about 55.1 pc. To bring even the nearest currently missed site-minus-point MTS band into reach by changing this one drive alone would require **at least 48.59 km/s** of integrated absolute drive error (conservative triangle bound on the fixed linear system, not a measurement of the actual error). The snapshot discrepancy is not that integral. Next measure or bound the gas correction along the orbit and convolve it with these kernels; do not recompute the already solved modal evolution merely to inject a corrected source.
