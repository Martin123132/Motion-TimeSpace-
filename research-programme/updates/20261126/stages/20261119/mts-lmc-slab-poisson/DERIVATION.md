# Reconstruction, signs and replay
The parent derivation and original sky tests are in ../mts-lmc-fixed-occupation-axisymmetric-20261119/DERIVATION.md and final-force-comparison.csv there. The combined local comparison comes from ../mts-lmc-gas-history-correction-20261119/vertical-tides.csv and its per-history force packets. Both stages are retained.

## Wave and readout
Write the wave as g + x + i y about a real stationary g, using the normalized angular basis and the saved r-times-wave radial coordinates. With g†g=N and projection P=I−gg†/N, the retained weak-field equations give x'=H y, y'=−P(H x+B w+F), L w=−2 B†x. Since Hg=0, d(g†x)/dt=0. Zero initial displacement gives zero total linear occupation change but not zero local density change. Restore the radial Jacobian and angular product: δρ_physical=M_unit/(4π L³) × 2(g·Y)(x·Y)/(w_rad r_native²), where w_rad is needed for the quadrature-weighted site coefficients. The saved m=0 states already store the projected coefficients of this density; no factor of two is added to them. The static full ground uses the denser original rψ grid, not the separately polished linearization mesh. For m=1,2 use the real Fourier pair, phase exp(i mπ) at the probes, and the product of the normalized ground and complex displacement. The tracked lowest m=2 coordinate uses the older saved, phase-aligned amplitude; the higher modes are included only once. The corrected m=1 state uses the saved ordinary-centre recoil and the terminal integral of the gas correction. Uniform frame accelerations cancel between vertical endpoints.

For a radial coefficient D_l=4πGρ_l in physical units, define I=r^(-l−1)∫₀ʳ s^(l+2)D_l ds and O=rˡ∫ᵣ^∞ s^(1−l)D_l ds. Then Φ_l=−(I+O)/(2l+1) and Φ_l'=((l+1)I−l O)/((2l+1)r). These kernels include mass outside the probe. Integrate using shell Gauss quadrature and scaled interior/exterior recurrences; evaluate forces by differentiating the angular basis. At z=h, a_r=(R a_R+h a_z)/r and a_θ=(h a_R−R a_z)/r. Test both hemispheres against their own saved bands.

For a linear amplitude diagnostic use the unmodified final history a_ref and the reconstructed increment d: a_other=a_ref−d. For each force interval [l,u], the allowed multipliers are the sorted pair ((l−a_other)/d,(u−a_other)/d). Compare their intersection, without optimizing against the data. See fixed-shape-witness.csv and its signed input rows.

## Slab identity
With positive compression K=[a_z(−h)−a_z(h)]/(2h), Newtonian Poisson implies K=C+H, C=4πG Σ/(2h), H=mean_z(div_horizontal a). For Fourier order m, H uses ∂_R a_R+a_R/R+m²Φ/R². The last term is essential even when evaluating on a single azimuth. The code averages field derivatives, not derivatives of sky-band boundaries. It computes a separate 3D Laplacian and horizontal acceleration divergence for the direct satellite, splitting at any crossed interpolation knots. Its column is an equivalent source of the retained numerical potential. The ordinary point-source exterior has zero column but nonzero horizontal divergence.

For the independent ordinary-disk H, first integrate observer height analytically. At fixed separation t in the vertical force kernel the density becomes Σ(R') times Pr(t−h<z'<t+h)/(2h). Integrate the azimuthal ring kernel and differentiate R times this radial force before dividing by R. The exponential and gas probabilities are integrated analytically; no spherical enclosed-mass approximation. For the mean bar, integrate its physical density over azimuth and height; independently solve its Poisson multipoles for H. The comparison retaining the original spline is kept separately. The “inferred_horizontal” entries of components.csv use saved ordinary K minus its physical column and are not independent Poisson tests; independent-totals.csv uses the direct horizontal evaluation. Physical column and potential both refer to the azimuthally averaged bar preparation.

## Replay
In this directory, with the retained Python, NumPy and SciPy: run python -B slab_test.py, python -B ordinary_flux.py, python -B bar_control.py, python -B shape_test.py, then python -B verify.py. All new calculations use one numerical thread; source archives, grids, time integrations, signs and acceptance bands remain untouched. The extra quadrature and spatial derivative grids are for independent readout only. Scripts require an existing sealed data tree and do not overwrite differing results. input-hashes.csv identifies the wave states, operators and ledgers read by the main calculation; source-bank-hashes.csv supplements it with direct satellite and foreground inputs. verify.py seal creates the new inventory only in a fresh unsealed copy. No downloads or fits.
