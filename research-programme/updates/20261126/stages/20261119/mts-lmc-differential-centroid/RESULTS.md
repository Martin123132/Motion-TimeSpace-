# How far does the LMC displace the occupied Galactic wave?

19 November 2026 · Local follow-up to the fixed-occupation axisymmetric and two-lobed tests · Six saved orbits × two prepared backgrounds · No fit.

The extended wave feels almost the same external acceleration as the Galactic origin. Subtracting common free fall leaves just **1.73–3.42%** of the present planar acceleration at that point. Evolving the wave rather than moving its ground density rigidly yields a present offset of **75.8–183.2 pc** in these prescribed Galactic backgrounds. Omitting that subtraction would have given **739–1,672 pc**: much of that apparent motion is a frame artifact. These intervals span alternative histories/grounds; they are not observational confidence intervals. We did not change the original number, forces or bands.

**Source check.** Two otherwise different calculations agree: azimuthal dipole projection in the wave equation, and translating the satellite in the existing axisymmetric potential-energy integral, then differentiating in each of the two planar directions. Across 36 two-component tests the greatest discrepancy is 1.85 × 10⁻⁷ relative to the small *differential* acceleration, not merely to the much larger common acceleration. At the present epoch the nonspherical part of the wave-ground density accounts for only 0.87–1.94% of the vector magnitude in this decomposition (norm of component over norm of sum). This is not a statement that the satellite is spherical. Uniform forcing gives a null to 2.1 × 10⁻¹³ in the synthetic source check.

**Restoring force.** The two independently reconstructed baryonic curvatures agree with the spectral curvatures to within 1.88 × 10⁻⁵ relative. The lowest mode carries over 99% of the dipole oscillator strength, but that does not make a rigid translation exact: integrating a rigid oscillator on the very same differential drive changes the final position vector by **5.24–20.28%** relative to the deformable wave. The direct density–ordinary-gravity force plus wave-wall stress reproduces the modal restoring acceleration within 1.18 × 10⁻⁷ of its peak on both primary histories sets. The ground-gradient translation *shape* comparison is less precise (up to 1.50 × 10⁻⁴); it is not counted as a tolerance pass. The Newtonian reciprocal force, divided by the ordinary mass, would give mean ordinary accelerations of 17.4–43.9 (km/s)²/kpc from the wave perturbation on these snapshots. This is an equal-and-opposite force diagnostic, not a time integration of a moving stellar disk.

**Sky test.** At R=6 kpc, z=+1.5 kpc, with all previously retained contributions included once, in units of 10⁻¹⁰ m/s²:

| Readout of the induced dipole | Added radial | Added polar |
|---|---:|---:|
| Local acceleration in the prescribed foreground frame | +0.000475 to +0.001583 | +0.000189 to +0.000667 |
| Same field, site acceleration minus acceleration at the origin | −0.001814 to −0.000578 | −0.000184 to −0.000075 |

Neither readout moves a single prediction across an original band in either hemisphere on either foreground. Both radial bands and the inner polar bands remain missed; the outer polar bands remain satisfied. Static NFW keeps the identical direct satellite contribution and zero induced wave; it is not a live-halo simulation. The two rows describe the same evolution with different acceleration references, not two independent tests of a freely moving ordinary Galaxy.

**Resolution.** Enlarging the radial wave basis, enlarging the box from 30 to 40 ground lengths, comparing every other incident radial knot and reducing source degree from 25 to 15 all change the endpoint centroid vector by at most 3.78 × 10⁻⁶ relative. The time comparison (8 vs 16 source substeps) reaches **5.46 × 10⁻⁵** at the endpoint and **5.83 × 10⁻⁵** on the trace normalized by its peak. It does **not** meet the existing 2 × 10⁻⁵ relative criterion for all histories. No tolerance was relaxed. Among the 60 numerical comparisons, the maximum absolute change in a sky component is 1.74 × 10⁻⁷ in the table units, with no band reversals. This is numerical sensitivity on the checked grids, not a general astrophysical error bound.
