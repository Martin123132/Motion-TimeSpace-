# A relative dipole in the retained Newtonian limit

Start from the same positive, quadratic heavy wave obeying the common-metric Schrödinger–Poisson equations, about the same centred, flattened stationary grounds. The five-Gyr compact ordinary cloud of the earlier live-relative-dipole experiment is **not** a proxy for the ordinary Milky Way disk here. Use both recently prepared Galactic backgrounds with the six archived [Vasiliev histories](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.8015660). Observations do not normalize any new coefficient.

## What is driven?
Write the azimuthal m=+1 potential coefficient with the exp(−iφ) Fourier convention. Reflection-even terms have odd spherical degree 1,3,5,… . Never delete the entire l=1 coefficient: in the freely falling point-centre frame remove only the term linear in radius generated by the acceleration shared at the Galactic origin. For Y₁₁=−sqrt(3/2) sinθ exp(iφ), this gives
    Vdiff₁₁(r)=Vraw₁₁(r)+r(∂xΦ−i∂yΦ)₀/sqrt(6).
Constants vanish separately. Pure uniform forcing now gives exactly zero, but l=1 radial structure survives. Input checks compare the rotated, knot-split projection to direct Cartesian azimuth integration and differentiate the underlying source for the centre acceleration. Units of ∇Φ are (km/s)²/kpc. The finite input histories are simulations constrained at the present epoch, not measured time sequences.

With dimensionless Hamiltonian H, Poisson coupling B and positive L of this sector, let M=H−2B L⁻¹Bᵀ. The displacement d=a₊+a₋* obeys d″=−HMd−2HF. The imaginary sign convention below is X−iY; an actual vector norm is not the difference of two lengths. The linear dipole has zero angular integral and preserves total occupation without introducing a new population. All response tests here start with no added wave at the first saved time. The ordinary potential is held fixed in the frame following the external acceleration at the origin.

## Centroid and restoring coefficient
Let C read (X−iY)/Lunit from d: its coefficient in the mass-weighted spatial mesh is r/N times the ground times sinθ projected on the m=1 angular basis. The acceleration from the external field alone, in wave units, is −2 C H F. Defining t=N H Cᵀ, multiplication by H on the coordinate times the ground differentiates the ground: in unbounded space t is the radial/meridional shape of an infinitesimal horizontal translation. N C H Cᵀ=1 by the double commutator. Check rather than assume this identity on the finite mesh.

Write d=t X/(C t)+η, where Cη=0 and X now denotes the dimensionless complex centroid. Then
    X″+κX=−2 C H F−C H Mη,     κ=tᵀ M t/(N C t).
Only if the last term vanishes is the rigid oscillator closed. With H⁻¹-orthonormal modes vⱼ the positive weights wⱼ=N|C vⱼ|² sum to C t. Their frequency-squared mean gives κ, and Σwⱼ/σⱼ²=N C M⁻¹ Cᵀ is a separate static check. Neither a fitted spring nor the substitution of the lowest frequency for κ is justified. Diagonalize the symmetric positive reduction Hᵣ=DDᵀ, DᵀMᵣD, keeping the saved ground, Poisson self-gravity and exterior matching. The complex oscillator convolution integrates the locally quartic source with its sine/cosine kernel. A rigid comparison uses the *same differential external drive*. A second deliberately incorrect comparison keeps uniform gravity in this supported-background frame.

For an isolated translated density, wave self-gravity has zero net force. On the fixed ordinary foreground κ also equals −∫(∂xρ₀)(∂xΦb)d³x/N = ∫ρ₀∂xxΦb d³x/N after integration by parts. Reconstruct the ordinary potential from the total and the wave Poisson solution; check the equilibrium reconstruction against the pinned m=2 Hamiltonian before switching sectors. Check the independent material-force integral and keep the Dirichlet wave boundary stress: at linear order the m=1 complex centre acceleration from the outer surface is −⟨sinθ u₀′d′⟩/(2N), with u=rψ zero at the boundary and primes radial here. Comparing these with −CHMd distinguishes numerical momentum defects from deformation. A fixed potential supplies a force; it is not proof of total matter-plus-wave momentum conservation in a live disk.

## An independent check of the *small difference* in acceleration
For an external satellite centred at D, integrate [Φₛ(r−D)−Φₛ(−D)] against the normalized stationary wave mass. Its planar derivative with respect to D is the acceleration of the extended wave minus that of the origin. Thus the **existing m=0** projector at translated satellite positions supplies an independent test of the **new m=1** dipole source. Keep all density multipoles in that integral. Its spherical part is not in general zero for a penetrating or extended satellite. A strictly uniform tide gives zero centre drive on a centred density; for a spherical density wholly interior to an exterior point mass the shell theorem gives exact cancellation. Neither conclusion may be imposed on an overlapping, flattened astrophysical ground.

## Interpreting the observer
At the probes use exp(iφ)=−1. A dipole also exerts a finite acceleration at the origin, unlike the completed m=2 wave. Save both the induced local acceleration in the prescribed frame and the difference between the site and the origin. The latter is a relative-force diagnostic, not a self-consistent evolution in which all the ordinary Galactic mass follows its central point. Neither is evidence for an observed displacement of the entire disk. A live ordinary centre would require its own mean external pull and mutual force and, for shape evolution, its transport equations.
