## How the stress was matched

In conformal stress units let v be the homogeneous wave velocity, b its field, f,w the induced second-order canonical pair, and M² = a²m². From the retained stress tensor, E = v w + M² b f + E_q + 3v²Φ, J = k²v f + J_q, and P = v w − M² b f + (E_q − trace_q)/3 + 3v²Φ. Shear is the saved quadratic wave shear. This includes both the induced field and the products of linear waves; multiplying two already-averaged fields would drop part of the source. The recovered velocity was independently checked against the saved endpoint wave momentum. All 77 incident momentum bins are retained.

The 6π half-width, degree-eight projector removes the fast carrier and its harmonics. The explicit wave was extended to 90.4 **only** to supply the right half of the centered average; no additional photon drive after 89.6 was used. This is a retrospective match with known wave history, not a new causal closure inferred from a single fluid state.

Use e = E/ρ, j = J/ρ, p = P/ρ, d = e − 3Φ and ρ = ρ_match/a in the saved conformal normalization. The effective equations are d′ = −j − 3ℋp + S_e and j′ = −ℋj + k²(p + Ψ) + S_j. The effective pressure is c_s²(e + 3ℋj/k²) plus the directly projected residual. The current, pressure, quadratic source work, differences between averaged products and products of averages, and the commutator of changing phase speed with the projection are accounted for in phase_stress.py (not bundled here). The inhomogeneous pressure and source residuals are taken from the first saved launch and used unchanged for the second; they are trajectory-level information, not an independently derived law for arbitrary states.
