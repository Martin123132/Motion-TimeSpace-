# What the gravity and pressure tests distinguish
26 November 2026. Same retained cold and matched preparations; same second-order Fourier triangle, photon normalization and 0.5% gate. These are checks of the model against a numerically resolved history, not against observed CMB data.

**Gravity.** The compensating energy and current need not be evolved as two rapidly cancelling terms in the density constraint. Their single combination Y has an exact retarded evolution with no derivative of the fast current (MEMORY.md). Its change reproduces the archived compensation to 0.0000170% on the finer field sampling. We used the new variable in the actual gravity calculation for all 77 bins, carried both compensated routes and both null controls, and sent the resulting energy and current through the old scattering readout. On the primary photon setting, fine time substep, the cumulative joint momentum-vs-density route gaps are 0.659717% (p16) and 0.659708% (p32), essentially unchanged. The increment alone gives 0.462234%/0.462243%. This rules out differentiating the old fast current as the explanation for the cumulative miss. It does not turn the new phase-dependent pressure into a tested photon source. See VARIABLE-CHANGE.md and load-control/equivalence.json.

**Driven field.** Replaying the archived forcing from the earlier endpoint recovers the complex field and momentum at all 20 recorded later endpoints on each grid (worst 3.52e-9% relative to the endpoint peak), including its current. Next, with the updated first-order input, we evolved the second-order field on each route's recorded second-order potentials, including both microscopic drives. The energy/current balance works on each realization, but that alone does not give the right phase-averaged drift. Dropping the microscopic work is not a controlled approximation. Directly matching the preceding phase history at 89.65 Mpc helps the average current; it does not by itself recover the small pressure change.

**Pressure.** The physical comparison has common symmetric temporal support only at six centers, 89.83125–89.846875 conformal Mpc. The measured change in mean pressure is only 0.02647% of its mean level. On the p32 grid and finer sampling, 16 phase orientations with a common metric miss this change by 300.27%. Allowing the metric to change with the orientation, using the frozen cubic reconstruction from past samples, reduces that to 13.88%. All seven mean levels pass (pressure error 0.00202%); six of seven changes pass (energy 0.00162%, longitudinal current 0.00014% and constraint source 0.00163%). Both second-harmonic changes pass in every channel. The coarse field sampling gives the same qualitative result. Six channels are not six independent observations.

**Why not accept the pressure model?** The independent real-history reference changes by at most 0.003% of the measured pressure drift when every other sample is used. On that same scale the 8-to-16-angle effect is 2.79%, and the quadratic-to-cubic history reconstruction changes the prediction by 294.57%. The temporal fourth harmonic really is present: its cosine and sine changes are roughly 2.88 and 6.30 times the mean change. Thus the two-state mean can confuse the fourth harmonic with a slow change. The cubic continuation is a useful sensitivity test, not a derived gravitational law or a converged closure. See METRIC-ORBIT.md and orbit-scores.json.
