# The incoming wave, live matter, and two independent measures of gravity

On 24 original modes the scalar-field repair passes the same 0.5% shear test when ordinary matter, photons, collisionless particles and metric perturbations **evolve** rather than being fed future perturbations. The decisive numerical difference is initializing the heavy field and velocity at the actual incoming time. An otherwise similar earlier start fails the launch test, not the propagation test. This is a controlled comparison of equations at first perturbative order over 89.65–90.4 conformal Mpc, not an observed galaxy or CMB datum.

**Why matching the actual start matters.** Written in the native variables, alpha=(E/2+k eta_k)/(H k²)+3J/(2k³). Changing the fields on the knot just before the launch changes the incoming heavy density and the homogeneous expansion simultaneously. At the worst mode on the fine table those two terms contribute +0.50973% and +0.25552% of the original change, accounting for essentially all of the +0.76545% spurious launch shift. The heavy current contributes only +0.000207%. Joining b and v at the physical incoming scale factor removes this discrepancy without choosing a new curvature, changing the waves' initial transfer amplitudes or tuning to the later metric. The signed six-term expansion is in launch-work.json; it agrees with the direct native difference to below 0.000000001 percentage points on all 24 modes.

**Precision and scope.** The original shear reconstruction, integrated shear rate, cold comparison and alternate quadrature use the same clocks and units. The quadrature change is below 0.00000017 percentage points over the pilot. The native solver emitted a Romberg warning in the corrected run (reported integral 192.5761, estimated error 1.46e-13 for requested 8.32e-14); this is retained, not treated as a physics pass. The heavy-field table is canonical only up to the installed exact-field limit. Its separate late fluid tail is untouched. There is no change to archived second-order pressure, photon launch, fit results, or observed data.
