# The post-switch pressure correction can be predicted from an early state

**The 43 pressure exceptions were predictable, not an adjustment to the wave after the fact.** A product of the homogeneous and perturbed fields supplies an additional dynamical variable. Evolving it together with energy, current and pressure predicts the slow pressure that was missing from the effective-fluid formula. On this short interval the forecast also works when the later gravity is **not** supplied, provided the surrounding matter is evolved rather than inferred from a derivative of the total gravity. The much smaller difference between the two branches is a more demanding test.

## Setup and causal separation

PROTOCOL.md derives the evolution. For L=a(am)(vf+bw)/ρ₀, the pressure rate is p′=−3Hd−2(am)L−kj−(e+s)X, and L′=2(am)[p+k²(p−d)/(4(am)²)]−lX. The metric drives both parts of this oscillator; setting L to zero loses information. With the seven coherent products prepared from the saved initial wave there is no new response coefficient or phase fit. These products are an exact re-expression of the retained quadratic model, not independent evidence of that model. The matched wave still requires a physical preparation. The two branches here are the resolved and phase-averaged heavy descriptions, not two cosmological fits.

- **Known metric:** launch at 89.15 using only the prepared state, then use the actual saved gravity as forcing. The retained six-π projection has 30 fully supported centers, 89.7125–89.89375 conformal Mpc. No later microscopic pressure or its derivative drives the prediction.
- **Early inputs only:** stop all inputs at 89.275. Evolve the scale factor, expansion and synchronous metric driving as well. Score only the 11 centers at 89.83125–89.89375 whose **entire** projection, including the left wing, lies after the cutoff on both clocks. The windows overlap one another; they are not 11 independent observations. Inputs and frozen numerical packets precede scoring. Older preparations and results were already known; none of these are observational holdouts.
- **Surrounding matter:** the first early-only trial extrapolated the non-heavy trace inferred from derivatives of gravity. The direct alternative evolves cold matter, baryons, photons and massless radiation with their continuity, Euler and drag equations on the new metric. The massive-neutrino trace at launch follows from its own energy equation and native current. We approximate its subsequent change, the unresolved shear, opacity and thermal terms; we do not replace the native momentum bank or assert a new exact matter model. ORDINARY.md gives the assumptions and controls. The slow scalar contributes separately. In the matched gravity equation, the old fluid stress is retained; the new pressure is not fed back into the metric used to score it.

The small correction is the projected microscopic pressure minus the original sound-speed law evaluated on that forecast's projected density and current. Its own scale can be much smaller than total pressure. The worst early direct correction errors are 1.546% and 1.879%. For the original 43 matched pressure exceptions, even **the correction itself** passes everywhere (worst 0.1175%). Adding the predicted correction to the separately saved native fluid pressure reproduces the microscopic pressure within 0.0392% on all 782 modes. That last calculation is a **readout check**, not a self-consistent new evolution with a modified fluid law.

The direct metric drivers recover the stored X to 0.000362% or better on either branch, versus 0.9554% RMS for the resolved early trace-tangent driver. The resolved trace-inferred starting slope differs from the directly reconstructed slope by 46 times the latter's norm, whereas the matched slopes agree to 0.000094%. It was not safe to estimate a relatively slow source by taking two derivatives of an oscillating total. Holding the supplied higher-source pieces fixed rather than continuing their slopes still passes total pressure on every mode, but misses the small correction in 19 resolved and 14 matched modes. Alternate derivative stencils and the explicitly labelled massless diagnostic do not remove the remaining exceptions. The finer integration changes the early direct small correction by less than 0.042% on every mode on either branch.

**Paired test:** the matched-minus-resolved difference of the small correction, scaled by that difference itself, has 0.0427% RMS error in the early direct forecast; 15/782 modes miss 0.5%, worst 4.5934%. Even the known-metric case has eleven relative contrast exceptions. Their absolute sizes are retained in the score files. None was discarded because it was small. This result does not determine a percentage change in the photons: the previously scored full-window photon discrepancy at 90.4 remains 0.65971% on the original small launch.

## Next: can the existing preparation specify the microscopic response?

Use the *existing* switch-table density and current, rather than reading a microscopic perturbation from a saved state. With fixed background and forcing, the underlying perturbation is a two-dimensional linear inhomogeneous evolution. Integrate two unit initial quadratures and a zero-initial forced response, phase-project their density and current with the retained canonical switch ruler, then test whether the resulting 2×2 map is invertible and well conditioned in each mode. Subtract the forced offset before inversion. In the constant-background harmonic limit the mean density measures the in-phase quadrature and mean current the out-of-phase quadrature; k=0 or a vanishing background is degenerate. This argument motivates a test, not a rank guarantee in the expanding calculation. Use only the prepared density/current as input. Freeze inferred states and the bilinear current, then check the withheld switch pressure, later microscopic replay and branch difference. Multiple incoherent populations need not be determined by these two moments. Do not install a photon source or infer galactic occupation from this result alone.
