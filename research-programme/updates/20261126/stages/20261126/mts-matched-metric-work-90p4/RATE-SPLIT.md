# Why can a photon potential-rate discrepancy survive a conserving companion?

This is a diagnostic of actual trajectories, not a new force or replacement forecast. Let subscripts d,m refer to density and momentum routes of the matched calculation. Define r_d=kappa_d−Phi_d and C_d=D r_d. The native equations evolve kappa_d′=pd_d, pd_d−pd_m=(J_d−J_m)/(2K)−H(Psi_d−Psi_m). The photon source actually uses the spline derivative of Phi_d minus the independent momentum rate. Hence its rate gap is the sum of:

1. minus the derivative of C_d(launch)/D(t);
2. minus the derivative of [C_d(t)−C_d(launch)]/D(t);
3. (J_d−J_m)/(2K);
4. −H(Psi_d−Psi_m);
5. the numerical remainder spline(kappa_d)′−spline(pd_d).

The first term tests an oscillating denominator acting on an existing numerator. The second tests the varying numerator on the density trajectory; it does not assert that this variation is independent of the incoming state. Keep the direct photon lapse source Psi_d−Psi_m as a sixth component. Project all six complex vectors independently before comparing their sum with the route difference. Include both the old matched branch and the metric-work branch, both gravity grids. A physically required correction can be too small to explain the observed gap. This decomposition distinguishes a metric-rate bookkeeping effect from the next perturbation/source calculation worth doing.
