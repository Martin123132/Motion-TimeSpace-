# Is the small resolved pressure drift well defined at this accuracy?
The original two-sided reference and its gate stay fixed. Subsampling it tests numerical sampling, not possible bias from decomposing a single physical history into a slow mean and harmonics. At the very small pressure drift scale, test the latter independently before using it to choose a new law. For the same six centers, both gravity routes and both resolutions, repeat the temporal projection with windows of 4, 5 and 6 pi on each side. At each width compare the original (slow degree 8; second harmonic degree 6; fourth degree 2), one with a sixth harmonic of degree 2, and a richer fixed set (slow degree 10; second degree 8; fourth degree 4; sixth degree 2). Coordinates run from -1 to 1; require full rank, numerical reconstruction of basis vectors, and record condition numbers. For the shorter support the higher-order columns may be hard to distinguish; failure of conditioning is a diagnostic, not a physical error. Check the derivative of the mean from the actual equation for V against the derivative obtained by differentiating the fitted intercept curve, without changing the primary definition.

These are stress-mean *definitions/approximations* rather than a statistical confidence band. Test each on an analytically constructed moving-envelope signal with frequencies and amplitude scales taken from the saved history. The known mean, not data agreement, judges that control. Report separately whether the original benchmark appears robust; do not pick an alternative that makes a proposed evolution pass.
