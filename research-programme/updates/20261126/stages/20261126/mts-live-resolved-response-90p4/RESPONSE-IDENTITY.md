# From a constraint discrepancy on one orbit to photons on two

Do not subtract two constraints evaluated on different states and call that a conservation law. At fixed background the retained second-order resolved evolution is affine in its state; all quadratic incident sources are supplied functions of time. Let x_M be the momentum trajectory, x_D the density trajectory, r_M=eκ x_M-g x_M-b and R=eκ-g. Let L be the full dynamical response to a unit change of the used potential at fixed state, including the lapse and bin response. The density-route RHS differs at a common state x from the momentum RHS by -L(Rx-b). Therefore d=x_D-x_M satisfies exactly

    d'=(A_M-LR)d - L r_M,
    Phi_D-Phi_M=g d-r_M,
    Psi_D-Psi_M=g d-r_M+slip_state(d).

The common explicit anisotropic stress cancels in the last expression, even though it changes r_M. Compute d by running the homogeneous density-route matter/operator and all 77 responding bins with **only** the virtual density-source numerator -D r_M. This is an algebraic representation of the difference, not a new gravity interaction. The absolute calculation must carry the actual nonzero difference of the two incoming route states. Differences of two source experiments start with zero *change* in that route-state difference. Keep the full spectral and bin dynamics; the unchanged incoming microscopic sources cancel.

Before opening the full later comparison, use each independent three-state launch forecast for the absolute r_M, and the independent source-only scalar for repaired-minus-original δr_M. Propagate these predictions to the two-route potential/lapse difference. The predicted metric derivative is the derivative of Phi_D-Phi_M; compare against the actual density derivative minus the *independent native* momentum rate, rather than assuming these are numerically identical. Use the usual matched-minus-resolved sign, full visibility weights and old cumulative photon boundary. Do not fit a phase or subtract the final gap by hand. An accurate forecast of the inconsistency is a diagnostic, not a reason to remove it from the equations.
