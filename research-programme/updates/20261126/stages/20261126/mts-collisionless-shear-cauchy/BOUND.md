# How much influence can the unretained angular boundary have?

For a collisionless momentum node, fix the entire metric driving, scale factor and k. Start two solutions with identical F₀ through F_L. Suppose the difference in the supplied F_(L+1) histories is bounded by M throughout a window. The difference of the low moments obeys a linear inhomogeneous transport equation with an input only in row L and multiplier at most kv(L+1)/(2L+1). The interior streaming matrix is tridiagonal and its maximum absolute row sum is at most kv. To reach row 2 from this input requires at least L−2 internal links. The time-ordered series of the forced solution, including integration of the boundary input, therefore gives

    |ΔF₂| ≤ M (L+1)/(2L+1) Σ_{j=L−1}^∞ x^j/j! ,   x = ∫ k v dη ≤ k Δη.

This is a conditional bound, not a claim that M equals one, nor that the closure has no effect. The terminal free-streaming prescription used by the code is equivalent to supplying F_(L+1)=(2L+1)F_L/(kvη)−F_(L−1). The statement applies to that boundary compared with any other specified boundary on the same geometry. For massive shear multiply each node's bound by its positive weight 2Kwv/3 and sum. It does not automatically bound induced gravitational or photon feedback; those are compared by actually propagating the difference. tail_bound.py uses a positive series and a geometric upper bound on its numerical remainder, and checks it against the matrix exponential for a unit constant boundary at fixed speed. The separate angular-resolution rerun tests the chosen data and closure directly.
