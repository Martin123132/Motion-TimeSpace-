# Turning the remaining matched-branch photon error into a physical test

The matched solver does not use the same homogeneous heavy wave as the previous resolved solver. Its own Host.environment reads a fluid expansion history, evaluates an independently integrated Klein–Gordon oscillator b,v and changes the density-potential denominator by 3(v²−rho)/2, where rho=rho0/a is the homogeneous heavy-fluid enthalpy in conformally weighted units. Distinguish this from simply interpolating a bad velocity table.

For K=k² define T=D−K+3(H′−H²), inferring H′ independently from the saved background rather than imposing the pressure constraint. Then T_host=T_fluid+3(v²−rho)/2 on that fixed history. Test the scale and sign of both terms. An instantaneous oscillating enthalpy need not equal the coarse one. This comparison is on the homogeneous coefficients, not the full perturbations.

If the other species and fluid metric are fixed, embedding the explicit canonical wave into that *same* homogeneous stress budget leaves a unique difference in the heavy stress components: E_c=rho−(v²+μ²b²)/2 and P_c=−(v²−μ²b²)/2. Their sum is rho−v², exactly the missing homogeneous enthalpy. This is bookkeeping for a two-description comparison, not new matter. With constant mass, b′=v, v′=−2Hv−μ²b and alpha=a′/a, the residual in their common energy balance is

    E_c′ + H(E_c+3P_c) = (H−alpha)(rho+μ²b²).

Test it by differentiating E_c independently, and by integrating its predicted change from the incoming energy on each saved grid. Neither this identity nor cancellation of T after the homogeneous difference is included proves the matched *perturbation* equations already contain its metric, pressure or current response. Never change D alone or force the photon gap to zero; derive the perturbation of the same stress, with the already installed memory contribution counted once.
