# A real background consistency repair, not a new photon result

**26 November 2026.** We tested the phase-dependent energy/current source on the retained 89.65-Mpc restart. The new microscopic calculation predicts the physical wave's energy and current but does **not** improve the existing pressure result. Following that failure isolated a different, testable problem in the wave history itself. Evolving the homogeneous field and its velocity together removes most of the disagreement between two independent first-order gravity equations in a controlled 24-mode calculation.

## What the frozen prediction actually did

- Native first-order Cauchy data through 89.65, on 782 wave numbers, reproduce the original projected quadratic energy and current to better than 0.000000003% at the incoming knots. Sixteen angles were prepared before testing them. Nonheavy forcing after launch is the change predicted by the previous 782-mode hierarchy, not a new fit. At the real orientation the forecast errors on the short-window changes are 0.02601% (energy) and 0.000047% (current), fine sampling. These are internal numerical comparisons, not measurements of the CMB.
- We then evolved the new angular load with its delayed compensating current, U, ordinary matter and 77 collisionless bins. With the combined-load equations, fixed six centers, unchanged normalization and 0.5% tolerance, the p32 /1280 momentum-route pressure-drift error is **0.13314%**, and the density-route error is **14.74004%** rather than the previous **5.36337%**. Independent reduced readouts give **0.1430% / 14.7237%**; p16 agrees. Starting the current compensation one rather than two half-periods before launch gives 14.73366% on density. The split between p16 and p32 is not the reason for failure. The unchanged physical orientation, the older passing momentum calculation and the new miss stay on record. **Do not promote the new density correction.**
- Projecting a *single* independently evolved physical orientation over the complete temporal support separates the angular preparation from errors in the forward physical source. The errors in that physical source, not merely angular sampling, dominate the small gravitational load harmonic. An exact finite-difference product split attributes ~109% / 103% of this forward discrepancy (cosine / sine signed projection) to the linear shear time-coordinate conversion; the background and other contributions partially cancel it. About 99% of the signed discrepancy in each component arises after launch. These percentages use a very small harmonic, not the total gravitational force. The linear projection is evaluated over 1510 ordered slots drawing on 106 distinct legs of the 782-mode bank.

## A test of the gravity equations themselves

The gauge transformation gives α'+2Hα=etak/k−Π/k², with α=σ/k. A frozen shear-transport prediction improves the short physical energy-change error from 0.0260% to 0.00707%. However, when we integrate the equation against the *archived* Weyl/stress history, 1 of the 782 wave numbers fails the 0.5% change gate: index 37, k≈0.5646661 in inverse conformal Mpc. Removing it is not legitimate: the spatial operator uses it. Its total change is only 0.001426% of the shear level, so an apparently tiny absolute discrepancy on this mode matters. The archived gate is still failed. We did not put that shear variant into a photon or pressure forecast.

We called the original native solver for the complete 24-mode block containing that mode, on one continuous interval and two time samplings. Its own reported shear derivative agrees pointwise with the derived rate to better than 1e-10% of the peak rate. Its integral differs from its own reported change by about 1.10–1.13% at k37, including on the unspliced run. The Weyl/anisotropy convention is independently confirmed: the **full native** dgpi has coefficient one in k²(Phi−Psi). The factor 3/2 applies only to the separately normalized massive-species S in the projected hierarchy. No interpolation order or fit was chosen to erase the discrepancy.

The pressure-free combination of Einstein constraints and total conservation in CONSERVATION.md accounts for the independent k37 residual to 0.000000017 percentage points of the small shear change. The pressure-free stress residual carries ~99% of its signed projection. Under exactly the same 24-mode experiment the cold reference passes, with a 0.000216% k37 residual (MTS 1.1264%). The heavy energy equation, not a baryonic or radiation null, supplies the main MTS residual. Its separate momentum residual is small. The product-rule decomposition in SECTORS.md identifies the two homogeneous field/velocity defects as dominant and opposing; the product-rule vs sampled-source difference is itself up to 0.107 percentage points, so this is not a precision attribution of every small term.

## Controlled repair: evolve the background as a pair

We kept the original incoming state and measured surrounding source *histories*, removed the tabulated heavy contribution from the totals, and evolved b, v and the heavy perturbation as field equations together with a,H,etak and α. Two independent versions used the density or the transported shear to drive the wave; neither reset curvature. On both time steps (1/640 and 1/1280 Mpc) the discrepancy between the two constraints **within each repaired trajectory** is at most **0.01450%** on all 24 tested modes. Cold passes at **0.00936%** worst. For the formerly failing heavy mode the two routes yield 0.01447% and 0.01449%. They differ from the *unrepaired archived shear change* there by ~1.139%: the original target has not silently been relabelled a pass. All four runs used the original 0.5% limit. This is conditional on supplied surrounding matter, not a cosmological fit or an independently predicted 782-mode solution.
