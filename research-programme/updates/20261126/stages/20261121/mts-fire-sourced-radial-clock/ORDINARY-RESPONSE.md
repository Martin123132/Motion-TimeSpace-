# What ordinary matter would do at the shared launch

A frozen foreground is useful as a control but it need not be a conserved, dynamically matched ordinary source in both fields. Here the density is the one **implied by the retained ordinary Newtonian potential**, \(\rho_o=\Delta V_o\) in the existing dimensionless Poisson units. It is a foreground model with FIRE-2 m12i baryonic changes, not a catalogue of measured velocities, a new independent galaxy, or a positive three-dimensional particle census. Report weighted signed mass moments, not tracer counts.

## Second derivative: no fitted pressure or velocity

Suppose the ordinary phase-space distribution starts the same at snapshot 590 in both arms, and that ordinary matter couples universally to the two gravitational potentials. At that instant all ordinary density, current and stress **differences** vanish. The difference in acceleration is \(-\nabla d\), \(d=h_c-h_r\). Ordinary momentum and continuity then require
\[
 \delta\dot J_i=-\rho_o\partial_i d,\qquad
 \delta\ddot\rho_o=\nabla\cdot(\rho_o\nabla d).
\]
No presumption of pressureless matter is required for this initial derivative: the shared initial stress cancels. For different nonmetric forces the equation must include their difference. These derivatives are not predictions for 22 Myr of ordinary evolution with a rigid halo.

In each of the previous two complete radial halves use \(b=[4p(1-p)]^3,\ p=(y-y_a)/(y_b-y_a)\) on the open interval, zero outside. This compact weight is \(C^2\). The initial weighted ordinary mass and its second derivative are \(M_* \int y^2 b\langle\rho_o\rangle\,dy\) and \(-M_*/t_*^2\int y^2 b'\langle\rho_o d'\rangle dy\) with \(t_*=L/v\). Positive curvature means more ordinary mass weighted by that particular radial window in the coupled history, not more total baryons. With \(B_b=\int y^2 b\,dy\), the regular Gauss equation further gives the initial curvature of the **ordinary matter's own radial gravitational acceleration**, averaged with \(y^2b/B_b\):
\[
 \delta\ddot{\bar g}_{o,b}=-(v^2/L)t_*^{-2}\int y^2 b\langle\rho_o d'\rangle\,dy/B_b .
\]
This is not the same as the differential acceleration *on* that matter, which weights \(-d'\) by ordinary mass. No baryonic rotation speed or fitted normalization enters either. The equivalent particle weight for the gravity observable is \(W(s)=-\int_{\max(s,y_a)}^{y_b}b(y)dy/B_b\) inside the outer edge and zero beyond it. Displacing the same matter distribution in the two leading gravitational fields checks the mass and gravity moments independently. The aperture calculation includes a padded region so that matter can move into as well as out of it.

**Support needed to keep an ordinary foreground frozen.** In a regular spherical projection the same density history in both arms implies the same monopole radial current. Let \(\Pi_t=(\Pi_{\theta\theta}+\Pi_{\phi\phi})/2\), and let stress differences below be angular averages. The radial momentum equation integrated against \(b y^2\) then requires
\[
 \int y^2 b\langle\rho_o d'\rangle\,dy =
 \int [y^2 b'\delta\Pi_{rr}+2yb\,\delta\Pi_t]\,dy .
\]
Its absolute left side divided by \(\int [y^2|b'|+2y b]dy\) is a lower bound on the maximum magnitude of at least one of those stress differences if gravity is the only changed force. The bound does not choose an equation of state or equate total stress with scalar gas pressure. Alternatively there must be ordinary motion or an externally supplied force. Thus **keeping both ordinary density history and ordinary stress unchanged is not a free dynamical closure** in this comparison.

We calculate the force density in two ways. The potential inferred from the archive is piecewise polynomial in \(yV_o\). Its distributional Laplacian includes element-face density terms proportional to \([V_{o,y}]\); include them explicitly, not as a physical sheet of stars. In the independent weak evaluation integrate the source against \(b\,d'\) or \(-b'd'\) by parts, using exact finite-radius multipole Green values and the Poisson equation for \(d''\). Repeating the test with a polynomial in \(V_o\) rather than \(yV_o\) exposes the interpolation variation. No unknown molecular gas, unsampled galaxy or boundary mass is filled in to enforce these identities.

## Third derivative: which current is needed?

For a collisionless ordinary species with a shared initial distribution and purely gravitational change, characteristics give for either radial test weight \(W\)
\[
 {\cal O}^{(3)} =
 -\int \rho_o\,\nabla W\cdot\nabla\dot d\,d\Gamma
 -\int {\bf J}\cdot(3\,{\rm Hess}\,W\,\nabla d+{\rm Hess}\,d\,\nabla W)\,d\Gamma .
\]
Here \(d\Gamma\) denotes the position volume in native units, and \({\bf J}\) is the shared ordinary mass current. The common velocity **dispersion** cancels at this derivative; it is not generally absent at later orders. An initially shared collisional medium requires its force/stress evolution to match for this collisionless expression to apply. Obtain \(\dot d\) by applying the same open Poisson map to each wave's \(2\Re(a^*\dot a)\) from its own saved Hamiltonian. The current term in an axisymmetric experiment does not involve \(J_\phi\): azimuthal rotation does not by itself supply the missing information. Validate the formula first with displaced characteristics and then via independent cubic Taylor coefficients of radial distance and the test weights. Velocity controls are synthetic, not inferred galactic streaming.

**Using the saved geometry rather than assigning all velocities.** For a radial weight, the vector multiplying the current with its minus sign can be written as \(-\nabla F + H\hat r\), where
\[
 F=W'(d'+2d/y),\qquad H=-2(W''-W'/y)(d'-d/y).
\]
The continuity equation gives \(-\int{\bf J}\cdot\nabla F=-\int F\dot\rho_o\); the weights eliminate surface terms. Moreover regularity fixes \(\langle J_r\rangle=-\partial_y\langle\dot V_o\rangle\) using \(\Delta\dot V_o=\dot\rho_o\). Hence the entire unspecified term in either readout reduces to
\[
 \int y^2\langle J_r(H-\langle H\rangle)\rangle\,dy .
\]
The remaining correlation is between **radial mass flow and angle**. It need not vanish in a flattened galaxy. It is not an arbitrary dispersion tensor, unknown azimuthal rotation curve, or a new gravitational constant. Both formulas were checked with the current \(-\nabla\dot V_o\) and with an independent divergence-free, axisymmetric poloidal circulation; the latter has no density derivative but a real residual. Neither is designated as the galaxy's actual current.

If the radial flow magnitude on the retained source measure is bounded by \(u_{\max}\), the absolute remainder is at most \(u_{\max}\int y^2\langle|\rho_o|\,|H-\langle H\rangle|\rangle dy\) in the corresponding units. Use total variation, including the small numerical face measure; do not assume every reconstructed cell has positive density. The implied speed threshold is necessary, not a measurement or a sufficient profile. The determined part still needs a density time derivative. We use the right-hand slope of the retained 590→600 interpolation and separately the left-hand 586→590 slope. They need not agree: neither is an instantaneous measured velocity of the Milky Way. This distinction matters more here than most numerical quadrature changes.
