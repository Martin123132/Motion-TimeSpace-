# Four pions in the real-photon gravitational response

**21 November 2026 · Local matching calculation, no fitted parameter.** On the same pion effective action and common metric as the saved stress calculation, the leading direct four-pion channel **adds** to the low-energy inverse moment. From the equal-mass threshold (0.5582816 GeV) to 0.800 GeV it contributes **+4.51176 × 10⁻¹⁰ GeV⁻²**. That is **7.84 parts per million** of the saved +5.75150 × 10⁻⁵ GeV⁻² two-pion reference over exactly the same window. This is a signed interference calculation, not an integral of a positive cross section. It does not assume that the two-pion term itself stays unchanged when inelastic channels open.

The conditional product of norm ratios there is 9.39 × 10⁻⁵, **not an experimental bound**. **A comparison that distinguishes a numerical error from an inadequate physical approximation.** For two real photons forming a charged pair plus two neutral pions, we computed the *unpolarized physical rate* separately from the singlet spin-two interference. At five fixed energies our rate differs by 0.20–0.24% from the corresponding lower-curve Monte Carlo markers in Fig. 5.9 of [Duell's independent chiral calculation](https://users.ph.nat.tum.de/T39/T39_files/T39_people_files/duell_files/Dipl-MultiPion.pdf). The comparison is of tree theories, not observations; we did not normalize to Duell's points. [Five extracted/calculated pairs](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `external-comparison.csv`). The fraction refers to the difference between central values, not a common estimated error bar.

At 0.85 GeV, our central point prediction is **0.0672 nb**, against the first [ARGUS tabulated central value](https://doi.org/10.17182/hepdata.29853.v1/t1) of **22.5 ± 11.7 nb**, with a separate 13.5% systematic. It is outside our integration window; the table provides the energy center, not a bin-response function. The large ratio of central values is not a precise exclusion significance. We did not rescale the photon wave to that point: an unpolarized single-charge rate does not isolate the required signed spin-two overlap. The small direct tree contribution is therefore a useful baseline for matching, **not** evidence that real inelastic corrections are this small.

**Numerical controls.** Both photon Ward identities hold; dropping their contact term produces a large residual. Charged-pair Born angular dependence and its sign, four-pion threshold, photon interchange and pion permutation are checked. The tensor projected directly over charges, polarizations and beam directions agrees with the fast representation. An explicit two-pion angular integration recovers the previous inverse-moment normalization. Maximum relative difference across the retained grid comparisons on the four primary integrands/observables is 0.048%; the two finest window evaluations differ by 0.00165%. These concern numerical integration, not chiral truncation. No local curvature contact, LMC preparation or force band was altered.

**Next.** Derive the inelastic contribution *back into the two-pion production amplitudes*: project the on-shell four-to-two strong transition onto isospin zero and spin two, then contract it separately with the photon and total-stress sources here. [Bijnens and Husek](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.104.054046) give an independent six-pion amplitude in the same low-energy matter theory. Check crossing, symmetry factors and unitarity before using it. Its discontinuity alone will not fix a dispersive subtraction or deliver the absolute parent photon–curvature coupling. Keep the inadequacy of the absolute unpolarized rate visible; do not repair it by fitting a common multiplier to the mixed product.
