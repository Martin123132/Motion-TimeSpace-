# Which pressure term is actually constrained?

The autonomous pilot deliberately switched off the gas-pressure response. Rather than assign it an arbitrary speed of sound, the saved FIRE-2 m12i snapshot provides a distinct input: specific internal energy \(u\) in (km/s)². See [Wetzel et al., DR1 §4.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.06969). Four raw-field receipts record the public file, version identity and hashes. Only the required launch fields were read; stable native-within-shard ordering and verified ParticleIDs link each energy to its own mass and cell. This is simulation thermodynamics, not an observed Milky Way temperature or an adiabatic derivative measured by a single snapshot.

**Derivation.** Give a parcel an adiabatic equation of state \(e(\rho,s)\), and let \(J\) be the determinant of its deformation gradient. Its energy per original volume is \(F(J)=\rho_0 e(\rho_0/J,s_0)\). The first law gives \(F'(1)=-p\) and \(F''(1)=\Gamma_1p\), with \(\Gamma_1=(\partial\ln p/\partial\ln\rho)_s\). The determinant has first variation \(\mathrm{div}\,\xi\) and mixed second variation \((\mathrm{div}\,\xi)(\mathrm{div}\,\eta)-\mathrm{tr}[(D\xi)(D\eta)]\). Therefore
\[
  H_p[\xi,\eta]=\int p\{(\Gamma_1-1)(\nabla\!\cdot\xi)(\nabla\!\cdot\eta)
                 +{\rm tr}[(D\xi)(D\eta)]\}\,dV.
\]
This formula is a material energy variation; no numerical gradient of the noisy gas temperature or density is required. It does not include exterior boundary traction or feedback heating. For the curl-free physical direction \(a=-\nabla(\Phi_{w,c}-\Phi_{w,r})\), the gradient is symmetric and
\[
 H_p[a,a]=\int p\,[\|{\rm dev}(Da)\|^2+(\Gamma_1-2/3)(\nabla\!\cdot a)^2]\,dV .
\]
It is nonnegative for \(p\ge0,\Gamma_1\ge2/3\). With \(M_a=\int\rho |a|^2\,dV\), the returning *pressure contribution alone* to \(\int dm\,a\cdot\delta X\), relative to the leading \(t^2M_a/2\), is \(-t^2H_p[a,a]/(12M_a)\). This is a short-time coefficient: the induced displacement is initially \(t^2a/2\), the linearized material pressure force is minus the energy Hessian applied to that displacement, and integrating twice gives \(-t^4 H_p/24\) in this projection. Other fourth-order terms such as initial transport, common forces evaluated at different positions, time-varying gravity and non-adiabatic exchange are not bounded by this coefficient. In particular it does **not** supply a clock-pressure bound. That mixed observable requires \(H_p[\nabla K_C,a]\), boundary/weight treatment and control of the kernel derivative at the two radii.

**Implementation.** Differentiate the smooth wave potentials, not the point-mass distribution of the conjugate grid (its radial second derivative contains sheets). Use all four independent entries of the spherical axisymmetric Hessian, including both tangential connections and both copies of the radial-angular off-diagonal. Check the trace independently with the radial Poisson expression. The squared traceless and divergence terms are integrated separately over angular source bins. Finite homogeneous deformations of an ideal gas, including traceless and shear examples, test the full determinant formula independently. No feedback parameter was obtained from the clock or energy residual.

We evaluate two **conditional** ideal-gas rules, \(\Gamma_1=\gamma=5/3\) and \(7/5\), \(p/\rho=(\gamma-1)u\). Neither forces the effective FIRE cooling/feedback law to be adiabatic. The raw-gas result is an interior contribution; the exterior is not assumed vacuum. The *represented* version assigns the same measured specific energy to the positive template gas and evaluates the energy of that effective material, not a passive tracer's independent pressure in the original FIRE fluid. These are separate from the pressureless live pilot. A time at which the leading coefficient would be one percent is a scale diagnostic, not a prediction that the Taylor expansion holds that long.
