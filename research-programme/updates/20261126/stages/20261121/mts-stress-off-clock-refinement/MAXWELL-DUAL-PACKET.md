# A finite Maxwell packet that isolates the displayed curvature–photon source

This is a **separate analytical consequence** of the retained low-energy action, checkpoint 4947 (not bundled here), not a cosmological measurement or a term added to the stress-withdrawal simulations. The earlier planar test (not bundled here) removed the density signal but its plus-polarization readout also killed the displayed Weyl–photon vertex. Here the source has finite total energy and there is a readout in which that vertex survives. Work classically on a locally flat background at leading weak gravity and quadratic order in source amplitude, signature −+++, c_light=1, same κ for matter and gravity. Write c_W=c_IR for the coefficient of +C_abcd F^ab F^cd, with **no** extra factor 1/4. Higher derivative and higher field orders are not included.

**Use two different source preparations, not a change of gravity.** Pair a smooth source-free vacuum Maxwell solution F with its electric–magnetic dual. Their canonical stress and Poynting flux agree pointwise. Their integrated four-momenta also agree for these finite packets. This follows directly from E→B, B→−E. No magnetic charges are needed: both initial data satisfy the ordinary source-free divergence constraints. This operation is not a claimed symmetry of the curvature correction. Use identical gravitational boundary conditions; do not hold inconsistent constraint data fixed when the curvature source changes.

**Local conserved source.** Let W^abcd be the algebraic Weyl projection of F^ab F^cd: first remove its totally antisymmetric 4-form, then its Ricci and scalar traces. On the flat background the only first variation of the vertex is W^abcd δR_abcd. With the same Riemann sign as the previous test, two integrations by parts yield

    T_W^ad = 4 c_W ∂_b ∂_c W^abcd .

It is identically conserved by antisymmetry and trace-free by projection. Its integrated energy and momentum vanish for sufficiently decaying sources (at least one derivative is spatial). The local density need not vanish. The electric part W_0i0j=(E_i E_j−B_i B_j)^TF/2 gives

    T_W^00 = −2 c_W ∂_i∂_j(E_i E_j−B_i B_j)^TF .

W flips sign under the dual; the minimal stress does not. Thus **original minus dual** in the trace of the freely falling tidal acceleration A=−R_00 is −2κ T_W^00 for this operator. This is Ricci focusing, not a far-field gravitational-wave strain. The stress improvement has zero net energy without either physical electromagnetic preparation having zero energy.

**Finite packet and its time curve.** Use divergence-free initial E=0 and B=B_* (−y,x,0) exp(−r²/2L²)/L and its dual partner. In the original, the subsequent B is the curl of e_z ∂t φ and E is curl curl(e_z φ), where φ solves the free wave equation with φ(0)=0, ∂tφ(0)=B_* L exp(−r²/2L²). The radial Kirchhoff solution at the origin is φ(t,0)=t B_* L exp(−t²/2L²). Put s=t/L; the on-axis electric field at the origin is e(t)=2B_* (s−s³/3) exp(−s²/2). There B remains zero, its antisymmetric spatial gradient has strength e′/2, and the electric field has zero first spatial derivative. Applying the divergence-free product rule gives the complete Weyl-vertex prediction there:

    ΔA_W(t,0) = 4 κ c_W [ 5(e′)²/6 − 2 e e″/3 ] .

The primes are physical time derivatives. A free Fourier propagation provides an independent numerical test. In particular at t=0 both canonical fields vanish at the central point, yet ΔA_W=(40/3)κ c_W B_*²/L². Spatial derivatives of the fields are what matter. The canonical Maxwell tide difference vanishes, including away from that one point.

**Finite detector and same-order scalar nuisance.** At launch average over a normalized three-dimensional Gaussian detector with standard deviation R per axis and the same inertial tetrad in both preparations. Integrate the double divergence by parts. Since r·B=0, its angular moment and the Gaussian fourth moment give

    ⟨ΔA_W⟩ = (40/3) κ c_W (B_*²/L²) (1+2R²/L²)^(−7/2).

**Eliminate the field normalization.** In the displayed Maxwell units the original and dual pulse each have total canonical energy E_pkt = π^(3/2) B_*² L³/2. The same detector result is therefore (80/[3π^(3/2)]) κ c_W (E_pkt/L⁵) (1+2R²/L²)^(−7/2). The canonical curvature difference vanishes. At fixed prepared energy and fixed R/L, doubling the packet size suppresses the nonminimal difference by **32**, without knowing c_W. This ratio is meaningful only for a nonzero difference; at c_W=0 the absolute prediction is zero at either size. An independent radial check normalizes ∫B²/2 first, then transfers both derivatives to the detector: the remaining integral is 4κc_W/(3R⁴) times the Gaussian average of r²B² because r·B=0. This uses a proper detector length in the same fixed frame, not a change of electromagnetic or gravitational units. Pure curvature corrections to the linear gravitational propagator act on identical minimal stresses in the pair; their product with the curvature–photon vertex is beyond this derivative order.

Do not interpret any dual difference as unique to the Weyl operator if other matched operators are allowed. At this order a parity-even Ricci vertex b R_mn F^ma F^n_a is b R_mn T_Maxwell^mn + b R F²/4. The first term has identical flat-background variation for the two fields, so only an effective scalar coefficient d multiplying R F² can contaminate this test. Its metric source is 2d(∂^a∂^b−η^ab □)F². Defining Y=⟨Δ_spatial F²⟩ and Z=⟨□F²⟩ in the **original** Maxwell field, the pair gives ΔA_d=2κd(3Z−2Y) and ΔR_scalar=12κd Z. The Weyl vertex has zero scalar curvature source at this order. Therefore the readout

    ⟨Δ(−R_00)⟩ − [1/2 − Y/(3Z)] ⟨ΔR_scalar⟩

retains exactly the finite-detector Weyl prediction while rejecting d, when Z≠0. At the point centre the weight is **7/18**. For the finite packet let v=(R/L)²/[1+2(R/L)²] and f=(B_*²/L²)[1+2(R/L)²]^(−3/2). Direct Maxwell differentiation gives Y=f(8−56v+80v²), Z=f(24−48v+80v²). An unnormalized combination 6Z⟨ΔA⟩−(3Z−2Y)⟨ΔR_scalar⟩ does not require dividing by a small Z. The scalar curvature is a second geometric readout requiring more information than one tidal trace alone.

**What this can and cannot identify.** A normalized measurement of the combination at known B_*, L, R would constrain κ c_W in this fixed common-metric frame. κ is the same independently calibrated source normalization as in the Newton sector. The calculation neither derives a numerical c_W nor distinguishes an MTS contribution to it from conventional electromagnetic matching. Curvature–photon terms are also generated in conventional curved-space QED ([Drummond & Hathrell, original calculation](https://journals.aps.org/prd/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevD.22.343)). Generic parity-violating terms, higher operators, evolving the photon on a substantial external curvature, material detector response, or a change of field variables without transforming the rods are outside this isolation argument. No claim of experimental sensitivity. This is a conditional *shape and null* prediction without an extra gravitational fit, additional to the [GR 3+1 matter framework](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0703035).
