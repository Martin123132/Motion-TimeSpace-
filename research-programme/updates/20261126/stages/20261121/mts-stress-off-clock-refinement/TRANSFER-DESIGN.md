# Test the physical transfer, not just the zero-stress diagnostic

Operational gate: require the late-third minus late-half final contrast below 1 ppm of the original Newton magnitude on each sign, width and reader before spending compute on the new gain-on branch. This is a phase-sampling safeguard, not a replacement for the 20 ppm physics band. If it fails, retain the original clock results and investigate phase dependence first.
If both complete half-step stress-off histories are finite and reclaim the original endpoints on native replay, evolve the **unmodified** wave and cold matter from exactly the same formation at the same half-step clock, on the same 960 mesh for both signs. This is needed because the zero-stress intervention is an experiment, not the physical retained action. Do not add a fitted stress coefficient. Use only the existing mechanical forecasts, frozen here before any native-stress half-step results, to test:

- Physical branch versus matched Newton alone and matched Newton plus the propagating stress (old published forms). Same inputs, both apertures, both readers, all four clocks, same 20 ppm rule.
- Physical minus withdrawn tide D. The competing predictions are zero, the induced density P alone, and P plus the retarded transverse stress and moving contact T. This compares two trajectories with identical initial data and a conserved change of source, not a new rotation-curve fit. The contact belongs in the differential comparison; do not silently change the original absolute Newton-plus-TT benchmark.
- Report U=(D−P)/T. Unit transfer is the predicted response in this experiment. For a descriptive inverse test, the original 20 ppm tolerance implies |D−P−uT|≤20×10⁻⁶ |N| per row in the same units. Intersect the resulting closed intervals without treating any clock or reader as a new independent sample. It is *not* a statistical confidence interval on Newton's constant or a proof of the whole action. A tighter agreement of the numerical values is not in itself a tighter uncertainty bound.
- Check that the two gains have identical spherical evolutions. If they do not, stop and inspect instead of subtracting unlike baselines. On native stress the linearized material angular stress is retained along with the complete metric and surroundings. There is no reason to assume the absolute Newton residual and the causal stress response have the same amplitude.
