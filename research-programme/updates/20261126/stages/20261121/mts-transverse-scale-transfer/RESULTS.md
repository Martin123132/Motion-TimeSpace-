# Transverse momentum test and a signed W-boson contribution

**What changed in the retained theory:** the known electroweak part of the local photon–curvature coupling no longer needs a sign-unknown W allowance. In the existing mostly-plus optical convention the one-loop W contribution is **−5.8344 × 10⁻⁴¹ m²**, 1/600 of the old absolute ceiling. This uses the minimally coupled SM gauge-vector sector already retained. The sign is relative to the **positive** Dirac optical coefficient. The heavy-bath, gravity and galaxy preparations were not changed. This is a signed match to a published matter result, not a derivation of the electroweak sector from the parent or a full coefficient for light propagation.

- **Two momenta:** we derived a signed spectral integral for each of the two frozen transverse projections. A local dimension-six Weyl term cancels exactly in the difference. In 20 scalar/fermion comparisons the integral reproduces direct changes of the independently normalized determinants. Dimension-eight terms survive; a three-scale difference removes a term linear in squared momentum but not the next order. Positive and negative parts of the transverse spectrum were resolved explicitly. A positive charge-running spectrum cannot be substituted for this one. [Derivation and controlled limits](TRANSVERSE.md).
- **Absolute information:** for the two known free determinants the bounded signed spectral density proves high-momentum decay. This makes it possible to recover the induced local coefficients (+1/180, −1/90 in Euclidean units) from scale differences plus a controlled tail. An arbitrary independent local parent contribution remains invisible. For the scalar lattice check the leading Maxwell signal was explicitly added and removed on each grid; the finest tested *differences* still miss the continuum by **3.56% and 12.88%** on the two sets of momenta. Do not normalize a hadronic measurement on one coarse lattice.
- **Independent real-photon check:** an angular projection of scalar pair production reproduces the published helicity-flip cut. Integrating the cut gives the scalar and fermion low-energy signs used in the earlier optical matching. Bringing the gauge-vector cut into that same convention gives the signed coefficient above. The known lepton plus W subtotal for the previous binary sight line is still about **+5.46 × 10⁻⁵² s**. It is a computed subtotal, not an observable timing detection. [Physical input, signs and units](REAL-PHOTONS.md); signed update (not bundled here).
- **A real hadronic handle:** a charged two-pion cut involves the equal-helicity D wave for two real photons times the conjugate pion spin-two stress form factor. With the 140 [Belle energy bins](https://doi.org/10.17182/hepdata.51842.v1/t1) we bounded the measured angular and energy **part** of that inverse moment per unit of the maximum pion stress form factor. Measured-region result (not bundled here). No pion form factor was set to one in this bound. In the solvable point-scalar control the omitted angles actually **reverse the sign**, and most of its inverse moment lies below the Belle window. This gives a reason to tackle the lower-energy elastic channel first rather than fitting the visible resonance alone.
