# A mass dependence that an unknown finite intercept cannot imitate

**Conditional low-energy statement, not a four-dimensional existence theorem or a measured hadronic coefficient.** Work at leading order in the external electromagnetic coupling in isospin-symmetric QCD (no dynamical photon), with two equal light quark masses varied together, fixed nonzero strange/heavy masses, standard spontaneously broken SU(2) chiral symmetry, no extra massless charged matter, and the same renormalized metric and current at every mass. Use an analytic, mass-independent short-distance matching convention. These assumptions are imported properties of the retained matter sector, not derived here from MTS. No result here bounds the finite physical-mass QCD contact.

**Why the charged scalar benchmark matters.** At leading chiral order the covariant kinetic term is proportional to tr(DU DU†) with the physical electromagnetic charge matrix in the vector derivative. Expanded to quadratic order in the pion fields this is one complex minimally coupled charged pion, of charge e(Q_u−Q_d), plus an electromagnetically neutral pion. The factor of the decay constant cancels in canonical normalization. A curvature-only constant has no two photon derivatives; a scalar curvature improvement drops out on the TT shear. The vacuum has no linear pion source for these vector insertions. At one loop the quadratic fluctuation determinant therefore supplies the leading nonanalytic inverse-pion-mass-squared term. This is not the assertion that all pion form factors or all neutral hadrons are pointlike.

In the local Taylor limit define c_E(M) as the Euclidean Weyl coefficient selected by the three readouts, for pion pole mass M at those quark masses. Under standard mesonic counting, external eA is order p, its field strength order p² and curvature order p². A pure local curvature–two-field-strength operator starts at p⁶. The one-loop leading-order action is order p⁴ and, when expanded at |p_external|≪M, supplies p⁶/M². The next order receives two-loop leading-action terms, one-loop p⁴ insertions and p⁶ local terms; at this order the allowed mass dependence **of the coefficient** is a constant, log M² and at most log²M², with appropriate dimensionful constants. The subtraction scale stays fixed. NLO couplings and chiral logarithms need not be numerically small at available lattice masses. See [Donoghue & Leutwyler on curved chiral EFT](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01560453), [curved-space source extension](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.102.076023) and [this stage's scalar determinant](LOOPS.md). The test follows by combining these ingredients; it is not a new prediction of QCD from the gravitational sector.

    c_E(M) = A/M² + B₀ + B₁ log(M²/μ²) + B₂ log²(M²/μ²)
             + O(M² times up to cubic chiral logs) at the next chiral order,
    A = e²(Q_u−Q_d)² / [180(4π)²].

The two-mass difference [c_E(M)−c_E(rM)]/[M⁻²−(rM)⁻²] removes B₀ even before taking M small. A cleaner asymptotic test takes masses squared M²,2M²,4M²,8M² and forms

    A_est = 8 M² [c_E(M) − 3 c_E(√2 M) + 3 c_E(2M) − c_E(√8 M)].

The index j enters the logarithm as log M²+j log 2. The third difference kills all quadratics in j, while ∑ (−1)^j binom(3,j)2^(−j)=1/8. Thus the first *allowed* chiral correction after the stencil is of order M⁴ times possible logarithms and inverse powers of a hadronic scale, not a known error bar. At a fixed nonzero ε/M the known leading loop predicts the **nonlocal** finite-momentum value for that design instead of 1/180. [Analytic and injected controls](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `chiral_slope.py`) · [finite-momentum test](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `chiral-stencil-controls.csv`). Equal independent input errors amplify by √1280 M²; the worst absolute amplification is 64 M². With actual covariance use wᵀCov w, never independent-error counting by default.

**Order of operations:** normalize and renormalize the conserved currents and quark+gluon stress; include their metric contact responses. Check volume and continuum limits (for a flowed EMT, continuum at fixed physical flow time before zero flow time); recover the infinite-volume low-momentum coefficient at each nonzero pion mass before taking its light-mass limit. On periodic ensembles, small ε and large volume cannot be chosen independently. A quark mass scan at fixed N or fixed bare parameters is not enough. Correct for imperfect mass ratios or solve the analogous constraint matrix at the measured masses, with covariance. Do not use a four-point extrapolation for a physical precision claim outside the chiral regime. A change of scheme allowing an arbitrary nonanalytic mass-dependent finite source term would remove identifiability; the fixed scheme matters.

**Interacting estimator:** for a common fixed gauge field U let S(U;h,A,B) include both the gauge action and the negative sea-quark log determinants with conserved external U(1) links. At zero sources the third derivative of −log Z is

    ⟨S_hAB⟩ − Cov(S_hA,S_B) − Cov(S_hB,S_A) − Cov(S_AB,S_h)
           + Cum₃(S_h,S_A,S_B).

This follows by differentiating the normalized measure, not by assuming factorization. Each S derivative of a fermion determinant contains the associated coincident link derivatives as well as separated propagators. Gluon stress can enter through the covariances even if its gauge action is independent of A. Disconnected pieces and genuine same-operator local matching terms are not all nuisance directions. The prior EMT/flow and renormalization contract (not bundled here) still applies. [Ji & Jung's photon matrix-element construction](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/0101014) is relevant precedent for Euclidean two-current insertions, not a numerical substitute: their displayed forward matrix element lacks the nonzero metric momentum needed here.
