# A signed low-energy two-pion contribution to photon–gravity matching

**21 November 2026 • Local research result.** The equal-helicity photon amplitude and the total pion stress form factor can be joined with a common final-state phase. From **0.281 to 0.540 GeV**, the result is **+8.94 × 10⁻⁵ GeV⁻²** for the spectral contribution to `−F₃(0)` in the retained Euclidean convention. In the previously matched mostly-plus optical convention this slice contributes **−3.48 × 10⁻³⁶ m²**. The sign is a prediction of the specified dispersive reconstruction. It was not extracted from the sign of a measured cross section. No parameter was adjusted to match this result.

| Pair energy (GeV) | Signed reconstruction | Alternative magnitude | Point charged pion |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.281–0.350 | 0.836 | 0.811 | 0.779 |
| 0.350–0.450 | 4.179 | 4.362 | 3.753 |
| 0.450–0.540 | 3.925 | 3.819 | 3.348 |
| **Combined** | **8.941** | **8.992** | **7.880** |

Units throughout the table: 10⁻⁵ GeV⁻². The second column of results uses the plotted **magnitude** of Dai–Pennington’s isoscalar helicity-zero D wave and the same stress modulus. The integrated magnitudes differ by 0.57%; the middle slice differs by about 4.2% relative to the alternative. Their agreement is **not** a 0.57% uncertainty or independent experimental confirmation. The photon reconstructions share some experimental/theoretical inputs. The tabulated alternative cannot on its own distinguish an overall minus sign. Across 101 energies the calculated modulus falls within the other authors' plotted low-energy dispersive band. The integral of its two edges gives 8.50–9.47 in table units with the stress held fixed, not a confidence interval.

**What changed physically:** relative to the point-pion integral in the same window, resolving the pion increases this contribution by 13.5%. Keeping the Born photon magnitude and inserting only the reconstructed stress modulus gives 9.004 in table units: most of the difference comes from the stress response, not a large change in photon production. The tiny remaining slice from the charged-pion threshold to 0.281 GeV contributes 7.85 × 10⁻¹¹ GeV⁻² in the model; it uses an explicit short continuation, not measured differential amplitudes. We keep it separate from the main comparison.

**What fixes the sign:** charged scalar pair production fixes the reference convention; the signed, Born-subtracted photon dispersion equation determines the relative sign of the physical continuation. Its crossed-channel and right-hand integrals are evaluated, not replaced by the modulus of a fit. In the elastic interval the two strong phases cancel in the product, leaving a real sign-sensitive moment. With all other inputs fixed the subtraction coefficient alone would have to move from the source fit's 0.22 to about **22.7 GeV⁻⁶** to reverse the integrated sign through zero. This is a conditional sensitivity, not a bound on the coefficient or on other missing channels.

**Accuracy and ownership.** The alternative singular-integral calculation agrees within 4.6 × 10⁻¹³ in the complex partial wave at six probes. Reversing the pole prescription instead leaves an imaginary product of 1.47 × 10⁻³ at the last probe. A separate spacelike figure checks the stress reconstruction to 0.69% over the tested 0.05–2 GeV². Numerical convergence does not assign errors to resonance saturation, the input phase, the digitized figure, shared fits, or omitted channels. Tested subtraction, cutoff, tail and stress-slope alternatives are in moments.json (not bundled here), not combined as random errors.

This is **imported ordinary matter physics** connected to the retained common-metric photon matching, not a new strong interaction proposed by MTS. The result is for two pions in the specified energy interval only. It neither determines the full hadronic coefficient nor removes a possible independent local curvature–photon term. A finite counterterm has no cut and cannot be bounded by a small quadrature error.

**Next calculation:** extend from 0.540 toward 0.8 GeV. Separate the strictly elastic sliver from the open four-pion channel. Derive the correction to the single-phase product with multichannel unitarity, and identify which independent production or stress inputs can bound it. Check a signed partial-wave solution against the measured charged/neutral angular distributions where available before assigning a sign in that new range. Do not simply continue the positive modulus or treat the two low-energy fits as two independent experiments.
