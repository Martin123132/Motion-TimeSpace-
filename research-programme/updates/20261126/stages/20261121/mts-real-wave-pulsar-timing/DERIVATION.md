# From a real scalar's pressure to the two clocks of a binary-pulsar measurement

**Question.** What would the *same occupied field* used for the LMC force calculation do to pulse arrival times in real directions, and what part of that signal does a timing fit remove? This calculation neither identifies beam area with a timing residual nor substitutes a local fitted dark matter density. We use the fixed-occupation linear response (not bundled here), its restored common phase (not bundled here), and the published geometry already saved for 26 real binaries (not bundled here). The established gravitational timing mechanism is due to [Khmelnitsky & Rubakov](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.5888); its projection on these prepared MTS states and design/noise tests are local calculations. This is not a new direct coupling to electromagnetism.

## Einstein source, not an assumed oscillating lapse

Use the common Einstein metric, canonical real positive-mass scalar and the nonrelativistic occupied branch. Write the real field locally as Re[u exp(−iωt)] times the matter normalization, such that the cycle-averaged mass density is ρ_unit |u|². At leading order in spatial-envelope gradients and slow time derivatives, the density has no fast harmonic but the pressure does:

    p_fast/c² = −ρ_unit Re[u² exp(−iΩt+iφ)],  Ω=2ω.

The normalization follows from the opposite signs of kinetic and mass energy in pressure; for a uniform real oscillator the energy density is constant and pressure ranges between its positive and negative rest-energy density. In native L₀,V₀ units 4πGρ_unit=(V₀/L₀)². In the rotating bound-state frame u²=g²+2g(x+iy+iθg) at first order. g, x, y here are physical pointwise envelopes (undo the radial Jacobian and mass weights); θ follows from the common-phase projection of the driven matter equation. Do not square the first-order satellite perturbation or infer the ground occupation from these sightlines. Ω uses the previously retained pole and binding energy; the slow time variable here is referenced to that same rotating frame.

In Newtonian gauge ds²=−(1+2Φ)c²dt²+(1−2Ψ)dx², without assuming Φ=Ψ for the oscillatory gradient corrections. The isotropic spatial Einstein equation at leading temporal order gives Ψ̈=4πG p_fast/c². Equivalently the particular harmonic metric has spatial potential phasor B=4πGρ_unit u²/Ω²; c²R₀ₙ₀ₙ=Ψ̈+c²∂ₙ²Φ≈−Ω² Re[B exp(−iΩt+iφ)]. This is the pressure-sourced curvature, excluding unrelated incident metric waves. It is not a Poisson solve using a spurious oscillating Newtonian mass density.

An independent flat-space finite-momentum check uses natural units and φ_scalar=A cos(p·x) with p²=−m². The double-frequency trace-reversed stress coefficient is A²(−p_μp_ν/2+m²η_μν/4). The Lorenz-gauge linear Einstein solution is h_μν=−4πG S_μν/m²; its longitudinal p_μp_ν term cancels from the linear Riemann tensor. Direct differentiation gives R₀ₙ₀ₙ=−2πG A²[(p⁰)²−pₙ²] cos(2p·x). At rest this is −4πGρ with ρ=m²A²/2. This checks sign, normalization and the order of the gradient correction. It is not a proof that every subleading field of the inhomogeneous bound galaxy equals that plane wave.

## Free-falling emission and reception

Define y=δ(ν_observed/ν_emitted), positive for a blueshift, and R as extra arrival time (late positive), so Ṙ=−y. n points from Earth to pulsar, distance D. Evaluate perturbations along the past ray (t−s/c,x_E+ns). To first order in the metric, with quasistatic endpoint positions for this isolated signal, the clock, motion and light terms are

    y = Φ_P−Φ_E + n·(v_E−v_P)/c + ∫₀ᴰ ds/c (Φ̇+Ψ̇).

The *oscillatory gravitational parts* of endpoint velocities obey v̇=−c²∇Φ. Differentiate at reception and integrate by parts along the ray to get

    ẏ = ∫₀ᴰ ds/c [Ψ̈+c²∂ₙ²Φ] = c∫₀ᴰ R₀ₙ₀ₙ ds .

This makes cancellation of a time-dependent uniform lapse and of a uniform acceleration explicit. We also check the endpoint formula against the curvature formula for manufactured inhomogeneous oscillatory potentials. A spatially homogeneous **physical pressure** does not in general cancel: Earth and pulsar see different retarded phases. Its response vanishes at zero separation and at integer carrier light wavelengths. A common time-reference error at the terrestrial end is a separate instrumental degeneracy.

Let z=s/L₀, ε=V₀/c, ν=1+ε²E, k=2ν/ε and B=(ε⁴/4ν²)u². The complex forcing for the frozen fractional-frequency response is Y_F=−ik∫ B(z,T−s/c) exp(ikz) dz. In a stationary envelope this is precisely

    B_E−B_P exp(ikD/L₀) + ∫ exp(ikz) dB/dz dz.

For a varying envelope the derivative in the spatial identity is along the past cone, not at fixed time. Integrate the whole source and separately evaluate the endpoint-only approximation; do not choose two phases independently when calculating the coherent single-mode prediction. For the slowly varying Y_F(T), the timing phasor satisfies (∂_T−iΩ)²R_c=iΩ Y_F. Its exact particular solution for a quadratic envelope is (−i/Ω)[Y_F−2iY_F′/Ω−3Y_F″/Ω²]. The homogeneous additive offset and linear trend are degenerate with the ordinary timing fit. In code T is in Julian years, and R_c is converted to seconds. Five computed reception epochs span −30 to 0 years: endpoints and midpoint fix the polynomial, and the two quarter epochs are independent checks. MJD 60000 is a common phase/time reference, not an observed epoch of LMC pericentre.

## Which part is actually predicted?

The pole and metric coupling are retained action inputs; these two specific ground occupations and the six alternate orbits are conditional preparations. The 26 longitude/latitude/distance triples and quoted errors are the same as in the saved acceleration work. Use its Sun at (−8.1,0,0) kpc; this replaces the preceding *synthetic* 8.3-kpc observer. No catalogue spin-down acceleration or timing residual sets a normalization. The ground and the **difference** driven by the LMC are both reported; a common physical phase rotates all their complex amplitudes. A number-diagonal ensemble has zero one-point fast stress; its stochastic two-point fluctuations are not thereby proved absent. GR given the same minimally coupled scalar, occupation and clocks reproduces every leading timing term here.

The source uses Chebyshev time interpolation of the archived mode solution and finite-element interpolation of the real and imaginary spatial wave with all retained even multipoles. Split each path at crossings of radial element boundaries and at closest approach. Use exact Legendre/plane-wave moments for full and partial intervals. Reconstruct both the emission phase and density as distance varies. Scan D±the reported error at ≥48 samples per light wavelength (at least 32 intervals); extrema are sampled, not guaranteed absolute extrema. A flat integration over this interval is a sensitivity summary, **not a posterior**. The refined Simpson summary has its own half-grid check; retain the less accurate original trapezoidal comparison rather than treating the two as equally precise.

The model treats the endpoints as freely falling in the added perturbation. It does not model all Solar System, companion, moving-observer, or intrinsic spin dynamics from scratch. The tables give a leading nonrelativistic Born-path template: static host propagation and conversion between parallax distance and optical travel time perturb the emission phase; the previous fixed-affine-shell correction must not be transplanted onto a different observable. Effects of true source velocities, stochastic coherence, other occupied modes and subleading metric/gradient corrections are not covered by a numerical basis check.

## What passes through a real timing fit?

Two real cadence pilots were fixed before scoring: J1713+0747, for continuity with the earlier 24.46-year calendar-only test (not bundled here), and J0613−0200. We use the **shorter, different** packaged series from [NANOGrav Discovery's pinned EPTA data](https://github.com/nanograv/discovery/tree/18ab4c010b2e8e09eb81fdb145b296da024659fa/data/epta_dr2). See the [EPTA DR2 release](https://www.epta.eu.org/epta-dr2.html). Only TOAs, formal uncertainties, published timing design, radio frequencies, backend labels and packaged noise settings are retained. Bytes of the two public source files transit memory; the residual column is never selected. Check the original Git blobs plus locally retained input hashes. No publisher output for the full release or data likelihood is being reproduced.

For white or equal weights, project the signal through the measured timing design by scaled SVD. Independently verify that constant, spin and spin-down span is contained in it. Form the Gram matrix of the six annual-time polynomial times sinusoidal functions after projection. Sweep the unknown absolute phase without selecting the best one; singular values give minimum/maximum RMS, and generalized eigenvalues compare post/pre RMS at the *same* phase. A long-baseline fit cannot be replaced by an observation count.

For the second noise treatment follow the [packaged single-pulsar model](https://github.com/nanograv/discovery/blob/18ab4c010b2e8e09eb81fdb145b296da024659fa/src/discovery/models/epta.py) and [signal definitions](https://github.com/nanograv/discovery/blob/18ab4c010b2e8e09eb81fdb145b296da024659fa/src/discovery/signals.py): C=diag[EFAC²(σ_TOA²+EQUAD²)]+FFᵀ. Fourier variances at f=j/span are A² year³(f·year)^(−γ)/(12π² span); multiply sine/cosine columns by (1400 MHz/ν_radio)^α. Use each packaged count and its fixed spectral hyperparameters; red α=0, dispersion α=2, scattering-like α=4. J1713 has 73 red and 72 dispersion harmonics; J0613 has 90 dispersion and 57 index-four harmonics. The matrix check uses an independent dense submatrix inverse. Marginalize timing-design columns in whitened coordinates. Returning the residual of an injected signal to seconds is distinct from its dimensionless optimal S/N sqrt(sᵀC⁻¹_perp s). A correlated-noise fit can leave more nanoseconds of a waveform while giving it less statistical weight. Fixed deterministic offsets cancel between injection and control; dip-parameter uncertainty, common red processes, ephemeris systematics and hyperparameter uncertainty are not marginalized here.

Finally combine the two independent single-pulsar noise blocks on the *same* phase reference. Compare with adding an arbitrary common terrestrial sine and cosine at the carrier frequency. Eliminate these two columns by their joint Schur complement, not by fitting independent pulsar sinusoids. A pure common Earth tone then vanishes. The integrated pulsar terms need not. The conditional optimal S/N is a forecast for an injected signal, **not a measured significance**, a detection probability or a density upper limit. Randomized two-quadrature signal and nuisance injections with noise drawn independently from the covariance factors test recovery and interval coverage; these are algorithm controls, not blind analysis of the telescope residuals.
