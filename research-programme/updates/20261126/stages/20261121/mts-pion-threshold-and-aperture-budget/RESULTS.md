# The two-pion light–gravity contrast survives filling the missing energy window

21 November 2026 · local, frozen 3/6-fm point-lens test · no fitting or new observational signal.

**Main result:** the conditional two-pion contribution from threshold through **1.60 GeV** is **[+0.20044,+4.17065] × 10⁻⁶ GeV⁻²**. Previously only two separated windows had a positive combined result. The new test keeps the signed low-energy reconstruction below 0.54 GeV, but allows either interference sign throughout 0.54–1.60 within measured intensity budgets and the retained angular assumptions. The pre-existing 2/4-fm control still straddles zero. At 4/8 fm the conditional range is [+0.29495,+0.58225] in the same units. This is the form-factor contrast contributing to the delay, not an arrival time, a probability interval or an MTS-vs-GR detection. **Threshold:** derived (H/β² → 0.1333031) in the retained hybrid, close to the analytic Born value 2/15; the difference comes from the previous subtraction and the two dispersive integrals, not a tuned parameter. Eight previously specified input variants give a signed gap contribution from +0.07457 to +0.07674 in the table units. This describes model sensitivity, not an independent determination of the sign. Threshold components (not bundled here).

**What faced measurements:** the missing neutral bins come from the [Crystal Ball data](https://doi.org/10.17182/hepdata.22923.v1/t1), with the 11% low-energy systematic restored from the [collaboration paper](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.41.3324). The [Mark II data](https://doi.org/10.17182/hepdata.22881.v1/t1) retain separate statistical, pointwise systematic and shared scale errors. These are external inputs, not held-out tests of the low-energy reconstruction. The binwise budgets (not bundled here) retain acceptances and full native widths. Explicit complex amplitudes reproduce both endpoints in all four individual gap bins. [Endpoint checks](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `extremizer-tests.csv`).

**Where the sign could fail:** the assumption about the higher angular waves is consequential. Using total rates only and dropping the orthogonal floor still gives a lower margin of +0.13618 × 10⁻⁶. To undo this result within the covered range through angular leakage alone, at least one native bin must reach **6.64%** in a precisely defined projected amplitude norm. This is a requirement for reversal, *not* a measurement of how large the leakage actually is. Finite apertures can miss such modes. The lower elastic sign and the pion stress input are separate conditions. [Angular leakage](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `higher-wave-margin.csv`) · forward-mode counterexample (not bundled here).

**What else would it take?** A signed tail starting only at 1.60 GeV needs at least **90.64 GeV⁻²** of negative spectral weight to undo the main lower margin. We also derived a way to bound such a tail with the correctly normalized vacuum stress correlation at a single Euclidean time, without reconstructing a timelike spectrum. At 0.5 fm, the necessary stress correlation for reversal is 2.11 × 10³ GeV⁵ for states above 1.60 GeV, versus only 3.14 × 10⁻¹³ GeV⁵ for a four-pion channel beginning at 0.558 GeV. The retained two-pion contribution below 0.54 alone already puts 3.53 × 10⁻⁷ GeV⁵ in the total correlation there. Thus a total-stress measurement cannot by this simple unitarity substitution certify the low four-pion window on that reference; a channel-specific production or stress estimate is needed. No zero-temperature full-QCD measurement has been inserted. [Derivation](DERIVATION.md) · Euclidean budgets (not bundled here).
