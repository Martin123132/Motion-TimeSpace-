# What the motion scalar adds after subtracting GR with the same ordinary matter

Local vacuum comparison · 21 November 2026 · no fits, no assigned wave occupation.

**The clearest consequence is a force-and-light relation independent of the photon coupling.** For one extra minimally coupled real motion field, its virtual pair strengthens both the inward force on a test mass and the polarization-averaged deflection of light. At equal orbital radius and impact parameter, small compared with the pair range, the **fractional deflection change is 2/9 of the fractional force change**. More generally the former is less than three quarters of the latter for this positive spectral contribution, including a finite mass. The predicted sign of the shift in lensing-inferred mass relative to dynamical mass does not require knowing the scalar mass or fitting a galaxy. The effect is tiny: per field at 1 cm the force changes by +1.2473 × 10⁻⁶⁷ and the inferred mass ratio by −9.7010 × 10⁻⁶⁸ within the saved low-energy window. It is a vacuum effect, not a proposed explanation of the observed galaxy discrepancy. The canonical GR-plus-the-same-scalar control gives exactly the same gravitational answer. **Polarization separates a change to the metric from a change to photon physics.** Neither of the retained direct photon contacts makes the required equal-helicity angular quadrupole at tree level. The existing curvature-photon vertex *does* make one via a graviton: the scalar pair dresses the metric carrying the already matched coefficient. It is not a second independent photon vertex. The bare local term cancels in the old two-distance test, but this dressing survives in proportion to the local coefficient. A separate check using the curvature of the common time delay cancels that dressing as well. It would be wrong to count the same pair loop as both a new photon interaction and a propagator correction.

**There is also a specific route to an intrinsic signal.** If the optional even photon kinetic coupling and the derivative stress coupling are both active, their mixed photon–scalar scattering loop produces a signed, frequency-dependent quadrupole. Either coupling squared alone has zero helicity-flip discontinuity for this intermediate state. Projecting the crossed loop and passing it through three ray distances removes the unknown local dimension-ten subtraction. For 1/2/4 cm the finite-window normalized change is **+0.00289571 times the signed product of the two couplings**. The minimally factorized branch gives zero for this term; the calculation did not assume the missing coupling exists. At the original femtometre separations, the supported low-energy interval contributes very little and the full hadronic/UV matching is still needed. The force comparison reproduces the independent finite-mass result of [Fröb](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.03129) at seven resolved masses through 2mr=40 (maximum relative difference 2.0 × 10⁻¹⁵). A direct angular tensor calculation, high-precision small-distance weights, metric gradients, and two differently subtracted loop integrations check the other readouts. The 3-fm constant-vertex benchmark is explicitly *not* a full UV-controlled prediction. These checks concern the isolated orders and channels stated, not every term of the quantum effective theory.
