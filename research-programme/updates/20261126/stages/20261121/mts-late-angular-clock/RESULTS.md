## Identify what makes the two central momentum readers disagree

They take a radial derivative of the same angular cross velocity in different orders: one differentiates a face field; the other first interpolates it to cell centres and then differentiates. The retained geometry gives their leading signed difference, **−r S²(D_old I_c−D_even)V_f/2**, with S=B/(rA). Evaluate the expression on the actual fields at the start and end and subtract, without using the measured end discrepancy to set a coefficient. Across ten branch/window/step combinations it predicts the **change in first-cell independent-minus-native momentum** to at worst **5.88 × 10⁻⁷ relative**. Core norm errors in this difference are at most 2.82 × 10⁻⁵. The first-cell material-current readouts agree to roundoff. This explains the disagreement between the readers, not the absolute continuum constraint or which derivative converges fastest. We applied no correction to a reported momentum or tide. On the positive host, **97.05% (wave) and 92.92% (cold)** of the *final change in first-cell residual between native and half step* comes from differences already present at tick 960, subsequently evolved with identical half steps. This is an exact signed telescope for these runs; the two terms happen to have the same sign there. Propagating only the measured initial angular metric and velocity differences through the independently integrated, source-free flat principal equations predicts the **carried first-cell geometric** momentum difference to **2.81% and 1.91%** at the end. Even the homogeneous shear part alone is within 2.70% and 1.80%. Over the entire inner 0.1 Mpc, the full flat forecast misses by **78% and 42%** in norm. A local mechanism is identified; it is not a substitute for the inhomogeneous, curved matter equations. ## Controlled nonzero angular curvature

Two opposite smooth angular curvature pulses leave the initial metric and all matter variables unchanged. The two zero-pulse continuations reproduce the uninterrupted native runs exactly at all five comparison times. The shared fields and signed monopoles stay bit-identical for the two pulses. We predict the odd angular momentum response in two independent ways: the continuum quadrupolar Green function and a discrete evolution of the geometry alone. For both matter branches at the end, the continuum predicts the **evolved momentum** over r<0.1 Mpc within **18 ppm** in norm. For the *independent* momentum, the discrete prediction including the second measurement stencil is within **0.025% (wave) and 0.031% (cold)** across that region. These are transfer accuracies, not the 20 ppm proper-tide test. The positive and negative disturbances really were evolved, not imposed at the readout.
