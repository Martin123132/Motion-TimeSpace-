# From mass identity to a conservative source and two metric readouts

Everything here is on the retained two-preparation, fixed-occupation branch. The collider, strong gauge sector and compact-object boundary conditions are not inputs to this experiment. The question is narrower and necessary: **which ordinary mass distribution can actually source the same metric that the material responds to?**

## 1. Carrier identities and mass marginals

For FIRE's binary split convention a generation-\(g\) child retains the \(g_0\) low bits of the child number of its generation-\(g_0\) ancestor, with the same root ID. Reversing child bits turns a prefix into a contiguous interval. We require a unique containing launch interval and nondecreasing generation. Exclude an entire involved gas/new-star root with overlapping leaves, an incompatible child code, or a generation at/above the alias-risk cutoff (30); this deliberately errs on the side of leaving mass unresolved. An ordinary continuing star is already independently matched by its triple and formation time in the previous stage. A new star enters the gas descendant set **once**, never a second time as a new injected source. A cell with no surviving identity is a missing *carrier*, not proof that its material disappeared. [FIRE-2 DR1, §§4.2 and 4.6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06969).

For parent \(p\) and identified descendants \(j\), let
\[
 c_p=\sum_j2^{-(g_j-g_p)},\quad M_p=\sum_jm_{1j},\quad
 t_j={m_{1j}\over M_p}\min(c_p m_{0p},M_p).
\]
Zero descendant mass retains nothing. For complete lineages this keeps the maximum old mass that can occupy the observed descendants without exceeding their endpoint capacities; the split among daughters follows their measured final masses. For incomplete lineages \(c_p\) is a **symmetric-branch assumption**, not a measured original mass fraction. An alternate test retains daughter by daughter \(\min(m_{1j},2^{-(g_j-g_p)}m_{0p})\). Exact IDs do not identify which individual molecules arrived during the interval.

Every retained portion transports its launch label \(w_p\). The remaining donor stock \(s_p=m_{0p}-\sum_jt_j\) and receiver demand \(d_j=m_{1j}-t_j\) are nonnegative. Existing stars contribute their measured mass loss to the donor column and retain their old label on their remaining mass. Include the whole simulated box before taking the positive target aperture. Keep any difference between total raw supply and demand as an explicit unobserved source or sink, separate from mass outside the 500 kpc force grid. A new external raw supply has zero label in the reference construction; a sensitivity bound instead allows it any original label in \([0,w_{\max}]\). This does not bound an arbitrarily assigned external tag.

## 2. Exact continuity without claiming an observed deposition history

A transfer \(T_{ij}\ge0\) with row sums \(s_i\) and column sums \(d_j\) gives endpoint label mass \(u_j=\sum_i w_i T_{ij}\). Consequently \(\sum_j u_j=\sum_iw_i s_i\); no tag is recreated when mass changes carriers. For any frozen spatial test \(K\), the transfer contribution to the *change* is \(\sum_{ij}w_iT_{ij}(K_j-K_i)\). One can construct a weakly conserved current explicitly: transport each parcel along a connecting curve \(X\), with integrated current \(w_i T_{ij}\int \delta^3(x-X)\,dX\). Pairing its divergence with \(K\) gives the endpoint difference with the continuity sign. Summing with the known carrier transports gives the full discrete mass balance, not just cancellation in the total mass. There are many such currents. This construction supplies neither their local momentum distribution nor the gas stress-energy tensor needed for full covariant conservation.

Two positive allocations are evaluated without fitting a force: independent mixing \(T_{ij}=s_i d_j/\sum s_i\), and matching first in an identical spatial cell, then in successively larger *nested* radial/angle groups. At each level the latter uses the largest possible group-local mass, with donor and receiver participation proportional to their remaining amounts. Cells are axisymmetrized and folded about the disk: a local cell is not a measured three-dimensional contact. For a group partition \(\mathcal G\), any allocation must take at least \(\tfrac12\sum_g|S_g-D_g|\) mass across groups. Nested matching attains this raw lower bound at every level, but the individual donor–receiver pairs remain assumptions.

For one scalar kernel the only uncertain piece is \(\sum_{ij} w_iT_{ij}K_j\). Order donors by weight, and receiver capacities by \(K\). Matching equal mass quantiles gives its exact maximum in the same order, minimum in reverse order: swapping crossed pairs improves the pairing by \(\epsilon(w_2-w_1)(K_2-K_1)\). These are sharp **separate** extrema in the retained cell representation at fixed retention, bookkeeping and marginals. They do not require extreme values of different observables to coexist. Independent small linear programs verify the implementation. Smaller ranges condition on the proportional participating masses at each nested level but allow all pairings within each group; these narrower ranges are not data-only bounds.

As a kinematic cross-check, clip radius (or absolute height) and bound each mass parcel's projected coordinate by its original cell interval. For each level \(r\), construct the largest/smallest possible donor/receiver cumulative masses there. The gap between disjoint cumulative ranges lower-bounds the mass crossing that level. Its integral lower-bounds the mass-weighted travel distance for the exchange component; division by mass and elapsed time gives a lower bound on mean speed. A one-sided cumulative deficit even after expanding the accessible distance by \(v\Delta t\) forces at least that much raw mass to travel faster. These are necessary projected-displacement bounds **conditional on the retention model**, not inferred ejecta speeds or proof of a physical local assignment.

## 3. Forces and the leading clock comparison

The retained Poisson field is linear in binned ordinary mass. At each original probe, evaluate radial and polar acceleration using the original angular multipoles. At fixed \((R,z)=(6,1.5)\) and \((8.3,1.5)\) kpc also evaluate \((\Phi_{\rm outer}-\Phi_{\rm inner})/c^2\). This is the leading difference of fractional proper-clock rates for **stationary** observers of the shared weak metric, free of an arbitrary potential offset. It is not a prediction of intrinsic clock evolution or a measured Milky Way clock experiment. Independently integrate the cylindrical component of the gravitational acceleration between the clocks, checking \(-\int \mathbf g\cdot d\mathbf l/c^2\). The trained stellar/galaxy velocity profiles were not used to calibrate this relationship. Outside the truncated source grid bound the direct acceleration per unit positive mass by \(G/(500-r_{\max})^2\), and the clock difference by this number times the clock separation divided by \(c^2\).

For the wave response, integrate both original preparations using the retained nonlinear implicit midpoint system and the **same time-interpolated combined source**. The common ordinary force cancels from the wave-arm contrast at a given epoch; the wave self-gravity need not. To propagate mass-exchange uncertainty, differentiate the implicit wave step and its self-gravity, apply the transpose in reverse time from each terminal force/clock gradient, then the transpose of the original Poisson multipole map back to mass cells. The interpolation fraction at each step must multiply the sensitivity because it is the endpoint source that varies. Check each transpose against the forward tangent and the mass-space result against the direct potential-space projection. Sorting these kernels gives sharp bounds for the **linearized** paired response about the nested reference. Finally run the original **nonlinear** system on the extremizing fields themselves. Agreement is a test on those fields, not a proof that the nonlinear functional has no additional interior extrema.

These are finite-epoch initial-value tests of the retained universal coupling. Promoting a conserving coarse mass history to a gas stress model requires velocities and pressure/feedback exchange; a favorable force or clock comparison does not supply them.
