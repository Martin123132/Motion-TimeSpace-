# Clock feedback, missing transfers and the independent indexing check

**Observable.** Let \(C=(\Phi_{\rm out}-\Phi_{\rm in})/c^2=\int K_C(x)\rho(x)d^3x\), for the same two stationary clocks as the force test. This stage distinguishes (a) the common ordinary-field change over 590→591, (b) the wave-only difference of preparations at 591, and (c) the extra change caused when ordinary matter itself responds differently in the two wave fields. They are *not* interchangeable clock signals.

**First live derivative.** Start the two preparations with identical ordinary positions, velocities, pressure/stress, and non-gravitational forces. Their density and first density derivative agree. Write \(d=\Phi_{\rm wave,coupled}-\Phi_{\rm wave,reset}\). The shared metric gives a difference in material acceleration \(\delta a=-\nabla d\). At launch the transport derivative of \(\int K_C\,dm\) contains a velocity Hessian term and an acceleration gradient term. The former is identical in both arms. Thus for conserved labels
\[
 \delta\ddot C_{\rm ordinary}(0)
 =-\int \rho_0(x)\,\nabla K_C(x)\cdot\nabla d(x)\,d^3x .
\]
The same result follows from \(\delta\ddot\rho=\nabla\cdot(\rho_0\nabla d)\), with compact represented source or vanishing boundary flux. Arm-dependent impulse, species conversion or sources not shared at launch would require additional terms. This is the first feedback of ordinary motion on the metric; there is no assumption that pressure continues to cancel after the two arms have moved apart. The original even-multipole clock kernel and the wave acceleration use the original units; time here is in Myr. The control moves source quadrature points infinitesimally in both directions, reconstructs the Poisson potential and differences it rather than differentiating the force formula. Radial spreading, two angle rules, three angular cutoffs and a coarser wave grid test numerical sensitivity. Checks (not bundled here).

**How hidden gas turnover can alter an apparently firm sign.** Endpoints do not label each atom. Additional simultaneous inflow and outflow can leave an element's mass unchanged. Consider releasing raw mass \(E\) formerly required to stay on identified edges. Any feasible relaxed transportation plan can be restored to the forced-edge constraints by successive two-edge swaps: route a deficit on a forced edge from a free edge in its row and one in its column, returning the same amount to their cross-edge. Work with the residual rows/columns after already-restored mass has been removed. Each swapped unit changes the scalar objective by at most \({\rm range}(w){\rm range}(K)\); mass requiring restoration is at most \(E\). Consequently the original distance from zero divided by that product is a **necessary**, generally weak, lower bound on turnover for sign reversal. A separate uniform release fraction from *all* retained gas and surviving stars, followed by extreme sorting, produces a **sufficient** example, not an estimate of actual feedback. It permits stellar replacement that need not be physically possible and is intentionally severe. Results for the original broad alias policy: thresholds (not bundled here).

**Second-stage tracking check.** The initial protocol excluded whole risky roots instead of selecting matches by a favorable force or speed. After those bounds were calculated, test the conservative exclusion against the published FIRE catalogue from the **same simulation**. Its two native arrays point *back* from particles at snapshot 600 into the concatenated star-then-gas indices at snapshots 590 and 591. They are not a direct gas-donor catalogue. Join on the common 600 witness; require a unique nonnegative counterpart *in both directions*, no one-sided missing witness involving that pair, and an identical root/child/generation triple occurring exactly once at each epoch. Only apply this second retention case to previously excluded roots. No position or clock cut is used. Missing descendants at 600, ambiguous pointers, or repeated triples do not justify an identity. A recovered pair constrains retained mass only under the **same** maximum-retention assumption as safe gas, not by proving which atoms it contains. Cross-check unambiguous births and a sparse stable gas control separately; do not treat a blank pointer as a contradiction. [FIRE tracking documentation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.06969), [dataset portal](https://flathub.flatironinstitute.org/fire).

For each newly corroborated pair retain \(t=\min(m_{590},m_{591})\). Deduct \(t\) from its donor row and receiver column and add \(w_{590}t\) directly at the destination. This is equivalent to fixing previously free entries, so every resulting scalar interval must lie inside its earlier interval. Re-run both nonlinear preparations on those sources instead of relabelling earlier wave results. This check cannot determine the still-free cross-family depositions or a galaxy's wave occupation.
