## A finite observer: beam area rather than an unobservable absolute travel time

For a finite endpoint the projected-delay equation contains an endpoint derivative. Coordinate time alone also lacks the emitter/receiver proper-clock and motion terms. Rather than set them to zero, use a distinct local operational definition: an observer with a fixed tetrad at z=0 looks up or down the axis to a source screen at a specified **affine distance** D normalized at the observer. Start the screen Jacobi map at zero with unit slope there. To first order around flat propagation its trace gives the convergence κ=−δD_A/D_A,

    κ(D)=(4πG/c²) ∫₀ᴰ dl l(1−l/D) ρ_null(t_obs−l/c,l).

This follows by integrating the linear focusing equation D_A″=−R_kk D_A/2 with these vertex data and using Einstein's equation. The unperturbed optical shear is zero; Weyl shear is not discarded from a shape measurement, but does not contribute to the *area trace* at this order. See the [optical-scalar treatment of distance observables](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4357/833/2/247). A constant Newtonian potential is not a local Ricci-focusing signal. Unlike a physical source selected at a measured redshift, this test fixes the affine screen: clock, Doppler, peculiar motion and distance remapping are still needed for that different comparison.

For a northern source the incoming photon has n_z=−1, so the current sign is **plus** in ρ_null=δρ+2εj_z(north). For a southern source the spatial and propagation signs both reverse. The one-sided density retardation and current do **not** cancel by the full-path parity argument. They are evaluated at the past times from the terminal state, not on half of the full crossing centred at the earlier epoch. In native units the focusing prefactor becomes ε² multiplying ∫z(1−z/Z_s)[δρ+2εj_z] dz. Differentiate using the modal rate and the *driven* acceleration −σ²c−F, not a derivative of a fitted distance curve. A second-order one-sided three-time difference stays within the saved interval. The reported drift is per Julian year, at fixed observer, direction and affine source distance; the same frozen profile has zero drift on those specified paths.

For three sources on the **same** line at D=20,35,50 kpc, define the dimensionless combination

    κ_shell=(50κ₅₀−70κ₃₅+20κ₂₀)/15 .

The factor l(D−l) in Dκ makes the coefficient of every slice below 20 kpc vanish, even for a varying foreground, since all sources sample the same event on the observer's past light cone there. Between 20 and 50 kpc its weight is l min(l−20,50−l)/15 in kpc, positive throughout the shell, and zero above 50. We integrate the shell twice: as this positive spatial kernel and by differencing the three *separately calculated* convergences. The shell can nevertheless be negative because the **induced** source may be negative relative to the unperturbed host. Neither the density of the whole galaxy nor the total distance need be negative. The common near-foreground null is an exact first-order property at the stated geometry: different sky positions, distance errors and moving observation lines spoil it. There is no cancellation of other matter in the selected shell.

## Scope of the crossing correction

ρ and j here are the first two phase-averaged nonrelativistic contributions to T_kk. Instantaneous kinetic energy and longitudinal pressure at order ε² are **not** computed as part of an absolute post-Newtonian normalization. For slowly evolving, reflection-even pressure/energy the frozen contribution cancels from the full moving-minus-frozen comparison; its first surviving time-sampling correction is two powers higher in a regular velocity expansion. This does not bound a fast oscillating pressure in the underlying real scalar. Nor does it average over a short real observing campaign by assumption. The retained real heavy-bath action (not bundled here) and phase-resolved stress remainder (not bundled here) explain the distinction. At 9.586×10⁻²⁵ eV the double-carrier period is about 68 years and its light-path wavelength about 21 pc. A long path averages many cycles, but the predicted drift is so small that cancellation must be evaluated with the actual window and unknown phase, not taken on trust.
