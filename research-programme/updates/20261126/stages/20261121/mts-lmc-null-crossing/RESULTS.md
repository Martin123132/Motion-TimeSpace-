# What changes when the wave moves during the light crossing?

**On the two full axial paths, the first-order change cancels. The surviving change is small and positive on every saved LMC history.** At the same mid-crossing epoch the frozen wave gives 2,589–4,967 seconds of differential Einstein delay from the induced mass. Sampling the changing density alone shortens it by 136–314 microseconds. Including the longitudinal matter current lengthens it by 261–522 microseconds. Paired on the same initial state and orbit, the net is **+95–225 microseconds** (0.021–0.085 parts per million). Adding endpoints of independently quoted ranges would give the wrong result. The old 2,524–4,876 s calculation refers to a *later frozen epoch*; the epoch shift is not a light-crossing correction. GR with exactly the same stress has exactly the same ordinary signal.

**Numerical status:** all twelve signs survive changing the retained mode basis. Only **5/12** full-delay corrections agree between the two bases at the unchanged 20-ppm *relative-to-the-small-correction* target. Worst difference: 54 ppm of that correction. The spatial integration change on that correction is at most 0.053 ppm. Neither number is an error bar on the measured galaxy. The exact time calculation recovers each preceding saved force readout; a double-light-speed control gives the expected quarter-size change to better than 0.9 ppm for the delay. The transport identity (not bundled here) tests the full-delay expansion a separate way, comparing the density acceleration against the axial and radial mass currents. No particle number, wave grid, source, or tolerance was adjusted.

**A finite-observer test is more informative than treating those minutes as an observed clock difference.** At a fixed Galactic-plane observer with cylindrical radius 8.3 kpc, we computed the induced focusing of beams to three specified affine distances up the symmetry axis: 20, 35 and 50 kpc. One weighted combination cancels *every* common foreground slice inside 20 kpc. The calculated 20–50 kpc shell signal ranges from **−1.54×10⁻¹⁰ to +7.80×10⁻¹¹** in dimensionless convergence: choosing a different LMC history can change its sign. Its **secular drift is negative on all twelve**, −2.88×10⁻¹⁷ to −2.24×10⁻¹⁷ per year, compared with zero for the same frozen profile and fixed lines of sight. The shell amplitude and drift both pass the saved basis comparison target; all 96 finite-observer rows across both radii do too. This is a prediction of the *phase-averaged induced wave*, not a measured magnification or an independent MTS victory over GR.
