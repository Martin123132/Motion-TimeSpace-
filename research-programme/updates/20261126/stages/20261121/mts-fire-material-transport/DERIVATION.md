# Passive material labels, the ordinary stress and the weak-field source

This is a repair to the **galaxy source construction** of the retained theory, not a new fundamental field. The same two heavy-bath preparations, occupation, Newtonian coupling and ordinary metric are used. The experiment has two separate parts: transport along measured FIRE stellar tracks, and a test of what the retained gravitational fields do to identical test stars. These are not the same trajectories.

## Why moving a label matters for the Einstein/Newton connection

For a collisionless population with constant rest masses and universal coupling, a fixed positive label \(w_i\) can multiply each worldline action, \(S=-\sum_i m_i w_i c\int ds_i\). Its Euler equation is still metric geodesic motion. The corresponding point-particle stress is linear in the constant \(m_iw_i\), and its covariant divergence vanishes distributionally for geodesics without endpoints. In the Newtonian limit this gives the same acceleration for every label and the ordinary source \(\sum m_iw_i\delta^3(x-X_i)\). This is a conditional consequence of the **retained** universal coupling, not a new derivation of its strength. If masses vary or particles enter/leave, the other components must supply the corresponding current and four-momentum. The FIRE worldlines illustrate transport; they are not automatically geodesics of MTS.

By contrast an Eulerian weight \(w(x)\) applied at the destination gives \(\partial_t(w\rho)+\nabla\cdot(wJ)=w s+\rho\,\partial_t w+J\cdot\nabla w\). The last term is concentrated at group faces for piecewise constant weights. Even for conserved dust one has \(\nabla_\mu(wT^{\mu\nu})=T^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu w\), generally nonzero. Treating a varying weight as an actual mass field in a worldline action would introduce an extra projected force proportional to \(-c^2(g^{\mu\nu}+u^\mu u^\nu/c^2)\partial_\mu\log w\). **We do not add this force.** Instead the continuum label satisfies the passive transport equation along characteristics. Velocity at a position is still not supplied by a density map.

## Preparing the labels without using the test epoch

Write \(F_{0j}\) for raw gas-plus-star mass in fine cell \(j\) at 590 and \(g(j)\) for its coarse angular/radial group. The existing target launch mass is \(T_j=L_j F_{0g}/F_{600g}\), with terminal local template \(L\). Group labels are \(w_g=\sum_{j\in g}L_j/F_{600g}\). They match every group but need not match its internal density. Fine labels \(w_j=T_j/F_{0j}\) match every occupied fine cell. Where donors are absent the mass remains explicitly uncovered. Do not redistribute it to make the comparison look better. The reference at 600 is inherited from earlier work; 591 was not used to choose weights, but the FIRE history is not a blind observation of the Galaxy.

For positive donor masses in a nonempty cell, this common label is the unique minimizer of \(\sum_i m_i(w_i\log w_i-w_i+1)\) under \(\sum_i m_i w_i=T_j\). More simply, subtracting the common value shows that any alternative has larger \(\sum_i m_i(w_i-w_j)^2\). Thus the lift that leaves within-cell phase and velocity proportions unchanged is determined once the **choice of donor** and the spatial bins are fixed. Neither that donor nor those proportions are forced by the MTS parent action. This statement applies to the positive transferred component, not to an arbitrary signed reconstructed Poisson density.

For each persistent star, let \(w_0,w_1\) be the frozen map at its two positions, \(m_0,m_1\) its measured masses. The mapped net mass change separates exactly:
\[
 m_1w_1-m_0w_0=(m_1-m_0)w_0+m_1(w_1-w_0).
\]
For an arbitrary spatial readout \(K\), replace the right side by \(m_0w_0(K_1-K_0)+(m_1-m_0)w_0K_1+m_1(w_1-w_0)K_1\). Add endpoints without counterparts separately. The first term is conservative transport. The second tracks measured change of stellar mass, and the third is an artifact if the weights were meant as material labels. No movement or birth rate is tuned to force the identity.

## Three tests that must not be conflated

1. **Empirical donor transport.** Match the global stellar triples (particle, child, generation), including stars outside the positive target. Test formation times independently and split new stars from mass returned by existing stars. Use the original stellar particles' 590 velocities for the derivative, then their actual 591 positions for an independent finite difference. Test both \(D\) (two old shells and two observables) and \(-B=-(2d+r d_r)\), the global radial-spread kernel. Changing the shell moments does not create total mass. Cell averaging errors, finite-time changes, and unknown Milky Way streaming should not be merged into one error bar.

2. **Forcing the parent waves.** At 590 keep the archived ordinary potential and both saved wave states. Replace only the *increment* of the stellar source: calculate the Poisson potential from the tagged-star endpoint minus its launch, holding any launch mismatch fixed. Keep the original gas increment in all arms. Integrate the same self-consistent nonlinear wave equation by its retained midpoint rule, linearly interpolating this two-epoch external source. This does not close the gas source. The ordinary force increment is common to the two arms; their difference in the reported force is the change of wave self-gravity, not a new coupling.

3. **What the parent fields do to stars.** For all stars in the original guarded launch aperture, evolve the *same* positions, full three-velocities and fine labels in each metric. This is a collisionless test-particle calculation; these probe paths do not replace their own source during propagation. Take the common ordinary potential from the fine measured-mass stellar increment plus the unchanged gas. Use open Newtonian multipoles for each wave endpoint and its Hamiltonian time derivative, with cubic Hermite interpolation between endpoints. In dimensionless variables \(y=r/L\), \(\xi=|\hat n\cdot e|\), the vector force is \(-V_y\hat n-(V_\xi/y)[{\rm sgn}(\hat n\cdot e)e-\xi\hat n]\). The common potential is differentiated from the retained piecewise representation of \(yV\). RK4 advances Cartesian coordinates, preserving azimuthal motion. Small negative times only test local derivatives; they are not a measured earlier trajectory.

   The shell mass weight is the original compact bump. For the gravity window integrate the retained radial weight once, setting its value to zero above the outer edge; the additive constant has no effect on paired contrasts. Directly compare \([\delta O(h)+\delta O(-h)]/h^2\) and \(3[\delta O(h)-\delta O(-h)]/h^3\) with the earlier curvature and cubic kernels. The contrast and its first derivative vanish at launch. The full 2.221 Myr calculation is for this **launch-selected population**, not a complete finite-aperture galaxy census. It is not an observed rotation curve or a proof that the gas and surroundings satisfy local conservation.
