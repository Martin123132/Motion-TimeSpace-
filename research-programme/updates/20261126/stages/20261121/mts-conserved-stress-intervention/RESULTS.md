# Where the extra tide comes from: remove the stress and measure the response

Local simulation, 21 November 2026. All numbers below are signed parts per million of the **original matched Newtonian tide magnitude**, not ppm accuracy on an astronomical measurement. No fit, new coupling or change of preparation.

**Result.** The propagating stress term was not merely correlated with the previously measured excess. Removing the active angular trace-free stress from the coupled evolution changes the tide in the forecast direction on both fine hosts. But this intervention also moves density: a trace-free stress is not generally a transverse wave. We derived that extra contribution from conservation and predicted the combined change before scoring the altered histories. A second, independent Poisson calculation checks the split.

**Why this matters for the Newton limit.** The temporal native tide exceeded Newton in magnitude by only 45–49 ppm, not by 93–94 ppm. The latter is a *causal difference between two GR trajectories*. Newtonian gravity also responds to the density moved by the altered stress. Once that density response is matched, the measured difference beyond Poisson is 58–60 ppm, as independently predicted. With the stress off, a signed residual against the matched Newton control remains: +9.25 to +10.28 ppm on the positive host and −12.99 to −12.30 ppm on the negative host. Increasing the tensor stress to fit that residual would spoil the withdrawal prediction. The Newton control evolves a conserved differential source on the supplied homogeneous background; it is not a fully re-evolved self-gravitating Newtonian universe.
