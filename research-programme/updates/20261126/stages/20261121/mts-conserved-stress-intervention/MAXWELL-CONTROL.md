# A physical tensor-only control from the retained Maxwell action

A freely specified trace-free stress need not be transverse. There is, however, a **Maxwell source whose interference term is exactly transverse**, so it tests the other side of the density-versus-propagation distinction. This is a separate analytic weak-field control, not a new cosmological run. Use the canonical term and the displayed Weyl–photon operator in the retained local parent action (not bundled here). The Einstein–Maxwell stress convention is the usual one ([Gourgoulhon, electromagnetic field in 3+1](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0703035)). No new gravitational normalization: κ=1/M_R² in natural units.

Take two plane-fronted, x-polarized pulses moving in opposite z directions. Set u=t−z, v=t+z, E=(f(u)+g(v)) e_x, B=(f(u)−g(v)) e_y. Compare the field with the second pulse phase-reversed; half the difference of the two stresses isolates the interference term. The canonical energy (E²+B²)/2 and longitudinal Poynting flux E×B are unchanged, but the difference of spatial stress is

    π_cross = 2 f(u)g(v) diag(−1,+1,0).

The original full fields have positive energy; zero energy applies only to the *difference*. Its spatial trace and divergence vanish identically. There is no Newtonian Poisson tide in this difference with zero incoming difference data. Yet the opposite-sign geodesic tide measured in the plus polarization, (T_xx−T_yy)/2, need not vanish. In linear Einstein gravity let h_xx=−h_yy=h. Then h_tt−h_zz=−4κfg. No incident gravitational wave and no pre-overlap perturbation give

    h=−κ F(u)G(v),      F′=f, G′=g, primitives taken from −∞;
    T_plus=h_tt/2=−κ [ f′G+2fg+Fg′ ]/2.

This is a driven geodesic observable, not the electric Weyl tensor alone while the source is present. The profile is finite per transverse area, not localized in the transverse directions; do not use it as an astronomical or engineering amplitude estimate.

**A conditional MTS operator selection rule.** The parent also permits c_IR C_abcd F^ab F^cd. Setting background Weyl curvature to zero is *not* sufficient to discard its metric variation: δC need not vanish. Instead test it against an arbitrary planar plus/cross metric variation, with compactly supported test functions of t,z. With a,b in (t,z), this variation gives δC_axbx=−∂a∂b h_plus/2+η_ab □h_plus/4, using □=−∂t²+∂z². The cross-polarized test does not enter this component. The counter-propagating photon cross dyad is proportional to k₊ᵃk₋ᵇ+k₋ᵃk₊ᵇ=−2ηᵃᵇ. Consequently its contraction with δC_axbx is **zero before integration or imposing the metric wave equation**. Both helicity-two tests are silent for this vertex. An independent transverse *trace* test h_xx=h_yy=w gives instead a cross contraction 8fg δC_yxyx=4fg □w/3, generally nonzero. Thus only the polarized interference source is protected at this order, not the entire gravitational response or all electromagnetic couplings. Planar symmetry decouples the scalar/trace field from the transverse plus readout in linear propagation. Higher gravitational derivative terms, nonplanar beams, a curved background, nonlinearities and incoming waves are outside the cancellation proof.

The verifier contracts the full linearized four-dimensional Weyl tensor for nontrivial test functions, including the nonzero trace control. A separate ordinary retarded spacetime quadrature driven by Maxwell stress checks both the metric solution and its time-differenced tidal response for compact pulse profiles. This is a way to test a field's *source* with no density signal to hide behind; it does not measure G or establish an observational detection.
