# Which part of the photon tidal response reaches two separated rays?

Use two real photons, a spacelike gravitational momentum \(-Q^2\), and the signed form factor \(f(Q^2)=-F_3(-Q^2)\) in the preceding matching (not bundled here). This is transverse position space, not a pair of off-shell photons. At first order in the weak metric and in the nonminimal vertex, source and detector are taken to infinity on opposite sides of the stationary lens; compare the same screen eigenlabels at both impact parameters. Require geometric optics (\(\omega b\gg1\) in natural units), a weak exterior field (\(GM/(c^2 b)\ll1\)), and negligible recoil. The femtometre examples specify a mathematical kernel, not a realized apparatus or an astrophysical mass placed inside a femtometre.

**Fix the sign and the normalization independently.** In the retained mostly-plus action \(-F^2/4+c_W CFF\), choose the signed polarization difference so that a positive local \(c_W\) gives a positive delay. For constant matching \(c_W=(\hbar c)^2 F_3(0)\). The static screen action and endpoint integral (not bundled here) give \(24t_M c_W\int dz\,b^2/(z^2+b^2)^{5/2}=32t_Mc_W/b^2\), \(t_M=GM/c^3\). Both modes retain the same leading Shapiro time \(4t_M\ln(b_{\rm IR}/b)\), up to an additive reference. A helicity-preserving loop is common too; it should not be called zero in the individual times.

**Keep the pole.** The leading traceless projected metric amplitude is proportional to \((q_x\pm i q_y)^2 f(Q^2)/Q^2\). For conserved static matter it is the transverse tidal Hessian of the projected Newtonian field: the nonminimal real-photon helicity-flip vertex has the common hard photon momentum twice contracted with the source, and two soft transverse derivatives. Source trace/contact terms without that hard contraction are subleading in the high-frequency eikonal. The scalar form factor multiplies that same tensor; take its ratio to the pointlike constant vertex, not a center-of-mass scattering phase equated directly with static timing. In [Bellazzini et al., v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05896v3), the eikonal matrix, transverse Fourier prescription and Appendix scalar loop supply independent controls. A convention caveat: with their displayed \(q_\pm=(q_x\pm i q_y)/\sqrt 2\), transforming the matrix element \(-4q_+^2\) on a radial \(H(b^2)\) gives \(2(\partial_x+i\partial_y)^2H=8e^{2i\phi}b^2 H''\); the subsequent eigenvalue formula prints 16. These displayed formulas do not give the same absolute coefficient when used together. We normalize the static observable to the independent screen action above, not to that printed eigenvalue. Reversing polarization labels only changes a common sign.

Write the window contribution as \(f(Q^2)=C+\int s\,dI/(s+Q^2)\), \(s=W^2\), plus higher local terms. Here \(dI=2\kappa\,\beta^3 {\rm Re}[(h_c+h_n/2)A_\pi^*]\,dW/W^3\), \(\kappa=\alpha/(4\pi)\), using the existing angular and stress waves. This is an interference measure, not a probability density. The two-dimensional Green functions for the partial fractions of \(s/[Q^2(Q^2+s)]\) are \(G_0-G_s=[-\ln(b/b_{\rm IR})-K_0(Wb)]/(2\pi)\). The screen difference applies \(\partial_b^2-b^{-1}\partial_b\): the pieces become \(1/(\pi b^2)\) and \(s K_2(Wb)/(2\pi)\). Define \(U(y)=y^2K_2(y)/2\), \(b_{\rm nat}=b_{\rm phys}/(\hbar c)\). At nonzero separation,
\[
 R(b)=-{b_{\rm phys}^2(t_+-t_-)\over32t_M(\hbar c)^2}
     =C+\int [1-U(Wb_{\rm nat})]\,dI .
\]
Additional finite polynomials \(Q^{2n}\), \(n\ge1\), have only contact support after the traceless projection for a point lens. A constant is *not* a contact: it retains the inverse-square tidal transfer. Splitting a renormalized subtraction from the integral differently shifts \(C\) but does not affect
\[
 D(b,B)=R(B)-R(b)=\int[U(Wb_{\rm nat})-U(WB_{\rm nat})]\,dI .
\]
Both modes are needed at each distance. Plain differences of arrival times at two distances do not implement this cancellation. Since \(U'(y)=-y^2 K_1(y)/2\), the bracket is positive for \(B>b\) and is no larger than \(U(Wb_{\rm nat})\). The unknown band above \(W_c\) contributes at most \(U(W_c b_{\rm nat})\int_{>W_c}|dI|\); without the total variation this is a bound *per unit norm*, not a bound on the entire omitted contribution. Nonlocal massless channels, if present, do not have pion-range suppression.

**Resolved lens.** At \(b>a\) the azimuthal average of the logarithmic potential of any circular projected source of radius \(a\) is the point log (mass normalized). The corresponding Yukawa average is \(K_0(Wb)\langle I_0(Wr)\rangle_\Sigma\) by the cylindrical addition theorem. Thus the finite-range difference acquires the factor \(2I_1(Wa)/(Wa)\) for a uniform disk, without changing the canceled local piece. Independent transverse-Hessian integration over the disk tests both terms. This does not apply inside the material or at arbitrary source orientation. In a nonspherical geometry the local response has to be canceled using the two *actual* signed screen transfers (or their matrices if eigenaxes do not agree). Two equal projected point masses at \(\pm0.3\), with rays at 1 and 2 along their axis, leave a local residue of −0.24615 in units of the point local transfer at 1 if the point weights are used. Divide each split by its calculated local response to cancel this term, provided both are nonzero and the same polarization quantity is measured. Finite endpoints, plasma, and instrumental polarization require separate treatment.

**Point scalar, no independent contact.** Set \(\kappa=m=1\), \(x=mb\), \(v=\sqrt{1-4m^2/s}\). The polarization-projected Born amplitude is \(h=(1-v^2)\int_0^1 P_2(z)/(1-v^2z^2)\,dz\); \(dI=v^4 h\,dv/2\). Independent checks are \(\int dI=1/180\), \(\int s\,dI=1/6\), \(R(x)/x^2\to1/24\). For an independent momentum-space test take the published *closed scalar form factor*, not our spectral integration, and perform its oscillatory \(J_2\) transform. Write \(B(p)=1/6-p^2 f(p^2)\), \(L=\operatorname{asinh}(p/2)\). The Appendix expression reduces to \(B=-[(48/p^2-24)L^2-84+72\sqrt{1+4/p^2}L]/(6p^2)\). Then \(R=x^2[1/12-\int J_2(y)B(y/x)\,dy/y]/2\). The subtraction uses \(\int J_2(y)dy/y=1/2\). For large \(x\), \(h\sim 2v^2/15\) at threshold; the remaining Gaussian integral of \(v^6e^{-xv^2}\) gives \(D(x,2x)\sim\pi e^{-2x}/(16x^2)\). This coefficient is a control for a point charged scalar, not a pion polarizability assumption.

**Angular bounds.** On the higher interval multiply the old spin-two Cauchy projection at each energy by the *new* two-distance kernel; square that kernel only in the per-bin rate budget. Do not multiply a bound on the unweighted signed moment by a mean weight. Retain the full original energy-bin widths, opposite-helicity nonnegative budget, higher Born remainder, shared charged normalization and the pointwise statistical/systematic boxes. These are conditional outer intervals, not likelihood intervals. The unit-kernel row reproduces the earlier numbers. The lower elastic wave is kept as a signed reference; there is no fitted update to it or to \(C\).
