# Where could the three-lobed wave be seen?
       
The single meridian does not separate this small term from the foreground. A different observable on the *same theoretical solution* does. At each radius take the north–south average of spherical radial acceleration and half the north-minus-south polar acceleration. For either real profile D(φ) on a ring, define
       
    A₃ = (1/π) ∫₀²π D(φ) exp[−3i(φ−π)] dφ.
       
Then D₃(φ) = Re[A₃ exp(3i(φ−π))]. The real part is the meridian measurement; the sine coefficient is minus the imaginary part. This projection removes an axisymmetric force, the first and second harmonics, and a spatially uniform acceleration. It does not remove the satellite's own third harmonic or any physical m=3 ordinary-matter response. Subtract the same direct field in both model and control. On a finite uniform grid the estimator aliases harmonics congruent to ±3 modulo the number of sites. The 16-, 128- and 256-site controls in the ring calculation (not bundled here) refer to ideal equal point samples, not existing angular coverage, finite observational apertures, errors or probabilities.
       
At 128 angles we evaluate the native satellite potential and force on the test rings; the independent subtraction uses the archived radial Hermite representation and Cartesian force differentiation. The collisionless comparison uses each simulation's own initial and final dark host field twice, via its archived multipole and basis representations. Their difference can include assembly and numerical field representation as well as any wake. It is **not** a common-initial-state, particle-for-wave matched collisionless evolution on these two MTS grounds. The two representations of a given model are not independent measurements. Static spherical NFW contributes exactly zero to this harmonic after subtracting the direct satellite; that is not a null prediction for a live halo. The scientific comparison is whether the complex wave harmonic is distinguishable from *uncertainty in the subtracted field*, not whether the signal is larger than zero to floating-point precision. The spread of scenarios is not an observational covariance and cannot be quoted as a statistical significance.
