# Which mass responds, and which clocks are being compared?

## One common interaction, two possible physical sources

Keep the positive quadratic wave, its canonical occupation, and its metric coupling from the parent action (not bundled here). The previous material test (not bundled here) ruled out identifying a general material weight with a universal force multiplier. Here the choices are instead (i) raw gas and stars or (ii) the represented mass. Each sources the wave potential and receives the conjugate force using *the same mass*. The interaction is \(E_\times=\int\rho_w V_o\,d^3x=\int V_w\,dm_o\) with the isolated Newton kernel. Variation in the wave and parcel coordinates gives the wave potential and \(\ddot X=-\nabla V_w\), respectively. A passive tag changes a readout, not the acceleration. The fixed part of the inherited host field is external to this transaction. No new force coefficient or occupation is fitted.

Write the crossed difference as \(\mathcal D=(\mathrm{raw}-\mathrm{represented})(\mathrm{inherited}-\mathrm{reset})\). In each wave arm, use the identical initial canonical wave for both hosts. The leading Newtonian launch potentials add; their crossed difference vanishes. This does not hold after evolution. Keep separate the fixed-position wave response, motion of the active ordinary source, and wave response induced by that motion.

Let \(U=V_{o,\rm raw}-V_{o,\rm rep}\), let \(K\) be the outer-minus-inner Newton potential-clock kernel (including \(1/c^2\)), and let \(C_w=\int K\,dm_w\). For initially identical waves the changes in density and current vanish at launch. The *difference in the time derivative of the current* is \(-\rho_w\nabla U\): velocity transport, quantum stress and wave self-gravity initially agree. Continuity, integration by parts with the isolated wave boundary, and then subtraction of the two preparations give
\[
  \mathcal D\ddot C_w=-\int (dm_{w,c}-dm_{w,r})\,\nabla K\cdot\nabla U .
\]
This uses both radial and polar gradients, not a fitted quadratic in time. With initially stationary parcels the common acceleration and common positional derivative of that acceleration cancel in the host difference through second order. The next terms in the displacement are \(t^3 g[\Delta\dot M_w]/6+t^4 g[\Delta\ddot M_w]/24\), with \(g\) the linear wave-to-parcel acceleration. A local real potential leaves the launch density derivative unchanged in the continuum. The small nonzero cubic term in the native projected calculation is recorded, not inserted as a physical source.

For local momentum exchange use the cross part of Newtonian field stress:
\[
 4\pi G\,t^\times_{ij}=\partial_i V_o\partial_j V_w+\partial_i V_w\partial_j V_o
                -\delta_{ij}\nabla V_o\cdot\nabla V_w.
\]
Its divergence is the negative of the two matter force densities combined. The radial weak equation for test vector \(f(r)e_r\) includes tangential stress as well as both boundary tractions. A delta sheet at a shell endpoint must use the same inclusion convention on both sides: open, half, or closed. This is a Newtonian interaction identity, not a claim of a local covariant gravitational stress tensor.

## A relativistic term that survives the crossed comparison

Use the retained normal-energy and trace-free constraints (not bundled here) for lapse \(e^{\Phi/c^2}\) and spatial metric \(e^{-2\Psi/c^2}\delta_{ij}\) on the slow scalar slice. Let \(W=V_{w,c}-V_{w,r}\); all potentials use a common isolated zero. Fix the canonical first-order wave preparation across hosts within an arm and the first-order densitized ordinary energy across arms within a host; hold initial ordinary anisotropy equal across arms. Ordinary parcels are initially at rest. Without these matching assumptions extra mixed matter sources survive.

Write \(P=c^2\mathcal D\Psi_{\rm corr}\), \(Q=c^2\mathcal D\Phi_{\rm corr}\), \(Z=P-Q\), where “corr” is the first term beyond the Newtonian potential. Below only their spherical means are taken. Bilinearity cancels the separate wave gradient energy, ordinary input and terms linear in either potential:
\[
 \Delta_r(P-\langle UW\rangle)=-\langle\nabla U\cdot\nabla W\rangle,\quad
 Z''-Z'/r=4\langle U'W'-\tfrac12\nabla_\perp U\cdot\nabla_\perp W\rangle .
\]
Here \(\nabla_\perp\) is the physical tangential gradient, and angular projection comes **after** multiplication. The independent normal source is \(\langle U\Delta W+W\Delta U+\nabla U\cdot\nabla W\rangle\). The shell force combination eliminates homogeneous \(r^{-2}\) and \(r\) forces. For a clock difference also impose a regular centre with no extra point source and a common isolated asymptotic clock. If \(S_Z\) is the right-hand side, \(Z(r)=\tfrac12\int_r^\infty(s^2-r^2)S_Z(s)\,ds/s\). Multipole tails beyond the wave support are integrated analytically.

## How the surfaces are identified

The coordinate-sphere log-lapse result is \([Q]_{1}^{2}/c^4\). To compare members of the same isotropic-coordinate sphere family at fixed physical area \(4\pi R^2\), solve \(r^2\langle e^{-2\Psi/c^2}\rangle=R^2\); to first order \(r=R(1+\bar V/c^2)\). Use proper area also when averaging the log lapse. Expanding and taking the crossed difference adds, at each radius,
\[
 c^{-4}\left\{R(\bar U\,\bar W'+\bar W\,\bar U')
              -4(\langle UW\rangle-\bar U\bar W)\right\}.
\]
Subtract the inner surface result from the outer one. Exponentiating the mean log difference to give a ratio of geometric mean rates adds another term, \([\bar U]_1^2[\bar W]_1^2/c^4\). Neither operation is optional if that is the observable being defined. In an axisymmetric geometry specifying the area alone does **not** uniquely choose a surface, an observer or a line of sight. These results refer to the stated sphere family, slicing and averages, not two individual clocks exchanging light.
