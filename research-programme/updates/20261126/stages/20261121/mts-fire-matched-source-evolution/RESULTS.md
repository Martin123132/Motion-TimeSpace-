# Changing the host, then comparing like clocks

**Private · FIRE snapshot 590 · 21 November 2026.** The same mass now sources and receives gravity on both sides of each test. Four matched runs separate physical replacement of the ordinary mass, motion of labelled parcels, and the wave response. No rotation velocities, clock measurements, or new fit were used.

## What moves the Newtonian clock?

Inside the 30.091 kpc launch aperture the raw source contains \(9.54290\times10^{10}\,M_\odot\), the represented source \(3.92244\times10^{10}\,M_\odot\). Their static starting rate difference is \(+8.20172\times10^{-8}\) with either wave preparation; it cancels in the crossed comparison. At 0.24 Myr the crossed fractional clock change (raw minus represented, inherited minus reset, outer minus inner) splits as follows:

| Contribution | Fractional rate |
|---|---:|
| Wave evolution in the replaced but position-fixed host | −2.0393943e−14 |
| Motion of the active ordinary mass | −1.0769541e−15 |
| Additional wave response to that motion | −8.9956e−21 |
| Total | −2.1470906e−14 |

The static host replacement explains 94.98% of this **difference**, not 95% of gravity. A different within-bin radial representation with a different time refinement changes the total by 0.088%. The moving-minus-fixed clock, paired across wave preparations, is −7.86870e−15 in the raw host versus −6.79174e−15 in the represented host. The latter reproduces the earlier run. In the raw runs the residual in exchanged energy falls about fourfold per halving of the time step, reaching \(3.04\) and \(3.40\times10^{-7}\) of the acquired kinetic energy.

The continuum force law derived in [the calculation](DERIVATION.md) gives a crossed initial clock curvature of **−7.08428e−13 Myr⁻²**, including −4.66877e−14 from the polar term. Its quadratic prediction at 0.24 Myr differs by **0.043%** from the fixed-host wave response above. This is a check between distinct calculations, not a fit. It is not an error bound at later times. For the 220,278 positive common tagged parcels, the host-induced path differences are only about \(4.0\times10^{-6}\) pc RMS. Their path-only clock shifts are \(+1.687\) and \(+1.679\times10^{-18}\), versus roughly \(+1.1\times10^{-13}\) from changing the material population in each arm. The leading short-time path prediction overestimates the small endpoint response by about 7%; the much better wave-clock agreement should not be assigned to that smaller signal.

## A check on the GR connection

At launch, crossed Newtonian potentials cancel. In the retained scalar Einstein equations the products of the host and wave gradients do not. Independent evaluations of the normal constraint, one from the direct energy source and one integrated by parts, agree to better than \(2\times10^{-14}\) in the tested compensated forces. For uniform mass density inside each original radial bin, the compensated spatial and time potential forces are respectively **−1.5830e−20** and **−2.3950e−21 m/s²**. These are combinations across three radii, not individual point accelerations.

| Launch clock definition, outer minus inner | Crossed correction |
|---|---:|
| Spherical mean log lapse at fixed isotropic radii | +2.59979e−16 |
| Shift to spheres of equal physical area across models | −3.08826e−16 |
| Change from angular to proper-area weighting | −5.92749e−20 |
| Proper-area mean log rate at matched areas | **−4.89063e−17** |
| Ratio of geometric mean rates rather than its logarithm | −1.03762e−16 |

The two sphere labels are 6.18466 and 8.43445 kpc. The sign reversal comes mainly from *where the clocks are compared*, not numerical precision. Uniform mass per unit radius instead of per unit volume gives −4.89056e−17 for the matched log rate. Solving the nonlinear area equation on the actual angular profiles reproduces the geometry correction within two parts per million at the smallest tested amplitudes for both layouts. That replay tests the area matching, not the entire nonlinear Einstein system. These launch shell averages and the later two-direction Newtonian readout are different quantities; do not add them.

## What was caught

The original shell momentum test missed up to **0.565%** when the integrated force included a full endpoint mass sheet but the surface stress used a half sheet. Matching those conventions closes every order-four shell test (both hosts, both preparations, launch and endpoint, three radial probes and all three inclusion rules) to **4.11e−15 or better** relative to the sum of absolute component forces. The failed tables were kept. The transported ordinary matter also generates a nonzero kinetic-energy/anisotropic-stress correction: at the endpoint the paired time-force contribution is +2.39546e−25 m/s² raw and +2.05123e−25 m/s² represented. This is a distinct matter term, not the total endpoint metric.
