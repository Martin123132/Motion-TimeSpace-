# Which way was the wave flowing at snapshot 590?

**Independent verdict: undetermined.** The retained FIRE-2 m12i mass histories measure gas and stars, and the published denser cadence tracks a baryonic potential. Neither provides the isolated wave response. Our archives with the correct heavy-wave variable were generated by our own solver. The nearby LMC, force-band, timing and Galaxy Lab products do not independently measure that variable as a function of time on these shells. No observed density or potential was used to select a sign. This conclusion is scoped to the products checked in history-gate.json, not to all possible datasets.

## Test against our own saved evolution
We reconstructed density *from the saved finite-element wave amplitudes*, not from their potential, and took one-sided time differences of shell densities at the first three recorded times. The launch density agrees with the frozen Poisson reconstruction to <4.4e−14 on the angular RMS scale. The fine step is about 469 years, with the 938-year calculation as a resolution check. The eight series cover both steps, both preparations, and both raw and represented baryonic sources.

At both resolutions the forward forecast passes for both preparations and sources; the reverse fails on the same tests. The worst fine-step volume-and-angle RMS discrepancy is 0.00472% of the forecast; the reversed slope misses by about 200%, as expected for a sign change. The largest coarse-to-fine discrepancy is below 0.0019% on either metric. These are numerical comparison errors, not astrophysical uncertainties. The original 0.5% numerical ceiling and 1e−9 parity tolerance are unchanged. The angular rate is somewhat more sensitive to operator choice than to radial quadrature (native vs flux at most 0.0151% in the retained max-component diagnostic). The earlier phase-control failure is not regraded.

**What would actually distinguish the flows:** an independently prepared history measuring the same isolated mass redistribution with a known time direction, aligned apertures and uncertainty on the rate. A time-resolved *total* potential would need independently constrained ordinary and other source changes before subtraction. Even a successful monopole test would constrain net shell flux, not the complete transverse current. Until an appropriate data product or separately justified tracer exists, keep the frozen forecasts; further replays of the same solver will not select a physical direction.
