# Did an independent measurement choose the flow direction?

**No.** The [candidate measurements](SOURCES.md) reviewed do not supply a total mass derivative and an ordinary-matter derivative in the same physical shells, system and time window with the uncertainties needed to isolate the frozen wave. A missing residual is not zero. Neither flow direction gets an observational score. The forecast, bins and both tolerances stayed unchanged.

**New measurement from independent simulation particles:** in the original FIRE-2 m12i foreground we could account directly for ordinary matter: 24 retained gas/star particle packets, four pieces per species at epochs 589, 590 and 591. The enclosed sums and separate shell sums agree at the frozen numerical tolerance. This is the raw analog, not Milky Way data, nor a second measurement of the hybrid wave. The unequal snapshot separations are 22.210 Myr and 2.221 Myr; the centered estimate weights the two slopes for these actual time intervals. No assumption of a uniform mass inside a bin was needed.

Units: solar masses per year **in the shell**. These are not accretion rates onto the whole galaxy. The centered rates **inside the three spheres** are +14.562, +12.098 and −17.903 respectively. Outer face minus inner face gives the tabulated shell rates. An independent estimator on the single launch snapshot, summing gas and stellar velocities through the faces at all preselected bandwidths and both centre motions, gives −0.66 to −0.18 in the inner shell and −36.47 to −27.05 in the outer. These ranges describe numerical estimator/frame sensitivity, not observational error bars; SECANT AND FLUX ARE FROM THE SAME SIMULATION. We cannot transfer the FIRE baryonic subtraction to a different galaxy. The discrepancy between methods in the inner shell is especially important: a smooth centred slope is not an exact instantaneous mass flux.

**What discrimination would require:** the reversed wave contributes exactly the negatives of the forward entries. Thus the two possible outer-shell residuals differ by 0.02091 (reset) or 1.96558 (coupled) solar masses/year; the inner separations are 0.10365 and 1.99161. A standalone sign measurement centered on the true value would need total uncertainty below the distance to zero, not merely below the full separation, at its stated confidence. Use the covariance of total and ordinary fluxes across adjacent faces, plus other source and cadence uncertainties. There is no justified variance to attach to the absent total measurement here. FIRE's own collisionless halo is not missing from the *public simulation*: subtracting baryons from native total would measure that halo instead, a distinct experiment.

**Best observational leads and limits:** the [stellar radial-motion study](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/521/3/4074/7081358) explicitly investigated detection of mass change, but its quantitative test is in simulated shells at 15–45 kpc, not these radii. [Pulsar accelerations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.15808) are measurements of gravity, not by themselves its change. Real [timing second derivatives](https://pure.manchester.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/110416649/Frequency_second_derivatives_and_timing_noises.pdf) mix line-of-sight jerk, motion and intrinsic timing terms. physical-targets.csv gives the wave's predicted sphere-mean radial acceleration derivative, roughly 2–3e−29 m/s³ for reset and 3e−28 m/s³ for coupled (magnitudes); these are not line-of-sight predictions for a named pulsar. A sign-blind upper limit cannot choose between equal and opposite coefficients in isolation.

**Recommendation:** close this as an open, frozen prediction instead of searching for another static mass profile. Before spending more on this observable, run a small mock recovery at actual tracer positions/noise with both signs, spatially varying ordinary forces, moving source and observer, and a baryons-only control. If the observable cannot recover the sign there, prioritise a different test with data already able to constrain it. No extrapolation of an instantaneous wave slope over a whole FIRE interval.
