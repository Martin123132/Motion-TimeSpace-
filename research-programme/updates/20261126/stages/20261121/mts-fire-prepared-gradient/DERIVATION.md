## 1. Measure the wave before opening the comparisons

For each real or complex mass-amplitude use its host's own dimensionless norm N. With D(f)=∫|∇f|² and the Hermitian gradient product B, use

    d²(a,b) = min_p D(pa−b) = D(a)+D(b)−2|B(b,a)|,     |p|=1.
    E_distance = v_*² d²/(2N),     a_* = v_*²/L_*.

This is a quotient distance squared times a unit conversion, not excitation in the full Hamiltonian, work or an inferred particle number. A projected quadratic force has norm at most C D with C≤0.782087227800. Polarizing that form gives |δa|≤a_* C [d²+2√(D(b)d²)]. For a real ground, split the optimally aligned change into real u and imaginary w. The finite density change is 2bu+u²+w²; w has no linear term. For that specified preparation replace d² under the square root by D(u); keep d² in the quadratic term. No such real-reference replacement is made for comparisons to a moving complex wave.

For each site, project the reference's radial and polar forces onto their signed rectangle. These are orthogonal physical components, so the Euclidean norm of the missed components is a necessary vector change F. With f=F/(a_* C), the necessary squared gradient distance is [f/(√(D(b)+f)+√D(b))]². Use the upper enclosure for D(b) to obtain a *lower* requirement. Maximize over four sites within each history, not over incompatible satellite alternatives. Repeating at the propagated state tells how much a further disturbance would have to supply there. The triangle inequality also bounds that increment by the positive part of (distance required from ground minus distance already traversed). The raw untransferred m12i wave uses its own simulated ground but is never compared to the Milky Way rectangles.
