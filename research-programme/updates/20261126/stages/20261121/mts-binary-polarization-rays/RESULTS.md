# One photon coupling, two measurements: from a metric tide to a binary pulsar

**Local result, 21 November 2026.** For the saved central orbit of PSR B1534+12 (J1537+1155), the first derivative of the propagation-time difference of the two polarizations is **5.67635 × 10⁻²² s/m²** near superior conjunction, rather than the archived 8.42157 × 10⁻²². This predicts 0.568 μs **if** the coefficient is 10¹⁵ m². It does not estimate the coefficient. Two methods with the same physical endpoints agree; two real Parkes observing windows instead sample low-response parts of the orbit. No timing residuals were fitted.

## Deriving the transfer rather than adopting a timing allowance

Use the retained action and optical normalization (not bundled here), with L = −F²/4 + c_W C_abcd F^ab F^cd. Its two Schwarzschild angular characteristic factors are reciprocal, with W₊=(1−8ηu³)/(1+16ηu³), u=b/r, μ=M/b, η=c_W M/b³, M=GM_comp/c_light², b the **straight** chord impact parameter. The local tangential squared-speed slopes are ±24 in this convention. The characteristic itself is also found by [Chen & Jing](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.01088); this is not a new photon operator. We compute a finite-source transfer for the [archived measured orbital geometry](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.4836), not a fixed-pericentre travel time. See [frozen design](DESIGN.md) and five geometries (not bundled here).

Between fixed emission and reception positions the ray integral obeys u′²=1/(jW)²−(1−2μu)u²/W. Here j is the conserved impact parameter divided by b; it is **different** on each shot ray. Integrate the common-radial-time-subtracted derivative (c_light/b)t′_reg=j/(1−jWu′). The same tortoise-coordinate subtraction at the endpoints cancels from the delay difference, even for a receiver at infinity. A finite static receiver converts coordinate time to proper time by √(1−2M/r_obs). In the stationary optical path, variation of the path contributes no first-order endpoint term. Differentiating the angular part of the optical metric gives the independent GR-ray integral

    K = d(t₊−t₋)/dc_W = [24 M j /(c_light b²)] ∫ u_GR³ dθ .

We solved the common GR boundary problem by collocation and also shot both polarized rays at ± coupling, at three steps. All 84 conjunction/finite-receiver shots hit their endpoints to 9.64 × 10⁻¹⁵ or better in u; the two derivative methods agree within 1.12 × 10⁻⁹ relative. Only the derivative at zero is used. The first bending correction follows by solving y″+y=3cos²θ with zero endpoint values for u=cosθ+μy+… . Its omitted term quarters when μ halves. A single finite difference misses the mass-scaling reference by about 4.0 × 10⁻⁵ in dimensionless units; the fourth-order extrapolation, not the single difference, passed. Signed rays (not bundled here) · [weak limit](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `weak-limit.csv`) · conjunction results (not bundled here).

Bending changes the central result by −105.4 parts per million relative to the *corrected* straight-ray law. It does not explain the larger change relative to checkpoint 4945: that comparison also changes the angular/endpoints treatment and uses the full-index normalization rather than simply rescaling the [historical formula](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0306021). At the conservative saved geometry K=4.98405 × 10⁻²² s/m². A hypothetical 1 μs allowance would convert to 1.76 × 10¹⁵ m² centrally or 2.01 × 10¹⁵ m² on that geometry. Neither is a likelihood or a measured limit. Static isolated companion, primary image, Newtonian orbit identified with exterior coordinates, geometric optics and equal emission event are assumptions. This omits companion motion, other metric terms and plasma; precision of the integrations is not precision of actual sky timing.

## What is distinctive over an orbit?

With v=sin(i)sin(ω+f), separation r and receiver at infinity, the leading weak coefficient is K=8M(2−v)(1+v)/[c_light r²(1−v)]. As a diagnostic only, extend the old conjunction law as K_old=24M/[c_light r²(1−v)]. Then K−(2/3)K_old = 8a_los/c_light³, with a_los=GM_comp v/r² in the saved sign convention. Marginalizing an unrestricted term of **that specific shape** would make these scalar laws indistinguishable after rescaling at this order. The projection residual is 1.72 × 10⁻¹⁶. It does *not* erase the whole signal, establish that such a nuisance is present, or imply that the historical paper predicted this off-conjunction extension.

At the reference periastron angle the computed transfer peaks at true anomaly 166.458° (mean orbital phase 0.436994). Its half-height window lasts 70.56 min, 11.65% of the approximately 10.10 h period. 180 orbital samples (not bundled here) and [nine direct shooting checks](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `orbital-shoot-check.csv`). Do not use this epoch's phase window for an arbitrary date: periastron advances.

The polarization orientation should rotate across an exposure. In a fixed sky frame with ascending node Ω, define p=cos(ω+f)+i cos(i)sin(ω+f). An eigenaxis lies in the plane containing the companion, source and observer; its projected double angle is exp(2iΩ)p²/|p|². The orthogonal mode reverses its sign. Thus for a stable pulse template the coefficient multiplying the two derivative shapes is the complex average ⟨K exp(2iχ)⟩, not merely ⟨K⟩. Changing Ω rotates both components; reflecting the inclination conjugates the node-zero template. For 16 min centered on the **reference** peak, the weak scalar average is 0.55806 μs per test coefficient but the rotating-axis norm is 0.53818 μs (96.44%). The curved-ray *scalar* average is 0.557995 μs; we did not trace a full dynamic binary screen. Less than 0.06% cancels this way during either actual, low-response visit.

## Calibration decision

No independent coupling is available to fit to each experiment. In the saved exposure family let Y be the measured metric tide after baseline subtraction, normalized to κAB; w is its saved Weyl response and L the preparation length in metres. Then the consistency test is Δt−(K L²/w)Y=0 to retained order. At T=.12 and .24 positive placement, K/w is respectively 8.01964 × 10⁻²² and 1.02082 × 10⁻²¹ s/m²; mirror placement reverses sign. This transfers the coefficient between stress and propagation, but is not a measurement of either observable. If the baseline subtraction or length calibration is uncertain it must propagate into the test.

The retained matching contract (not bundled here) gives c_W=c_nonQCD+c_QCD^r, **not** a free coefficient of 10¹⁵ m² predicted by MTS. Propagating just its assembled non-QCD interval gives about −5.46 × 10⁻⁵² s centrally (mode-label convention), roughly **45 orders of magnitude below one microsecond**. The undetermined finite QCD term is neither zero by assumption nor bounded by this comparison. Computed transfer and partial scale (not bundled here). A timing campaign could screen an anomalously large total coupling; it is not a realistic way to resolve that known contribution.

**Where to invest next:** prioritize the parent finite mixed stress–two-current matching calculation or an independent calibrated source observable, rather than fitting this off-peak pair. For a sky test, first obtain time/channel-resolved, polarization-calibrated profiles that straddle conjunction, with independent off-conjunction controls at the same frequency and instrumental setup. Do not mix instruments separated by fifteen years as if the intrinsic shape were constant.
