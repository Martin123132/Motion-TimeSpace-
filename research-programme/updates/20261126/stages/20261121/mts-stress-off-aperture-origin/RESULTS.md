**Why the equations recover a Newtonian scalar source.** The Hamiltonian constraint supplies a density term plus an expansion perturbation. The momentum constraint supplies the opposing expansion term in the shear. Combined with the spatial Jacobi equation they give the linear longitudinal tide **P(δε−3Hj)+π_L** on homogeneous flat slices. Here P is the Newtonian trace-free tide operator, j the material current potential, and π_L the longitudinal anisotropic stress. The same source normalization enters both constraints. This is an application of the Einstein term of the retained common-metric action, not a separate postulated MTS law. Eighteen direct four-dimensional metric controls recover the identity; dropping the current fails on the expanding controls, including those with vanishing anisotropic stress. [Derivation and scope](SCALAR-LIMIT.md).

The numerical split shows why it is unsafe to fit an additional force here. The two final expansion/shear contributions on the 640 grid are approximately −748.409 and +748.410 ppm on the positive host, +569.682 and −569.683 on the negative host. Neither is a separate missing force. On the cubic reconstruction, directly measured density minus Newton and the proper-ruler correction each contribute less than 0.04 ppm to the two-window difference. Linear instead of cubic reconstruction can shift the separate terms by up to 0.29 ppm; the sub-0.04 attribution is not a certified absolute precision. The independent Hamiltonian defect contributes +0.364 and +0.676 ppm, respectively; its interpolation sensitivity can reach 0.97 ppm across the controls. **Momentum and exterior matter.** The original two windows leave a single exterior longitudinal-current moment with a derived coefficient. The resolved contribution through 2.8 Mpc at the final clock on the 640 grid is +0.000299 and −0.000293 ppm; even integrating the absolute normal and tangential contributions gives less than 0.0049 ppm on those histories. This is not a bound on the unknown exterior. The additional three-window observable cancels both exterior moments. There the predicted current correction is around a millionth of a ppm; the total radial tide differs from the scalar prediction by −0.072 and +0.064 ppm at the fine endpoint, and by up to 1.48 ppm at an earlier fine clock. These differences include non-scalar curvature and numerical error. Agreement of tiny **signed** numbers does not verify longitudinal dominance of the kinetic remainder; changing interpolation shifts it by more than the predicted current itself. **A numerical trap large enough to change the answer.** Reading just every third cell of the 960 calculation can shift a measured narrow tide by hundreds of ppm. On the positive final narrow temporal readout the three sampling phases shift it by +578.84, −339.71 and −239.13 ppm. Their mean differs from the full measurement by only 0.00004 ppm. Across *both* signs and both readers, the three-phase mean stays within 0.00011 ppm of the native readout, an independent full-grid cubic quadrature also stays within 0.00011, and full-grid linear reconstruction stays within 0.45. The signed cubature uses all cells; thinning is not a valid shortcut. Crossing the coarsened positive fields and rulers puts the large shift in the sampled **tidal field**, not the distance conversion. **Raw centre and its reach.** At 960 the negative-host first-cell polar momentum defect is 7.81 and 11.17 times the wave and cold polar current. It worsens even while aperture agreement improves. For the scalar constraints we derived how a central defect reaches these windows: its Hamiltonian weight is proportional to radius to the fourth power and its leading current weight to radius cubed. The [bound](CENTRE-BOUND.md) keeps the first cell rather than masking it. On the positive finest narrow aperture the individual first-cell contribution is bounded by 0.000063 ppm on the sampled endpoint constraints, without wave/cold, time or host/zero cancellation. Summing the sharper per-shell bound to 0.025 Mpc gives 0.106 ppm; extending it to 0.3 Mpc gives only a loose 894 ppm bound. Do not infer pointwise regularity or exclude an accumulated dynamical error from an endpoint bound.
