# The source in the Newtonian scalar tide is a density–current combination

For the Einstein term in the retained common-metric low-energy action (not bundled here), consider first-order longitudinal scalar perturbations about homogeneous spatially flat FLRW, on the unit-lapse, zero-shift slices used here. No restriction to subhorizon wavelength is needed for the linear constraint argument. Work at nonzero spatial wave number, or with a test functional that annihilates the elliptic homogeneous solutions. Let P be minus one half of the trace-free spatial Hessian of the inverse flat Laplacian (take its radial component for a radial detector). The source variables ε, J, π are in Einstein-equation units. Extract J_L=grad j; π_L is the scalar part of the anisotropic stress tensor.

1. The Hamiltonian constraint gives δR₃/2=δε+2Hδτ; τ is the mixed extrinsic-curvature trace, τ₀=−3H.
2. The scalar part of the spatial Ricci tide is P(δε+2Hδτ), by the contracted spatial Bianchi identity. The Jacobi equation adds Hσ_L+π_L.
3. The longitudinal momentum constraint gives σ_L=−2P(δτ+3j/2), tensorially with the same Hessian convention. Thus the slicing-expansion perturbation cancels without fitting it:

       T_L = P(δε − 3Hj) + π_L .

In conventional units with c=1 the physical energy and current in P carry the **same** κ=8πG; the last term is κ times the physical anisotropic stress. In the slow, negligible-stress and negligible-Hj limit this is the Newtonian trace-free tidal tensor sourced by ΔΦ=4πGδρ. This reduction is the standard Einstein scalar constraint expressed for the retained MTS source and detector. It does not determine the numerical value of G, set the independent tensor solution to zero, or assert that curved spherical hosts are homogeneous. Adding a second calibrated Newtonian coupling here would break the constraint identity. The relation concerns a trace-free tide, not an absolute potential, mass monopole, or galaxy rotation curve.
