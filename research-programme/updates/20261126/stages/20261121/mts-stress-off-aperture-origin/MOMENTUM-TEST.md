# Test fixed before reading the projected currents
Use the saved, stress-off angular extrinsic curvature and the matter flux measured independently from the energy-momentum tensor. In a homogeneous orthonormal spatial frame let Q(f) be the radial trace-free Hessian of the inverse Laplacian of f, and P(f)=-Q(f)/2 the existing Newton tide. The flat momentum constraint is div σ−(2/3)grad τ=J. For its longitudinal scalar part, σ_L=Q(τ+3j/2), where J_L=grad j. This follows by taking a divergence of the Hessian and projecting the momentum equation on gradients. The leading curvature combination from the Hamiltonian and Jacobi equations therefore obeys H[2P(τ)+σ_L]=−3H P(j). This neither assumes the entire shear is scalar nor neglects the measured nonlinear spherical background in the evolution. Test whether the scalar projection provides the measured change; a mismatch is evidence against that *explanation*, not a change of tolerance.

The two-aperture test removes constant exterior tidal fields. A longitudinal-current inversion has a second ambiguity: an exterior divergence can add j∝u²Y₂ inside, hence a tide proportional to u². Introduce a third diagnostic window, same centre and shape, fractional halfwidth 0.225. Weights 1, −12/7, +5/7 on widths 0.15, 0.225, 0.30 cancel both 1 and u². Their second moments are proportional to 1+width²/11. This is an additional observable, not a replacement for the two original bands. Use the physical radius u on the homogeneous ruler. Do not equate the common part of the individual offsets to an external tide. The independent Hamiltonian defect in the original two-aperture difference can also be tested without any exterior assumption.
