# Does four-pion rescattering change the dominant two-pion response?

**21 November 2026 · Local.** Yes, in the retained leading pion theory the photons and the pion stress get **different** four-pion corrections. On the saved 0.5583–0.800 GeV matching window, the calculated feedback opposes the direct four-pion signal. It cancels **9.94%** of that small signal when one stated set of low-energy constants is held fixed, or **6.77%** when an additional low-energy coefficient is held fixed for each source. These are two bookkeeping choices, **not an uncertainty interval or a bound on nature**. In neither worked example does this feedback dominate the direct term. It is not a percent-level change in the much larger two-pion signal.

| Energy | Four-pion contribution to Im H (photon) | Four-pion contribution to Im A (stress) | Leading transition probability, isospin-zero spin-two pion pair → four pions |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.60 GeV | −8.06 × 10⁻¹¹ | −1.92 × 10⁻¹⁰ | 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁰ |
| 0.70 GeV | −6.04 × 10⁻⁸ | −1.46 × 10⁻⁷ | 1.02 × 10⁻⁷ |
| 0.80 GeV | −1.042 × 10⁻⁶ | −2.494 × 10⁻⁶ | 2.377 × 10⁻⁶ |

**What was derived.** Of the contact and ten pion-exchange partitions in the six-pion amplitude, only six exchanges survive this spin projection. None hits a physical propagator pole in the four-to-two region. Their exact projected kernel drives both sources, without a new coupling. The same kernel gives a pure isoscalar D-wave transition cross section of **0.207 μb at 0.80 GeV** in this leading theory. That is not an unseparated charged-beam measurement. Near threshold all three quantities in the table grow as the 11/2 power of excess energy; both source coefficients have a derived negative sign. At 0.80 GeV the isolated relative-phase change on real Born photon and unit stress amplitudes is −1.46 × 10⁻⁵ radians. It is not a measured phase or a change assigned to the full dispersive solution. **Where to spend the next calculation.** The numerical error in this small mechanism is not the main uncertainty in connecting hadronic matter to the photon–gravity coupling. The previous absolute photon-production tests still disagree with data, and neither an inclusive intensity nor a tiny tree phase calibrates a signed gravitational amplitude. Use the *existing* 12 charged Mark II and 136 neutral Belle angular bins to test the inadequately modeled photon S wave: replace only the one-loop S-wave spectator with an independently sourced complex isospin-zero/two reconstruction, holding the D wave and data cuts fixed; split changes in rate from changes in angular shape. Identify any data previously used in the reconstruction. If its relative phase or normalization isn't available, calculate the allowed interference envelope rather than tuning it to the bins. This is a diagnostic of the model used as matter input, not a fit of a new MTS parameter; the absolute parent local term remains separate.
