## A force prediction from geometry

At both northern sites the density outside the probe sphere supplies **inward radial** acceleration; the interior m=2 density contribution is **outward**. They partly cancel in the radial reading. They add with the same, negative sign in the polar reading. For example, at cylindrical radius 6 kpc and height +1.5 kpc the exterior supplies **90.83–90.93%** of the two-lobed polar force in every saved ground/history pair. Full interior + exterior complex fields reproduce the saved force to better than 4.8 × 10⁻¹⁵ relative across the tested settings. This is a check of two evaluations on the same discrete models.

Why does this particular wave help the radial force while hurting the polar force? The lowest angular degree of m=2 has angular dependence proportional to sin²θ exp(2iφ). For sources exterior to a sphere through the test point, its regular interior potential is proportional to r² sin²θ exp(2iφ), or R² exp(2iφ), independent of height. For sources interior to the sphere the potential outside them is proportional to r⁻³ sin²θ exp(2iφ). Differentiate each potential, keeping its source distribution fixed, to get the following **signed** identities for degree two:

    exterior matter:   aθ =  (z/R) ar
    interior matter:   aθ = −(2/3)(z/R) ar

These are spherical radial and polar components at a specified cylindrical R and z; at positive height, a horizontal inward pull gives a negative polar component. The same relation applies to either complex azimuthal quadrature. The identities alone do not determine the two radial amplitudes; those come from the evolved wave and its density. They do not apply unaltered to the direct satellite or the axisymmetric foreground.

Applying this degree-two rule to the **full** two radial contributions, with no coefficient adjusted, predicts the actual signed polar force with relative errors **0.078–0.331%** at R=6 kpc and **0.027–0.279%** at R=8.3 kpc on the two original hosts. The maximum error for the full complex coefficient is 0.406% across both apertures including the larger-box and richer-basis checks. We reconstructed the discrepancy separately from degrees four and up. These are errors of the simple shape approximation, not a claim that all harmonics equal degree two to the inherited numerical tolerance. ## Is the wave pointing the wrong way?

Keep the measured site, stationary foreground, direct tide and m=0 response fixed. For each history let the **entire existing** m=2 pattern take an arbitrary common azimuthal phase α. At each site, its hypothetical radial or polar contribution is Re(A exp(iα)); the physical archive corresponds to α=0. This is deliberately generous: rotating the driven wave alone is not a solution of the same forced system. Use the complete m=2 field once; do not add it to a subtotal already containing its lowest mode.

We checked the extrema of each signed component *and* minimized the worst distance from the eight bands (expressed in their full widths) over one shared phase. The objective is the maximum of zero and sixteen affine sinusoids. Its minimum occurs at a stationary point of an active sinusoid or an intersection of two active sinusoids; constant and coincident cases are included. A dense independent angular sweep bounds the result within its explicit sampling error. At the actual phase both MTS and static NFW pass only the outer north/south polar bands on each of the twelve preparations/histories. **Not even a separately chosen most favourable phase could put any of the other six components inside.** The best shared phase still leaves the worst MTS component 2.407–2.582 full band widths outside. The static NFW control, with the same direct satellite force and no wave, gives 1.224–1.334 widths; these are interval distances, not standard deviations, likelihoods or tests of a live collisionless wake. ## What the tide actually transfers

Let the two angular-number amplitudes be a₊ and a₋ for exp(±imφ), m=2. With d=a₊+a₋* and p=H⁻¹d′=−i(a₊−a₋*), the excess angular charge divided by ℏ is m(||a₊||²−||a₋||²)=−m Im(d†p), in the saved occupation normalization. The stationary wave has azimuthal number zero. Therefore an unknown second-order field has **no cross term with the ground in the integrated angular momentum**: the ground is annihilated by −i∂φ. This remains true over a complete azimuthal shell. Unlike a pointwise second-order density prediction, the leading nonzero angular momentum from this sector is determined by its first-order perturbation. No corresponding assertion is made about a fully nonlinear final state.

For the H⁻¹-orthonormal normal modes with d=Σvⱼcⱼ, the charge is −m Im Σcⱼ*cⱼ′. In the saved time/velocity units, c″+σ²c=−2f. Thus the external torque per mode is 2m Im(c*f), and its oscillator energy E=(|c′|²+σ²|c|²)/2 obeys E′=−2 Re(c′*f). Here the star denotes complex conjugation. With no initial excitation and B±=∫ f(t) exp[±iσ(t−t₀)]dt,

    charge / ℏ = (m/σ)(|B₊|²−|B₋|²),    E = |B₊|²+|B₋|².

The positive and negative frequency parts of the **complex** tide must be retained; a power-only or azimuthally averaged real forcing loses the difference. We integrated both directly on the recorded quartic time panels with oscillatory Legendre moments, compared both complex travelling-wave amplitudes to the saved final positions and velocities, then independently integrated torque and work using local quadrature on three resolved modes per history. Manufactured opposite circular drives give opposite charges with equal work; a constant-phase drive has zero charge. The all-mode spectral action discrepancy is below 4.6 × 10⁻¹⁵ of ΣmE/σ for all three groups (whole, lowest, remaining); the independent 36 selected-mode integrals agree with the angular and energy budgets within 6.4 × 10⁻¹⁵ of their respective scales. These compare algorithms on the represented source, not uncertain satellite orbits. The net change is **negative in the saved +φ convention for all twelve pairs**: −5.94 × 10⁻⁵ to −3.74 × 10⁻⁵ ℏ per ground particle. No conversion to a particular Galactic spin sense is assumed. Modes above the lowest carry **28.1–29.8%** of this signed total. The second-order charge and the first-order force are different observables; one does not infer a force from a shell’s share of angular momentum.

For a radial aperture, lift d=QVc and p=QV⁻ᵀc′ using the saved orthonormal radial trial map Q and square modal matrix V, with VᵀHᵣ⁻¹V=I. This is the canonical dual in the **retained Galerkin system**, not an inverse on discarded modes. Integrate −m Im(d*p) over angles and radius, including the two element contributions at each shared radial node. A 10-point Gauss rule separately integrates the product of the two degree-seven field interpolants. The enclosed charge is **0.0320–0.0341%** of the total inside 6.185 kpc, and **0.225–0.239%** inside 8.434 kpc. These are net signed fractions at this epoch, not an energy density, a circular speed, a measured galactic spin or proof of a local torque deposition rate. Aperture results (not bundled here).

Across all twelve physical pairs the coarser forcing time samples, changed source angles/radii, and larger radial modal basis preserve the sign. Enlarging the wave box from 30 to 40 ground lengths changes the total angular charge by up to **0.0106%**, and its enclosed value by up to **0.0408%**; these exceed the inherited 0.002% numerical target in some cases. They do not change the physical signs or rescue a band. No infinite-box accuracy or universal exclusion is inferred. Numerical variations (not bundled here).
