# Carry the same contact from a scattering angle to a mass column

**Operational distinction.** A finite-angle photon–scalar scattering experiment has an almost vanishing absolute cross section. For a coherent weak source, however, a photon responds to its stress along an entire path. We can calculate that using the saved wave solutions without assigning the dark material an incident particle flux or fitting another rotation curve. The comparison here is a hypothetical parallel pair of light rays through the Galactic host, **not a measured LMC light curve, lensed pair, or the orbit of the satellite**.

## Transfer, with sign and boundary terms

The ratio of the local stress vertex to the Einstein pole in [the on-shell calculation](SCATTERING.md) is −λ M_R² t/2. For a weak nearly forward ray, t≈−q_perp², so the contact response to a scalar stress distribution is −(λ M_R²/2) times the transverse Laplacian of the *scalar-sourced Einstein delay*, for localized sources and asymptotic endpoints. There is no Coulomb tail from the contact by itself. This is the coherent, forward tree response, not the phase of the elastic electron loop.

Independently, varying −F²/4+(λ/2)T_EM^{μν}∂μφ∂νφ gives a light-travel-time shift τ_λ=+(λ/2)∫dz T_kk of the canonical minimally coupled scalar to first order in λ. For example in a homogeneous rest frame with only a time derivative, the electric and magnetic kinetic coefficients become 1+λφ̇²/2 and 1−λφ̇²/2 respectively, yielding the same positive delay for λ>0. Here k=(1,0,0,1), and T_kk=(k·∂φ)²; the trace part drops without replacing the actual stress by an electric charge. For an ordinary pressureless rest frame T_kk is the rest energy density to leading order; for general sources keep energy, longitudinal momentum and directional pressure. Apply this particular mixed vertex only to the scalar stress it owns; it is not a prescription to add λ times *every* foreground.

For the weak metric let H=−h_kk/2 and τ_E=∫dz H(t₀+z,b,z), in units c=1. Linearized Einstein gravity on a conserved source gives (∇²−∂t²)H=−T_kk/M_R². Integration on the unperturbed null line gives

    ∇_b² τ_E = −Σ_kk/M_R² − [(∂z−∂t)H]_ends,    Σ_kk=∫dz T_kk(t₀+z,b,z).

This follows from ∂z²−∂t²=(∂z+∂t)(∂z−∂t); no time independence is needed for the identity. It presumes parallel rays compared at the same central epoch; moving the endpoints or their clocks requires additional terms. For vanishing endpoint derivatives this exactly reproduces the scattering-ratio sign. If the scalar exchanges momentum with other sectors, use the conserved total source for the full Einstein calculation. Isolating its static Newtonian rest-mass contribution here is a controlled leading decomposition, not a conserved relativistic halo stress in isolation. A finite Galactic observer does not in general give zero endpoint terms.

## The two actual saved preparations, not a spherical halo substitution

For both original prepared grounds and all six saved LMC histories, use only the final, satellite-driven, axisymmetric, reflection-even **density increment**, not the whole halo. Keep its sign at every angle. A frozen host and phase-averaged nonrelativistic scalar are the leading approximations for this first optical test. View along the symmetry axis with impact b=6 and 8.3 kpc and endpoints far outside the prepared source. These are two lines, not the previous off-plane force points. For b inside the mesh, integrate both halves through each radial element. With the saved d_l(s)=s²δρ_l(s), Y_l=√(2l+1)P_l(cosθ), native length L₀ and velocity V₀,

    Σ_m = [V₀²/(4πG L₀)] ∫ dz_native Σ_l Y_l d_l(s)/s² .

The physical factor follows from the **same Poisson normalization** as the force calculation. Do not divide again by the fixed occupation. Independently integrate inside a cylinder using spherical shells: for the polar caps take μ₀=√max(0,1−b²/s²). The shell fraction for l=0 is 1−μ₀; for even l≥2 the angular integral is [P_(l−1)(μ₀)−P_(l+1)(μ₀)]/(2l+1), before multiplying by √(2l+1). This includes parts of shells *outside* b. It gives M_cyl in units V₀² L₀/G. Differentiating that independent cylinder mass recovers 2π b Σ_m at both impact radii, maximum relative discrepancy 7.57×10⁻¹². A density proportional to (1−s²/4)² in a sphere of radius 2 checks both column and cylinder mass against closed forms; the absolute mass normalization is also checked against the archived spherical mass increment.

For this frozen axial projection, in SI units,

    τ_E(b₁)−τ_E(b₂) = (4G/c³)∫_b₁^b₂ M_cyl(<b) db/b,
    τ_λ(b₁)−τ_λ(b₂) = (λ/2)[Σ_m(b₁)−Σ_m(b₂)] c², with natural-unit conversion.

For the second expression convert kg/m² to eV³ by c²/J_per_eV × (ℏc in eV m)² and the resulting inverse eV to seconds with ℏ in eV s. There is no arbitrary absolute logarithmic delay or finite time origin. Across all 12 cases δΣ_m is positive on both sampled rays (inner 0.00424–0.00815, outer 0.00235–0.00443 kg/m²). The ordinary differential gravitational delay **from the induced mass alone** is +2,524 to +4,876 s. Under the inherited coefficient cap the magnitude of the contact increment is at most 6.27×10⁻⁴⁸ |Zψ/K₀| s. Their paired ratios range from 1.260 to 1.289×10⁻⁵¹ per unit kinetic ratio at the cap. No physical value or sign for λ is selected. Einstein gravity with the same redistributed mass gives the same ordinary delay; GR with the same local Wilson coefficient gives the same contact delay too. A static reference with no LMC-driven wave increment has no such *increment*, not zero total light-travel time. The six histories are alternatives, not independent observations. ## What is worth doing with the result

Light can test the **distribution** of the gravitating wave far more effectively than the direct stress–photon contact on these length scales. Agreement of two integrations is not accuracy at the 10⁻⁵¹ level. The frozen columns omit evolution during propagation, momentum/stress of the moving wave, other angular sectors, ordinary foregrounds and finite-source/observer geometry. At a symmetric axial full path, even density and odd axial energy current cancel the odd-in-z first light-crossing correction at the central epoch. This cancellation is not available on an arbitrary one-sided Galactic sightline. Before comparing with a sky timing or lensing observation, put useful finite paths through a saved evolving state, include the observer terms, and select an identifiable differential observable rather than equating a static asymptotic delay with a residual in data.
