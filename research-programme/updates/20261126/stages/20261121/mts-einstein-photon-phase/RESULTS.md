# A phase-safe GR baseline and a Galactic light-path calculation

**The Einstein forward pole is real, but it is not a polarization signal.** In photon scattering from the neutral scalar, both outgoing helicities acquire the same divergent elastic phase. Removing a divergent number from just one helicity gives a convention-dependent result. Comparing the two helicities of the *same photon at the same angle* cancels that phase and leaves a finite contribution from the electron's spin-zero stress. Direct angular integration with two different regulators verifies the cancellation. The retained local stress interaction changes this baseline by a calculable amount per unit λ; GR with the same scalar, charged matter and λ is identical in this channel.

At 90° and CM energy 1 meV the magnitude of the isolated GR electron-trace contribution to the normalized Stokes quadrature is 3.86×10⁻⁸⁶ on both retained very-light-mass examples. This is not a proposed detector sensitivity or the full polarized rate. A simpler hard-angle counting ratio can shift by about 0.035% at the illustration's perturbative cap, but the larger of its two pure Einstein cross sections is only 1.90×10⁻¹³¹ m². This makes a laboratory counting interpretation unattractive even before asking what provides the scalar flux.

**The useful transfer is to the saved mass distribution.** The same scattering ratio predicts that the local vertex samples stress *on* the ray, whereas ordinary gravity samples the projected mass *inside* the impact radius. Taking two parallel frozen light paths at 6 and 8.3 kpc through each saved LMC-perturbed host, the redistributed wave's ordinary differential Shapiro delay is **42.1–81.3 minutes**. The extra local contact accounts for at most **1.29×10⁻⁵¹ |Zψ/K₀|** of that delay under the saved EFT cap. These are alternative conditional calculations, not an observed time difference; no galaxy or photon data were refitted. The result points toward testing the gravitating wave profile and away from pursuing this contact as the missing Galactic signal.
