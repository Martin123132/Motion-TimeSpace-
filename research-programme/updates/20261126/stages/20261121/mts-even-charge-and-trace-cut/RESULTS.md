# Does the motion field change the electric charge of the vacuum?

**No direct change from the charged matter in this parent. A separate gravitational response survives.** The charged determinant on 4919/4943's common physical metric has no dependence on a constant motion field: its contribution to the even photon kinetic coefficient η is exactly zero. Rescaling all masses and the measuring ruler together does not change that answer. Consequently the previous direct ηλ signal vanishes on this branch. If an electron mass *ratio* genuinely depended on the field, the signed three-distance response would instead be −4.48415×10⁻⁶ κλ/M_R² at 1/2/4 cm per dimensionless mass curvature κ. This is a calibrated sensitivity, not an MTS input or a measurement.

**What the known electron does produce.** Its stress couples to the motion stress through the Einstein metric. Combining that known spin-zero vertex with the retained stress contact selects a single angular projection. It changes sign at crossed energy squared v≈4.44152 m² for either saved very light scalar mass m; the location is conditional on that mass and minimal stress, but not on fitting a coupling. The spin-zero vertex is also present in GR with the same matter. The mixed term needs the inherited stress contact; its strength λ is not fixed by the electric charge or Newton’s constant. GR matched in that contact reproduces it too.
