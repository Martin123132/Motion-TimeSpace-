# Predict a single positive aperture for each exposure duration

The fixed-geometry calculation is already known. This is a prospective test of new geometry, not blind to the fixed-geometry result. For each separate exposure, both apertures remain stationary; their sizes and centres may be prepared differently **between** experiments. No time-window weighting or tuning to the numerical response.

Let s=T², u=1/(1+2p²), h=(uX)². The two nuisance responses divided by their common nonzero factor m u^(3/2) exp(−h/u), m=√h, have regular even expansions. At launch the ordinary one equals 2(h−2u); on its zero line the spin-two one equals 8(2u²−1). The Jacobian in (h,u) has determinant 64u at u=1/√2. The real implicit function theorem therefore gives a locally unique positive (p(s),X(s)) that nulls both while W remains nonzero. Use smoothness of free Gaussian sources, the finite retarded domain and the nonzero regulator width; no assertion about arbitrarily long times.

Derive the order-8 and order-12 geometries from the launch equations alone. At each order in s solve with that same launch Jacobian. Freeze these geometries and the W forecasts for durations .12, .24 and .32 before new retarded readings there. Use both centres; do not repair a miss using a value from the test. Compare both approximations and two integration resolutions; require 2e−8 in κAB units for an accepted finite-duration cancellation. Tabulate the actual ordinary and spin-two leakage even on a pass. Distinguish a zero in the model from simulated precision, experimental registration and time control. A design miscalibrated in duration is tested separately.
