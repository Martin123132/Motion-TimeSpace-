# From a scalar shell mean to two locations, the other metric sectors, and a light link

## Same matter problem; no new force

Use the matched-source comparison (not bundled here) with its canonical preparation, ordinary densitized energy and isolated boundary conditions. This is the crossed difference (raw minus represented ordinary mass) × (inherited minus reset wave). The Newtonian potentials from those separate differences are \(U,W\) in (km/s)². Use physical kpc, time in kpc/(km/s) unless explicitly converted to Myr, and \(x^0=ct\). Initial ordinary mean velocities vanish. No point mass at the origin, no added external harmonic solution. Other sectors and fast free gravitational radiation are not varied in this comparison. This is the slow near-zone weak-field branch, not a general radiative spacetime. For context on the scalar–transverse-vector–tensor split see [Bertschinger's Poisson-gauge discussion](https://ned.ipac.caltech.edu/level5/March02/Bertschinger/Bert4_6.html); coefficients below follow from our retained equations, not a fit to that reference.

## Angular constraints

Put \(g=\nabla U\cdot\nabla W\). Write \(P,Q\) for the crossed spatial/time log-potential corrections times \(c^2\), \(Z=P-Q\). The retained Einstein normal and trace-free equations (not bundled here) give
\[
 P=UW-\Delta^{-1}g,\qquad
 T_{ij}=2(U_iW_j+W_iU_j-\tfrac23\delta_{ij}g),\quad
 Z=\tfrac32\Delta^{-2}\partial_i\partial_j T_{ij}.
\]
The scalar part of \(T\) is \(D_{ij}Z=(\partial_i\partial_j-\delta_{ij}\Delta/3)Z\), not \(T_{rr}\) alone. Directly solving \(\Delta P=U\Delta W+W\Delta U+g\) is an independent normal-constraint check. The isolated inverse Laplacian kernel is \(-1/(4\pi d)\); the biharmonic one is \(-d/(8\pi)\), with \(d=|x-x'|\). Integrate by parts twice. In the azimuthal average \(d\) has coefficients
\[
 k_l=a t^l[-1/(2l-1)+t^2/(2l+3)],\quad a=\max(p,r),\quad t=\min(p,r)/a .
\]
For \(L=l(l+1)\) contract \(T_{rr},T_{r\theta},T_{\theta\theta}-T_{\phi\phi}\) against \(P_l,\partial_\theta P_l,P_l^2(\cos\theta)\), respectively (the last is the **associated** Legendre function of order 2). The radial multipliers are \(k''-k'/r+Lk/(2r^2),\,2(k'/r-k/r^2),\,k/(2r^2)\); multiply the resulting angular means by \(-3r^2dr/4\). The host ends at degree 64 and the finite-element wave density at 68; their products extend to 132. No high degrees beyond 64 are assumed to vanish in the resulting clock. Exterior vacuum multipoles continue to infinity.

For the rest of the stress compute \(X_i=\Delta^{-1}\partial_jT_{ij}\), \(V_i=X_i-2\partial_i Z/3\). Then \(T=DZ+\partial_i V_j+\partial_j V_i+T^{TT}\); in the continuous isolated problem \(\nabla\cdot V=0\) and \(T^{TT}\) is traceless and transverse. This is a spatial decomposition, not a detection of gravitational radiation. Writing spherical mode coefficients as \(a P_l,b\partial_\theta P_l,d P_l^2\) in the three components above, the radial/polar divergence coefficients are \(a'+(3a-Lb)/r,\ b'+[3b-a/2-(L-2)d/2]/r\). For a vector \(xP_le_r+y\partial_\theta P_le_\theta\), \((x+(l+1)y)/(2l+1)\) and \((ly-x)/(2l+1)\) invert with radial Laplacians of degrees \(l-1,l+1\). The radial \(l=0\) uses degree 1. Integrate source derivatives by parts in each radial interval. The compact manufactured controls use independently prescribed scalar, solenoidal and TT fields. Local angular RMS ratios are **not** globally orthogonal fractions of power.

## An independent Einstein check from both momentum equations

With the metric convention \(g_{0i}=B_i/c^3\), the transverse momentum constraint is \(\Delta B=16\pi G j^T\). At the matched launch let \(A\) be the crossed second time derivative of the *total* Newtonian potential. Ordinary acceleration and wave continuity/momentum give
\[
 F_i=(\Delta U)W_i+(\Delta W)U_i=-4\pi G\,\dot j_{\times i},\quad
 X_i^m=\Delta^{-1}F_i,\quad A=\nabla\cdot X^m .
\]
The first term in \(F\) is the ordinary response to the wave and the second the wave response to the ordinary host. Common initial pressure and wave kinetic stress cancel in the crossed launch derivative. With \(Y=\Delta^{-1}A\), the shift derivative is \(\dot B=-4(X^m-\nabla Y)\). This calculation uses the densities and forces, not the gravitational stress projection. Independently, \(\partial_jT_{ij}=2F_i+2\partial_i g/3\). Consequently,
\[
 \boxed{\Delta Z=g+3 A},\qquad Z=\Delta^{-1}g+3Y,\qquad \boxed{\dot B=-2V}.
\]
The first relation is also the trace spatial Einstein equation: its crossed geometric trace is \(-2\Delta Z+2g+6A\) and the matched mixed spatial material stress vanishes initially. Thus the existence of a scalar solution alone is not being used as evidence of consistency with moving matter. The angular tensor source and both independent material currents must give the same \(Z,\dot B\) with the same isolated boundary conditions. A TT remainder supplies the source of the transverse spatial metric; it is not silently reassigned to the clocks. No fast or independently prepared TT wave is included.

## Physical comparison of positions

The two launch points are at cylindrical \(s=6.0,8.3\) kpc, \(z=+1.5\) kpc. The coordinate comparison uses \([Q]/c^4\), outer minus inner. To match physically, specify axial orbit circumference and shortest proper distance on the slice to the equatorial reflection plane. At Newtonian order the spatial line element is \(e^{-\Phi_N/c^2}ds_{\rm Euclidean}\). The undeflected vertical line extremizes the height at zeroth order, with its foot free on the plane; its bending does not enter the first variation. At fixed labels \(\delta s=s\Phi_N/c^2,\ \delta z=c^{-2}\int_0^z\Phi_N(s,u)du\). The extra crossed log rate at a point is
\[
 c^{-4}\left[s(UW_s+WU_s)+W_z\int_0^z U\,du+U_z\int_0^z W\,du\right].
\]
This uses two independent physical labels; it is neither a sphere average nor a redefinition of the source. Spatial tensor corrections to position at second order multiply a first-order clock gradient only at the next order. A nonlinear root replay uses vertical path lengths to test their **first variation**, not a claim that a finite-amplitude geodesic is vertical.

## From clocks on a slice to clocks exchanging light

Define the measured log rate as \(\log(d\tau_{\rm receive}/d\tau_{\rm emit})=-\log(\nu_{\rm receive}/\nu_{\rm emit})\) for neighboring pulses. Launch near the matching slice. Write \(D\) for the Euclidean flight length, \(u\) for distance along the directed unperturbed ray, \(n\) for its direction. The null coordinate travel-time element through the needed time-dependent orders is \(dt/du=c^{-1}[1-2\Phi_N/c^2+B_n/c^3+\cdots]\). Differentiate it between pulses, include endpoint clock rates and the possible endpoint motion. In the crossed difference both launch potential and first time derivative vanish at Newtonian order. At order \(c^{-4}\) the flight adds to the launch log rate
\[
 c^{-4}\left[\tfrac12D^2 A_{\rm rec}-2\int_0^D u\,A(x(u))\,du+\int_0^D n\cdot\dot B(x(u))\,du\right].
\]
For rods maintained throughout, add \(c^{-4}(\pm D s_{\rm rec} A_{\rm rec})\) on this horizontal ray from the receiver's induced velocity; the crossed initial rod velocity vanishes. Use the physically matched launch rate in that case. For worldlines resting at fixed coordinates use the coordinate rate and no rod Doppler term. These are *distinct observers*. Common first-order path bending or travel-time changes multiplying a time derivative first contribute at higher order under the slow near-zone counting. Static 1PN terms along the ray alter its flight time but do not themselves change a stationary clock **rate**. The finite-duration Newtonian check does not reconstruct all higher-order relativistic terms.

For an instantaneous coherent reflection at the outer observer the inbound leg starts one flight time later; it is not a second independently launched pulse. The static endpoint rate cancels for the complete link, as does the leading line integral of \(\dot B\) on the retraced path. The round-trip rate correction times \(c^4\) is \(2D^2 A_{\rm in}-4D\int_0^D A\,du\), plus \(2D(s_{\rm out}A_{\rm out}-s_{\rm in}A_{\rm in})\) for maintained rods. The supplied fixed/maintained worldlines are supported clocks, not freely falling stars. Finite ray and response tests, convention checks and limits are reported in [the results](RESULTS.md).
