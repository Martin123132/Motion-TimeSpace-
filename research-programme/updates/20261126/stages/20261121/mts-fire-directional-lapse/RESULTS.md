# What the two off-plane clocks and their light link actually give

**Private · FIRE 590 · 21 November 2026.** Continued the [previous matched-host experiment](../mts-fire-matched-source-evolution/RESULTS.md). Every number below is the difference between the two host choices, differenced again between the two saved MTS wave preparations. It is not the total clock rate or a measurement. No new coupling, population, mass or clock parameter was fitted.

## What changed in the physics

There are two independent routes from the saved matter to the directional metric. One solves the trace-free Einstein equation by separating scalar, transverse-vector and tensor terms. The other uses the accelerations of **both** ordinary matter and the wave. They give the same angular scalar slip on both clock spheres (relative discrepancy below \(3.0\times10^{-14}\)). The analytic-gradient calculations of the **time derivative of the transverse metric shift** agree within \(6.0\times10^{-13}\) relative to its angular RMS. A finite-difference derivative instead gives \(2.35\times10^{-6}\) or better. These check the [derived trace and momentum identities](DERIVATION.md); they are not the precision of a real galaxy prediction.

The non-scalar part of the crossed directional source is **6.25%** of its angular RMS at the inner radius, **6.40%** at the outer one. The vector ratios are 5.98%, 6.03%; the transverse-traceless tensor ratios are 2.26%, 2.63%. They do not add directly: the pieces need not be orthogonal on a single sphere. A scalar-only metric does not exhaust the directions of the source, even though it recovers the shell mean. A tensor source on this slice is not evidence for an emitted gravitational wave.

## Clocks: location, then light travel

At the original cylindrical radii 6.0 and 8.3 kpc and height +1.5 kpc, the crossed **outer-minus-inner log lapse** is \(+2.67425\times10^{-16}\) at fixed coordinates. Its monopole alone reproduces the preceding \(+2.59979\times10^{-16}\). Matching the locations instead by axial circumference and proper height above the symmetry plane gives **\(-6.71495\times10^{-17}\)**. Most of the shift is from matching circumference; height contributes another \(-6.46\times10^{-17}\). The earlier matched-area value was a *shell average*, not a point measurement.

Light takes about **7,502 years** to cover this 2.3 kpc separation. Comparing neighboring pulses instead of simultaneous log lapses changes the answer. Sign convention: positive means the receiving proper-time interval between arrivals exceeds the emitting interval in the crossed comparison. Predictions below are for observers supported to maintain the same physical circumference and height; reverse and outward one-way flights start separately at the same launch epoch.

| Link | Derived launch prediction | With finite matter/wave evolution |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inner emits, outer receives | −2.41221e−17 | −2.44161e−17 |
| Outer emits, inner receives | −9.87276e−17 | −9.85460e−17 |
| Inner emits, outer reflects immediately, inner receives | **−2.03762e−16** | **−2.03997e−16** |

The one-way prediction includes the evolving potential along the ray, the receiver's later potential, the changing transverse shift and the Doppler effect of maintaining the physical locations. For the closed path the static launch clock difference and the leading shift contribution **both cancel**. Counting the same-time reverse experiment as the return leg would be wrong. Holding *coordinates* instead of physical rods gives a much smaller predicted echo of −3.11251e−17; the identified worldlines matter.

## How much to trust the numbers

- The launch calculation used the full saved wave-density bandwidth through degree 68, ordinary through 64, and their products through 132. Two radial quadratures, two within-bin mass laws, two normal-constraint formulae, and the old monopole give independent checks. Changing the within-bin model changes the outward maintained-rod prediction by about 0.008%, not enough to select the sign.
- The analytic decomposition recovered compact scalar, transverse-vector and transverse-traceless manufactured examples, both separately and together, including points outside their support. A second test followed actual null pulses in a manufactured spacetime with a changing shift, two worldlines, and a reflector. This checks the signs without reusing the flight-rate formula.
- The sum over **all** normal-constraint mode errors missed its numerical target with Gaussian angular projection (1.96e−11 versus 1e−11). We kept those results. Replacing the angular product integrals by exact factorial weights before rounding resolves the discrepancy, without changing either source or the threshold. The correction to the reconstructed spatial log lapse at either actual observer is below 1e−27. See independent normal products (not bundled here).
- A cross-check initially failed when it compared the full directional wave-acceleration kernel to the earlier degree-64 clock kernel. Explicitly isolating degrees 66–132 accounts for the entire 1.95-parts-per-million difference. With identical bandwidth the two acceleration calculations agree within four parts in 10¹⁵. The full and capped values are both retained in the bandwidth test (not bundled here).
- After the predictions were saved, the four original Hamiltonian matter/wave combinations ran for **two light flights**, first with 8 and then 16 steps per flight. The replay used the actual Newtonian time and direction dependence of the potential; the one-way links retain the derived launch shift, not a separately evolved 1PN vector metric. The largest wave-norm change was below \(2\times10^{-14}\).
- At 16 steps the finite maintained-rod outward link differs from launch theory by **1.22%**, inward by **0.18%**, and the echo by **0.115%**. The independently resampled spline calculation changes the echo comparison to 0.118%. Holding the evolution fixed while changing ray sampling moves the echo more than decimating stored times. Neither comparison is an error bar for a galaxy. See coefficient tables (not bundled here), finite histories (not bundled here), and sampling checks (not bundled here).
