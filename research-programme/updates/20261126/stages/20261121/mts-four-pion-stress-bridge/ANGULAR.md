# What the angular distributions test

The two-pion spectral matching measures \(H=h_c+h_n/2\), not either physical cross section by itself. A comparison needs the angular waves that interfere with this D wave. As a fixed diagnostic, use the one-loop equal-mass chiral spectator amplitudes of [Gasser–Ivanov–Sainio, charged](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/0602234) and [neutral](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/0506265), and carry their limitations into the comparison. Their higher-order terms are **not** silently included. \(F_\pi=92.4\) MeV, \(\bar l_6-\bar l_5=3.0\), \(m=139.5704\) MeV as in the retained threshold calculation. This is not a refit of the spectator amplitudes or a physical-mass isospin-breaking analysis.

In units of \(2e^2\), let \(G=-[1+(m^2/s)(-2\operatorname{atanh}\beta+i\pi)^2]/(16\pi^2)\). The charged equal-helicity amplitude is the exact angular Born pole plus \(s[G+3/(48\pi^2)]/(2F_\pi^2)\); the opposite-helicity amplitude is the Born \(\beta^2(1-z^2)/(1-\beta^2z^2)\). The neutral equal-helicity amplitude is \((s-m^2)G/F_\pi^2\); the neutral opposite-helicity one vanishes at this order. Squaring the retained amplitudes defines the rate comparator; this is not a complete higher-order chiral cross section. The ratio and sign between neutral and charged correspond to Cartesian pion flavor and a positive Born charged amplitude. The corrections are independent of \(z\), so the isoscalar helicity-zero D wave of the control is exactly the charged Born projection \(B\).

For the alternative, hold all other waves fixed and add \((10/3)(H_{\rm ref}-B)P_2(z)\) to **both** equal-helicity amplitudes. A pure isoscalar has equal physical neutral and charged amplitudes, and \(\int_0^1P_2^2=1/5\), giving the unique change with unchanged isotensor wave and the requested \(H\). Direct projection at four energies checks the combination to \(6\times10^{-16}\). Thus failure of this conditional cross-section forecast is not a measurement of the missing gravity response or proof that the D wave has the wrong sign.

Use the folded differential cross section in nb per unit \(|z|\): \(2\pi\alpha^2\beta/(s)\) times the helicity intensity for charged pions, half for identical neutral pions, with \(1\ {\rm GeV}^{-2}=389379\ {\rm nb}\) approximately. Integrate over actual energy and angle bins with uniform bin averaging, not at one plotted point. No detector response beyond published corrected rates or unknown within-bin luminosity weighting was fitted. The largest change going from 9 to 16 quadrature nodes per variable is below 0.0002 nb per unit \(|z|\).

- [Mark II Table 2](https://doi.org/10.17182/hepdata.22881.v1/t2) gives nb **per angular bin**, not per unit cosine. Convert using width 0.1; summing bins also recovers the scale of the separate [Table 1](https://doi.org/10.17182/hepdata.22881.v1/t1) central rates to within 5% in both windows (different energy binning). Two complete 0.10-GeV windows, 0.55–0.65 and 0.65–0.75, six angles each. Exclude 0.75–0.85 rather than slice its counts at 0.8. Angular rows have statistical errors; do not invent the missing covariance.
- [Belle v1, neutral](https://doi.org/10.17182/hepdata.50151.v1) Tables 1–4. Ten 0.02-GeV bins from 0.60–0.80. Actual aperture is 0.6 through 0.72 GeV, then 0.8. Input entries already give nb per unit cosine. Statistical and systematic errors remain separate. Neutral and charged data do not share identical energy/angle intervals; they are not subtracted row by row. These are **exposed data** in the history of the external amplitude analyses, not independent holdout validation of MTS.
- Integrated rates use the sum of reported differential values times the angular widths; shape-only numbers are half the \(L^1\) distance between normalized positive angular histograms. Neither is a fit, p-value or likelihood. One-loop spectator waves are especially limited in this energy range; agreement or failure of a total rate does not identify a single partial wave.
