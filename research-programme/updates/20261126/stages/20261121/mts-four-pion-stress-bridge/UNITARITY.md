# What controls the sign above the four-pion threshold?

Use the strong isospin limit, real analyticity, time reversal, and first order in each external probe. Work in the **same** \(I=0,\ J^{PC}=2^{++}\) channel with real-production-source conventions. Normalize the multiparticle amplitudes to absorb their phase-space weights (including identical particles); the four-pion indices then also integrate over internal configurations. Write \(f\) for the equal-helicity photon source, \(a\) for stress, and subscript 1 for two pions. On the upper cut the production unitarity equations are \(f=S f^*,\ a=S a^*\), with unitary symmetric strong \(S\). Below 0.8 GeV in the equal-mass strong theory the four-pion continuum is the new channel with these quantum numbers; three pions have the wrong G parity. Do not apply the equal-mass threshold as an exact experimental threshold for every charge channel.

1. Write \(S_{11}=\eta e^{2i\delta}\), \(v_1=|v_1|e^{i(\delta+\theta_v)}\), and \(r_v=\|v_\perp\|/|v_1|\). Cauchy–Schwarz on the *measured channel's row* gives
\[
 (1-\eta)^2+4\eta\sin^2\theta_v\le(1-\eta^2)r_v^2.
\]
Thus even a precise \(\eta\) near unity need not fix a production phase unless there is information about the other source components. Conversely \(1-\eta\le 2r_v^2/(1+r_v^2)\) follows from any genuine bound on the production ratio, including for a source other than photons. A [global dispersive scattering fit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15327v2) finds an effective onset of isoscalar D-wave inelasticity around 0.9 GeV. Setting \(\eta=1\) below the onset in a parametrization is not an experimental upper bound on four-pion production.

2. There is a stronger relation for the *relative* phase that does not need \(\eta\). Since \(f^\dagger a=(Sf^*)^\dagger Sa^*=(f^\dagger a)^*\), the complete mixed spectral product is real. Put \(\Delta=\arg(f_1 a_1^*)\), and \(r=r_f r_a\). The normalized omitted-channel contribution must have imaginary part \(-\sin\Delta\). Its real part \(d\) consequently obeys
\[
 d^2+\sin^2\Delta\le r^2.
\]
On a connected interval starting from the positive elastic branch, if the two-pion amplitudes never vanish and \(r<1\) throughout, \(\cos\Delta\ge\sqrt{1-r^2}\). The real inclusive cut divided by \(|f_1 a_1|\) must then lie between \(1-r\) and \(1+r\). These are pointwise statements, not an upper bound on how much the two-pion **magnitudes** change. Using an integrated cross section at one energy cannot certify continuity and the inequality at every energy. A fixed subtraction in the final response is separate from this absorptive question.

3. Put the stress calculation from [STRESS.md](STRESS.md) into this formula **conditionally**. At 0.8 GeV the norm ratio is 0.004176 for both sources of stress evaluated at tree level (four-pion versus two-pion), or 0.002476 for tree four-pion stress divided by the independently reconstructed two-pion stress. For the latter, a relative-phase crossing through 90° at that energy would require \(r_\gamma\ge403.9\). This is not a measured exclusion. An upper bound on inclusive all-charge four-pion photoproduction suffices for the numerator: the positive \(I=0,J=2,\lambda=0\) intensity cannot exceed that inclusive rate with the same photon flux and helicity averaging. The frozen two-pion reference is \(\sigma_{\gamma,1}=2\pi\alpha^2\beta(10/3)|H|^2/s\) in natural units. At 0.8 GeV this is 0.865 nb. A pointwise upper limit below 1,411 nb on total four-pion production would suffice for \(r<0.1\) **at that energy**, conditional on both denominator and stress inputs. Input targets (not bundled here) gives the corresponding values elsewhere; these are requirements, not reported experimental limits.

4. Coverage check: [ARGUS Table 1](https://doi.org/10.17182/hepdata.29853.v1/t1) starts at a tabulated energy of 0.85 GeV and covers only \(\pi^+\pi^-\pi^0\pi^0\). [TPC/Two-Gamma Table 4](https://doi.org/10.17182/hepdata.3824.v1/t4) describes a four-charged channel, but its retrieved table has no numerical rows; neither that omission nor a single charge measurement bounds the inclusive total. The angular tables used here are **two-pion** production. Single-virtual-current \(e^+e^-\to4\pi\) does not measure the real two-photon tensor source. No whole-window empirical inelastic budget was established by these inputs.

5. Numerical controls: 240 symmetric-unitary systems with dimensions 2, 3 and 5 verify the inequalities and cancellation. Equality in the first Cauchy step holds for exactly one other scalar channel. A two-channel fixture with \(\eta=0.999\) and large orthogonal source components gives \(\cos\Delta=-0.6\): merely invoking near-elastic strong scattering would even get the sign wrong. This is an existence counterexample, not a pion model.
