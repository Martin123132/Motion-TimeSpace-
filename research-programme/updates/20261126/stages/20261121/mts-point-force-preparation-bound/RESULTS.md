# Point-force preparation bound: changing the shape alone is not enough
       
21 November 2026, private continuation. On the two original Milky Way wave states and all six archived LMC histories, the strongest of the four site requirements is at R=6 kpc, z=−1.5 kpc. Even allowing arbitrary angular harmonics and a finite density change, the specific kinetic norm of the change from the reference wave must be at least **268.76–299.78 (km/s)²** to enter the original signed force bands (tabulated endpoint values, rounded). The coherent wave reconstructed from the actual m=0,2,3 histories supplies **4.69–9.11 (km/s)²**. The necessary value is **at least 29.83–63.27 times its conservative upper value** across matched cases. This means an arbitrary rearrangement with only the measured gradient distance available cannot fix the force; it does not bound what a different, physically supplied formation state or foreground can contribute.

Force units are 10⁻¹⁰ m/s². The Newtonian force inequality is continuum and does not use an angular cutoff. Its application to this galaxy is conditional on the stated ground, calibrated mass, common metric, original force intervals and retained ordinary matter. NFW gets exactly the same geometric test; it has no assigned wave kinetic norm.
       
**Derivation.** The sharp constant controlling a directional inverse-square force by the wave's gradient norm lies between 0.782087227798 and 0.782087227800; an exact rational certificate covers the infinite angular tail. This yields a nonperturbative lower bound on the gradient distance to a wave that can reach a given force vector. The reported quantity is *not* the total halo kinetic energy, work done by the LMC, or the fraction of all wave particles which must have moved. A point force alone cannot give such a mass fraction.
