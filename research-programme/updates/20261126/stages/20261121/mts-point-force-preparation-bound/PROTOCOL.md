# Can the point-force bands say anything about an arbitrary wave shape?
       
Work on the retained Newtonian common-metric, positive quadratic wave branch. Keep baseline ordinary matter, direct satellite, calibrated mass, two ground states and all six histories unchanged. No fits, new observational data or galaxy-state selection. The previously sealed three-sector result is a comparison, not a restriction on the new necessary inequality.
       
For each signed northern/southern site take the distance from the stationary-plus-direct radial/polar force vector to its original rectangle. Use the maximum necessary condition across sites (not a sum or a likelihood). Tabulate the identical geometrical miss for matched static NFW, without attributing a wave kinetic cost to it. Derive an all-angular, nonperturbative bound on the gradient norm of the change from the fixed reference wave. Use a two-sided numerical enclosure of the angular constant and of the ground energy; use the conservative endpoints for all lower bounds. Check the actual normalized driven endpoints reconstructed from the saved m=0,2,3 states, including momentum and relative phases, and compare to the bound on each original host/history. Treat a pass of a necessary condition as inconclusive. A gradient distance is not Hamiltonian energy injected by the LMC.
       
The comparison is to mathematical point forces on the prepared models; this is not a claim about deconvolution of finite astronomical survey apertures. For finite apertures give the separate mass-transport scaling without assigning an unreported survey resolution.
