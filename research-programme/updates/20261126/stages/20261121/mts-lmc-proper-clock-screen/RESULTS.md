# For this LMC shell, source range matters more than the gravitational clock correction

21 November 2026 · Private extension of the [real scalar carrier calculation](../mts-lmc-carrier-window/RESULTS.md). Two fixed host preparations, six archived LMC tracks, no new fit, no observed screen distances.

**The proper clock passes; the synthetic shell is not a clean sky measurement.** The corrected 20-year LMC-induced drift remains negative for all twelve cases and every common starting carrier phase under the matched preparation. Its least negative tested value is **−1.167 × 10⁻¹⁷ per year**, retaining the previously computed secular drift and adding the newly transported coherent phase. An unmodelled rate of change of the middle screen's affine range equivalent to **0.98–1.25 km/s** gives the same slow signal from the undisturbed ground alone. Correcting for travel time cannot remove a changing range.

**We tried to remove the range dependence, not just flag it.** A fourth screen fixed at 42.5 kpc admits an exact cancellation of a *common* additive range drift on the reference ground while keeping the original near-foreground null and uniform-density normalization. The near-degenerate equations require weights **+70.29, −643.55, +965.43, −392.17**. Applied unchanged to the second host, they leave 30.0% of the original common-range derivative. The target grows about 45.5–45.7×, but independent equal measurement errors grow 208.4×: signal-to-noise falls to **about 22%** of the original. An independent range drift of only 0.32–0.40 km/s on the most sensitive new screen mimics the *new* signal. The fix is useful for genuinely shared motion, not a general improvement. No nonzero set of area weights cancels arbitrary independent source velocities: each individual ground-area derivative is positive. Range or velocity information must enter from elsewhere.

**Numerical checks.** All 12 paired spatial/time refinements and all 12 independent modal-basis comparisons pass the original 20-ppm gate. The flat complex response replays to <0.009 ppm. The affine Hamilton-equation control has the predicted quadratic remainder (ratio 3.998 on halving the metric), and both constant-potential tests pass. The four-screen drift passes its separate quadrature and basis comparisons. No equivalence to a measured pulsar timing residual is assumed. The earlier fixed-force bands and seven full-delay basis misses were not changed. GR with the same scalar stress gives the same phase transport.
