# A clock-based local tide recovers the driven GR prediction

21 November 2026 · private, fixed common-metric formation problem. No parameters fitted or new sky data. This is not the LMC experiment.

**What changed.** Instead of deriving both tidal readings from spatial data, measure the change in radial shear with time. The two independent expressions for the tide follow from the radial geodesic and Gauss equations. The calculations use the original sign of geodesic acceleration, its proper-distance windows, and the same host-minus-zero, wave-minus-cold contrast from formation. The signed hosts have separate initial states and forecasts. The threshold remains 20 parts per million (ppm) of the matched Newtonian increment, not of the much smaller stress signal. Four clocks, two proper apertures per host. On the fine reference at the final clock the excess over Newton is 45.50–49.16 ppm in magnitude, in the forecast direction for both signs. The forecast contribution is 57.98–59.84 ppm. The measured excess is **77.8–84.0%** of the forecast stress contribution; it is not a measurement that all the excess originates in that stress. Keeping the local anisotropic stress in the Weyl/Riemann conversion changes the final signal by only 0.117–0.122 ppm of the Newton scale; this local algebraic term is *not* the accumulated effect of a propagating stress. **Independent time check.** The instantaneous shear-rate observer uses the evolution equations, not the spatial curvature reader. A further observer uses *differences of shear in actual short evolutions*, not the instantaneous RHS as its readout. It takes four steps forward from launch and four backward from the endpoint at each of two spacings, staying within the source bank. On both fine reference hosts both spacings keep all four endpoint readings inside the bands; there are four physical endpoint comparisons, not eight independent observations. Against the archived spatial reading the largest difference on these fine references is 3.68 ppm; the coarse endpoints miss with both spacings. Reducing the spacing does not always reduce the discrepancy; this is not a time-convergence proof. The altered order-20 histories are included separately and are **not** substituted for the reference. All readings (not bundled here).

**The source of the tide is not a free vacuum wave.** The radial field equation splits into an explicitly curved free tensor part, the remaining change in the shape of the two-spheres, extrinsic curvature and the actual matter sources. At full time on the fine positive wave branch, the tensor and shape contributions to the first-cell change in radial metric acceleration are about +1.304 × 10⁻¹⁴ and −1.304 × 10⁻¹⁴ in retained geometric units; the evolved change is −3.499 × 10⁻¹⁸. Across both fine hosts and both matter branches the full reconstruction differs from the evolved *change* by 17.5–27.6 ppm in bulk norm (0.6 < r < 2.1), but 2.39–5.21% at the first cell. These are not aperture scores. Twenty nonzero metric controls, including nonzero energy and anisotropic matter stress, independently test the formula in four dimensions. **What the centre says.** On the retained fine preparations the first-cell change in the geodesic tide differs by 9.0–16.3% between temporal and spatial measurements. Increasing the angular flux order makes this 31.1–39.8%, not an improvement. These percentages use the local signed *change since formation*, not the large static tide or the Newtonian normalization. The independently measured central disagreement survives even though both finite apertures pass.
