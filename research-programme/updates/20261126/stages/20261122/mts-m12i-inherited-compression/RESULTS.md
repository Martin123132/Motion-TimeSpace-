# Earlier formation dynamics reinforce the decrease in the inner wave share

We calculated how much second-order compression **generated between z≈332 and z=99** changes the subsequent z=99→19 transport of the same wave and ordinary-matter realization. The earlier wave correction is **−0.000155 percentage points** for the innermost patch, not enough to reverse the previous −0.03276 pp. In the paired cold cosmology the earlier baryon correction has the opposite sign. No phase, mass, initial-state amplitude or force parameter was chosen to produce either result.

## Most of the early scale sensitivity is a *mixed* interaction
The shared accuracy criteria allow input Fourier magnitudes 0.5501–2.3991/Mpc over the full interval, versus 0.2941–2.3991/Mpc in the wider input. Of 74,321 stored input coefficients, 812 fail the earlier screen; the allowed nonzero output reaches 4.0218/Mpc. On the **same screened readout**, the earlier inner wave contribution from screened/screened pairs is −0.00006723 pp; excluded-long/excluded-long gives −0.00000177 pp; one excluded long input times one screened input gives **−0.00008652 pp**. The latter accounts for about 98% of the contribution lost when the longer inputs are removed on this readout. Generating long/long separately on a small Fourier lattice and checking against the original spatial grid distinguishes this from just asserting the difference must be mixed. The screened result is an independently defined restricted calculation, not an uncertainty band on the full result. ## Controls that affect interpretation
- The two older native snapshots at the ends of the new 65-date interval are recovered to better than 2e−14 relative l2 in the checked columns. The native linear radiation and matter transfer, same seed, and original wave pressure are used as **inputs**. The generated response is Newtonian/nonrelativistic at second order. On the screened input, the matter-only linear solver misses the final native wave density/momentum by 3.66e−5/7.74e−5 relative; supplying the native linear lapse gives 1.57e−6/2.49e−6. Linear agreement does not bound omitted second-order radiation or stress. Validity tests (not bundled here).
- The growing-dust test recovers the quadratic tidal invariant from a separate expression for the force at moved positions. With the actual growing incoming state, the independent solution recovers the final dust compression and velocity at relative errors below 7e−11 on the finest full-interval integration. An arbitrary common second-order gravitational source also cancels out of the *relative* pressureless response (numerical gain below 3e−12 pp per unit rms). It does not exactly cancel for the dispersive wave.
- For the heavy screened inner patch, directly sampling the forces on moving fluid elements initially gave −0.00006683 pp versus the Eulerian −0.00006723 pp, a 0.60% discrepancy on this small contribution, above the 0.5% comparison level. The independent direct and replacement-lapse results are retained, not rescaled. The coordinate audit identifies why these two routes initially differed. The transfer for the volume-corrected density does not generate exactly the velocity of a conserved particle, and taking a time derivative before shape-preserving wavenumber interpolation differs from taking it afterwards. The measured coordinate slip adds −5.0185e−7 pp and the force interpolation correction adds +9.4670e−8 pp to the inner result. The direct native wave-pressure part of the former is only −2.18e−10 pp; the discrepancy is not evidence of a new physical force. Together these terms bring the material result to −0.0000672351 pp versus −0.0000672308 pp, a difference of **0.00645% of the small correction**. Across both starts, both output selections, both cosmologies and four inner cuts, 32/32 finest comparisons meet 0.5%, **18/32** meet the tighter 0.002%, worst 0.0556%. This repairs consistency in the chosen approximation, not relativistic particle conservation. Term-by-term correction (not bundled here) · continuity residual (not bundled here).

## How much could the genuinely unknown part move it?
For the all-mode inner wave forecast, an arbitrary incoming second-order density/scaled-divergence vector at a=0.003 needs joint **box** rms of at least 6.34e−6 in the most effective alignment to cancel the updated drift. A missing common second-order lapse over the early interval needs at least 7.72e−6 rms in the defined density-equivalent space/time norm if acting by itself. These are sensitivity thresholds; neither rms nor alignment was measured. If both, or additional intrinsic stresses, are present, their signed responses add. [Conventions](EQUATIONS.md) · all thresholds (not bundled here).

An actual incoming **growing adiabatic curvature** perturbation is more restrictive than independently specifying every species. A perturbation already of second order in amplitude propagates linearly. Its direct contribution uses the native change between the two relative-density transfers, including linear metric and radiation; it is an alternative to, not an addition on top of, propagating that same incoming state and its native forcing separately. A phase-locked primordial local quadratic term b(R₁²−mean) on the saved input band changes the all-mode result by −0.00000611 pp per unit b on covered output modes. It would require b≈−5386 there to cancel the calculated drift. About 12.1% of the template's **rms norm**, not necessarily 12.1% of the signal, is at nonzero wave numbers below the original native transfer coverage; we do not extrapolate those responses. Couplings to absent primordial wavelengths, scale-dependent or other shapes, isocurvature, and gravitationally generated higher-order structure are not constrained by this test.

For scale only: [Planck 2018 IX, sections 2 and 5](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.05697) reports local f_NL=−0.9±5.1 (68% statistical, CMB). Under the *additional* conventional scale-independent extrapolation |b|=3|f_NL|/5 and an illustrative |f_NL|≤11.1, the covered phase-locked contribution is at most 0.0000407 pp, about 0.12% of the conditional drift. This is **not** a Planck constraint at these wavenumbers, an MTS CMB fit, or a probability for the flow direction. Adiabatic readouts (not bundled here).
