# Palomar 5: release-time ambiguity and the ordinary tide that drives release

**The two frozen flow directions produce different tracks, but the tested observations do not select a direction for either preparation.** The signed histories cover **111.158776 million years**. That is 5.73% of the [1.94 Gyr trailing-debris drift estimate](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0307446), or 2.22% of a [5 Gyr disruption model](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.06647). Neither is a measured birthday for every star. The 425.18 Myr archive common to the two preparations and the 1,065.51 Myr inherited archive are forward formation histories; neither extends the matched forward/reverse debris calculation.

At nominal distance, 22.05 kpc, the frozen forward-minus-reverse velocity-gradient shifts are +0.06511 and −0.07981 km/s/degree (reset and inherited); the quoted uncertainty is 0.15. Even across the three saved distances the maximum split reaches only **0.645 of that error**, and no individual sky bin moves by more than **0.448 of its error**. A tracer can separate by up to **68.08 pc** in three dimensions without producing an identifiable sky pattern. The strongest 20-bin position contrast, 1.263 independent-bin error units, becomes 0.168 if an unconstrained track tilt is removed. The cases are not independent detections; we neither sum the statistics nor compare observed residuals to choose a sign. [Signed track integrations and all 180 refinements](../mts-pal5-joined-flow-586-600/RESULTS.md).

**New stripping-time test.** For each tail, preparation, trial distance, observable (RA, declination, line-of-sight velocity) and each choice of injected sign, take the *middle-release* prediction as the target. Does the other sign bracket it at two neighbouring release dates? Use only the three saved common dates (centre ±10 Myr), no new model or observations. The largest early-to-late timing swing need not point along the sign difference, which is why we explicitly check bracketing. **68 of 72 individual targets are bracketed**, with identical classifications on both saved orbit steps. Assuming continuity, those 68 can be matched *in that coordinate* by the other sign somewhere in the tested release interval. Piecewise-linear equivalent time adjustments are at most 3.84 Myr; the bracket, not that interpolation, is the robust result. All four unbracketed targets are declination at 23.2 kpc: the direct same-date differences are only 0.000034–0.000103 degrees. These are modelled recent debris, not measured individual release dates. Independent coordinate brackets need not use the same time; this is not a joint fit, a confidence interval, or a stripping law. ## Why improving release physics matters

Recompute all four independent ordinary axisymmetric Newton tidal components from the original continuous gravitating source at **two fixed** saved launch locations. Subtract a locally equal-density analytic shell, integrate the remaining kernel and restore the exact shell term; do not set a component from the Poisson trace. Compare to gradients of direct forces on a shared quadrature grid. All 40 gradient checks (including both off-diagonal derivatives), six independent density checks, and 128 integration/order/extent/subtraction comparisons pass 0.5%. A moving-node difference of two otherwise accurate forces can miss almost 48% of the small implied density. Uniform mass *redistributed* within saved cells changes some components by up to 6.5%; this is a source-model sensitivity, not an integration error. See [derivation](DERIVATION.md), component scores (not bundled here), [eigenvectors and eigenvalues](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `eigen-comparison.csv`).

At the denser reset-forward launch the strongest ordinary compression falls in magnitude from 1249.58 to 1038.96 (km/s)²/kpc² (**−16.86%**), although the bulk force changed only 1.51%. The largest stretching eigenvalue rises 1.20% and its axis turns 1.22°. This comparison is made *at the same site*, not between two different galaxies or preparations.

For an initially stationary spherical stellar population within a sphere of radius A, let b_j be the relative acceleration of gravity component j between star and cluster centre, p_r the stellar pressure at the surface, M_star the selected stellar mass, and M_j the external gravitating mass within that sphere. The initial specific-energy curvature due to j is

    <E_j''> = <b_total · b_j> − 4πG p_r(A) M_j(<A) / M_star(<A).

This follows from the equilibrium Jeans equation and the divergence theorem, for a fixed initial selection evolved with its stars. It does not assume that the tide is spatially constant. A manufactured nonuniform field gives opposite signs for the exact finite-volume work and a centre-only trace; direct evolution verifies the former. ## Source-preserving finite stellar response

Independent interpolation of acceleration components passed all 18 fine off-grid checks yet failed three of six aperture mass checks, all at the diffuse site. Instead solve the **scalar cylindrical Poisson equation** locally with the original continuous density inside and the global isolated Newton potential around its boundary. An analytic quartic is recovered to 1.3e−14. Differencing this scalar gives mutually consistent accelerations. This does not retune G or the source.
