# Early gravity carried to 12.8 Mpc: what actually reaches the photon fluid?

**Both saved matter preparations reached twice their previous conformal time. A measurable distinction between the numerical backgrounds survives in the photon energy and current, but it is not directly observable as light from this epoch.** At the fixed later epochs the difference, after allowing the primordial amplitudes chosen from earlier gravity, is **0.398–0.946% of the cold model's forced quadratic photon-vector norm**. These numbers are not fractions of the total microwave background, error bars, or a fit to sky data. No model coefficients changed, and we did not consult the old 87.62-Mpc gravitational potentials.

## What was propagated

All five signed Fourier probes, 782 incoming wavelengths, two independent-curvature choices and both the cold and retained heavy-scalar MTS backgrounds. We continued the 6.4 state, including its previously accumulated mass lag, through 12.8 conformal Mpc. The main cold (not bundled here) and MTS (not bundled here) endpoints have 77 actual neutrino momenta, angular orders 0–44 and separately saved first-order incoming histories through order 52. Old low quadratic restart entries were preserved. Previously unrecorded upper moments were calculated from the earlier driven equations. The restarted total metric agrees at the join to better than one part in ten billion.

The analytically predicted thermal response was frozen before reading the new exact results. Differences below are measured against the *small correction itself*, over the window, across all probes and both curvature choices. The unchanged 0.5% tolerance is numerical, not observational.

## Two failures and what explains them

1. **A false 23% discrepancy in the thermal gravity increment.** The first continuation compared a massless incident wave saved on the fine old grid with finite-momentum waves saved on the coarser old grid. At 39 momenta the apparent discrepancy reached 23.18% cold / 23.32% MTS. Without changing the thermal law or the quadratic restart, we replayed the incoming waves from their original preparation on the same fine grid. An independent zero-mass transfer predicted the difference between the two gravitational corrections to better than **0.010% of the artifact's peak** on both backgrounds. It is not valid bin by bin in the extremely slow tail; the full-bin answer includes those particles. The raw runs are retained alongside the matched ones. [Derivation and comparison](TWO-NUMERICAL-EFFECTS.md) · cold attribution (not bundled here) · MTS attribution (not bundled here).
2. **A real old angular-boundary error.** In the unmodified main restart, the highest output wavenumber missed the 0.5% endpoint native linear check for cold neutrino density (0.567%) and shear (0.510%) and MTS shear (0.513%). Some pointwise fractional errors at crossings are larger; the verification ledger keeps them. An independent flight driven by the *native* potentials agrees with the native endpoint moments within 0.00016%; varying the native angular accuracy setting did not change the readout. From earlier driven histories we derived the term discarded at the old order-18 boundary. Returning just this predicted common angular current reduces the worst errors of these density/shear endpoints to **0.00581% cold / 0.00448% MTS**, with no fit to the failing mode. This is a diagnostic with the converged thermal stress held fixed, not an all-momentum corrected restart. ## Does the difference survive primordial freedom, and what carries it?

At matched scale factor, use separate complex growing-mode amplitudes in cold and MTS, determined solely at the earlier three epochs. Test them at the preselected later markers 8, 10 and 12.8. Across five probes, **64.47–120.30%** of the unadjusted two-potential difference remains; an early cancellation can *increase* a later difference. Even freely rechoosing both amplitudes on the later three potential pairs cannot erase **21.80–55.66%** of the discrepancy. Using the independent gravity trace and/or native growing response changes the early-fixed residual vector by at most **0.026%** of its norm. Normalizing by a small oscillatory cold signal can make large percentages; the absolute complex-vector norms and conventions are in the [matched gravity table](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `matched-window-contrast-77.csv`) and robustness checks (not bundled here).

We then tested the [preregistered photon comparison](ACOUSTIC-TEST.md) rather than equating a difference in potential with a temperature on the sky. For the photon normal energy fraction and dimensionless longitudinal current, fixing the amplitudes from earlier gravity leaves **89.2–114.1%** of the unadjusted photon difference, or the **0.398–0.946% of cold quadratic response** quoted above. Fitting the free amplitudes *in this photon window itself* still leaves **23.65–71.28%** of the photon difference (**0.128–0.289%** of the cold quadratic response). Repeating with the independent trace changes any retained photon residual vector by at most **0.0233%**. These two photon components are sources in the actual material equations; they are neither a blackbody temperature estimator nor independent observational samples. [Photon vectors and freedom tests](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `acoustic-contrast.csv`).

The homogeneous Thomson optical depth from just 12.8 to 25.6 Mpc is **3557.07 cold / 3557.11 MTS**, with subinterval numerical refinement far below the imposed tolerance. Even across that short slab, the probability of no scattering is below **10⁻¹⁵⁴⁴**. This excludes direct unscattered light from the tested epoch as the useful channel on these backgrounds. The physical possibility is retention or transfer of information by the evolving plasma, followed by scattering into an observable at a later epoch; it has not been assumed to survive diffusion or last scattering. This uses the standard [line-of-sight transfer framework](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9603033), with the opacity evaluated on both retained backgrounds (not bundled here).
