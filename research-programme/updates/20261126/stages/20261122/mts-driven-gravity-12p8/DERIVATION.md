# Continuing a driven material metric, not continuing a curve fit

The independent variable is conformal time in Mpc with \(c=1\); it is not a kiloparsec-scale local-Galaxy flight time. We retain the two existing homogeneous backgrounds, phases of both input waves in each convolution, five output modes, common-metric matter action and both choices of independent quadratic primordial curvature. No observational amplitudes, coupling adjustment, compensation, late boundary data or refit of the incoming state enter the evolution. The transfer formalism is standard ([Ma & Bertschinger](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9506072); [Pitrou](https://arxiv.org/abs/0809.3036)). The continuation, contrasts and numerical error estimates here are specific to these retained MTS and cold preparations.

## The interface at 6.4

For \(q\) in neutrino-temperature units, \(b=a^2m^2,\ v=q/\sqrt{q^2+b},\ w=1-q\tanh(q/2)\). In a normal observer's frame the common brightness moments are \(N_l\); the dynamical zeroth variable is \(N_0-\phi\), not \(N_0\). For the massive distribution write
\[
 F_{2,l}(q)=N_l+(w+4)Z_l+\Delta_l(q).
\]
At the join carry *all* saved matter variables and the recorded \(\Delta_l(q)\) for every populated momentum node through \(l=18\). Do not reset the received spectral lag just because a new forcing file begins. The independently evolved states for \(\phi\) and its momentum are in physical units; normalizations of the solver's coordinates are undone and reapplied at the actual same time. Only the physical constraint calculation re-evaluates the metric at the seam.

Longer free travel requires angular information missing from that file. To recover it we replayed the earlier driven equations using the archived gravity history, not an extrapolation in \(l\). With \(\mathcal L_k X_l=k[lX_{l-1}-(l+1)X_{l+1}]/(2l+1)\), let \(U_l=N_l-\delta_{l0}\phi\). Then
\[
 U'=\mathcal L_k U+e_1 k(\phi+\psi)/3+S^0,\qquad
 Z'=\mathcal L_k Z+S_w .
\]
Both run from the original finite regular surface \(0.00625\), with the original low moments. Undetermined higher initial quadratic moments use the same truncation of the early gradient expansion as the old calculation, not inferred prehistory. In the new interval, only the missing upper moments are taken from this reconstruction. At first order the incident hierarchy is carried through \(l=52\); archived values through \(l=26\) take precedence at the join. The quadratic source is projected through \(l=44\). This is a finite angular representation, not a purported exact closure at \(l=44\).

## Velocity derivative versus the full thermal source

The first-order tangent is computed by differentiating the streaming and dipole terms before either new exact-bin test. At second order, in terms of the *physical* \(N\) (including \(\phi\) in its monopole), the one-channel law inherited from the previous window is
\[
 Y'=\mathcal L Y-\tfrac12 b\,\mathcal L(N+2Z)+e_1 kb\psi/6+S^{[1]}(-2).
\]
Its energy, normal flux, shear and stress trace are proportional to
\((Y_0+bZ_0,\;Y_1,\;-Y_2+bZ_2,\;2bZ_0)\). The spectral mean \(-2\) and coefficient \(\pi^2/6\) come from the specified Fermi-Dirac population; they were not learned at 12.8. The prior integrated \(Y\) is used at launch, including whatever error the expansion already had. Applying these sources to the matter equations gives the frozen two-potential predictions. Their comparison control has the **same true material state at 6.4** but omits the new explicit massive stress; its algebraically inferred potential at the seam can therefore differ from the physical potential. It is not a second primordial fit or the zero-mass universe.

For each populated finite \(q\), independently evolve the original first-order brightness *and* its logarithmic momentum derivative, which appears in \(S(q)\). The exact residual satisfies
\[
 \Delta'=v\mathcal L\Delta+(v-1)\mathcal L[N+(w+4)Z]+
 e_1 (1/v-1)k\psi/3+S(q)-S^0-(w+4)S_w .
\]
Use \(v-1=-b/[\epsilon(\epsilon+q)]\). Its four moments are integrated with the previous exact subtracted thermal measures. The flux acquires a factor \(k\) only at the momentum constraint. All affected scalar, material and gravitational variables are then propagated again; the second evaluation feeds the changed gravity back into \(\Delta\). The full-bin result does **not** fix the monopole by adjusting \(Y\) after seeing it.

## An angular check without a reflecting ceiling

At a chosen momentum let \(\chi' =v\) and \(B_l=F_{2,l}-\delta_{l0}\phi\). Its inhomogeneous source is \(S(q)+e_1 k(v\phi+\psi/v)/3\). For fixed \(k\), independent angular flight from a source at \(\eta'\) to the final time has kernel
\[
 K_{lj}(x)=\frac{i^l(-i)^j(2j+1)}2\int_{-1}^1
 P_l(\mu)P_j(\mu)\exp(-ix\mu)\,d\mu,\quad
 x=k[\chi(\eta_f)-\chi(\eta')].
\]
Propagate the *actual* initial \(B\) by \(K\), integrate each supplied source by the retarded \(K\), then restore \(\phi_f\) only to the monopole. Comparing this to hierarchy endpoints tests propagation along independent angular characteristics for selected thermal nodes. It does not independently verify all high-\(l\) source physics. Grid, source join, independent native linear transfer, alternate gravitational equation, and quadrature tests are therefore distinct checks.

## Gravitational balance and a future-window test

The scalar energy and longitudinal-momentum constraints set the physical two potentials. For an independent gravity check, also integrate the spatial Einstein trace from its original two constrained initial numbers; do not reset it to the constraints at intermediate times. When subtracting the common-speed control, include the independently derived divergence of that control's unequal energy/current/shear weights and its omitted direct source (earlier derivation (not bundled here)). This term belongs to the subtraction, not the MTS action. Compare the resulting difference to the *small* constraint-derived gravity increment rather than just to total gravity.

For a distinct field-level prediction, match the scale factor across backgrounds. In each output mode, allow the cold and heavy primordial complex amplitudes to be independent. Their least-squares values are locked on the three old epochs (cold conformal markers 1.6, 3.2, 6.4; smaller shared scale factor used at each marker). Test the resulting potential pair at new markers 8, 10 and 12.8 without re-estimating those amplitudes. Also report the minimum possible residual if amplitudes are instead chosen inside the new window; this is a degeneracy control, not that out-of-window forecast. Neither calculation supplies a sky transfer, instrument noise, an observed detection, or a derivation of a primordial occupation.
