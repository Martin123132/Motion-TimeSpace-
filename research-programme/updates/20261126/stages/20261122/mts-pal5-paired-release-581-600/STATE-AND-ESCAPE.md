# What the source has to predict, as distinct from what gravity predicts

At fixed release time the post-release Hamiltonian tracer flow is invertible. To match a forward star's final cluster-relative position **and** velocity in the other force history, place that final relative 6-vector on the rival cluster's own terminal centre. Invert the rival flow to its proposed release time, and compare the result with its prescribed trial launch. Replay both the native launch and the required alternative to check round-trip accuracy. This is a diagnostic use of simulated endpoints, never a fit to observed Pal 5 velocities. It does not authorize changing these two stars independently in a predictive model.

The injection has one symmetric radial offset L and one symmetric azimuthal speed b. If δx₋,δx₊,δv₋,δv₊ are the required shifts in the inner and outer arms, the two shifts this restricted family can supply are δL = e_R·(δx₊−δx₋)/2 and δb = e_φ·(δv₊−δv₋)/2. The remaining Cartesian vectors are orthogonal to those two permitted changes in their separate position and velocity spaces. Changing the width and speed alone is insufficient when those residuals are nonzero. The [inverse calculation](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `inverse_release.py`) records required offsets, residuals, and their numerical refinements in physical units.

The shrinking two-velocity difference is not a shrinking six-dimensional difference. For the frozen alternative time, [the other-observable calculation](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `other_observables.py`) projects the two trial stars to sky-tangent coordinates, distance and two proper motions relative to their own cluster centre. For two specified means differing by a vector d in one group of observables, an upper limit on the *individual independent common* error for ideal 95% classification using that group alone is |d|/[2 Φ⁻¹(0.95)]. This is a precision requirement, not a quoted Gaia uncertainty. Matched star identity and deterministic launch are optimistic; redrawing a population of stars is a different inference. In particular, the angular width of a stream is not a star's position-measurement error.

If a later population calculation uses a smooth phase-space release density f at a fixed time, the Hamiltonian trajectories preserve canonical volume. After translating by their own cluster endpoints, each conditional density evaluated at a simulated endpoint samples f at the corresponding inverse release state. A known bound on the gradient of log f over the connecting segment would bound how much the two conditional log densities differ; for two independent stars, bounds add. This observation cannot supply a likelihood without that density, its support, the release-rate history, and the selection function. A sharp release surface requires its own induced measure; do not insert a zero-dispersion spray model into a six-dimensional density formula.

**A direct physical control instead of assuming a launch prior.** For a spherical Plummer cluster of mass M and scale a, Φ_c=−GM/sqrt(s²+a²). The zero-binding speed relative to its freely falling centre follows from v²/2+Φ_c=0. The point-mass speed is an upper bound at fixed M and r for a spherical nonnegative mass distribution. The older 60 pc, 1 km/s trial already has *positive* cluster binding-plus-kinetic energy at the nominal mass. The escape-speed release and continued cluster force are switched independently in [the four-way test](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `escape_control.py`). Adding cluster gravity after launch does not give the cluster a self-acceleration. With that force included the relative energy obeys dE/dt = u·[a_gal(X+s)−a_gal(X)]. Without it, apply the same work check to relative kinetic energy; reporting Plummer binding-plus-kinetic energy in that control is a diagnostic only. Recompute the full trajectories, not just a change of initial speed in the plotted final numbers. Conditions and references: [physical control protocol](ESCAPE-CONTROL.md). The fixed mass and mass-follows-light assumption are not a derivation of the historical cluster structure.
