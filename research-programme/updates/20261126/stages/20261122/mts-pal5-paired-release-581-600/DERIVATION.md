# From the retained potential to a measurable clock/flow ambiguity

**The source and its common acceleration.** The conditional weak-field parent used here has the retained Schrödinger kinetic term, the ordinary potential and its own density potential (Poisson with the same G). Reversing the complex wave at 590 reverses its instantaneous current but not its density; conjugating a whole evolving solution at unreflected times would not solve its equation. We instead continue the saved inverse states at 586 on the actual preceding modeled drive. The two fields need not have the same density at 581. The ordinary source is the archived *represented* host, identical across signs; this is not a new time-dependent solution of the source-corrected host. The model galaxy is not an observation of the Milky Way. The fully occupied mass is not inferred from this stream.

A tracer with position X+s relative to a cluster at X experiences relative external acceleration a(X+s,t)−a(X,t), with a=−∇Φ including wave and ordinary gravity. A homogeneous acceleration cancels exactly in the relative equation; differences of tides, the cluster path and escape kinematics remain. Our trajectory code integrates finite forces, not just their linear gradient. We reuse two identical trial offsets and do not infer whether a star was actually unbound or assign a posterior to its escape epoch. A nonnegative stellar source should not be assumed to have an accurately represented Hessian on cluster scales: see [independent source checks](../mts-pal5-source-tides/RESULTS.md).

**How a different escape time changes an observable.** Write the cluster state as c(t)=(X,V), its first-order equations as c′=F(c,t), the instantaneous launch as R(c,t), and the star's later flow map as U(T,t,y). Holding the final observer time T fixed and using the composition law for U gives

    d y(T)/dt_escape = D_y U(T,t,R) · [∂t R + D_c R · F(c,t) − F(R,t)].

This subtracts the free evolution that would otherwise have happened during the change in launch time. Here the relative offsets are a e_R and b e_φ in the galactic cylindrical plane, with signed lengths ±60 pc and speeds ±1 km/s. The bracket is (a·ė_R − b e_φ, a_grav(X) + b·ė_φ − a_grav(X+a e_R)). Here ė_R=(V_plane−e_R(e_R·V))/R_cyl and the oriented azimuthal basis has ė_φ=±e_z×ė_R. The bracket vanishes for the cluster itself. A force applied equally to the two bodies also cancels in its velocity component. Propagate symmetric positive/negative multiples of this derived bracket on the *same* force history, through the actual observer projection. Independently change the actual launch date and directly re-evolve to check the derivative. See [clock tangent code](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `clock_tangent.py`). The code does not differentiate line segments through three saved velocity samples.

**A candidate release clock supplied by orbital dynamics.** In Galactocentric coordinates a pericentre satisfies X·V=0 with d(X·V)/dt=|V|²+X·a>0. Locate the inward-to-outward crossings, interpolating the simulated orbit with both its positions and velocities, rather than inferring a date from observed stream alignment. Contrast releasing both signs at the sign-mean orbit's passage with releasing each at its own predicted passage. Neither proves the cluster released stars precisely then. In the [lag test](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `lag_test.py`), forward stars escape at their predicted passage, the reverse pair share one permitted lag. Choose the lag using the paired radial-velocity difference on one orbit grid, freeze it, and directly re-evolve on other grids. This supplies a constructive counterexample to guaranteed sign recovery when the lag is unknown, not a fitted astrophysical clock. The same shift must serve both tail stars; no per-star timing adjustment.

**What the numbers measure.** For two line-of-sight velocities with independent Gaussian errors σ, two specified equally likely means separated by Euclidean distance Δ in km/s have optimal correct probability Φ(Δ/(2σ)). One allowed pair of opposite models with that separation bounds the *worst-state* accuracy of any classifier from above, without an age prior. A ceiling of 50% from the crossing of two straight lines in observation space need not survive true orbital curvature; use directly evolved witnesses. The tests assume known foreground, observer, common distance and preparation, zero internal velocity scatter and equal sign odds. The adopted errors are from [Kuzma et al.'s 109-star catalogue](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.04548), not measurements of our simulated pair. These are forecasts, not probabilities that either sign fits observed data. The symmetric and antisymmetric velocity contrasts are decompositions of the same data, not extra observations.
