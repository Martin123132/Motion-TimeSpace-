# Palomar 5: 223 Myr of paired gravity and a test at an earlier pericentre

**The two directions produce different streams. On this longer interval, the velocities of two newly stripped stars still do not distinguish them at existing precision.** The new effect is not just an extrapolation of the old one: the sign changes the predicted time of closest approach, which in turn changes the signal if stars escape during that passage. We also replaced the arbitrary launch speed in a separate comparison with a speed calculated from the cluster potential. No observed velocity residual, response coefficient, or per-galaxy occupation was fitted.

## What was actually evolved

We added 111.681740 Myr at the front of the old 111.158776 Myr, starting from all four saved inverse states at 586 on the same ordinary-matter drive. A third refinement goes back to the signed 590 launch and rebuilds **all three** legs (581–586, 586–590, 590–600). Fields and their wave time derivatives meet at the joins; normalized wave return and occupation errors are below 1e−9. Both the native forward formations and the conditional reverse alternatives are represented; only forward is the near-replay of its archived formation. At 581 the phase-free reverse-state discrepancies from the original archived wave are about 0.71% reset and 0.44% inherited at the ×4 refinement, versus roughly 0.00055% for forward. This is preparation information in the simulated host, not a sign determination for the Milky Way.

The **222.840516 Myr** of matched fields span 11.49% of the literature's 1.94 Gyr trailing-tip drift estimate, or 4.46% of a commonly assumed 5 Gyr disruption model. The actual trial stars did **not** all fly for 223 Myr: the three midpoint-control releases fly for 157–177 Myr, the pericentre releases for about 126 Myr. [References, newer age estimates and incompatible mass conventions](EXTERNAL-CLOCKS.md).

## A physical date changes the comparison

At the nominal 22.05 kpc, each orbit has one earlier pericentre within the new interval. Forward and reverse reach it 0.2634 Myr apart (reset) or 0.03667 Myr apart (inherited); the corresponding radii are about 5.28–5.30 kpc. We allow one common stripping date for the two tail stars, not a separately adjusted date for each velocity component.

| Nominal distance; velocity-pair separation (km/s) | Reset | Inherited |
|---|---:|---:|
| Original 60 pc / 1 km/s trial, both released at the mean orbit's pericentre | 0.02958 | 0.01135 |
| Same trial, each sign released at its own pericentre | 0.01636 | 0.01319 |
| Zero cluster binding energy at launch and gravity of the moving cluster retained, each at own pericentre | 0.01608 | 0.01198 |
| Ideal correct-direction rate for that last row, with preparation, date and foreground known | **51.46%** | **51.09%** |

The last line assigns both stars independent 0.22 km/s errors, the *best* error in the [109-star catalogue](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.04548), equal sign odds, no intrinsic dispersion. At its median 1.39 km/s errors the result is closer still to a coin toss. These are comparisons of model means, not likelihoods for the 109 observed stars. The two states give different tidal forces even when those forces are observationally hard to distinguish. Relative to the control in which only the old interval carries a sign difference, the earlier signed field changes the two-velocity contrast vector for the central 167-Myr trials by 0.000292 km/s reset and 0.00234 km/s inherited. These contributions need not add with the same sign as the late effect.

## What happens to the escape clock

The derivative of the predicted velocities with respect to release time was derived from the equations rather than from a line joining three sampled dates. The transported tangent agrees with an independent shifted-time orbit to within 0.141% at the refined midpoint. The linear interpolation can even predict a crossing of the two velocity curves that direct evolution at those proposed dates does **not** reproduce. Consequently the report uses directly evolved alternatives, not a claim of exactly identical velocities.

At pericentre, changes to the alternative lag of only about +0.33 Myr (reset) or −0.26 Myr (inherited) nearly erase the original two-velocity difference, with forward at its own passage. In the unbound fixed-offset trial the remaining velocity vector is so tiny that its components do not pass 0.5% relative convergence. It is an ambiguity example, not a precision measurement of that small remainder. Even without knowing the lag, the known-passage experiment already bounds guaranteed recovery for these stars. See the [test of a less extreme, fixed lag](LOOSE-WITNESS.md), [derived date sensitivity](DERIVATION.md), [three-grid directly integrated lag comparison](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `lag-test-8-2.csv`), and effect of changing the launch physics (not bundled here).

**Resolved ambiguity bound.** Take instead half those time shifts, without re-optimizing either: +0.164 Myr or −0.130 Myr. With escape speed derived and the moving cluster's gravity included, the two-velocity separations are **0.01017 / 0.00722 km/s**. All four orbit and field refinement checks pass (worst 0.205%). In this model no classifier of these two velocities guarantees more than **50.93% correct for reset or 50.66% for inherited** across allowed lags, even at the best catalogue error. These are upper bounds rounded upward, not success probabilities averaged over an assumed population. Directly evolved pairs (not bundled here) · [refinement results](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `loose-witness-checks.csv`).

## What would have to be independently constrained

- **Star-release physics:** 1 km/s at 60 pc is faster than zero-binding escape from the adopted 4,297-solar-mass cluster. At the retained distance and half-number angle, the assumed Plummer scale is 20.59 pc; its zero-binding speed there is **0.7633 km/s**. Turning the launch-speed change and the subsequent cluster force on independently gives a four-way physical control. All tested zero-binding launches with cluster gravity ultimately have positive cluster-frame energy, sourced by external tidal work; the maximum energy/work closure discrepancy on the finest field is below 0.00005% of the stated energy scale. The raw negative-energy dwell column can count a fraction of the first integration step from floating-point noise at analytically zero launch energy. After accounting for that half-step there are no sampled negative-energy episodes in those controls; this is not an asymptotic escape proof.
- **Full state, not just two velocities:** Using the trial model without post-release cluster gravity, inverse propagation gives explicit alternative starting states which match all six final coordinates exactly within the saved solver. At each sign's own passage, the required RMS changes are roughly **0.6 pc and 0.014 km/s** (reset) or **0.6 pc and 0.005 km/s** (inherited). Some fine Cartesian components fail the relative gate: these are scales, not precise measurements. A single symmetric change of launch radius and tangential speed cannot absorb the entire state difference. This does not establish that any populated stellar distribution supplies the required alternative states. Inverse budget (not bundled here) · [physical assumptions and phase-volume argument](STATE-AND-ESCAPE.md).
- **Other observables:** A velocity-matching alternative need not match the two proper motions, sky positions and distances. Precision requirements (not bundled here) are reported separately, conditional on the same deterministic pair. Instrumental sky-position precision is not the uncertainty in where a star escaped. Neither the existing 17 RR Lyrae nor the track bins have been added to these two velocities as independent measurements.
