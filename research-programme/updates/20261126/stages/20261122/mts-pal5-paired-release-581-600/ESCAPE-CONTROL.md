# Physical escape-energy control (supplement to the frozen trial stars)

The original offsets are unfitted, but not a prescription for a release population. [Chen et al., §II–III](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.01496v2) use a speed close to point-mass escape at the modeled release radius together with an N-body-calibrated *joint* position/velocity-direction distribution. Their calibrated distribution is not an MTS result. At 60 pc and 1 km/s, zero binding to a point mass requires about 6,975 solar masses. The [Ibata et al. profile estimate](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.06360) used in our earlier controls is about 4,297 solar masses under its stated population model. At this mass the old probe already exceeds the point-mass escape speed; a spherical extended mass makes the escape speed lower, not higher. Do not use a singular five-variable ejection prescription as a smooth six-dimensional likelihood for the old trajectories. [Gieles et al.'s public numerical snapshot](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.4739181) is a comparison simulation, not a measured series of escape events.

Before calculating: at nominal 22.05 kpc, take the same conditional 4,297 solar masses and projected half-number angle 3.21 arcmin [as previously sourced](../mts-pal5-local-tides/RESULTS.md); assume fixed Plummer mass follows light, a = D tan(angle), including in the earlier epoch. Factorial control: retain 60 pc offsets and prescribed opposite tangential orientations in all arms; independently switch (1) 1 km/s versus the derived Plummer escape speed sqrt[2GM/sqrt(r²+a²)] and (2) no cluster force versus continuous gravity of that moving Plummer cluster after launch. Represent the cluster only as a small tracer-level potential in the model host; no backreaction on the whole galaxy. Preserve the observed orientation convention. Repeat both signs, own pericentre and the already frozen alternative lag, no new tuning. Check the original trial is recovered and that the relative star-energy change agrees with integrated external tidal work. Positive energy at a single instant is not permanent escape. Report if a nominal escaper re-enters the bound regime rather than silently filtering that star. This is an external-assumption control, not a claim to have predicted the historical cluster mass, angular escape distribution or escape rate.
