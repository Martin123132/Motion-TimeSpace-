# Finite-size short-time prediction, before evaluating early work
The fixed shell has opposite velocities at every position, with the same circular speed. Its mean initial tidal power from each field is exactly zero, even for a finite cluster. Differentiating mean power once gives an unfitted, nonzero coefficient. For component j (ordinary host or added matter), denote b_j=a_j(X+ξ)−a_j(X), b=sum_j b_j, T_j=∇a_j(X+ξ), a_c the cluster's acceleration at ξ, and u its stellar velocity relative to the centre. At the launch instant, over the paired velocities,

    <E_j''> = <(a_c+b)·b_j + uᵀ T_j u>.

The explicit time derivative of b_j and terms proportional to centre velocity cancel because they multiply u and are identical at ±u. When the external field is affine over the shell, b_j=T_j ξ. The isotropic circular shell has <ξ ξᵀ>=R² I/3, <uuᵀ>=v_c² I/3 and a_c=−v_c² ξ/R². The internal terms cancel, giving <E_j''>=R² Tr(Tᵀ T_j)/3; for the total this is R²||T||²_F/3. Cross terms matter; summing the squares of the separate tides omits them. This describes the short-time mean work of this prepared shell, not permanent heating or a general stellar population.

Compute finite-size coefficients by external-force differences and independently differentiated tensors. Freeze them and the centre states before running short work integrations. Compare the actual mean work to half the predicted coefficient times h², and check its h→0 limit (h=0.08,0.04,0.02,0.01 Myr; Richardson cancel the first-order-in-h remainder). Preserve zeros for the host-only added component. Check each component and each shell/field; distinguish full finite-body prediction from linear small-body approximation, retaining dimensional errors when the latter fails. No fitted Taylor coefficients from the work samples. No assumption the same rule predicts late escape.
