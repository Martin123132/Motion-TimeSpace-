# Palomar 5: a resolved tidal response, not a measurement of the wave direction

**Decision.** Neither frozen preparation gives a distinguishable flow direction using the tested Pal 5 history and present measurement errors. The distinction is numerical versus observational: a small forward–reverse force difference survives a stricter orbit calculation, but plausible changes to the release time and the cluster itself matter more. No fit to observed residuals and no change to the forces, flow states or tolerances.

## How much of the stream is tested?

Both signs of both preparations have been evolved over **111.158776 Myr**. That covers 5.73% of the [1.94 Gyr drift estimate for the tip of the trailing debris](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0307446) or 2.22% of the [5 Gyr model disruption time](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.06647). These are different reference clocks, not a measured age for every star. The 425.18 Myr common forward archive and 1,065.51 Myr inherited forward archive are preparation histories, not two-sign stream simulations.

The [matched stream tracks](../mts-pal5-joined-flow-586-600/RESULTS.md) at nominal distance separate in line-of-sight velocity gradient by **+0.0651** (reset) and **−0.0798** (inherited) km/s per degree, against a quoted 0.15 error. Across the distance choices the strongest reaches 0.645 of that error. The strongest twenty-bin position contrast drops from 1.263 to 0.168 diagonal-error units if a single overall tilt is unconstrained. This is a sensitivity test, not a fitted observational significance. With measured velocities conditioning the [49-star comparison](../mts-pal5-astrometric-tangent/RESULTS.md), the respective gradient shifts are +0.04164 and −0.06208 km/s per degree, not independent predictions of those same velocities.

Moving the assumed stripping time by ±10 Myr in the separate recent-release test (not bundled here) changes the predicted observables between the early and late releases by at least **15.7 times** their same-release sign split. This is a release scenario, not an uncertainty distribution for all the old debris. Do not extrapolate these late forces to a gigayear or interpret positive cluster energy as a known stripping date.

## What the new test establishes

For the same 72 near-cluster stars, split a step wherever the star **or its moving cluster** crosses a native radial element. Carry velocity and position continuously through; no kick is added. For each star independently integrate the work of each gravity component: its acceleration difference between star and centre dotted with their relative velocity. Compare their sum against the change in the specific kinetic plus Plummer binding energy computed from the trajectory. There is no work from a spatially uniform external acceleration in this test. See the [frozen protocol](PROTOCOL.md) and [controls](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `unit-controls.json`).

- All **156** nominal step/field refinement comparisons pass 0.5%. At equal integration order, stepping past the interfaces instead fails **24/78** even though the overall energy budget balances. The last step halving changes the signed position vectors by **0.000462% / 0.000106%** (reset/inherited). No significant digits of the old 5.5% / 3.2% failures are hidden or counted as successes. Intermediate centre-return failures remain recorded; all final centres pass the separate 1e−9 check.
- Maximum same-star displacement over the three shells, comparing the **endpoints**, is 0.417 pc (reset) or 1.154 pc (inherited). These near-cluster probes are not the distant track bins. On the final grid every paired work history agrees with its paired energy change to better than 1.3e−9 in relative norm. In the reset-forward outer shell the mean energy gain is +0.6712 (km/s)²: +0.2071 from the ordinary field and +0.4641 from the wave along those paths. Its equal-reference-monopole static comparison yields +0.6711 on its own paths.
- At the nominal 4,297 solar masses, the ordinary field alone leaves zero probes energy-positive. Both signs, sign means and matched static fields yield the same individual classifications: **0/8/18** of 24 in the three shells end positive; **0/12/24** ever test positive at the saved epochs. Some stars return to negative energy. Under a separate 16,700-solar-mass stellar-population assumption at the same radius, these become **0/0/6** both ever and at the endpoint, again independent of sign. All 78 alternative-mass refinements pass. The mass choice is a sensitivity check, not a fitted interval; no escape rate was inferred. ## Initial tidal work from the equations

For initially paired opposite circular velocities on an isotropic shell, an affine external tide gives the initial mean curvature of component work as radius squared times tr(TᵀT_component)/3. T is the **total** external acceleration gradient: cross terms belong in the answer. The finite-separation expression in [the derivation](EARLY-WORK.md) passed 81 short-time comparisons at each of two resolutions; worst relative difference on the final grid was 0.0127% or less. This is an initial coefficient, not a constant stripping rate.

For a finite aperture in a non-streaming equilibrium population, the integrated Jeans relation gives an additional radial-pressure term proportional to tr(T_component); it need not vanish. In the one-scale-radius reset-forward example it reduces the initial total curvature by about 65%. All 81 independent phase-space integrations of the affine result pass. This does not model subsequent evolution of the whole population. [Derivation, selection assumptions and Plummer cutoff issue](APERTURE.md) · [aperture tests](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `aperture-test.csv`).

## Ordinary-gravity check before a longer evolution

At the **ordinary-only** launch point (galactocentric radius 21.964 kpc), the divergence of the represented ordinary acceleration implies −18,927 solar masses/kpc³. An independent surface-flux integration around a 19.24 pc sphere gives **−0.5641 solar masses**. It is the effective enclosed source of the saved potential, **not a measurement of negative mass in Pal 5**. The same ordinary component at the distinct reset-forward launch point gives +677.67 solar masses at that radius. Both fluxes converge at the unchanged tolerance. This issue exists without the MTS wave.

At the negative site the mode sums through degrees 0, 8, 64 and 68 give approximately +24,726, +4,074, +9,254 and −18,927 solar masses/kpc³ respectively. This identifies sensitivity to representation, not which truncation is correct. Before using these tensors for a longer stripping calculation: compare the potential's implied mass against the **saved positive ordinary gas/star cells**, separating the absolute background from the transferred time-dependent increment, at the flagged point and positive control. Test angular truncation and radial interpolation separately; do not clip the density or choose a cap to obtain the desired sign.
