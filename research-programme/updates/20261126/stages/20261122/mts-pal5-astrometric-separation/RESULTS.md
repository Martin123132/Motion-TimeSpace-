# What the Palomar 5 distances and proper motions can distinguish

**The limiting ambiguity is the initial wave preparation.** With the preparation specified, the two frozen flows separate by 0.39 or 0.38 measurement-error units across the 17 tail RR Lyrae. Without specifying it, reset/forward lies only 0.0671 units from inherited/reverse, and inherited/forward lies 0.0671 from reset/reverse. That is close enough to put a quantitative limit on *any* direction classifier under the adopted measurement model. The solution is not a more complicated scoring rule.

| Forecast on the same 17 stars | Reset | Inherited |
|---|---:|---:|
| Distance separation, including a shared distance calibration | 0.377 | 0.374 |
| Two-component proper-motion separation | 0.106 | 0.073 |
| Combined separation | 0.391 | 0.381 |
| Largest individual 3-observable separation | 0.125 | 0.117 |
| Ideal correct sign, **if preparation known**, equal sign odds | 57.76% | 57.55% |

These are predicted differences in noise units, not residuals to data. Maximum distance differences for individual stars are 58.0/56.9 pc, maximum proper-motion differences 0.00829/0.00578 mas/yr. Keeping the borderline eighteenth star gives combined separations 0.397/0.387. The observations supplied the starting geometry, membership and motions; treating them again as independent dynamical evidence would be circular. We used [Price-Whelan et al.'s RR Lyrae catalogue and uncertainty prescription](https://arxiv.org/html/1910.00595v2), including the saved Gaia DR2 per-star PM correlations. The 1% relative random distance and 2% common scale are approximations, not measured individual distance posteriors. The Gaussian scenario assumes no extra intrinsic dispersion or cross-star PM covariance. The spectroscopic catalogue had no 5 arcsec matches in the prior overlap check (not bundled here); it was not added to the statistic. The exposed literature radial-velocity gradient is the same conditioning law for every branch.

**Preparation unknown:** the two forward-minus-reverse patterns have noise-weighted cosine **−0.940**: where one preparation tends to increase an observable, the other tends to decrease it. Using the *actual four endpoints*, a single unit-noise linear contrast achieves at least **51.334%** correct for each possible state in the ideal Gaussian repeat experiment; no classifier, even nonlinear, can guarantee above **51.338%**. These are worst-state bounds, not a posterior probability or a classifier trained and scored on real outcomes. Freely removing common PM offsets and fractional distance zero point gives **51.308–51.310%** on the projected observations. No population mixture or training prior on the two preparations is required. [All four pairings and bounds](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `any-classifier-bound.csv`) · reproducible coefficients of the common contrast (not bundled here).

**Could these data independently resolve the sky tilt?** A physical rotation by **0.168°** about the cluster sightline absorbs almost all of the strongest archived sky contrast (inherited, 23.2 kpc; 1.263 drops to 0.083 independent-bin noise units). This is not identical to a linear declination tilt because it includes the simultaneous RA change. In the optimistic rigid rotation of the entire relative snapshot the 17-star PMs resolve a local angular change only at the **3.65°** scale (4.27° with two free PM offsets). That particular tilt is therefore 0.046 of the error. A rotation of positions alone with velocities held fixed gives even weaker angular information; heliocentric distances are exactly invariant. These are local sensitivities conditional on the specified rotation prescriptions, not a measured prior on the stream ridge. We did not use the tiny instrumental coordinate errors as the physical thickness of the stream or combine these stars with the 20 track bins as if independently sampled. All orientation and distance choices (not bundled here).

**Decision:** retire this 17-star sign test at current precision. To regain leverage, distinguish the two *preparations* without using the flow sign. At the common snapshot 590 each reverse launch is the complex conjugate of its corresponding forward wave. It therefore has the same density and instantaneous weak-field gravitational tide within a preparation. Compare the *between-preparation* density/tidal pattern at fixed host and occupation against an independent mass or force tracer with baryonic uncertainties, before asking a stream for its sign. The [original formation test](../mts-flow-formation-inverse-586/RESULTS.md) shows which branches the saved simulated histories populate; it is not a prior measured for the Milky Way. The [32-year launch calculation](../mts-pal5-finite-wave-stripping/RESULTS.md) is a different comparison and is not extrapolated over the 111.16 Myr here. The earlier ±10 Myr release scenarios are unchanged, not turned into an invented Gaussian error.
