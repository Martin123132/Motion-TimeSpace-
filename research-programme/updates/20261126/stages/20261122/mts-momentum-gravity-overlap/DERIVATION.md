# From a momentum-dependent distribution to a gravitational source

This is the scalar, common-metric branch, with the frozen occupation, phases, matter content and ordinary metric couplings of the regular start (not bundled here). Conformal time is in Mpc, \(c=1\), \(q\) in present neutrino-temperature units. The one-particle momentum is measured by observers normal to the spatial slicing. A normal flux is not its longitudinal divergence: the latter includes a factor of \(k\) and the previously retained first-order products for conversion to the covector current. Those products are common in the comparisons. Nothing here infers a new free matter coupling, Newton's constant, or a primordial distribution from measured gravity.

The Einstein–Boltzmann scalar moment framework is standard; see [Ma & Bertschinger](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9506072) for massive linear phase space and [Pitrou](https://arxiv.org/abs/0809.3036) for second-order kinetic and polarization conventions. We use the previously derived normal-frame geodesic source and exact pair projection (not bundled here). The particular subtraction, stress transport, constraint subtraction identity, overlaps and cold/heavy numerical comparisons below are calculations on our own states.

## Exact speed minus the retained thermal control

Let \(b=(am)^2,\ \epsilon=\sqrt{q^2+b},\ v=q/\epsilon,\ W=-q f'_0,\ w=\partial_{\ln q}\ln W=1-q\tanh(q/2)\). At zero mass the incoming first-order distribution \(F\) is independent of \(q\). The previously derived second-order distribution is \(N+(w+4)Z\), not \(N\) at each \(q\); \(Z=-[\vartheta_1^2]/2\) projected on the output direction. With \(L\) the massless Legendre streaming matrix (including \(k\), no gravity term), evolve the **difference**
\[
 \Delta_q'=v L\Delta_q+(v-1)L\{N+(w+4)Z\}
       + e_1 k(1/v-1)\psi_2/3 + S(q)-S_{\rm br}-(w+4)S_Z.
\]
The \(\phi_2'\) monopole cancels identically. Here the two first-order quantities entering \(S(q)\), \(F_1(q)\) and \(\partial_{\ln q}F_1(q)\), were evolved separately for every bank momentum and all 782 input wavelengths in the native first-order metric. The derivative includes the derivative of the streaming speed \(v(1-v^2)\) and of the lapse force. It was checked by perturbing \(q\) logarithmically and re-integrating. \(S_Z\) is the coefficient of \(w\) in the massless quadratic source. The direct source subtraction is performed at matched time cadence before interpolation. In the exact zero-mass test the nonlinear difference vanishes at numerical roundoff although the finite-\(q\) spectral distribution is not a constant.

At the earliest radiation launch \(x=am/q\propto\eta\). Constant linear lapse \(P\) and monopole \(-P/2\) give \(F_{11}=k\eta P/(6v)\). Its logarithmic derivative is \(-(1-v^2)F_{11}\); the leading quadrupole is unaltered since \(v F_{11}\) is. For the quadratic dipole collect \(A v+B/v\) from constant incoming monopoles, lapse and the projected metric/temperature products. Put \(g=C+P,\ T=-P/2,\ p=[1^2],\ M_0=N_0+(w+4)Z_0\). With the free curvature included in \(\psi_2,N_0\),
\[
 A=kM_0/3+kgT p/6,\quad B=k\psi_2/3+k(gP/2-P^2)p/3-w kPT p/6.
\]
The two spatial orderings of two proportional first-order fields supply half the output wavevector each in the projected sum. The control requires \(A+B\) to reproduce the earlier regular dipole coefficient including \(w\), for all momenta and both cases. Integrating the two speeds from the origin gives
\[
 \Delta_1(\eta_i)=\eta_i[(\operatorname{asinh}(x)/x-1)A+
 ((\sqrt{1+x^2}+\operatorname{asinh}(x)/x)/2-1)B].
\]
All other mass lags start at zero to the retained leading order in gradients. This is a regular finite-surface expansion, not an exact relativistic solution for arbitrarily small \(q\) at finite time. Switching off the dipole correction is tested. Two later overlaps separately restart the *actual* distribution or deliberately discard the earlier accumulated lag.

## Stress and its return to gravity

Write \(A_b\) for the saved massless-limit density coefficient divided by \(a^2(7\pi^4/120)\) in the parent's rescaled stress units. Compared with using the integrated \(N\) with finite-mass thermal **weights**, the normal energy, normal flux, scalar shear and trace shifts are
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \delta E/A_b &=\int W q^2\epsilon\Delta_0\,dq + Z_0\int W(w+4)q^2(\epsilon-q)dq,\\
 \delta j/A_b &= \int Wq^3\Delta_1\,dq,\\
 \delta S/A_b &=-\int W(q^4/\epsilon)\Delta_2\,dq-Z_2\int W(w+4)(q^4/\epsilon-q^3)dq,\\
 \delta D/A_b &=\int Wq^2 b/\epsilon\,(\Delta_0+(w+4)Z_0)dq .
\end{aligned}
\]
The subtracted massless integrals vanish by parts exactly, not by assuming finite thermal quadrature integrates a small difference exactly. Use \(\epsilon-q=b/(\epsilon+q)\). The input to the momentum constraint is \(\delta J=k\delta j\). \(\delta E,\delta J,\delta S,\delta D\) are inserted into the existing canonical matter equations and the Einstein equations; all other species and both metric potentials respond. Three passes alternate kinetic transport and material gravity, retaining a numerical fixed-point change. This is not an existence theorem for arbitrary masses or backgrounds.

## Why subtracting the old control in two gravity routes gave different tiny numbers

For the difference of two runs at identical first-order inputs, all explicit purely geometric quadratic sources cancel. Define the divergences of the *difference stress*, not of either universe in isolation:
\[
 r_E=E'+2{\cal H}E-{\cal H}D+J-3(e+p)\phi',\quad
 r_J=J'+2{\cal H}J-k^2[(E-D+2S)/3+(e+p)\psi].
\]
Differentiate the actual constraints \(E=-2k^2\phi+6{\cal H}F,\ J=-2k^2F,\ S=k^2(\psi-\phi)\). Substitution of the background identity \({\cal H}'-{\cal H}^2=-(e+p)/2\) gives the two exact scalar identities
\[
 F' = F'_{\rm spatial\ trace}-r_J/(2k^2),\qquad
 \phi'=-F-{\cal H}\psi-
 \{r_E+3{\cal H}r_J/k^2\}/\{2k^2+3(e+p)\}.
\]
Simply subtracting the two independent trace evolutions as if both material descriptions were conserving amounts to setting these residuals to zero. But one of the descriptions uses **finite-temperature massive energy/pressure weights with a unit-speed transport operator**. Its balance law is not the massive one. This matters on the scale of their difference, even when both give nearly identical total gravity.

The needed divergences can be computed **without using differentiated or fitted metric differences**. Define \(c_E,c_J,c_S=A_b\int W(q^2\epsilon,q^3,q^4/\epsilon)dq\); \(s_E,s_j\) are true energy-/flux-weighted quadratic sources minus respectively \(c_E S_{{\rm br},0},c_J S_{{\rm br},1}\), with the analytic spectral subtraction as above. Directly integrating the monopole and dipole transport gives
\[
 r_E=s_E+k(c_E-c_J)N_1,\qquad
 r_J=k\,s_j+\tfrac{k^2}3[(c_S-c_J)(N_0-2N_2)+(c_E-c_J)\psi].
\]
Use the **old common-speed** \(N,\psi\) in this last formula: at the new metric the same streaming-weight mismatch of its induced common-sector response subtracts the change in those terms. This is a correction to the *comparison*, not an extra counterterm in physical MTS gravity. Separately, the integrated monopole and dipole of the supplied correction at a fixed new metric were compared with its time-evolved energy and current, with both the source and streaming terms individually removed as negative controls. Agreement of the two total potentials alone is not that test.

## A derived one-channel leading thermal law

At fixed geometry expand \(v=1-b/(2q^2)+\cdots,\ F_1(q)=F^0+H/q^2+\cdots\). Then \(\partial_{\ln q}F_1=-2H/q^2+\cdots\), and at first order
\[
 H'=LH-\tfrac b2 L F^0+ e_1 kb\psi_1/6 .
\]
Differentiate the complete ordered-pair geodesic driver analytically. At this order the derivative contains \(H\), its \(-2H\) spectral derivative, and is affine in \(w\); time variation of \(b\) is inside the source throughout the history. The resulting lag is \(q^{-2}Y(w)\) at leading order. Its integrated correction needs **one** response at \(w=-2\), not 39 or 77 velocities. Indeed \(I_1=\int qW\,dq=\pi^2/6,\ \int qW w\,dq=-2I_1\), whereas the zero-mass brightness measure \(q^3W\) has mean \(w=-4\). Define \(Y=Y(-2)\); it obeys
\[
 Y'=LY-\tfrac b2 L(N+2Z)+ e_1 kb\psi_2/6+S^{[1]}(-2).
\]
Here \(S^{[1]}\) differentiates streaming, angular deflection, redshift and the momentum derivative in the original driver; it is not measured from differences between finite-momentum outputs. The regular initial dipole is \(\eta_i b_i(B-A)/6\) evaluated at \(w=-2\). To leading order the four source corrections are
\[
 (\delta E,\delta j,\delta S,\delta D)
 = A_b I_1\big(Y_0+bZ_0,\;Y_1,\;-Y_2+bZ_2,\;2bZ_0\big).
\]
This coefficient follows from the specified unperturbed Fermi–Dirac occupation. A nonthermal background changes it. The inverse-momentum expansion is **not uniform at \(q=0\)**; it must not be used bin by bin there or extrapolated to later epochs without new exact-bin checks. Both squared-temperature shape and transport of the tangent matter. Neither a new coupling nor a preferred primordial amplitude was fitted.
