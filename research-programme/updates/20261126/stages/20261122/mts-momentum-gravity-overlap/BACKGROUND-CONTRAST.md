# Primordial-curvature ambiguity in the background comparison

Before evaluating the cross-background projections: use the same five pre-existing Fourier probes, the forced and the one-unit primordial control already propagated, in each background. Compare (i) the scalar Weyl potential alone at the final conformal time 6.4 Mpc, (ii) both potentials at that one time, (iii) Weyl alone at the three **fixed** times 1.6, 3.2 and 6.4 Mpc, and (iv) the two potentials at those same three times. Equal component/time weight in dimensionless complex amplitude; not observational inverse variances. Use the two-potential three-epoch case as the primary mathematical test. Run on both the exact kinetic result and the saved common-speed solution. Report the orthogonal residual in absolute amplitude, as a fraction of the original forced difference and of the cold forced signal. No cherry-picking a time or k to increase contrast.

Compare zero independent amplitudes, one shared arbitrary complex primordial coefficient per mode, and **separate** arbitrary complex coefficients for cold versus heavy. Use complex least squares solely to compute a lower limit allowing the nuisance the most favorable values. Record singular values, achieved amplitudes and condition number; an exactly saturated endpoint comparison is not a discrimination. Include a no-lapse contrast (Weyl-only), and estimate stability with the alternative metric route and stricter thermal grid. This is a conditional identifiability question on fixed seeded histories, not a fit or a forecast with real noise, nor an inference that those primordial modes are present.

Also align at equal scale factor rather than equal conformal time: at each specified time take the smaller of the two scale factors and interpolate each native history to that value, strictly inside its domain. Use equal-scale-factor results for physical interpretation. Native linear transfers provide an independent check on cancellation between nearly parallel primordial directions. This remains a controlled scalar-transfer comparison, not a line-of-sight observable.
