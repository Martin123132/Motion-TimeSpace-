# A passage between two wells is not a rule for populating the host

The gravitational field reconstructed around the simulated m12i galaxy contains a host–satellite exchange channel. It does **not** by itself select the old MTS halo's 213.905 billion solar masses. This distinction is measurable without using any stream to calibrate mass or radius.

### Geometry and Newtonian control

We traced the *same* host, embedded companion and external neighbour through 29 successive FIRE tree outputs, then calculated the forces independently at 544, 558 and 572, a span of 640.33 Myr. The companion centre moves from 194 to 223 kpc. In the catalogue field, the three radii of the embedded saddle, in kpc, are:

| Central source; same neighbours and cosmic term | 544 | 558 | 572 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Ordinary matter only | 151.6 | 163.3 | 174.5 |
| Ordinary matter + fixed wave | 169.0 | 181.5 | 193.7 |
| Ordinary matter + conventional halo | 179.6 | 193.1 | 206.3 |

Both descending paths lead to the predicted two objects at all three epochs, for both the embedded and external saddle checks. A direct path between host and embedded companion lies below *every* point on a 250-kpc sphere in all nine primary reconstructions. Thus an exchange between these two wells need not leave the larger environment. The barriers need not be the first or only saddle; there is no 29-epoch continuous saddle measurement. The wave-arm margins at 250 kpc are 3524, 2494, 1500 (km/s)² before unknown exterior mass. Even placing all omitted exterior **baryons** in their worst possible directions leaves positive refined path/sphere gaps of 1895, 845 and 58 (km/s)² using radii 250, 250 and 300 kpc respectively. The last is sensitive to other missing sources; these specific bounds do not constrain arbitrary dark tides. The independent low-order spatial force models are inward in every sampled direction at 50–500 kpc, with minima at 500 kpc of 4.94–4.98 (km/s)²/kpc for the wave and 20.43–20.56 for the native conventional model. This is not a theorem excluding small-scale saddles. Adding an *unmatched* frame acceleration produces quite plausible-looking wave saddle radii of 134–293 kpc. These do not establish a physical escape surface. The coherent difference in external gravity between host and surrounding space, not the host's absolute acceleration in isolation, matters. ### Spatial selection fails, even in a generously defined core

At 544 the whole ball through 120 kpc lies below the companion saddle in each main potential. Under the explicit co-location test, the mass of each of the five saved spherical +2σ cosmic waves **inside 50 kpc alone** is 1.96–4.81 times the target; inside 100 kpc it is 4.15–7.14 times. Just selecting everything on the host side of the neck cannot produce the target. Purely geometrically, each donor's target mass is already inside 2.98–24.94 kpc; even a centre mismatch smaller than 95 kpc cannot exclude all of that mass from the certified 120-kpc core. This displacement argument is spatial only, not a spectral result. Radii and masses (not bundled here).

### Does selecting by energy change that conclusion?

Yes, the answer now depends on the input state. For the same frozen field, compute a lower bound on the wave weight below the neck energy in the host sublevel region. A bulk relative velocity removes power from radial modes but sends some of it into angular modes—it does not erase that power. Restoring the angular modes and bounding the interpolation between speeds gives the following **minimum certified fractions of the target across every speed from 0 to 128.16 km/s**, independent of direction, **for co-centred donors**:

| Saved spherical input | Continuous-speed lower bound / target |
|---|---:|
| 8192, 24 Mpc, continued from a=0.6 | 1.158 |
| 4096, 24 Mpc, continued from a=0.6 | 1.511 |
| 2048, 24 Mpc, continued from a=0.6 | 0.186 |
| 2048, 24 Mpc, alternative sheet preparation | 0.775 |
| 2048, 40 Mpc, continued from a=0.6 | 0.097 |

This rules out identifying **all instantaneous subthreshold weight** with the target for the first two saved inputs under the stated conditions. The other three are inconclusive, not successful mass predictions. The upper end is the FIRE centre speed against a comoving reference, **not** a measured relative velocity for the cosmological donors. The numerical witnesses use the bounded sublevel domain, not an infinite outgoing-wave problem. A 64-function trial gives lower bounds 1.133 and 1.474 for the first two; increasing quadrature order at fixed 96 functions changes the reported floors by less than 3e-9 target units. Donor resolution differs substantially; do not average the five, call them independent galaxies, or infer a continuum population. The wave potential was frozen at its saved Galactic occupation; the imported donor did not backreact. ### Next physical calculation

Follow the wave **current** across a moving host region, rather than claiming its instantaneous contents belong to the galaxy. Start from the saved native complex amplitudes at all three epochs, not cosmological radial densities with an assigned phase. Use the continuity identity dM/dt=−∮(j−ρ v_boundary)·n dA, separating actual flow from the amount swept up when the boundary moves. First reproduce it on fixed spheres with the original wave and ordinary driving history, then try sublevel surfaces strictly below the companion neck and compare surface flux to direct changes of enclosed mass; do not divide by a vanishing gradient at the saddle. If three snapshots cannot resolve the budget, use saved propagation steps or report the unmeasured flux instead of fitting a boundary. This tests retention, not initial formation by itself; an independent occupation will still require density *and* phase from formation data for the same host.
