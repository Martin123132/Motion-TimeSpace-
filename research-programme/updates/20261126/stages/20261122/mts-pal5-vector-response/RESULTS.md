# Palomar 5 — the wave feedback as a force on stars

The saved density calculation predicted how much wave mass would enter or leave a sphere. We have now calculated the **vector acceleration** that change produces, using the entire occupied wave out to 388.41 kpc and the same Newtonian coupling and preparation. The experiment changes the ordinary gravity from its older approximation to the mass-sourced field. Its acceleration and energy differences diagnose that update; they are not detections of a wave in Pal 5 or measurements of a stripping event.

**The leading change in mean stellar energy is negative at every tested radius for both flow directions and both preparations.** It enters at fourth order in elapsed time, because the individual cubic responses cancel between opposite launch velocities. For reset-forward the fourth derivatives are −1.27662×10⁻⁷, −5.21737×10⁻⁷ and −1.21088×10⁻⁶ (km/s)²/Myr⁴ at 19.24, 38.47 and 57.71 pc. These are derivatives: the leading energy difference is the coefficient times t⁴/24, not the coefficient times t. Negative means more bound *relative to reusing the old wave on the same corrected ordinary gravity*, in this short-time expansion. It does not choose a flow sign or show that fewer stars escaped.

All twelve shell-averaged coefficients pass the fixed 0.5% tests (worst 0.098%), including source, global resolution, preparation, local chart and derivative step. Across the 72 release probes × four states, **288/288 fixed-position star-minus-centre force vectors** pass the tested refinements (worst 0.044%). Resolving a vector norm does not guarantee every component: 224/288 individual cubic energy terms, 108/144 sign differences, and 96/144 individual velocity-pair fourth energy coefficients pass. The shell averages were taken over the original complete ensemble, not just the passing individuals. ## A field inside a sphere is not determined by its enclosed mass
All twelve mean outward-force curvatures reproduce the earlier frozen mass predictions (worst difference 0.088%, limit 0.5%). But in the innermost sphere the **vertical quadrupole** for reset-forward has radial coefficient +0.002285 (km/s)²/kpc/Myr². About +0.002284 comes from the wave-density response *outside 67.32 pc*. This ratio refers to one quadrupole, not to the whole cluster force or to the fraction of stars lost. The exterior supplies a tide even though it contributes nothing to the spherical mean.

We calculated the isolated gravity of the wave without differentiating a noisy force grid. An exact product rule puts the local ordinary acceleration into a direct Newton integral and the potential times the density gradient into a full-domain Green integral. Four complex initial states; no adjustment of occupation, angular response or gravity strength to the desired sign. Independently moving the local matching boundary from 3.5 to 3.75 stellar scales changes the radial angular signals by at most 0.0042%. Setting the boundary from the radial force instead of the scalar potential changes them by at most 0.0053%. The independently integrated *interior* combination agrees for all 84 cases on its two finest source integrations. Matching to the less accurate alternate ordinary-field chart still changes 12/84 radial signals by more than 0.5%; these are principally the weak horizontal dipole, not evidence of exterior convergence from every chart. The earlier incorrect boundary-force formula and the uncorrected raw horizontal-dipole residuals are retained explicitly. ## What can be put into the stellar equations
Following the same corrected ordinary trajectory with either an updated or an old wave needs the **fixed-position** wave-force curvature. Translating the centre again would count part of the change twice. The first change in specific energy of an individual release probe is cubic in elapsed time; it changes sign when the velocity at the same position reverses. For the 24 initial stellar velocities in each sphere that term averages to zero. The next term survives: it is fixed by the existing forces, relative velocities and the spatial derivative of the new force. No third time derivative of the microscopic wave is needed for the symmetric pair. The calculation distinguishes work by the added force from the change in total cluster binding energy; these are not the same coefficient.
