# First noncancelling cluster energy and the common-metric clock test

The 72 saved release probes are not 72 measured stars. They comprise 24 tracers at each of three radii, including both senses of each tangential launch velocity. Reuse their original velocities and Galactocentric axes, rotated into the computational meridian. No escape velocity, source distribution or Plummer mass was fitted here. The old and updated waves start in the same state on the same corrected ordinary host for the live-minus-frozen stellar readout. The *field perturbation* is curvature from evolving that state on corrected versus native ordinary gravity; it is not an observed forward-versus-reverse mass difference.

Let K=δa_fix'' at launch, L=δa_fix''' and Δ denote star minus centre. Let v be the relative velocity, V the centre's velocity, a the initial relative acceleration (including cluster gravity), b the relative external acceleration (ordinary plus saved wave), and E=v²/2+U_cl. In consistent units and the retained velocity-independent Newton system:
```
δv_rel = t³ ΔK/6
       + t⁴ [ΔL+3((V+v)·∇)K_star−3(V·∇)K_centre]/24 + O(t⁵),
δx_rel = t⁴ ΔK/24 + O(t⁵).
```
The force of the unperturbed trajectory on the new position affects δv only at fifth order. Multiply out E; for two launch velocities +v and −v at the *same* position define the equal-weight pair mean. The unknown ΔL and all terms linear in the common velocity cancel:
```
individual δE'''(0) = v·ΔK,       paired mean δE'''(0)=0,
paired mean W_live⁽⁴⁾(0) = 3 [a·ΔK + v_i v_j ∂_j K_i(star)],
paired mean δE⁽⁴⁾(0) = paired mean W_live⁽⁴⁾(0) + b·ΔK.
```
The last term is the old external force acting on the feedback-induced velocity. Dropping it confuses work by the added force alone with the full change in the cluster binding energy. The calculation uses directly integrated ordinary gravity, the saved launch wave for b, the actual relative velocities in kpc/Myr and the original cluster acceleration. K is expressed in (km/s)²/kpc/Myr², hence the fourth coefficients in (km/s)²/Myr⁴. Tangential derivatives are tested using 1.2/0.6/0.3 pc stencils capped to remain within a native wave element and independent local gravity charts. The Taylor expansion requires smooth fields along each checked launch for sufficiently small times and does not continue through later element crossings. An independent, time-dependent manufactured two-body tracer calculation tests the odd work, even work, cluster energy and cancellation of an arbitrary cubic force in the pair mean. See manufactured dynamics (not bundled here).

## Same scalar field, leading weak-metric time rate

The scalar whose negative gradient gives the fixed-position wave force curvature is 4πG(ρU−u), with the units conversion in the [vector derivation](DERIVATION.md). A readout in the translating frame adds 4πG d(X)·grad Δ⁻¹ρ. For stationary endpoints referenced to the centre, report the weak-metric gravitational lapse difference after removing the uniform field at the centre:
```
[δΦ_w''(x)−δΦ_w''(X)+δa_w''(X)·(x−X)]/c².
```
This derived MTS coefficient uses the retained common weak metric. Its first-order spatial term vanishes by construction; curvature need not vanish. The standard local-GR point of reference is the freely falling metric expansion of [Manasse & Misner (1963)](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.1724316); that paper does not give the MTS source or these numbers. For each supported comparison we also integrate the solved acceleration along the line and along a broken path for diagonal probes. Actual clocks in orbit would require their motion, end-point events and signal transport; these numbers are not a clock observation. No potential offset or clock response coefficient was fitted.
