# Palomar 5: a repaired common-gravity replay at the four earlier stripping events

**The original wave solver missed a real local response. A replacement spatial calculation recovers the independently derived launch curvature at all twelve positions, then evolves it rather than extrapolating it.** At the six sampled times from 16 to 1,024 years the density response passes the unchanged 0.5% numerical refinement tests. The much longer 62,500-year attempt does not. Reset and inherited preparations, each with both signed flows, were kept separate. No observations, masses, starting phase or coupling were fitted.

### What was wrong, and what changed

Under the change from the old ordinary potential to gravity integrated from its positive stellar/gas source, the original angular-mode Hamiltonian gives just **about 0.07–3.1% of the required local wave-density curvature** across the four clusters/inner/outer points. None of the twelve satisfies the continuum check, despite stable occupation. An independent phase-only test distinguishes the full source from its reduced spatial representation: using the represented scalar potential fails at 12/12, whereas locally integrating the direct force passes 11/12 (remaining error 0.739%). These are kick tests, **not** a 62,500-year kinetic evolution. The projected failure is not evidence that the underlying coupling should be made stronger.

The independent calculation transports wave mass through cells and solves for gravity on the entire axisymmetric domain. It changes no gravitational coupling. On the third spatial grid, the largest error in the *initial* wave-density curvature against the earlier direct gravity calculation is **0.103%**. The new and old potential descriptions use the same incoming complex state within each numerical method. The matched continuous state is sampled, not re-normalized to force agreement. The wave, the moving cluster and both mock stars respond to their appropriate common potential. The historical baryonic source remains prescribed, rather than dynamically reacting to individual tracers.

|---|---|
| 16, 32, 64 years | All twelve density comparisons pass both spatial refinements and the tested time refinement at each date. |
| 256, 512, 1,024 years | All twelve pass again at each date. At 1,024 years the finest same-step spatial comparison changes the readout by only 0.012%; the second time refinement by 0.0034%. |

The physical launch coefficient is the limit as time tends to zero, not a universal square-law fit. By 1,024 years holding it constant would miss the finite result by as much as **12.4%**. That departure is larger than the measured numerical uncertainty. We have not claimed a continuous error bound for all intermediate times. ### How strongly does the changing wave then pull on the stars?

We separated **changing the ordinary pull** from the additional gravity **of the wave's response** to that change. For the second comparison both stellar trajectories already have the same corrected ordinary source and the same underlying live wave; only its *additional* self-gravity differs. This is not the total gravitational importance of the wave or a measurement of a flow direction.

- At 1,024 years the source replacement changes the eight cluster-relative velocity vectors by **5.89e−7 to 1.28e−6 km/s** in norm. The extra part induced by the responding wave alone is **1.35e−16 to 2.86e−16 km/s**, at most **2.29e−10** of the corresponding total change. These tiny differences were transported directly; they are not the subtraction of two catalogued velocities.
- The smallness has a derived reason: matched wave density and current give no instantaneous change in self-gravity or its first derivative. Solve Poisson's equation for the density curvature, then integrate its tidal acceleration: the leading *relative velocity* goes as **t³/6** times the star-minus-cluster wave-force curvature. The independently propagated impulse passes this prediction at **all eight launches through the 512-year sample**; at 1,024 years seven pass and the inherited-forward inner star differs by **0.552%**, just outside the same threshold. The impulse itself is stable under the measured refinements. Impulse and source comparison (not bundled here).
- Gravity is nonlocal. In the inner stars, **57–68%** of the wave-force curvature projected along the total vector comes from *outside* the galactocentric shell extending 0.10 kpc to either side of the cluster's radius; in the outer stars it is **14–18%**. This is not mass outside a ball around the cluster. The vector sum and shell contributions converge between the two finer grids; the very small isolated monopole does not pass relative convergence in every case. The ordinary-only and same-mass static-monopole star branches were retained, as were the original live-solver results when they failed. The accuracy of total stellar energy or conserved total wave mass would not, alone, have discovered the local wave error.

### Does the sign comparison itself survive?

At 1,024 years take the forward-minus-reverse **tidal work per unit mass**, computed at each orbit's own pericentre, then repeat with the corrected ordinary source. On the reset preparation the inner/outer contrasts remain positive and grow by **9.34% / 8.59%**. On the inherited preparation the much smaller contrasts both **reverse sign**: inner +7.94e−13 becomes −1.31e−12, outer +2.65e−13 becomes −1.29e−12 (km/s)². Reintegrating the ordinary force along the trajectories at two quadrature orders changes these four corrected live contrasts by at most **0.049%**, and replacing the interpolation chart by that trajectory integration changes them by at most **0.101%**. This tests the small contrasts themselves, not just the accuracy of the larger individual works.

There is no contradiction with the previous fixed-position result. Fixing the launch time and position isolates changing the wave direction; comparing each sign's *own* passage also changes the tidal environment where stars escape. Even ordinary-only trajectories give a nonzero contrast in that experiment. The sign reversal concerns this small **work difference**, not a reversal of either flow, a reversal of stellar velocity, or evidence in the observed stream. Some very small auxiliary contrasts/subtractions fail the relative gate; the [direct results with controls](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `passage-contrast-2-4-32-1024-4-direct12.csv`) and [comparison-by-comparison errors](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `contrast-checks.csv`) retain them.

**We crossed the two waves and the two passages to test that explanation.** We prepared the opposite wave at each launch's exact date, kept the cluster and stellar starting states fixed there, and propagated all four combinations. At 1,024 years the site/date term accounts for **98.1% (inner) and 95.4% (outer)** of the change in the inherited work contrast under the source correction. The intrinsic forward-minus-reverse work at fixed site remains negative under both descriptions of ordinary gravity. The reversal happens because the site term no longer outweighs it. Both no-change-of-wave controls give *exactly zero* intrinsic contribution. The positive reset contrast is also robust, but the much smaller *change* of that contrast differs by about 0.83–0.84% between the chart and direct path integrations, outside the 0.5% target for precision on the increment. ### Interpretation

This is a finite coupled Newtonian calculation with a checked short interval, not new evidence selecting a Milky Way flow direction. The previous two-star velocity odds and the 223 Myr available history are unchanged; the new **source-corrected** calculation must not inherit the old interval's accuracy by association. Before considering the 126 Myr stellar flights, establish a bridge across the unresolved interval with steps small enough to resolve the field, and keep ordinary orbit/date effects separate from a same-site change of wave sign. Everything remains private.

Four complete 1,024-year restart states are saved on the second grid with the 32-year step. Reopening the independent 512-year saves reproduced **all eleven endpoint arrays bit-for-bit in all four cases**, including both potentials. That is the safe starting point for a 1,024–2,048-year bridge; do not restart at 581 or extend the failed coarse long run.
