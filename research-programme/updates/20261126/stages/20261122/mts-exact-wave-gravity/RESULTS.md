**Force and lensing.** On this scalar metric, k²Ψ = −e₂/2 + 3ℋF₂ + U + S−Q. For k²(Φ+Ψ)/2 halve S−Q. In the saved decomposition, e₂ is recovered from the independently evolved total coordinate density using the Hamiltonian volume factor; F₂ from its current, S from material stress, U and Q from the first-order metric. Compare the sum to the spatial evolution, not to the energy constraint used to obtain the density. The density-only force term differs from the full quadratic force by **6.72–33.12%** across the heavy probes and **16.7–194%** on the cold probes. The signs and cancellations matter; these norm fractions are not percentages of matter in the Universe. This is a radiation-era, second-order mixed perturbation. It shows concretely which terms a density-only Poisson approximation discards, not that local Newtonian mechanics fails. **What can still move the answer.** Collision-equilibrating just the two photon quadrupoles at the starting slice instead of the existing launch changes the final lapse norm by 0.112% cold / 0.00716% heavy. This is one controlled alternative, not proof that arbitrary incoming states are erased. With the original launch fixed, omitted direct higher photon moments influence the two quadrupole equations only through the intensity and polarization octupole histories. The adjoint gives the perturbation those two histories must sustain to change a final lapse by 0.5%: at the most sensitive tested probe (29,1,1), its maximum joint amplitude must be at least **1.38 times** the saved octupole amplitude for cold, **1.99 times** for heavy. Other heavy probes require 4.07–59.1 times. These are lower limits on an *error needed*, not upper bounds on the unknown true error. They do not include intrinsic neutrino sources, extra ionization perturbations, independent primordial data or higher-derivative operators.
