# Pal 5: observational reach and the 2,048-year force check

**[Stream coverage and direction test](STREAM-RESULT.md).** Paired fields span 222.84 Myr; the tested pericentre ejecta fly only 125.80–126.28 Myr. Accounting for the ambiguity of a shared release time and the distinct published spectroscopic errors, the median upper bounds on guaranteed sign identification from the two velocities are 50.16% reset and 50.12% inherited. This is a sensitivity forecast, not a measurement of the sign. The shorter, more accurate calculation below tests a force correction: it does not lengthen the observable stream history.

## Did the short corrected calculation reproduce?

Yes for the finite density, differential stellar velocity and wave-only impulse. Restarted the four authorized 1,024-year states, ran the halved field time step independently **from release**, and changed the spatial mesh separately, with the opposite wave also initialized at each exact site/date. Four reported bridge epochs: 1,280, 1,536, 1,792 and 2,048 years. All 96 local density comparisons passed on each refinement (max 0.0041% time; 0.0358% mesh); all 64 wave-only impulses passed on each refinement (max 0.00115% and 0.01294%). Reopened endpoint states reproduce their saved response potentials and stellar velocity readouts. Preserved original 0.5% gate, both control models, and previous files. [Full comparison counts](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `gate-summary.csv`), [individual rows including failures](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `checks.csv`), endpoint replay (not bundled here).

At 2,048 years the inherited forward-minus-reverse tidal work for the inner/outer stars is +3.17/+1.05 ×10⁻¹² (km/s)² with the native ordinary field and −5.25/−5.16 ×10⁻¹² with the directly calculated ordinary source. Swapping both waves at each site shows **98.1% / 95.4%** of this change lies in where/when release occurs, not the intrinsic fixed-site wave sign. Both no-change-of-wave controls give exactly zero intrinsic contribution. The reset contrasts stay positive. At both source quadrature orders the corrected decompositions agree within the original gate. The convenient interpolation chart still misses the **small change** in the reset site and total terms by up to 0.96% and 0.85%: use the direct path results for that difference. A separately integrated interior anchor checks the time interpolation of the relative cylindrical force (max discrepancy 2.14×10⁻⁷ fraction). Actual trajectory offsets stayed below the original 10⁻⁸ kpc check. Factorial results (not bundled here) · independent epoch (not bundled here).

## What spoils the cubic extrapolation?

Derived the quartic vector coefficient without fitting a time law, with conservative discrete currents, isolated Poisson gravity and the change in force along each moving star and cluster. [Derivation and exact controls](DERIVATION.md). All 16 total quartic coefficients pass mesh refinement (max 0.349% difference). Some smaller intrinsic and explicit-source pieces do not independently reach 0.5%. On the fine time grid the quartic approximation passes all 16 full-vector tests at 1,024 years but only 8/16 at 2,048 (worst 1.076%); the original cubic passes 12/16 at 2,048. **A valid local Taylor coefficient does not make a better long-time forecast.** Actual endpoint wave-only velocities are 1.08–2.28×10⁻¹⁵ km/s for this *difference between force prescriptions*, not the much larger full difference between the flows in the observational test. [Prediction comparisons](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `quartic-test.csv`).

To find out *why*, integrated the saved wave-only gravitational field once at fixed release positions and once along the initial moving trajectories; compared the sum against the full propagating star solution. At 2,048 years the fixed-position field evolution accounts for **98.97%–100.55% of the signed quartic error along its measured vector** on the half step, essentially the same range on the third spatial mesh. Terms can oppose, hence a projection can exceed 100%. The remaining orbital/quadrature mismatch is less than 0.00020% of the full vector on the fine time grid. It is the finite evolution of the wave's own force at a fixed location, not uncertain star-path interpolation, that chiefly defeats this short polynomial. This identifies the responsible term but not yet its characteristic physical time. [240 decompositions](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `quartic-error-origin.csv`).

**Next decision:** for stream inference, prioritize an independently constrained escape/formation history or a more informative tracer rather than extrapolating 2,048 years to a gigayear. If pursuing the corrected force, use the full time-dependent fixed-site wave response (or derive its resolved propagation kernel); validate against these saved impulses before integrating a long debris flight. No selection of a Galactic flow, change to the wave coupling, new fit or public release.
