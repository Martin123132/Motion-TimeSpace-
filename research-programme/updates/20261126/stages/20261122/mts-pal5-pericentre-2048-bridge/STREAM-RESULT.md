# Palomar 5: can the saved histories distinguish the two directions?

**The two flows produce different tracks, but the two recent tail-star velocities do not reliably tell them apart.** We did not use their agreement with any observed velocities to select a sign. This result is for the retained conditional Milky-Way-like host, not a fit to an individual star.

**Three time scales.** The matched two-direction field histories cover 222.841 Myr. At the two directions' own simulated pericentres the released stars have 125.80–126.28 Myr of flight to the shared observation epoch. The separately source-corrected short calculation is not added to either duration. Clock table (not bundled here). Relative to [Odenkirchen et al., section 7.2](https://arxiv.org/html/astro-ph/0307446), the fields cover 17.69% of the leading-tip drift estimate (1.26 Gyr) and 11.49% of the trailing-tip drift estimate (1.94 Gyr). The stellar flights cover about 10.0% and 6.5%, respectively. Relative to [Chen, Li & Gnedin, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19899v2), the fields cover 3.59% and the flights about 2.03% of a model-inferred 6.2±1.7 Gyr duration. Neither dates the two individual mock stars. The five-gigayear disruption simulation is an assumed clock, not another age measurement.

**Compare in measurement units, not parsecs alone.** In a shorter archived 111.159 Myr comparison at 22.05 kpc, maximum mock cluster-relative 3-D separations were 48.43 pc (reset) and 54.37 pc (inherited). Those are maxima on those tracks, not projected separations at every measured star. The line-of-sight velocity-gradient changes were +0.0651 and −0.0798 km/s/degree against a quoted 0.15 uncertainty. The later pericentre test is a distinct release experiment. In it, the velocity-pair separations are 0.01608 and 0.01198 km/s. The 109 [published Kuzma et al. velocity errors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.04548) range down to 0.22 km/s; their median is 1.39 km/s. We used their actual error values, not the measured velocities as labels.

**Joint clock and precision test.** Treat the two directions as equally likely for a given pair of initial states. Two model velocity pairs with the same independent Gaussian errors have optimum classification probability Φ(d/2), where d² is the sum of the two squared differences divided by their respective error variances. One alternative common stripping-date shift for both stars (+0.16418 Myr reset; −0.13019 Myr inherited) is physically allowed in the saved numerical experiment; it is not an age measurement. If both clock explanations are admissible, the least-favourable of these explicitly constructed pairs gives an upper bound on any classifier's guaranteed recovery, without assigning a probability distribution to stripping time. A search through more permissible ages might decrease the bound, not increase it.

The median and best are over all 109×108 assignments of two distinct catalogue errors, **not** over a stripping-age prior, sky selection, independent experiments or posterior probabilities. The last row is a necessary precision improvement for these two stars under this noise model, not a promise that smaller errors would resolve other degeneracies. Existing frozen inputs were verified against their hashes; the measurement comparison was independently re-evaluated. Numbers (not bundled here). In the separate [17-star proper-motion and distance calculation](../mts-pal5-astrometric-separation/RESULTS.md), ideal recovery is about 58% with known preparation, but at most 51.34% guaranteed if the preparation is unknown. Tests with overlapping inputs have not been added together.

**Decision for the observational programme.** Do not choose a direction from these velocities. Before paying to simulate the stream for gigayears, identify older debris with an independent constraint on its stripping history or a different observable whose *projected*, noise-weighted response remains distinct after allowing release time and wave preparation to vary. Freeze predictions for both signs before scoring. Increasing the precision of the force solver is a check on modelling error; it is not an increase in observational information.
