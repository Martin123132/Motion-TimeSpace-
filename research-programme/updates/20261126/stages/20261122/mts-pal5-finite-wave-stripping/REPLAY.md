# Inputs, finite-time theory and replay

The source, occupation, preparation snapshots and 72 star positions and velocities are inherited from the sealed vector calculation (not bundled here). The comparison law and window were set in [the inner freeze](FROZEN.md) and outer extension (not bundled here). Finite wave evolution does not read frozen force or energy coefficients. The fully paired orbit histories used for the observational question come from a different, older calculation: interpret.py checks the corresponding input table digests against the original seals.

Use the retained Python with NumPy and SciPy and one numerical thread. In this directory, run `python -B verify.py` to verify the existing results without starting a new field calculation. Use `python -B manufactured.py` for independent analytic tests. To reproduce the most accurate inner reset-forward integration from saved fields, use `python -B finite_stars.py history-3-4-32-reset_forward-32-physical.npz 4 direct`. The same command accepts a final shell number (1, 2 or 3). Existing outputs must match byte-for-byte, not overwrite a mismatching calculation. For each shell use `python -B score_window.py 1` (substitute 2 or 3). Then `python -B interpret.py` rebuilds the comparison with observational errors and the control summary. For a full reconstruction see ordinary.py (mesh level/source integration order), drive_fd.py (same arguments), finite_wave.py (level, order, step count, saved state, years and branch), finite_stars.py, outer.py, term_budget.py and location_test.py. Numeric filenames retain all the run arguments.

**Why the wave difference can be measured without losing it to rounding.** In axisymmetry evolve the complex radial variable u=rψ with the radial and angular kinetic operators and real potential kicks. Evolve the native u and the difference δu, not two large separately rounded amplitudes. A potential kick changes phase alone. Each Hermitian kinetic step transports mass through its faces by the midpoint current; summing the signed current differences leaves no internal net flux. Compare the transported density difference independently with 2 Re(u*δu)+|δu|² locally. Solve the isolated Poisson equation on the whole supported radial domain for both common and differential wave gravity; the exterior mode has decaying radial slope. Grids resolve the star region without pretending that a small local patch contains the whole mass. Potential interpolation in time uses actual evolved samples, not the predicted Taylor coefficient. Compare the differential ordinary acceleration with direct source forces (largest fine-grid star-minus-centre discrepancy 0.121%). Independent compact monopole/quadrupole sources test the Poisson interior and exterior.

**What actually gets scored.** Stars and centre move separately; stellar binding is kinetic energy relative to the moving centre plus the cluster Plummer potential. Pair opposite initial velocities before averaging. Independently integrate the work, including how the existing external tide does work on the changed orbit. The stored fourth-power comparison is 24 ΔE(t)/t⁴ with time in Myr. Numerical gates check halved wave and stellar steps, spatial refinement, independently evaluated ordinary gravity and the work balance. The change in the wave force across a stellar trajectory contributes the time integral of s vᵢvⱼ ∂ⱼδgᵢ at the launch position. The other launch-site terms are t b·∫F(s)ds and a_cluster·∫sF(s)ds, where b is initial external relative acceleration and F is the new-minus-old wave acceleration relative to the centre. This is a short-displacement expansion checked against the propagated stars; none of its integrals imposes a power of time on δg. The remaining difference is retained, not assigned to the gradient. The distance to a native radial element boundary in gradient-by-release-position.csv is a diagnostic, not a causal attribution.

All operations are local. No observed residual, galaxy fit, new occupation, model selection, change in data errors or recalibration of tolerances enters these calculations.
