# From the measured response inside a ball to the next vector-force calculation

Let δ mean the difference between source-based and original ordinary gravity with the same initialized wave. Work in the retained Newtonian branch. Let X be the launch point and a_b the acceleration sourced by ordinary matter alone. The matched-wave initial acceleration cancels in δa_cluster. In the two translating frames with initially identical velocity, the continuity/momentum equations give

    δρ_w'' = −div F,     F(x)=ρ_w(x)[δa_b(x) − δa_b(X)].

The common-coordinate change includes the centre-acceleration term; its spatially constant subtraction is not a modification of the potential coupling. Gauss gives the mean normal force in the three moving spheres tested here. It does not give the vector force on each star. For the isolated full 3-D system there is also a unique curl-free vector response with this divergence and isolated boundary condition:

    δa_w'' = 4πG P_L F,      P_L = grad Δ⁻¹ div.

In a fixed inertial frame omit the subtraction; translating the readout adds (δa_b(X)·grad)a_w at launch and gives exactly the form above. In Fourier space P_L(k)=k kᵀ/|k|² at nonzero k. Thus only the longitudinal part of the actual density-weighted acceleration sources this gravitational response, even if the density-weighted product has curl. This is an inference from the existing wave and Poisson equations, not an added force law or a new value of G. For the energy norm on the full space, ||δa_w''||₂ ≤ 4πG ||F||₂. Uniform external acceleration is exactly invisible to this relative response.

In real space the projector contains a local F/3 contact term and a principal-value kernel (I−3 n nᵀ)/(4π|x−y|³). For omitted sources at distance at least b from a specific evaluation point, their contribution is bounded by 2G ∫_exterior |F(y)|/|x−y|³ dy. A small local gradient does not establish a small bound for the exterior. A run restricted to the Pal 5 patch cannot be promoted to an isolated vector or an orbital force by appending a zero exterior. The sphere mean is a useful exception: Gauss fixes it without the exterior. The tests in this stage do not bound the actual whole-galaxy vector using this inequality.

A useful next calculation is to apply this operator to the full supported wave and an angularly resolved ordinary acceleration. Compute nearby contributions without angular projection, carry exterior multipoles with an explicit remainder bound, then check all three measured sphere means before using a vector acceleration in the next kinetic step. This avoids evolving hundreds of unoccupied angular channels simply to recover a local source term, without silently excluding their effect.
