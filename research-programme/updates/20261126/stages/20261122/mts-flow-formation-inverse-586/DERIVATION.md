# What does a frozen current direction require of the earlier source?

**Retained model.** The normalized wave evolves by a real kinetic-plus-multiplicative, self-gravitating Hamiltonian with the archived common ordinary potential. An additive constant in the Hamiltonian changes only the overall phase. Let U denote the interval evolution map with its external drive fixed; the density-dependent potential is recomputed on each solution, so U is not a linear operator on states. At the launch time let ψ be the archived wave and ψ* its complex conjugate in the real spatial basis. They have equal occupation, density, Newtonian source and energy; their currents are opposite. The earlier required states are the separately calculated inverse images U⁻¹(ψ) and U⁻¹(ψ*). Generally the latter is not the conjugate of U⁻¹(ψ): backward evolution is not just conjugating all frames of a driven trajectory.

**The discrete inverse.** With endpoints u,v, the retained implicit midpoint map solves i(v−u)/h = [H₀ + V_b,mid + (Φ[u]+Φ[v])/2 − μ](u+v)/2. Swapping u,v and changing the sign of h gives the same equation. We reverse the chronological order of the midpoints but not the sign of V_b. The implementation closes the implicit solve under its existing 2e-14 iteration criterion; no artificial normalization or phase correction in the dynamics. Native resolution is needed to test exact archive replay. Higher resolutions start from the *same* 590 arrays and test errors in the inferred state, not agreement with an archive evolved under a different discretization.

**Measuring mismatch without measuring an arbitrary phase.** For unit states the optimal chord distance is d=min over α of ||v−exp(iα)u||. Calculate it from the aligned vectors, not by subtracting a near-unit overlap from one. The projective angle is 2 asin(d/2); pure-state trace distance is d sqrt(1−d²/4). The total variation of any normalized position measurement cannot exceed the trace distance. Here position probabilities use the native radial and angular quadrature; the comparison is made before collapsing to three shells. No limit on all possible observations of a galaxy follows from a wave-only distance.

**Beyond the two saved preparations.** Write Q for orthonormal columns spanning the two archived 586 states, with complex coefficients allowed. Cauchy–Schwarz gives max over normalized v in the span of |〈v,ψ_req〉| = ||Q†ψ_req||. Equality is attained by the normalized projection. Thus ||(I−QQ†)ψ_req||² is the fraction of norm-squared of the required pure wave outside the *entire* span, not a loss attributed to either normalization convention. Even a mixed state supported there has fidelity 〈ψ_req|ρ|ψ_req〉 at most ||Q†ψ_req||². This is a supplementary geometric diagnostic, not a prior on what other initial states can exist. In particular a nonlinear flow acting on earlier mixtures need not stay in this two-dimensional span. Conditioning, orthogonality, direct least squares and step-size dependence are checked.

**Work in the common ordinary field.** In simulation units E=〈H_bare〉 + ½〈Φ_self〉 + 〈V_b(t)〉. The symmetric Poisson response cancels the implicit density-variation terms against Hamiltonian evolution, giving dE/dt=〈∂t V_b〉. The constant rotating-frame offset drops out. At discrete midpoints the same polarization identity leaves the work h(〈∂t V_b〉_i+〈∂t V_b〉_{i+1})/2. Results are specific energies, multiplied by v_unit²/N to give (km/s)². Since the two 590 energies are equal, E_reverse(586)−E_forward(586) must be the negative of the difference of their forward works. The calculation compares this with direct evaluations of both endpoint Hamiltonians. This is a balance, not a fitted energy injection.

**If one insists on the same earlier state.** There is a different, conditional bound. Follow trajectory 1's unitary Hamiltonian propagator along that trajectory. In its interaction picture, path 1 is fixed and normalized path 2 moves under the transformed difference H₂−H₁. Projecting dψ₂/dt perpendicular to ψ₂ gives speed equal to the standard deviation of that difference (in units with ħ=1), so the length from a shared initial ray is at least the final projective angle θ. This is the [geometric evolution-speed identity of Anandan & Aharonov](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.65.1697), applied to these two prescribed evolutions, not a new QFT result. The evaluated necessary condition is
`∫ σ₂(ΔV_total) dt ≥ θ × t_unit × v_unit²`, with dt in Myr and ΔV in (km/s)². It holds for the common kinetic term, allowing different multiplicative potentials along the two paths. Source-independent constants contribute zero. This lower bound is on the *total* potential-generator contrast, including induced wave self-gravity. It is not a bound on the external field alone, and it is not a claim that equal initial states produce two solutions under the same well-posed equation. The actual inverse test uses different initial states and does not owe this extra drive.

**Newtonian readout.** For the radial amplitudes u_l(x) in the archived orthonormal angular basis (x=r/L), M(<r)=m_scale ∫₀ˣ Σ_l |u_l|² dx and dM/dt=−(m_scale/t_unit) Im Σ_l u_l* ∂x u_l. Split radial quadrature at every element and sphere boundary. The isolated wave's average inward radial acceleration over that sphere is G M(<r)/r² even for an axisymmetric wave; it is not the local force at each sky position. Comparing this readout to the original isolated Poisson flux at 590 checks both sign and units before using the earlier source contrast. The universal Newton coefficient is held fixed, not derived anew here.
