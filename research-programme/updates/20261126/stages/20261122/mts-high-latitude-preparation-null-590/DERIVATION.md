# From an off-plane force measurement to a bound on state preparation

Coordinates are Galactocentric cylindrical \(R,z\), \(r=\sqrt{R^2+z^2}\), \(\alpha=\arctan(z/R)\). Newtonian weak-field acceleration is \(-\nabla\Phi\) with the same positive \(G\) on both sides. The spherical-null potential \(A=\Phi(r,\alpha)-\Phi(r,0)\) has derivative \(T=R\Phi_z-z\Phi_R\); the acceleration along increasing latitude is \(-T/r\). \(Q=T-2\cot\alpha\,A\) also removes any axisymmetric degree-two term, since its equal-radius potential difference is proportional to \(\sin^2\alpha\). This deletes genuine wave quadrupoles too. For any scalar readout the two disk end members define bands shifted by the wave. If convex mixtures of those disks are allowed, the gap between disjoint bands is \(|\delta_{\rm prep}|-|b_I-b_{II}|\) when positive. This is not an uncertainty distribution. Within one model, unresolved independent relative stellar-mass changes of magnitude \(f\) widen a scalar prediction by \(f\sum_j|b_j|\) in either direction, by linearity at fixed shape. The two preparations' intervals first touch at \(f=|\delta_{\rm prep}|/(2\sum_j|b_j|)\).

**Force, not a spherical mass assumption.** Let \(r_p=\sqrt{11.9^2+7.3^2}\) kpc and \(\mu_p=7.3/r_p\). The measured radial-force proxy is \(M_{\rm eff}=r_p^2\partial_r\Phi/G\) at this *one* position. Only its monopole is the actual enclosed mass. The non-spherical part includes exterior material. In the retained axisymmetric amplitude space the wave is a polynomial in \(\mu=\cos\theta\) of even degrees through 34. Its density therefore ends at degree 68. For any normalized wave of this bandwidth the exact isolated-Poisson readout \(M_{\rm eff}/M_{\rm wave}\) is the expectation of a fixed kernel \(K\). Split at \(s=r_p\) in the source integral:
\[
 K(s,\mu)=\sum_{\ell=0,2,\ldots,68}P_\ell(\mu)P_\ell(\mu_p)
 \begin{cases}
 (\ell+1)(s/r_p)^\ell,&s<r_p,\\
 -\ell(r_p/s)^{\ell+1},&s>r_p.
 \end{cases}
\]
The inner term comes from differentiating the exterior field of an interior shell; the outer term from the interior field of an exterior shell. Each shell has measure \(2\pi s^2 ds\,d\mu\) before hemisphere folding. In the monopole-only case this is exactly the enclosed-mass projector. No monopole approximation is needed in the tests.

**An analytic inequality, not a fitted wave.** In a normalized Hilbert space let the reference be \(u\), let \(W\) be the Hermitian readout and let \(W\ge L\). Define \(e=\langle u,Wu\rangle\), \(\sigma=\|(W-e)u\|\). For a new state at phase-independent angle \(\theta\), write \(v=u\cos\theta+w\sin\theta\), \(w\perp u\), after removing a global phase. The two cross terms and the diagonal remainder give
\[
 e-\langle v,Wv\rangle
 \le 2\sigma\sin\theta+(e-L)\sin^2\theta .
\]
Thus to reduce the readout by \(g>0\), \(\sin\theta \ge g/(\sigma+\sqrt{\sigma^2+(e-L)g})\). This is a necessary condition, not a constructive identification of a successful state. A very conservative continuum bound is \(L=-\sum_\ell(\ell+1)=-1225\). Tighter: compress multiplication by \(P_\ell(\mu)\) to the orthonormal even Legendre amplitude space. For each of the interior/exterior coefficients sum the resulting symmetric matrices up through each successive degree. For \(0\le t\le1\) their weights \(t^\ell\) or \(t^{\ell+1}\) decrease monotonically; Abel summation writes the weighted matrix as a convex mixture of prefix sums and zero. Extremal prefix eigenvalues bound every radius. Upper and lower enclosures use a numerical margin of \(10^{-8}\); independent Gauss rules integrate the angular polynomials exactly up to roundoff.

**Two distinct norm conventions.** The continuum calculation uses the continuous normalized wave and the uncompressed kernel variance (a conservative upper bound). The saved finite-element evolution uses Euclidean norm of mass-weighted nodal coefficients. The reconstruction map \(B\) from native normalized coefficients to the continuous inner product is not exactly isometric for all candidate states. In particular its radial Gram matrix has a smallest eigenvalue near 0.467, despite the actual saved waves having almost equal norms in both conventions. Do not silently use a continuum angle as a native speed limit. Form \(B^\dagger K B\) on native coefficients; compute its action and variance directly. A Gershgorin row-sum upper bound \(c=1.57650\) on \(\|B\|^2\) makes \(L_{\rm native}=c L_{\rm angular}\) safe for negative \(L\). The native and continuous angular quadratures, direct Poisson predictions, cross terms, time refinements, and all three source alternatives are tested separately. The native bound is the one quoted for formation work. This argument covers all pure states in the specified representation, not unbounded angular structure, a different occupation or open-system mass exchange.

**Source integral.** In the interaction picture of the matched reference trajectory a second trajectory starting from the same state has angular length at most the path integral of the standard deviation of its effective potential *difference*, divided by \(v_{\rm unit}^2 t_{\rm unit}\). This includes the induced Newton self-potential and is insensitive to adding any spatial constant. Combine with the required angle above: \(\int {\rm sd}_{v(t)}(\delta\Phi_{\rm ordinary}+\delta\Phi_{\rm self})\,dt\ge v_{\rm unit}^2 t_{\rm unit}\arcsin(s_{\min})\). The archived projected multiplication operator's ordinary spread is bounded by half the range on the native angular/radial samples. Range is convex under linear interpolation, so the time integral of the knot half-ranges is an upper bound. The triangle inequality then bounds the *necessary* self contribution given that source budget, not the self contribution actually generated. Measured total/ordinary/self generator integrals exist independently for the counted 589 anchor and older harmonic proxies. The direct cold/hybrid quadrature endpoints have independent force checks but no direct-path action receipt here; do not substitute harmonic action for those. The continuum drive-floor file is illustrative for continuum-normalized evolution; native-drive-floor.csv and measured-action-budget.csv are the archived-evolution comparisons.

**Virial and shell checks.** With \(I=\langle r^2\rangle\), specific kinetic energy \(K_{\rm kin}\), negative specific self-binding energy \(W_{\rm self}=\langle\Phi_{\rm self}\rangle/2\), and \(C\) the conversion from km/s to kpc/Myr,
\[
 \tfrac12 I''/C^2=2K_{\rm kin}+W_{\rm self}-\langle{\bf x}\cdot\nabla\Phi_\star\rangle-P_{\rm wall}.
\]
Use the Dirichlet radial wall pressure \(P_{\rm wall}=4\pi(\kappa/C)^2 r_{\rm wall}\overline{|u_r|^2}/(2M)\) for \(u=r\psi\); do not assume it vanishes because the amplitude does. Compute the binding energy from the interior half of the symmetric Newton double integral, checked against a Gaussian and independent finite-volume self-potential. The local enclosed mass has \(M'=-4\pi\kappa\,\overline{\mathrm{Im}(u^*u_r)}\), \(M''=-4\pi F_0 +4\pi C^2\overline{|u|^2\partial_r(\Phi_{\rm self}+\Phi_\star)}\) with the saved kinetic flux. Independent discrete density accelerations recover both readouts. Global width, enclosed mass and local radial-force equivalent are deliberately separate quantities. No homology, equilibrium or long-time extrapolation follows from an instantaneous virial test.
