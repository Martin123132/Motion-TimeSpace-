# A young-star gravity test of the two frozen MTS preparations

**Both fixed densities fail the same radial shape test with Cepheids as they did with red giants. The discrepancy is not just a low normalization.** A separately published stellar disk and bulge brings the reset wave within 1.42 km/s of the inner Cepheid speed, but then predicts a rise to 241.85 km/s in the next bin, where the stars indicate a fall to 236.54. No sign or occupation was selected. These are conditional tests of particular prepared states on specified ordinary sources, not an exclusion of the action.

## What the two bins require
With the frozen wave and original positive host, an arbitrary **additional** nonnegative spherical component would need its enclosed mass to **fall outwards**, even granting opposite published error endpoints. Using the actual stellar radii the minimum fall is **5.96 billion solar masses (reset), 5.87 billion (inherited)**. In contrast ordinary matter plus an unrestricted nonnegative sphere passes the radial test, in both local and full domains. That is a feasibility control, not a prediction of the sphere. Across the nine individual signed variants and both radius assignments, none removes the failure. Even the most favorable convex **density** mixture of the two saved waves still requires a decrease of at least 4.86 billion solar masses on those scenarios. This does not cover coherent superpositions or other self-consistent occupations.
  
For actual bin weights the correct mass coordinate is d=1/mean(1/R), not simply mean(R). The required mean of GM uses weights proportional to 1/R; it must increase across disjoint ordered bins. The ratio test on the nominal weighted local bins permits at most 0.516 (reset) or 0.520 (inherited) times the fixed wave force if a free positive sphere may be added. This is not a measured universal coupling, an inferred formation probability or permission to reduce G. Without changing the physical states, over the twelve weighted bins the required decline is at least 35.15/34.68 billion solar masses. ## A physical repair, and the price it pays
An external quadrupolar tide is a real Newtonian alternative to demanding negative spherical density. For an aligned, centered, reflection-symmetric leading tide, its potential is A(R²−2z²)/2. Making A negative produces the helpful outward force, but **increases** the potential rise to height h by −A h². We independently optimized arbitrary positive shells plus that tide, preserving exactly the same ordinary and wave fields for both measurements. See [derivation](TIDE-TEST.md).

At the nominal weighted two bins the required A is at most −135.78/−133.63 (km/s)²/kpc² for reset/inherited. At R=8.178 kpc and h=0.4 kpc the lowest possible total rises are then **343.09 / 342.57 (km/s)²**. For the no-wave control it is **274.45**. For comparison [Widmark et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14030) report **301.8±6.0** from seven radial samples combined, *not* at that single point. On the original foreground all seven local minima across the tested solar and wedge variants exceed 307.8; the most favorable outer-group value 309.62 is close and modest broadening can change that outer-group comparison. A common shell profile need not attain each pointwise minimum simultaneously. Bounds (not bundled here).

Checked ordinary-source sensitivity, **replacing**, never adding to, the original host with the stellar parts of [Pouliasis et al., Models I and II, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/1611.07979). Thin/thick Miyamoto–Nagai disks, a Plummer bulge in I, no bulge in II; excluded their halo, did not insert a separate fitted gas correction, and kept the same G. Their parameters are literature Galactic models, not new independent precision density measurements. The two local radial contradictions shrink to 4.18–4.27 and 3.51–3.60 billion solar masses respectively but do not vanish. One outer-group minimum for II is **301.20** (inherited), illustrating exactly why an aggregate result should not be repeatedly applied to individual radii. A fixed wave in a substituted field tests instantaneous gravity, not equilibrium or subsequent wave evolution.

## What the phase-spiral profile actually says
We recovered the **21 separate plotted values and their vertical errors** from Widmark Fig 6 (seven radial centers for each of three solar-height assumptions). A necessary provenance check **failed**: directly weighting these plotted values by their inverse plotted variances gives 302.0, 300.1, 302.4 rather than the 304.8, 295.3, 305.2 in their Eq. 29. The displayed bars do not reproduce the reported aggregate by this calculation. We did not change the sample range, silently substitute weights, attribute an error to the authors or use an invented combined likelihood. Extraction and discrepancy (not bundled here).

Instead, test the displayed **individual** measurements with a **single** common sphere and tide over all radii for each chosen solar height. Permit every nonnegative spherical density; for the vertical objective represent shells on both sides of every sampled radius. The observable of a shell between consecutive tracer radii and vertical path endpoints is affine in 1/r_shell; endpoint mixtures span its responses there, with arbitrarily narrow shells admitted in the limit. Fit no physical law: minimize the largest normalized vertical residual subject to all radial intervals. Each set of seven boxes is a deterministic sensitivity test, not a seven-point independent chi-square. The three solar heights are alternatives, not 21 independent measurements. Galaxy potential evaluated at the reported radial group centers; within-group weighting and phase-spiral inference systematics are not reconstructed.

With the two solar radial bins, even after a generous two-pixel allowance on both plotted means and errors, the most favorable original-host box multiplier across the tested survey variants and solar heights is **4.82 for inherited, 4.85 for reset, and 2.39 even without the wave**. With Model II and the nominal actual Cepheid radii it is **3.85, 3.88, and 2.21** respectively. A multiplier is how much the individual plotted vertical bars would have to expand simultaneously, not a sigma significance. The important control: *radial feasibility* distinguishes the wave from the ordinary baseline, but *all* these simplified models have trouble with the seven-group vertical shape. It would be wrong to attribute all of that tension to MTS. Same-profile tests (not bundled here), ordinary alternatives (not bundled here).
