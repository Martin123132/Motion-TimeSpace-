# Same starting phases, different motion: m12i from z=99 to z≈19
**We have now evolved a specific three-dimensional wave and cold realization, not just assigned starting weights to future halo particles.** The historical m12i seed sets the phases. The retained parent cosmologies supply the field mass, initial transfers, metric and ordinary components. We evolved linear perturbations with the original compiled hierarchy at 65 epochs, with photons and neutrinos evolved explicitly instead of the previously diagnosed late truncation. The previously saved reference epochs and selected columns replay to better than 2e−5. Switching from the old start to the radiation-repaired one changes heavy launch density by 0.898% and spatial phase by 1.635% rms; the seed itself has not changed. This is a conditional same-realization experiment, not the claim that FIRE simulated this scalar or that m12i is the Milky Way.

**Theoretical result — a relative-flow equation without an adjustable force.** Subtract cold geodesic motion from heavy wave motion in *the same metric*. Both feel the same gravitational lapse, so it cancels. What remains is driven by the pressure of the retained averaged scalar. The difference can be evolved using only its initial density and current differences, the expansion, and the CDM rest-density history in that spacetime. A separate solver reproduces the hierarchy's z19 relative density to **2.60×10⁻⁷** and relative momentum to **2.78×10⁻⁸** in seed-weighted relative rms through 2.4/Mpc. This checks equivalence of the two descriptions of the retained linear equations, **not** a measurement of equivalence-principle violation or a new empirical coupling. Substituting the ordinary source from the *other cosmology* changes the relative-density result by 215% rms. Thus an external cold-only density cannot simply be used as the MTS background. **Where the wave actually goes at linear order.** Evaluate the comoving number-density contrast on the frozen initial window of the simulated particles that eventually lie inside 13.96 kpc of the snapshot-544 centre. These are wave fields sampled on a tagged *initial region*, not masses inside an evolving physical sphere. Same source seed, drifted tag window, 192 grid, 0.2925–2.4/Mpc:

| Species and evolution | z=99 mean contrast | z=19 mean contrast |
|---|---:|---:|
| Ordinary CDM, paired cold cosmology | 0.08054 | 0.38248 |
| Heavy wave, retained MTS cosmology | 0.01989 | 0.10442 |
| Ordinary CDM **inside that MTS cosmology** | 0.02979 | 0.12030 |
| Heavy with wave pressure switched off *after launch*, weak-field control | 0.01989 | 0.10734 |
| Heavy with its initial current set to zero, same density, weak-field control | 0.01989 | 0.07351 |

The wave **grows**, but starts with much less small-scale structure than the paired cold model. Removing its launch current lowers this region's final contrast by about 30%; removing subsequent wave pressure increases it by about 2.8%. Those interventions need not add. On the full band, dropping baryons from the gravitational source changes the wave density by 9.19% rms. The weak-field material Poisson evolution reproduces native wave density to 2.86×10⁻⁵ and current to 4.18×10⁻⁵ — within the 0.5% check but outside 2×10⁻⁵. Supplying the native lapse instead reduces both errors below 7×10⁻⁸. The particle-inverted seed, both mesh windows and three frequency caps have separate rows; the absolute window contrast is sensitive to the *physical bandwidth*. Extending the global seed calculation through 5/Mpc raises wave density rms from 0.08718 to 0.08733 but cold CDM from 0.19864 to 0.26162. **What the later simulation actually checks.** Retrieved only missing z19 fields from the four [FIRE-2 m12i shards](https://users.flatironinstitute.org/~mgrudic/fire2_public_release/core/m12i_res7100/output/snapdir_001/), about 205 MB total. Verified the IDs of *all* 5,513,331 coarse tracers, and used the 2,087,470 native coarsest cells (99.54% of the box) for a position/current test in real simulation data. These are merged matter tracers, not measured dark haloes or baryonic gas in the zoom. Apply the same spatial mask and bandpass to the predictions. The mask admits long-wave leakage; retaining the independently seeded linear FIRE field *outside* the comparison band as a common foreground gives these conditional errors versus the simulation:
 
| Through 2.4/Mpc | Displacement rms error | Peculiar velocity rms error |
|---|---:|---:|
| FIRE's own growing seed, linear | 3.94% | 6.36% |
| FIRE displacement through second order, full native particle initial data | 1.73% | 2.35% |
| Paired cold parent, mass-weighted species | 9.32% | 15.59% |
| MTS heavy parent, mass-weighted species | 39.57% | 38.70% |

The second-order row uses all its own source modes, **not** an added exterior; no double counting. Before the exterior sensitivity control the FIRE seed gives 7.89% and 9.31%, the paired cold model 11.54% and 16.98%, and MTS 40.16% and 39.29%. Eroding the mask by another cell changes the conditional error ratios by at most 0.00012. The ordinary control works best in the simulation evolved with its own matter dynamics and normalization. The separation from MTS is real in this experiment; these values are **not observational errors, a likelihood against MTS, or an independent survey fit**. In the refined region, predicting forward with the original native two displacement orders gives 0.898 comoving kpc/0.620 km/s residual on 137,744 dark tracers, but 4.044 kpc/1.349 km/s on 34,436 independently sampled gas tracers. Only three gas/dark sample cells coincide: no population-level matched-pair gas force can be inferred. **What to do next.** On the realized band at z19, nonlinear density transport evaluated on the linear fields is already **14.86% of the linear density rate for the wave and 28.94% for cold CDM**. The corresponding quadratic acceleration contributions are 3.31% and 6.92% of their respective linear accelerations. The two grids reproduce the quadratic forcing to roundoff; these are *instantaneous source strengths*, not accumulated corrections. The appropriate next evolution is to integrate the quadratic source on both branches from the same seed, including its induced common potential, and compare with a finite-amplitude evolution or independent cold second-order control. Separate quadratic evolution generated *after* z99 from inherited second-order data; do not set the latter to zero and call it a formation prediction.
