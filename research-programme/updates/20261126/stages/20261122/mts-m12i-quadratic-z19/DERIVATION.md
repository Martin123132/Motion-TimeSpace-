# Quadratic mass and current on the same weak gravitational field

Scope: number transport in the subhorizon, leading nonrelativistic chart, irrotational first-order fields, no shell crossing or wave nodes in the perturbative construction. Spatial coordinates are comoving Mpc, c=1, theta=div(v), H is the physical expansion in inverse Mpc. Superscripts 1 and 2 mean coefficients of epsilon and epsilon squared (no factorial in the coefficient). A dot below is a conformal-time derivative. Upstream conventions (not bundled here). Its native linear D is the volume-corrected averaged density, not an exactly conserved relativistic particle number. The native wave continuity equation has an additional pressure term. We use the native linear histories as sources and interpret the quadratic transport as a leading number approximation, not an exact second-order Einstein/Boltzmann hierarchy.

## Common gravitational response
For every species, dot D2 = -theta2 - div(D1 v1), and
dot theta2 + a H theta2 = -lap(Psi2) - lap(v1²/2) plus the pressure contribution for the wave. In Fourier variables the first force is +k²Psi2, where k²Psi2 = -sum(C_i D_i2)/(2a), C_i=8 pi G a³ rhobar_i. Linearly all fluids free-fall in that same potential. The source is not the observed dark-matter-only halo. The leading wave term in dot theta2 is k⁴ D_h2/(4a²m_h²) plus lap[-D_h1 lap(D_h1)/2 - |grad D_h1|²/4]/(2a²m_h²), obtained by expanding lap(sqrt(1+D))/sqrt(1+D). Gravity in this weak-field limit is a retained coupling, not a new prediction of the magnitude of G. We do not add a gas pressure, radiative or second-order relativistic stress that was not calculated.

Define B=a_i²H_i, X=(D2, -a theta2/B). The reduced system is linear in X with quadratic first-order forcing. The final X is G(f,i) X_i plus the integral of G(f,t) times forcing over ln a. Both terms use the same homogeneous matrix. A backwards matrix integration with identity final condition has derivative -G A; nested composite Boole quadrature integrates the sources. Nonlinear number current is J2=v2+D1 v1. Periodic derivatives conserve the mean, not the mass in every region. Retaining the whole generated support includes k outside the input band but does not supply unmodelled input modes. At very long generated wavelengths the assumed subhorizon matter closure needs its own validation.

## Relative mass in a moving region
Identify uniform comoving CDM labels q by the undisplaced fine lattice; fix the mean displacement to zero and take the longitudinal s_c(k)=ik D_c1/k². The labels define a conditional moving tube in each cosmology; a matching tag is **not** evidence that an MTS particle subsequently arrives at the FIRE destination. The specific number ratio in CDM mass coordinates is [(1+D_i)/(1+D_c)] at q+s_c+O(epsilon²). Its coefficients are
    r1=D_i1-D_c1;  r2=D_i2-D_c2+div(s_c r1).
Indeed, the ordinary ratio gives the term -D_c1 r1 and moving the evaluation point gives s_c·grad r1. No second-order displacement of the reference tube is needed here. In the number approximation its rate is
    dot r2 = -div(J_i2-J_c2) + div(v_c1 r1 - s_c (theta_i1-theta_c1)).
An alternative is to apply the CDM convective derivative to the Eulerian ratio, using -(1+D_c)^(-1) div[(1+D_i)(v_i-v_c)]. At second order that gives -div(v_i2-v_c2) -div[D_i1(v_i1-v_c1)] + D_c1(theta_i1-theta_c1) - s_c·grad(theta_i1-theta_c1). These two forms agree independently in the computed realization. Quotient rates in the table are rates of this NR operator, not exact derivatives of a relativistic transfer.

## What part of the initial quadratic state is derivable?
Separate uniform labels for each species in the same convention. Write x=q+s1+s2, v=dot x. Expanding exp(-ik·x) for the conserved number and for velocity times number gives in Eulerian coordinates:
    D2=-div(s2) + (1/2) partial_i partial_j(s1_i s1_j);
    J2=dot s2 - partial_j(s1_j v1);
    theta2=div(dot s2) - div[(s1·grad)v1].
Here div(s1)=-D1 and v1=dot s1 in the number approximation. The last equation follows after subtracting D1 v1 from J2. The displacement convention matters: a relabelling would redistribute the individual terms. Within this convention the coordinate terms are fixed by first-order data, with no classical growing-mode assumption for the wave. The remaining physical compression and its rate are **not** thereby zero. The scalar response uses the divergence; this does not prove the absence of independent vector modes in a general initial state. An independent growing-dust test adds the known second-order displacement invariant to the coordinate terms, then propagates them and compares to the earlier inherited-state calculation.

## How strong would the missing initial condition have to be?
Let e read species-minus-CDM density in the 2N-component vector. On an allowed Fourier band, the elapsed fraction depends linearly on a second-order launch X_i through the real Fourier inner product with L=p(W_j-W_H)[e(G(f,i)-I)] (windows follow the negative Fourier exponent convention; conjugate in the inner product). The -I matters: the initial share changes too. Project L onto the chosen species/velocity subspace. Cauchy–Schwarz gives |delta f| <= ||L|| ||X_i||; the minimum joint box rms to erase a known elapsed drift d is |d|/||L||, attained by X_i=-d L/||L||². The mean mode is excluded; reality is respected. The norm sums density and scaled divergence in quadrature over species, in the **whole box**, not just the host. It is not an energy norm, a local amplitude, a probability model or a claim that such aligned phases are physical or small everywhere. There is no bound here on unknown fields outside the modelled band. The saved threshold table uses the retarded/generated-plus-geometry reference and its listed d; for the additional fixed kinematic contribution use its updated d with the same L. In pressureless cold matter the gravitational acceleration cancels: common species-identical data or independent density-only launch data cannot change the relative drift through the homogeneous channel. The corresponding heavy pressure terms break that null.
