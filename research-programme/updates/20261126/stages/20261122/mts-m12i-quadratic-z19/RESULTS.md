# Which way does the early wave move relative to ordinary matter?

**In the calculated part of the same-host history, the inner wave share decreases from 8.0781% at z=99 to 8.0453% at z=19.** These are shares of the selected host labels, on the finite input band, through quadratic order with the inherited *coordinate-transport* terms included. They are not the mass inside a present-day sphere. The change is −0.03276 percentage points. Its sign is not secured against the remaining physical launch deformation. No stream velocities, galaxy fit or new coupling set this answer.

**Numerical checks and physical limits.**
- Retained generated long and short modes, all species' matter gravity and conserved zero density mode. Nested time quadrature, dealiased grids and reuse of independently saved source terms check the transport. Finest two fixed-window grids differ by at most 8.83e−6 relatively on the main patch density and current means; individual Fourier window coefficients need not reach that precision. Four direct undisplaced-tag sums agree to 4.8e−14 absolute. Moving-tube flux/product identities agree to 2.2e−13 relative or better.
- The direct nonlinear complex wave/dust test recovers the four predicted mixing products. At raw amplitude 0.1 the worst per-mode heavy error is about 0.00124, passing 0.005 but not 0.00002. Removing the leading even-in-amplitude error using 0.2 and 0.1 gives at worst 2.26e−6 for heavy and 1.44e−6 for cold on the four tested modes. This is a manufactured 1D positive-density test with the isolated Nyquist mode suppressed; it is not a precision statement for the whole Universe.
- Independent growing-dust dynamics in the simulation cosmology reproduce full-band growing density and velocity divergence to 6.91e−11 and 1.14e−10 relative rms. Leaving out the inherited term gives 19.2% and 21.2% errors respectively. The dust result shows why a zero wave launch cannot be inferred from numerical convergence. The kinematic/dynamical split also reconstructs the separately saved growing-dust inherited state to 2.55e−15.
- The native linear sources include physics the quadratic NR solver does not: finite pressure in continuity, radiation, baryonic stresses and relativistic corrections are not a derived second-order closure here. Missing initial wavelengths and unknown second-order dynamics are not contained in the numerical errors. The same classical labels in MTS need not end up in the same galaxy.
