# Palomar 5: tidal stretching, a mass-matched control and a finite-star test

**Result.** The saved fields can now drive changes in stellar binding energy without assigning a release time. In a controlled 111.16 Myr experiment, adding the frozen wave mass to the ordinary host takes initially circular outer stars through zero cluster-binding energy. But a **static spherical component with the same reference monopole produces the identical energy classifications, star for star**. This is evidence of sensitivity to the gravitational mass and the resulting orbit in this analog, not empirical evidence specific to wave flow. Reversal does change the calculated tidal field. The signed tensor is numerically resolved after repairing the ordinary host interpolation; the tiny differences of individual endpoint trajectories are not.

## What was held fixed

- Same 586→590→600 potentials, cluster sky position, 20.6 kpc distance, bulk PM and heliocentric velocity as the [previous stage](../mts-pal5-astrometric-tangent/RESULTS.md). The Milky-Way-like drive is prescribed, not measured for Pal 5. Compare host-only, both flow signs in reset and inherited preparations, their sign-mean potentials, and the host with each preparation's static spherical 590 wave monopole. The ordinary term is present in **all nine**. The last two kinds are controls; neither is a new solution of the parent wave dynamics. The static component is deliberately mass-matched, not an externally fitted dark halo. The star experiments condition on the same present cluster centre, so their historical centre paths can differ. Tensors were also compared on common paths.
- [Ibata et al. §V](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.06360v1) give a profile mass of 4297±98 solar masses conditional on a depleted low-mass stellar population. A Salpeter mass-function estimate is 16700 within their aperture, not an equally favored measurement, and their formal error omits mass-function uncertainty. [Gieles et al., Methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7617085/) infer a **projected half-number** radius 3.21±0.06 arcmin from the same underlying optical profile. At the retained 20.6 kpc this is **19.24 pc**. These constraints are independent of the wave fit but not of each other. We assume mass follows the light in a fixed Plummer profile, with this projected scale. Neither dark remnant segregation, old cluster mass, collisional relaxation nor a full distribution of stellar orbits was inferred. The Ibata profile's fitted cutoff is not an independent Jacobi radius.
- Each field starts with the same 72 **relative** stellar states around its own backcast centre, 24 orientations at each of 19.24, 38.47, 57.71 pc, initially circular in the isolated model. The velocities/axis choices do not represent a draw from a measured phase-space distribution. A probe feels the **exact finite difference** of the represented external force, plus the moving cluster's internal restoring force. No stars are removed, reinitialized, or given a stripping time. The isolated control agrees with analytic circular motion to 1.09e−5 or better in the maximum relative position error.
 
## What the tensor and force balance say

[Derivation and units](DERIVATION.md). The signed acceleration gradient is evaluated as −Hess Φ, not as enclosed mass divided by a spherical radius. Kepler, isotropic oscillator and Cartesian quadrupole controls pass. On each sampled source element, direct differentiation of the actual force verifies the expression to better than 4e−7 relative in the native runs. A finite star need not see exactly the linear field. The finite force, rather than the tangent approximation, drives the stellar orbits.

At the earliest sampled time the wave-bearing centre paths reach their smallest sampled Galactocentric distance (8.28–8.35 kpc for the signed runs); the pure ordinary path never gets inside 15.99 kpc in this window. **This is a window endpoint, not a measured pericentre.** The different centre orbits are part of the mass response. The nominal local rotating force-balance scale for the signed runs is then **15.1–15.5 pc**, inside the model projected radius of 19.24 pc; at the present epoch it is roughly **37 pc**. The ordinary-only scale bottoms out near **50.5 pc**. The higher-mass stellar scenario instead gives about 33–34 pc at the early signed endpoints. That structural uncertainty matters far more than which wave sign is used; those high-mass stellar orbits have not been replayed here.

Actual finite axial pulls give roots 15.10/15.14 pc on opposite sides for reset-forward at strongest sampled stretch, 15.48/15.51 pc for inherited-forward. All 54 two-grid axial comparisons agree at 0.5%. Nevertheless the remaining **perpendicular acceleration at these roots is 55–57% of the cluster binding acceleration**. Time-dependent frame rotation matters. An axial root is not an escape point or a rule for which star leaves.

## What actually happened to the model stars

Number out of 24 per starting shell in the native-host replay. “Positive” is energy relative to the cluster alone in its translating frame, not a conserved energy of the whole system.

| Field | 1 projected radius: ever / end positive | 2 radii | 3 radii |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Ordinary host only | 0 / 0 | 0 / 0 | 0 / 0 |
| Reset wave, either direction | 0 / 0 | 12 / 8 | 24 / 18 |
| Inherited wave, either direction | 0 / 0 | 12 / 8 | 24 / 18 |
| Either sign-mean or either static spherical control | 0 / 0 | 12 / 8 | 24 / 18 |

This held for every matched probe's ever-positive, positive-then-returned and final-positive labels, across all eight non-host-only models and all three resolutions. Four middle and six outer probes per such model went positive and later negative. The ordinary-only comparison alone would have given a misleading impression of specificity. Fractions here are neither a predicted release rate nor measured escape probabilities. Individual centre-relative endpoints can differ even when their labels agree; the high-precision sign residuals of those endpoints are not validated below.

## Numerical fault located and partially repaired

The derivative formula was correct for the original resampled host, but the tiny difference of two tides evaluated on their **different** centre paths was unreliable. Its coarse-to-fine history discrepancy was **31.17% reset / 32.04% inherited**. On a common forward path it was only 0.0110% / 0.0068%. Re-evaluating four combinations of fixed trajectories, fixed wave banks, and downsampled radii isolated almost the entire own-path discrepancy to radial interpolation of the **ordinary** term, concentrated near 8.4–8.6 kpc. It cancels at identical positions but not at displaced positions. Local error attribution (not bundled here).

Recovered the native element polynomials for the ordinary potential at all three source epochs. Their reconstruction reproduces the original saved fine potential/slope arrays at all checked radii exactly with the original evaluator, and to <7e−15 in relative norm with a separately implemented evaluator. The wave banks, time interpolation, geometry, integrators and 0.5% criterion were not changed. All nine dynamical experiments were rerun. Both stages, including their failures, remain on disk.

- With the native host, own-path signed tensor history discrepancies are now **0.0430% / 0.1267%** on field refinement and **0.0109% / 0.0323%** on the final orbit-step refinement. All 8784 pointwise checks of individual tensors and symmetric finite chords now pass the 0.5% criterion; 24 originally exceeded it. All 54 native finite-axis comparisons also pass.
- This does **not** fix all small differences. Of 270 history-level checks **23 failed originally and 24 fail natively**. The native failures are in small forward-minus-reverse or mean-minus-static endpoint **position, velocity or energy contrasts**, not in the individual histories, tensors or discrete stellar classifications. In particular the final step change in the signed endpoint **position vector** remains **5.53% reset / 3.18% inherited**. Those fine residuals cannot distinguish the flow or justify sub-parsec accuracy. [Side-by-side refinements](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `host-repair-comparison.csv`) · original failures (not bundled here) · native failures (not bundled here).
- The native ordinary potential is continuous to ~1.1e−11 (km/s)² across 28 bracketed centre crossings, but its one-sided radial accelerations differ by up to 0.0344% of the full force. This is a feature of the retained numerical representation, not evidence for a real thin mass shell. Each star may encounter an interface at a different substep. The inferred crossing-time first-variation scale at one projected radius is at most 0.000323 km/s on these sampled centre crossings. That motivates explicit crossing-time integration; it is not by itself proof that every residual comes from the faces.

## Next

Integrate star and centre crossings of the native element faces at their actual times rather than stepping across them. Alongside that calculation verify the finite-time work identity from the equations: change of cluster-only binding energy equals the integral of relative stellar velocity dotted with the external force difference. Compare ordinary, mass-matched static, and both signed fields with the same initial stars and stop conditions. This should distinguish genuine tidal work/return from errors in the small differences. Before interpreting the probe counts as a physical mass-loss history, vary the independently constrained stellar mass/profile and initial distribution and require a meaningful escape/recapture definition.
