# Which way does the saved wave move when the stars supply the gravity?

**Changing the stellar potential produces a favorable radial response in all four frozen waves. The current already in the wave determines whether the total discrepancy actually falls.** This is at fixed occupation, with the inherited weak-field coupling and no new parameter or rotation-curve fit.

Start the reset and inherited snapshot-590 states and their complex conjugates from identical densities on the original positive ordinary source and on each of the two published [Pouliasis stellar disks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.07979) (their halo excluded). The comparison within each pair changes only the ordinary potential. Measure the self-gravitating wave on the actual two [Cepheid](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.21780) bin populations, 130 and 138 stars, and seven [phase-spiral](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14030) groups out to 400 pc above the plane. These are fixed drives, exposed data, and measurement-conditioned locations, not a history of the Milky Way. The previous data gaps for these disks were 3.51–4.27 billion solar-mass equivalent. In the table, negative change reduces the radial discrepancy; the numbers are **force expressed as equivalent mass**, not mass counted inside a sphere.

The stellar-drive change alone removes **4.24–4.82 parts in 100 million** of the original discrepancy. Its closed-domain density curvature is a divergence, not mass creation. At launch, about 42% (I) or 51% (II) of the reset radial effect is nonspherical; an equivalent enclosed-mass calculation alone misses it. Moving to the stellar drive also makes the vertical potential rise larger. At each tested solar height the gain in radial agreement exceeds that differential vertical cost in the previous joint sphere-and-tide feasibility test. But the *total* joint discrepancy worsens for both forward waves and improves for both reverse waves over this window. The forward states are the directions favored in the saved formation experiments; the reverse is a control, not a license to choose the sign giving a better answer. No improvement in absolute observational fit of the forward states is claimed.

**Why the signs differ.** Initial density change comes from the divergence of the saved wave current; its sign reverses on complex conjugation. It predicts the reset and inherited forward radial rates of −1,573.50 and +873,572.95 solar-mass equivalent per Myr, independent of which stellar disk will begin accelerating them. The gravitational and kinetic stresses give the shared second-order change, even in sign. The current prediction reproduces the finite sign-odd part in all 40 readouts to 0.018% at 8,000 years. The total sign-even term agrees with the weak stress in all 40 at 1,000 and 2,000 years. At 8,000 years only 12/40 still agree with that *launch quadratic* within 0.5%, including all four radial mass-ordering comparisons. No long-time cure is inferred by projecting that quadratic forward.

**Independent check and a numerical failure caught.** A free Gaussian showed the original mesh's absolute kinetic acceleration to be wrong by a factor of about 761 on the radial mass diagnostic. We did not treat it as a physical instability. Derived the weak kinetic flux from the native polynomial amplitudes, verified Gaussian and anisotropic exact solutions, and checked the interface terms against a commutator that only uses first derivatives. Re-evolved the total motion on a smooth spatial mesh using the same wave and conserved host mass. All 80 stellar total changes pass temporal and spatial refinements there. For the smaller differential vertical signal in model II, 28 intermediate spatial comparisons missed 0.5%; all 40 final model-II readouts pass on another spatial refinement (worst 0.422%). The finite density-difference reconstruction and independent integrated-flux readout agree to better than 1e-9 on the recorded checks. A failed quadratic extrapolation is distinct from a failed integration refinement; both are retained. **Can observations select the preparation?** Tested the sign-even force difference on all twelve Cepheid bins and the seven local vertical groups. A constructive, nonnegative spherical profile on the weaker branch can mimic the difference down to **0.00889 of a quoted-error unit** in the worst datum. This does not repair the previously observed discrepancy common to the branches. We then derived an observable at equal spherical radius that *cannot* be imitated by any spherical distribution and evaluated it directly. At the seven local sites even its largest preparation contrast is **less than 0.00065 of one vertical error**. That tells us which local test to retire and which observable is worth seeking elsewhere. [Derivation, witness and limits](IDENTIFIABILITY.md).
