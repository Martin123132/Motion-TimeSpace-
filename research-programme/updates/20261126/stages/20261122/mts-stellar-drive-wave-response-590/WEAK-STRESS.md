# Which part of the acceleration is trustworthy?

The wave in this stage is the saved complex radial transform \(u=r\psi\), even in \(\mu=\cos\theta\). The Hamiltonian has real ordinary and self-gravity terms and the kinetic operator \(-\kappa L/2\) in frequency units, with \(Lu=u_{rr}+\partial_\mu[(1-\mu^2)u_\mu]/r^2\). Use Myr, kpc, and the same positive gravitational constant throughout. The cell integral per unit solid angle is \(|u|^2 dr\,d\mu\), not \(|u|^2r^2 dr\,d\mu\).

**Why the subtraction works.** Continuity gives \(\partial_t\rho=-\nabla\cdot j\). At an identical launch, the kinetic and self-gravity stresses of two runs cancel in their current acceleration. Changing the ordinary potential by \(\delta\Phi\) changes the acceleration by \(-C^2\nabla\delta\Phi\), so \(\delta\ddot\rho=C^2\nabla\cdot(\rho\nabla\delta\Phi)\), \(C\) in kpc/Myr per km/s. A constant added to either potential changes no density. The response integrates to zero for closed boundaries: an enhanced local field is redistribution and has a nonspherical component. The initial density curvature is even under complex conjugation; the initial density rate is odd. At finite times both sources continue to feel their own wave gravity. The total wave motion and the difference between the motions are not interchangeable.

**Why the absolute stress required another method.** Twice differentiating continuity on the old nonuniform mesh produced plausible-looking but grossly wrong accelerations even for a freely spreading Gaussian. Supplying its exact kinetic generator, without changing the gravity readout, largely removes the discrepancy. Changing flux faces alone does not pass the Gaussian radial test. These controls are retained. On the replacement numerical mesh the radius spacing is constant across all stellar sites, then stretches smoothly outside 24 kpc; the box boundary is unchanged. The original ordinary mass cells transfer by volume overlap, conserving their integral, without renormalizing the wave. These are numerical refinements, not a new physical bank.

**Weak kinetic readout from the saved elements.** For Legendre order \(\ell\), project
\[
 F_\ell={\kappa^2\over2}\,{\cal P}_\ell\Re(u^*\partial_rLu-(Lu)^*u_r).
\]
Let \(H_\ell\) be the analogous angular current paired with \((1-\mu^2)P'_\ell/r^2\), including the same projection normalization. Then \(n_\ell''=-\partial_r F_\ell+H_\ell\). Pair this with the interior/exterior powers of the isolated Newton Green function; integration by parts uses only up to three regular element derivatives. Keep the opposing flux terms at the actual observation radius for the radial force. At each native interface retain \(w'[P]-w''p+w h\), where \(P=\kappa^2\Re(u^*Lu-|u_r|^2)/2\) is projected, \(p=\kappa^2{\cal P}_\ell\Re(u^*[u_r])/2\), and \(h\) is the angular projection formed from the slope jump. The saved amplitude is continuous but its slopes need not be. No probe is placed on an interface. Endpoint currents are closed, and the far gravitational field is isolated, not periodic.

**Independent sign and surface checks.** Free isotropic and non-spherical Gaussian packets have analytic density acceleration and Newton potential. The latter uses a quadrupolar solid harmonic and independently includes the generated density monopole, quadrupole and hexadecapole. An additional test uses a smooth, compact radial window \(w\). Angular integration gives separate radial modes \(u_\ell\), weighted by \(1/(2\ell+1)\). For a free mode the twice-differentiated expectation is
\[
 \kappa^2\int\left[w''|u_\ell'|^2
 -{1\over4}w''''|u_\ell|^2
 +{\ell(\ell+1)\over r^3}w'|u_\ell|^2\right]dr .
\]
This follows by commuting the radial kinetic operator twice with multiplication by \(w\), including the centrifugal potential. It only needs first wave derivatives and does not insert interface jumps. Compare it directly with the bulk-current integral plus interfaces. A manufactured continuous element bump makes slope jumps large enough to falsify omission of the interface terms; it is never used in the physical prediction. The narrow 1.36-kpc unmodified window is poorly conditioned (absolute integral much larger than signed integral) and remains flagged. The 4-kpc window checks the original waves and the manufactured bumps. These are checks on finite projected observables, not claims of pointwise fourth derivatives at native element faces.
