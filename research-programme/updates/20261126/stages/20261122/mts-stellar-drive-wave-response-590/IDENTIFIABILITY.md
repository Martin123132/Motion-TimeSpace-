# What ordinary matter can imitate, and one measurement it cannot

**Question.** At the same occupation, can the *difference* of the reset and inherited fields be distinguished, even if each is individually a large contributor to total gravity? This is a forecast on already exposed survey sites, not a fit to a held-out stream, not a new mass component for MTS and not evidence that the constructed sphere exists.

The plane force at each star is \(v^2=R\,\partial_R\Phi\); the off-plane measurement is \(\Phi(R,0.4)-\Phi(R,0)\). Both are linear in the source and both fixed signs share a preparation's initial density. In these units any common foreground cancels from the preparation contrast. Use all twelve nominal [Feng et al. Cepheid bins](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.21780), not just the two bins used in the dynamical test, and the seven plotted [Widmark et al. phase-spiral groups](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14030). The Cepheid comparison unit \(2v_i\sigma_i\) is the half-width of the published speed interval after squaring. It is not a covariance for squared speeds. No joint likelihood, extra Gaia zero point, or no-systematics assumption is needed for the deterministic bounds below. Only the nominal release contains these exact per-star bin weights.

For a positive sphere the effective mass in bin \(i\) is an average of \(M(<R)\) weighted by \(1/R\); disjoint ordered bins therefore have nondecreasing effective mass. For reset-minus-inherited write \(m_i=d_i\,\Delta v_i^2/G\), \(e_i=d_i\,2v_i\sigma_i/G\), \(d_i=1/\mathrm{mean}_i(1/R)\). To mimic the shape to within \(b\) comparison units, intervals \([m_i-be_i,m_i+be_i]\) must admit nonnegative nondecreasing choices. The exact minimum box size is
\[
 b=\max\{0,\max_i(-m_i/e_i),\ \max_{i<j}(m_i-m_j)/(e_i+e_j)\}.
\]
Necessity follows by pairwise ordering. Sufficiency: take the running maximum of zero and all lower endpoints. Put its nonnegative increments in shells in the actual gaps between samples, with the first below the innermost star. This constructs the desired force at every sampled radius without treating a disk as spherical. The shell positions are an existence witness, not an inferred matter distribution. Use the same shells in the vertical rise, \(G\sum_a m_a(1/\max(R,a)-1/\max(\sqrt{R^2+h^2},a))\). No further fit and no exterior tide.

**Result.** Without any nuisance mass the largest radial contrast is 0.558 squared-speed comparison units. The witness costs \(7.696\times10^8\,M_\odot\); the unavoidable remainder is only **0.00889** units, set by bins 9 and 12. At all three solar heights the seven vertical residuals of the *same* witness are smaller than that remainder. The radial lower bound is therefore also an attained joint optimum for these comparison units. This is an upper bound on distinguishability if that spherical freedom is admitted, not a universal permission to add a halo. If external data disallow that mass profile, the witness need not be available. A shared physical source and permission for the two hypotheses to use *different* uncertain foregrounds are distinctly different assumptions.

**A spherical-null observable that could avoid that degeneracy.** Compare points at exactly the same spherical radius, not the same cylindrical radius:
\[
 A(R,h)=\Phi(R,h)-\Phi(\sqrt{R^2+h^2},0)
 =[\Phi(R,h)-\Phi(R,0)]-\int_R^{\sqrt{R^2+h^2}}v_c^2(s,0)\,ds/s .
\]
Every spherical density, positive or otherwise, drops out exactly. On the constant-radius arc \(A=\int (R\partial_z\Phi-z\partial_R\Phi)\,d\alpha\). We checked both integrals against direct endpoint gravity through all seven sites, from the original globally supported fields at two quadrature orders and for both signs. A Plummer control vanishes; each stellar disk gives a nonzero response. Correct dimensions include the table's mass unit \(2.32\times10^7 M_\odot\); the independent integral check rejects omitting it.

At \(R=8.178\) kpc and \(h=0.4\) kpc, \(A_{\rm reset}-A_{\rm inherited}=0.0041553\,(\mathrm{km/s})^2\). The difference between the two fixed stellar models is **24.93** in the same units. Across the seven sites the preparation signal is at most **0.000648 of one vertical measurement error**, *even treating the plane integral and foreground as perfectly known*. This is a comparison to the published one-component uncertainty, not the uncertainty of an actually measured combined observable. In particular the nearby Cepheids do not provide a resolved independent rotation curve across each roughly ten-parsec integration segment. The null removes spherical nuisances, not stellar flattening. Do not choose a flow sign or a preparation from these measurements.
