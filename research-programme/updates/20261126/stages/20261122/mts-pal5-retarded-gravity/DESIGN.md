# Preregistered finite-time response to changing the ordinary source

Use the existing nonrelativistic weak-field equations, positive baryonic source and wave occupation with both initial preparations, both directions, and both actual release environments. No velocity data, measured sign, fitting or new clocks. Predict the gravitational response at 1,024–2,048 years from the native launch and the external potential *difference alone*. Do not use the finite-amplitude corrected history to drive the predictor. Compare it only after saving predictions.

Derive first and second derivatives of the discrete coupled Schrödinger–Poisson dynamics with respect to the prescribed change in ordinary potential. This Taylor expansion is in **drive amplitude at fixed time**, not a short-time series. Test native reference trajectory, conservation and null gauges, independent finite-amplitude scaling on a manufactured grid, numerical time and spatial refinement, the unit-amplitude finite evolution, the two tail velocities, and their much smaller residual relative to the inadequate local quartic time law. Keep absolute and relative error distinct. Keep 0.005 as the relative scientific gate; a small absolute remainder does not earn a measured coefficient without resolving it. Also gate the drive off at 1,024 years and evolve its response freely afterwards, retaining induced gravity: what fraction of the 2,048-year fixed-site tidal force comes from the early versus later drive? It is a controlled memory question, not a physical claim that the baryonic potential switched discontinuously.

Default production: level 2, 32 yr, all own and crossed cases; independent 16 yr from launch and third mesh at 32 yr for both own preparations/signs. Sample star-centre differential acceleration at fixed positions and along common ballistic paths, then compare their integrals with the separate saved fully evolved stellar impulse. The distinction between the finite response and the direction measurement stays intact.

The reference is an earlier numerical experiment whose broad behaviour was already known. Saving the new source-only prediction before scoring prevents leakage of the corrected evolution into its inputs; it does not make the experiment a blind observational test. For the fixed-site difference between directions, also compare the 32-year predictor with both finer saved nonlinear references, rather than judging two large individual measurements alone.
