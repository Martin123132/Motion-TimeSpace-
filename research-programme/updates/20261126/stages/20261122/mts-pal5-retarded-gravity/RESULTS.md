# Changing ordinary gravity leaves a calculable wave response

**A nearly equal present tidal pull can hide quite different velocity histories.** In the predeclared two-history experiment, applying one third of the source correction early versus all of it late left only **0.20–0.36%** of the continuous-drive wave tide as a difference at 2,048 years. Yet the difference in accumulated star–cluster velocity was **16.51–16.62%** of the continuous-drive impulse (fixed and moving paths together). The fractions are relative to the wave's response to the change of ordinary potential, not to the Milky Way's total acceleration. The moving-star velocity difference is only **1.78–3.79 × 10⁻¹⁶ km/s**. This is a useful prediction and a numerical test, not an observable selection of a Galactic flow.

**Why it works.** The ordinary potential first changes the wave's mass current, which then redistributes its density. The wave's own Newtonian potential responds to that redistribution. Velocity integrates the resulting force once more. This gives different time weightings to present force and velocity, derived from the retained equations with fixed occupation and the existing gravitational coupling, without fitting a memory coefficient. Here “history” means evolution of the matter state, not a delayed Poisson law. [Equations and conservative derivatives](DERIVATION.md).

## Source-only prediction versus direct finite evolution

- The independently generated force and impulse vectors use only the native initial wave and change in the ordinary potential. Against the archived constantly driven nonlinear runs, all **640/640** first-order vectors pass the unchanged 0.5% comparison. The maximum difference at the same discretization is 1.77×10⁻¹⁰ of the response. The meaningful numerical precision is set by independent refinement, not that small same-grid number: max 0.00056% under halving the time step and 0.0135% under spatial refinement for the total first-order response. All 160 moving-path impulse comparisons with fully propagated stars pass (max 0.00306%). [Force and impulse](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `response-comparisons.csv`) · refinements (not bundled here).
- Crossed both signs at each actual site on the second mesh. With the site and launch held fixed, the predicted forward-minus-reverse wave-only impulse agrees with the independent finite stellar impulse on the base, finer time and finer spatial grids in **120/120** comparisons (max 0.0116%). This is a contrast of the correction to the wave force, not a prediction from the full observational catalogue. [Signed projections](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `fixed-site-flow-contrast.csv`).
- Removing the external correction after 1,024 years left the extra wave pull at fixed release positions **2.96–2.98 times larger** 1,024 years later than at switch-off. It retained **74.74–74.85% of the continuous final force** and **87.41–87.47% of the continuous impulse**. Both wave preparations and both own-site signs give this behaviour. A fresh nonlinear pulse calculation agrees with the saved prediction in all 32 force/impulse comparisons. In these experiments the ordinary sources end equal, but the waves have different mass currents. Pulse replay (not bundled here) · before/after switch-off (not bundled here).
- The compensated histories offer a less forgiving check. The leading delay law predicts **zero** force difference and **one sixth** of the uninterrupted impulse; neither is exactly true. All 16 forces fall inside the original 0.5%-of-full-scale null band. Only 8/16 impulses are within 0.5% *of the one-sixth prediction*. The full retarded evolution, frozen before either of the two finite histories was run, passes all 32 comparisons against the actual small vector differences. In the two finest-spatial comparisons of the **small remaining force**, the inherited/forward inner tail misses the relative gate (0.586% fixed, 0.555% moving). This amounts to at most 0.00209% of continuous forcing and does not reverse the null-band conclusion. [Frozen experiment](COMPENSATED.md) · direct comparisons (not bundled here) · refinement on the small denominator (not bundled here).

## Where the numerical evidence stops

The second-order density, potential and force terms were derived as well. Manufactured central differences resolve both coefficients; constant forcing offsets are exact nulls and independent early and late drives superpose at first order. The finite density and potential patch remainders agree with the second-order coefficient in 28/28 cases. But after taking gradients and differencing star and centre the quadratic correction is only up to 1.77×10⁻¹⁰ of the first-order response. Only 362/640 of these tiny force/impulse remainders agree within 0.5% of the **remainder itself**, and only 133/160 second-order vectors pass physical mesh refinement. The total response is resolved; not every tiny nonlinear coefficient is. Patch checks (not bundled here) · [manufactured controls](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `controls.csv`). Same-equation numerical tests are not independent astronomical observations. Both representations of the source and the inherited occupation remain conditional; no externally fitted amplitudes or stripping dates were introduced.

**Decision:** retain the causal response in this tested weak-field interval instead of extrapolating a polynomial in time; do not invest in smaller Pal 5 numerical residuals as a way to select its sign. An observational test needs a larger projected signal and independently constrained escape/formation history. For the parent gravity programme, return to the separate saved radiation-era cold/heavy comparison and calculate the sourced collisionless-neutrino stress on the same metric, keeping any independent incoming perturbation separate. Nothing from the stellar test supplies that state. [Reproduction guide](REPLAY.md).
