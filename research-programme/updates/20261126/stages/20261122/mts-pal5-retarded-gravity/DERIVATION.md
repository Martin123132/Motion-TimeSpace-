# A causal source response at fixed wave population

Start with the retained nonrelativistic, positive quadratic heavy-bath branch in the common metric. This is a calculation in its Newtonian limit with the already calibrated G, not a derivation of the numerical value of G. The static Schrödinger–Newton linearisation is established mathematics, e.g. [Harrison, Moroz and Tod](https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/0208045) study spherical stationary states. No stationary eigenvalues, ground-state stability result, or cosmological population prior from that setting is used here. The calculation below differentiates the time-dependent driven, axisymmetric initial-value problem already saved for the two MTS preparations.

In the retained radial finite-volume variable u=rψ let P map cell-coordinate density to potential: P[n]=Poisson(4πG Δr n), with the native regular/isolated boundaries and angular weights. With κ in kpc²/Myr and C the kpc/Myr per km/s unit conversion, let g=C²/κ. Put Uλ=U₀+λD where U₀ is the native ordinary gravitational potential and D is the difference between the two saved descriptions of the ordinary source. λ is a bookkeeping variable, **not** a fitted coupling: the actual endpoints are λ=0 and λ=1. The common unperturbed Hamiltonian H=K+g(U₀+P[|u|²]) uses the original wave at every time. Subtract common spatially uniform potential phases in every channel in the numerical gauge. Write uλ=u+λa+λ²b+… with a(0)=b(0)=0. Direct substitution gives

    n₁ = 2 Re(u* a),                   n₂ = 2 Re(u* b) + |a|²,
    i a' = H a + g (D + P[n₁]) u,
    i b' = H b + g (D + P[n₁]) a + g P[n₂] u.

Thus the retarded propagator on (Re a, Im a) includes the real-linear feedback P[2 Re(u* a)]u. If L(t) is that homogeneous evolution generator and T(t,s) its evolution operator, then a(t) = ∫₀ᵗ T(t,s)[−i g D(s)u(s)] ds in realified notation. The stellar observable is the star-minus-centre gradient of P[n₁]; it is not D, and does not permit replacing the prepared wave population by a global cosmological fraction. There is no response from a future source. The native background is itself an evolving solution; a time-translation-invariant convolution or adjustable relaxation time is not assumed.

## Conservative realisation and checks

“Retarded” here describes the history dependence of the **matter response**. The Newtonian Poisson constraint is solved on each time slice. This is not a finite-speed relativistic metric propagator, propagation of a new force, or proof of a gravitational delay time.

For a potential half-step with γ=−i g Δt/2, let V=U₀+P[n₀], E=D+P[n₁], F=P[n₂] (gauge-subtracted). The updated fields after dividing by exp(γV) are u, a+γEu, and b+γEa+(γF+γ²E²/2)u. For every unitary radial or angular Crank–Nicolson substep use its midpoint fields m, A, B. First-order flux on link (i,j) is proportional to Im(conj(mᵢ)Aⱼ+conj(Aᵢ)mⱼ); second-order flux is proportional to Im(conj(mᵢ)Bⱼ+conj(Bᵢ)mⱼ+conj(Aᵢ)Aⱼ). With the original face conductances, the signed face transfers cancel exactly in the weighted total. Both variations conserve total occupation for identical initial population: they redistribute mass, not create it. Evaluating the literal density identities independently checks the cell-current accumulation. Subtracting two raw background densities is unnecessary.

The early-drive experiment uses the same linear response with D switched off after 1,024 years but P[n₁] kept on. Both kicks belonging to an interval use that interval's switch value, so the switch at its endpoint is integrated correctly. Full minus early gives the late-drive response by linear superposition, which is tested independently on a manufactured mesh by evolving the late drive alone. A constant-in-space forcing gives exactly zero response, and decreasing a symmetric finite-amplitude drive checks *both* Taylor coefficients against independently evolved nonlinear states. No earlier/later split is assumed linear at finite amplitude; that is why we also compare λ=1 to the saved nonlinear evolution.

## What the memory fraction means

At release, fixed occupation and phase give δρ=δj=0. Euler's equation for the perturbed mass current contains ρ δa_ext; continuity then gives δρ''(0)=−∇·(ρ δa_ext). Holding this leading spatial operator fixed for an envelope e(s) gives δρ(t) ∝ ∫₀ᵗ (t−s)e(s)ds. When e vanishes, the already induced excess mass current need not vanish. The density and hence its Newtonian force can therefore continue to change. This is ordinary transport of wave mass with zero net added occupation, not a phenomenological postulate about spacetime. For two positive source-interpolation envelopes (early at one third versus late at unit amplitude), this leading moment predicts cancellation in the final wave force but not its time integral. The fully evolved difference is calculated independently in the [compensated experiment](COMPENSATED.md).

For full vector acceleration change a_f and the early-drive contribution a_e, quote (a_e·a_f)/|a_f|² and the transverse component separately. This is a signed projection, not a fractional wave population or probability. As a controlled short-time reference only, for a constant drive and kernel proportional to delay, the first half of the drive contributes 3/4 of the final force and 7/8 of the final integrated impulse. Those numbers follow from integrating t−s and (t−s)² respectively; they are not inserted in the wave solver. For the pulse comparison we integrate at native grid times with composite Simpson quadrature, placing the switch on an even node, so no substep interpolation reaches across the switch. Final checks against fully propagated stars and against a denser quadrature of the uninterrupted channel keep time interpolation separate from physical nonlinearity.
