# Distinguishing a present force from its accumulated impulse

Prospective extension before running either new finite history: use two amplitudes in [0,1] between the existing represented host potentials, not a negative mass or a fitted coupling. History A applies one-third of the ordinary correction in the first 1,024 years and zero thereafter. History B applies zero during that first half and the full correction in the second half. Report only the difference in **induced wave** tide or velocity; these are imposed source histories, not observed gas migrations. Potentials remain prescribed from the same source bank. No parameter is tuned to a star or a finite result.

For a causal kernel proportional to delay, early/late fractions of the all-on final force are 3/4 and 1/4. Thus A−B cancels at 2,048 years, but its integrated impulse is (7/8)/3−1/8 = 1/6 of the all-on impulse. This supplies a nontrivial predetermined ratio for testing whether present gravitational force and accumulated velocity contain different historical information. Exact linear predictions from the saved wave equations are (4/3)early−all. Freeze vectors for both preparations, both sites and both tails first; then directly evolve the two nonlinear histories separately with their own induced gravity. Do not drive the finite evolution with these predictions. Report size relative to continuous forcing (null criterion: ≤0.005 relative to the continuous force), error against the actual smaller A−B vector, time and mesh checks and departure from 1/6. A near cancellation is not automatically an exact zero.
