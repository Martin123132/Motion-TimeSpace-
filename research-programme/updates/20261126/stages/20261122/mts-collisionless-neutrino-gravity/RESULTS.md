# How much do free-streaming neutrinos change the retained gravitational potentials?

Adding the collisionless neutrino source *during the saved interval*, with the incoming state held fixed, shifts the final lapse or scalar Weyl potential by at most **0.13789% on the cold background and 0.00843% on the heavy background**. Giving the same neutrinos a finite preceding history, carrying their density, current and angular stress across the interface and matching both Einstein constraints increases those maxima to **0.27534% and 0.01612%**. The effects refer to nonzero complex final perturbations, not the total field, statistical errors, evidence for a cosmology, or uncertainty over all possible incoming states. No observed quantities were fitted.

| Largest complex endpoint difference across five frozen output modes and two observables, relative to the previous photon-complete reference | Cold | Heavy |
|---|---:|---:|
| In-window neutrino forcing only, original compensated launch | 0.13788% | 0.00843% |
| Add 16 earlier cells, fixed non-neutrino normal energy/flux at join | 0.20336% | 0.01185% |
| Same 16 cells, fixed non-neutrino coordinate energy/flux instead | 0.20338% | 0.01185% |
| Add 32 earlier cells, fixed normal energy/flux | 0.27517% | 0.01610% |
| Same 32 cells, fixed coordinate energy/flux | 0.27534% | 0.01612% |

The 32-cell coordinate-matched maxima for lapse and Weyl separately are **0.27534% and 0.24671%** cold, **0.01612% and 0.00816%** heavy. On the designated primary mode (29,1,1) they are 0.01401% and 0.00839% cold, 0.00484% and 0.00250% heavy. The largest response is on the longer-wave (4,2,1) probe, not the primary. None crosses the previously fixed 0.5% endpoint gate. This is an interval test, not a universal limit. Separately subtracting the two complex 32-cell solutions rather than their magnitudes gives a maximum matching-rule difference of 0.00088% cold or 0.000032% heavy. For these windows the length of the supplied prehistory matters more than this choice of equal surrounding density.

## What was derived and checked
- From the metric geodesic and collisionless distribution equations, derive the scalar quadratic propagation, deflection and momentum-redshift source, including the massive FD shape derivative. Remove exactly the instantaneous boosted quadrupole already present in the parent stress. The independent direct angular integral (not bundled here) and bulk-stress check (not bundled here) test projection and normalization, not just timestep convergence. The previous numerical gravity histories are reproduced exactly when the new source is turned off.
- There is a real input approximation: 740/782 incident wavelengths obey the native short fluid closure throughout the sampled interval. Its archived massless quadrupole differs from the reconstruction by about 7% across the panel; on samples *not* obeying that closure the relative difference is below 2.1e-6. A native hierarchy comparison on 22 sentinel wavelengths checks multipoles and massive thermally integrated density/flux/shear independently. Replacing just the reconstructed massless moments 0–2 by the legacy values changes an endpoint by at most 0.00108% cold or 0.00032% heavy; also replacing the third by the closure-equivalent value gives maxima 0.00377% and 0.00041%. Those are controlled source substitutions on the held native metric, not a wholesale recalculation with a new linear cosmology.
- Independently integrating the phase-space driving terms and expanding covariant energy/momentum conservation agree to 2.2e-15 in relative window norm for massless neutrinos; at 18 thermal nodes both equations at every probe agree to better than 4.9e-6 for massive neutrinos. The error is larger at 12 nodes; deleting an actual geometry term worsens the agreement. These are comparisons of quadratic sources, not conservation inferred from a small final change. See [derivation](DERIVATION.md), [cold](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `stress-balance-cold.csv`) and [heavy](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `stress-balance-heavy.csv`).
- The main integration covers conformal time 87.62054–89.12579 Mpc (c=1); the two earlier windows reach 0.90855 and 1.81709 conformal Mpc before its start. They start with the instantaneous bulk quadrupole and zero other second-order normal moments; their driving comes from the sampled first-order simulated metric and reconstructed incident linear radiation, not a tuned incoming second-order amplitude. Incoming orders 3 and higher have no initial density, momentum, or shear, but do feed a later shear: up to 0.08595% cold / 0.00800% heavy on the longer window relative to the in-window solution. Their contribution cannot just be inferred from the initial quadrupole.
- First test the arriving angular stress only. Then [derive the initial density and momentum match](INITIAL-STATE-MATCH.md), include all the arriving moments, and compare propagation by the trace equation versus the conserved energy/current equations. All 48 actual initial-constraint checks pass; the two full propagations differ at the endpoint by at most 0.0000020% relative to the in-window reference. Time, angular and momentum refinements, and the numerical join to the retained source, are separate from dependence on the choice of preparation.
