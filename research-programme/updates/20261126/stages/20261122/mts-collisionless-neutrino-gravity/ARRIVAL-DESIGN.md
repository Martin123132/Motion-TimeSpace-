# Finite prehistory: what reaches the matched initial surface?
Before opening its gravity endpoints, fix two incident windows: the last 16 and 32 native prehistory cells before the primary matched launch, on each retained metric. Use exactly the same Fourier products and thermal/angle convention as in the main window. Initialise the wave-generated part at the earlier surface with the retained instantaneous boosted quadrupole and zero other second-order normal moments. Advance it with first-order squared geodesic forcing and free flight. This constructs a particular contribution from a finite measured-metric history, not primordial adiabatic second-order data. In particular, independent preceding second-order metric forcing, vector/tensor modes and earlier incoming radiation are not inferred.

At the junction subtract only the existing matched quadrupole; do not add its bulk twice. Save the full incident distribution, but keep the main compensated density, momentum, photon and matter launch intact: propagate three separately labeled changes, the incoming quadrupole, angular orders 3 and above (invisible to initial density, flux and shear), and both. The difference between the 16- and 32-cell preparations is a physical window test, not time integration error. Check both models, the same five probes, response linearity, one further prehistory refinement and the original 0.5% endpoint criterion. Never treat a failed endpoint as a reason to rescale the incident distribution.
