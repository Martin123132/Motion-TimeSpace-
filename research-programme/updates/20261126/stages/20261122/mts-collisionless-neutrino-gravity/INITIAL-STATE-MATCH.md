# Including the incoming neutrino density and current without silently compensating them

Keep the scalar potential convention of the parent curvature equations (not bundled here). Let \(e=8\pi Ga^2\rho,\ j=8\pi Ga^2i\hat k\cdot{\bf J}\), where \({\bf J}\) is the physical normal energy flux; \(F\) is the expansion variable of the parent. A delta denotes the difference between alternative second-order states at the same first-order metric and time. Products of first-order terms cancel from every **difference** below. From the actual arriving monopole and dipole calculate the massless \(\delta e_\nu=4e_r\Theta_0,\ \delta j_\nu=4e_r\Theta_1\), and add the thermal integrals \(8\pi Ga^2\int Wq^2\epsilon\Theta_0\) and \(8\pi Ga^2\int Wq^3\Theta_1\), respectively, with the original distribution normalization and conversion to physical density. The phases are complex and retained separately on each probe. The angular experiment withheld these two moments. A new matched launch uses them, not a normalisation fitted to the gravitational field.

The **Hamiltonian** and **momentum** constraints give
\[
 \delta e_{\rm tot}=2(-k^2\delta\phi+3{\cal H}\delta F), \qquad
 k\,\delta j_{\rm tot}=-2 k^2\delta F.
\]
There is no background flux, so holding non-neutrino flux fixed in either convention gives \(\delta F=-\delta j_\nu/(2k)\). Momentum matching also fixes \(\delta Y=k\,\delta j_\nu/e_\gamma\). The density constraint depends on which scalar is initially fixed:
* **Normal**: \(\delta e_{\rm other}=0\), so \(k^2\delta\phi=3{\cal H}\delta F-\delta e_\nu/2\).
* **Coordinate**: \(\delta(e^{4B}e_{\rm other})=0\) as a perturbation difference, so \(\delta e_{\rm other}=4e_{{\rm other},0}\delta\phi\). The denominator becomes \(k^2+2e_{{\rm other},0}\), with the same numerator. The non-neutrino background here is total minus both relativistic and thermal neutrinos, not just the scalar bath.

In both cases use \(\delta X=(\delta e_{\rm tot}-4e_0\delta\phi)/e_\gamma\). This is the whole change in the conserved launch, not an extra force during the future evolution. The trace propagator receives the implied \(\phi,F\); the conserved propagator receives the corresponding initial \(X,Y\). Feed the same physical moments, including the massive trace and the bulk-subtracted shear, to both propagators. Keeping the old total \(X=Y=0\) *and* inserting arbitrary new normal neutrino energy/current would impose a compensating change in the surrounding material; it is not equivalent to either condition above. These are aggregate matching conditions, not per-species preparation of the non-neutrino sector. The two proposed surfaces agree when the incoming source vanishes.

Checks in the two matching-constraints tables reconstruct \(\delta e,\delta j\) from the actual initialized metric, along with its coordinate density/flux, rather than accepting the constructor's parameters as its output. Finite late-time checks compare trace propagation against energy/momentum propagation, higher angular order with half-step against the primary, and the independent sum of the incoming low and angular responses. The signed difference of the two possible joins, not their sum, measures interface dependence. Neither join prescribes a primordial state.
