# Two prepared waves face the same vertical and radial gravity measurements

**The vertical data do not select a preparation. The radial data identify a common failure of the frozen mass profiles.** Near the Sun the two waves differ in their predicted circular speeds by at most 0.55 km/s, but both are about 28–29 km/s too slow at 8.19 kpc and rise across a radial interval where the published central speeds fall. No stripping-time choice, stream tilt or choice of forward versus reverse is involved. There was no fit to these measurements.

## What the stars measure vertically

[Widmark et al., Gaia EDR3 phase spirals](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.14030) infer a potential rise of **301.8 ± 6.0 (km/s)²** between the plane and 400 pc in their joint local analysis. Their **265.0 ± 16.0** foreground includes baryons *and the radial correction from the total rotation curve*. These are not two independent direct force measurements. Conditional on those inputs the excess available for the density of another component is 36.8; the standard error would be 17.1 if uncorrelated (10 to 22 over all possible correlations). This tracer is separate from the Pal 5 stars; earlier project exposure and cross-survey overlaps have not been ruled out.

The two comparisons answer different questions. Adding 30.20 to 265 would double-count the wave's radial curvature. The reset wave has a density contribution of 66.74 but subtracts 36.54 through the radial divergence of its own gravitational field; inherited subtracts 36.31. Against the source-conditioned rise, the two predictions differ by **0.495**, only **0.029 of the combined error** at the nominal radius. The wave totals are high by about 1.7–1.8 such errors at this radius, not a decisive exclusion.

The paper combined seven *radial groups*, not seven independent measurements of 301.8. We evaluated all seven centres, not just the nominal radius. For reset, the empirical-source prediction ranges from 328.66 to 334.88; the direct positive-host total ranges from 268.58 to 320.62. We did not invent the paper's per-subsample weights or attach the aggregate ±6 separately to each radius. The direct model has a much stronger trend with radius. With the published total rotation correction and either wave, the implied ordinary contribution to the height-weighted radial divergence is about −43 (km/s)² at the centre; the actual positive-source model instead gives +5.26. The former uses the paper's near-plane radial approximation, the latter the full modelled height integral. The whole-annulus radial curvature, i.e. the difference in v² divided by (R_out²−R_in²)/2, is **−64.96** in the measured central values (opposing quoted endpoints: −137.76 to −10.96) versus **+151.84** for ordinary alone and **+624.62/+621.56** with the waves, in (km/s)²/kpc². This is a finite annular force comparison, not a local numerical derivative. The raw between-wave separation over four bins is 1.68 purely statistical error units *if* the bins are independent, but allowing one common 0.265% relative velocity scale leaves **0.0072**. Neither preparation explains the absolute curve. The method is a disk-tracer Jeans inference with shared systematics; some astrometry and Galactic assumptions overlap other surveys. Do not combine this diagnostic with the vertical measurement as independent likelihoods.

**A constraint on what can fix the shape.** A positive spherical addition must have nondecreasing enclosed mass. In the reset case it would need 29.55 billion solar masses inside 7.28 kpc yet only 19.39 billion inside 8.71 kpc (inherited 29.89 versus 19.86 billion). Even giving each bin its most favourable quoted error leaves a contradiction of at least **9.03/8.90 billion solar masses**. This excludes *every* purely additive nonnegative spherical profile under the fixed host, wave shape and quoted intervals, not only a particular halo law. Ordinary gravity without these waves **can** be completed by a positive spherical component on the same four bins. For example, that control implies a total vertical rise of 305.46–306.75 at the nominal radius using the quoted radial boxes. This is an existence/sensitivity control, not an independently predicted halo.

If only the amplitude of this particular frozen wave density is varied in the **readout**, the maximum fraction compatible with these quoted bins and any positive spherical addition is **0.426 (reset), 0.429 (inherited)**. With the central speeds fixed exactly it is 0.250/0.252. The theory does not have permission to turn this diagnostic into a coupling fit. Changing the occupation dynamically would also change the wave shape. A positive nonspherical remedy is not subject to the spherical mass test, but it must supply at least **9.77/9.62 × 10⁻¹² m/s²** of departure from its own solid-angle-mean inward force at one of the two endpoints, for the stated boxes. That is a geometrical lower bound, *not* a requirement for negative physical mass. **Systematics matter.** The spherical contradiction can disappear if radial bins can shift in opposite directions by an additional **2.26%/2.22%** each beyond the quoted errors. A single common velocity rescaling within ±5% cannot do this. The paper reports total systematic estimates of roughly **2–4%** over its inner range; that does not tell us whether the systematics can vary by the required amount *between these particular nearby bins*. The quoted-box contradiction is established; exclusion after the actual spatial covariance is **not**. The two direct ordinary Poisson readouts give essentially the same nominal radius result; the alternative native representation is retained separately. Real baryonic mass, geometry and a simulated formation history are not fixed by the survey.

## What the saved evolution actually did

Between snapshots 586 and 590 (88.97 Myr), the reconstructed forward reset/inherited states lost about **3.22/6.21 million solar masses** from this shell. Their angular force changes offset some or all of the monopole effect; the changes in the equatorial equivalent-mass difference are at the million, not the billion, solar-mass scale. The reverse histories are counterfactual and were not used to attribute observed Galactic formation. These are realized motions under one saved drive, not upper bounds on a different state or a formation timescale.
