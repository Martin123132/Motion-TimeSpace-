# How the retained wave gravitates

**Scope.** Classical, minimally coupled positive quadratic real scalar, spatially flat FLRW, specified common adiabatic phases. The Einstein term, normalization of its measured G, cosmological background, matter content, seed and scalar mass are inputs from the retained branch. This derives how its Hilbert stress enters those equations, not G, the matter sector, or a Milky Way population from first principles. All quadratic maps here are the cross products between the original long and short sets of modes; not the entire perturbation or a late stream realization. Norms use Hermitian real-field weights. Scalar k=0 is removed from all inverse Laplacians, not assigned a direction.

## Einstein constraints before the slow-wave approximation

Use conformal time, metric ds²=a²[−exp(2A)dη²+exp(2B)δij dx^i dx^j], exp(2A)=1+2Ψ, exp(2B)=1−2Φ; write Φ=εΦ₁+ε²Φ₂, likewise Ψ (no factorial). K^i_j=−F δ^i_j/a with F=exp(−A)(ℋ+B'). Scalar-project with Pij=(3/2)(k_i k_j/k²−δij/3). At this order transverse vector and TT tensor modes generated at second order can enter the tensor equations but have zero *linear scalar projection* on the flat background. Use the mixed spatial stress, not a coordinate-covariant two-lower-index stress with a spurious background-pressure term.

Trace-free space equation times a² on this metric is the TF part of
exp(−2B){−∂ij(A+B)+B_i B_j−A_i A_j+B_i A_j+B_j A_i}.
The homogeneous-slice extrinsic-curvature terms are spatially isotropic. Expanding yields k²(Ψ₂−Φ₂)=S−Pij Qij, where S=Pij 8πG a² T^i_{j,2}, and
Qij=∂ij(Φ₁²+Ψ₁²)+2Φ₁∂ij(Φ₁−Ψ₁)+Φ₁,i Φ₁,j−Ψ₁,i Ψ₁,j−Φ₁,i Ψ₁,j−Φ₁,j Ψ₁,i.
All products in the realized result retain both long-short orderings. Half this slip is Ψ₂−(Ψ₂+Φ₂)/2, the potential difference relevant to the matter/lensing comparison. Gradient norms are comoving gradients of dimensionless potentials in Mpc⁻¹. This is **not** a predicted sky shear: propagation through the first-order perturbed path and source geometry are separate.

Let E=T_nn, J_i=8πG a j_i, j_i=−n^μ T_{μi}. Momentum constraint 2∂i F=J_i on the scalar chart; F₂=(−i k_i J_{i,2})/(2k²). The Hamiltonian constraint is
16πG a² E=exp(−2B)[−4ΔB−2(∇B)²]+6F²,
hence k²Φ₂=−4πG a² E₂+3ℋF₂+U, U=ΔΦ₁²+2Φ₁ΔΦ₁−|∇Φ₁|²/2+3F₁²/2, F₁=−Φ₁'−ℋΨ₁. Together these give both potentials when normal density, current and shear are supplied. U alone is not the curvature. Tests compute the covariant Einstein tensor directly from a time-dependent four-metric, connection and its derivatives at six points; independently sum the signed Fourier pairs for both U and Q on the retained phases.

For matter in its local energy frame the boost contribution to S is Pij 8πG a²(ρ+p)w_i w_j. ## The divergence is not the gravitational source

In a local flat patch write a real canonical scalar as (ψ exp(−imt)+c.c.)/√(2m), leading slow equation i∂tψ=−Δψ/(2m), n=|ψ|², ρ=mn. Its averaged spatial Hilbert tensor to this order is ρv_i v_j+[∂iρ ∂jρ/ρ−δij Δρ]/(4m²). The gradient dyad is exact in the carrier-phase projection of the traceless stress; the isotropic reduction uses the slow equation. The familiar Madelung momentum flux replaces δij Δρ by ∂ijρ. Their difference, Hilbert minus Madelung, is (∂ij−δijΔ)ρ/(4m²). Its flat spatial divergence vanishes, but its traceless scalar projection is generally nonzero. Even at first order the canonical field on a homogeneous background has **no scalar gradient shear**. Promoting the force-equivalent quantum stress to a metric source would introduce shear proportional to k²δρ/(4a²m²); its associated pressure change cancels it in the flat longitudinal force, not in the Einstein slip equation. The native comparison uses Newtonian-slice δ for this explicit counterfactual, not a new gravity law. Covariantly commuting the derivatives on a scalar gradient gives a Ricci contraction, so a flat divergence identity is not a license to improve a curved-space action. At fixed smooth density/velocity and wavelengths the gradient term decays as m⁻²; the ordinary bulk stress remains. No claim that cosmological preparations at different masses are thereby identical.

## Collisionless reaction in the specified differential experiment

Vary the wave-density-gradient contribution relative to bulk-only on the saved post interval, with fixed Φ₂, identical first-order states/fields, zero *difference* of initial radiation distributions, and all other unvaried second-order sources specified as common. Then the quadratic collisionless terms made solely from first-order quantities cancel in the difference. This is why a genuine kinetic response can be computed without a general second-order initial distribution. It is a fixed-curvature susceptibility, not two self-consistent alternative universes. No assumption that the canonical matching error either persists or vanishes is required. Standard starting conventions: [Ma & Bertschinger, equations for massless and massive phase-space transport in conformal Newtonian gauge](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9506072).

Write δf=−f₀ g B with g=d ln f₀/d ln q, f₀=(exp(q)+1)⁻¹. Along each unperturbed temperature-scaled comoving momentum q, ε=sqrt(q²+(am_ν/T₀)²), v=q/ε, the difference obeys B'+ikμv B=−ikμ ΔΨ₂/v. Each species' angular stress is projected with P₂(μ); for massless radiation it is 4(8πG a²ρ_r)⟨P₂B⟩. The angular plane wave gives ⟨P₂ exp(−ixμ)⟩=−j₂(x). Thus for D=k²ΔΨ₂ and direct shear S, D(η)=S(η)+∫K(η,s)D(s)ds. Massless K=−4 C_r j₂'(k(η−s))/(a(η)² k) with C_r=8πG a⁴ρ_r. The massive kernel is
C_m/(a(η)² I₀) ∫dq q³ f₀ g [ε(s)/ε(η)] j₂'(k∫_s^η v du)/k,
where C_m is the native relativistic-limit coefficient and I₀=∫dq q³f₀=7π⁴/120. The 0/0 Bessel expression has a finite analytic k→0 limit; no homogeneous TF mode is inserted. Solve causal quadrature by a triangular adjoint for the endpoint and independently integrate the directional equations for five Fourier modes. Thermal density and pressure are separately checked against the native massive-species background. A photon dipole is measured for the boost term, but the photon *response* is not treated as collisionless during Thomson coupling.

A useful continuum bound costs no interpolation assumption: |j₂'(x)|≤|x|⟨|P₂| μ²⟩=|x|[2/15+2/(45√3)]. Not a bound on a nonzero incoming quadrupole, collisional photons, an independent Φ₂ history, or a longer formation history.
