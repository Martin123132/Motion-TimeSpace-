# From scalar stress to gravitational slip on the retained phases

**The density-gradient term matters to gravity even when a rotation or acceleration calculation cannot determine which stress to use.** The canonical field fixes the stress by metric variation. Two stress tensors with the same flat-space divergence give the same acceleration but not the same metric source. In an independent exact real-wave control their divergences agree to 1.4e−15 relative, while treating the usual hydrodynamic momentum flux itself as the Hilbert tensor gives a 292% traceless-stress error in that example. The derived Hilbert trace-free part agrees to 1.3e−12 or better. This is an exact stress-choice distinction in the specified subfamily, not a new coupling or a data-dependent selection. The first-order counterfactual shear from the hydrodynamic choice would add 1.59% of the native linear slip in rms at the switch on these phases, despite being only 0.00376% of the lapse. **Does the improvement survive in the actual metric readout?** Yes. The old one-sided endpoint gives 27.74% slip error for bulk alone, 0.180% with the genuine gradient term. **Independent metric and radiation checks.** Recovered native energy, momentum and anisotropic stress on both backgrounds. The 00/0i normalized linear constraint differences are ≤3.3e−16; the native linear slip check ≤2.5e−10. An independently differentiated four-dimensional Einstein tensor agrees with all quadratic Hamiltonian, momentum and trace-free expressions in the manufactured tests to <9e−8 relative. Direct summation of wave pairs also checks the actual metric terms. At the first heavy epoch the photon *bulk* slip norm is 6.91e−13, geometric Q/k² norm 2.69e−13, canonical edge heavy norm 3.87e−14. Components are correlated, not independent variances; the intrinsic second-order radiation stress is not obtained by adding bulk terms.

We **did** solve the part of that radiation stress induced by the specified wave-gradient variation. The cold no-wave differential control is exactly zero. Thermal momentum quadrature reproduces native massive-neutrino pressure and density within 3.2e−8. Five direct momentum-and-angle integrations check the refined causal kernel to <6e−7 relative. **Decision.** Use the Hilbert tensor and the recovered trace and energy equation, not a force-equivalent stress chosen by hand. [The constrained second-order field and momentum equations are written out](NEXT-EVOLUTION.md): gravitational slip alone still leaves a common potential; the Hamiltonian/momentum constraints show precisely how to compute it.
