# Pal 5 timing sensitivity and a separate test of late environmental forcing at GD-1

**[Answer to the stream-lifetime and direction question](STREAM-ANSWER.md):** 111.159 Myr of matched Pal 5 history. In either preparation the two flows move stars, but the forecasts do not distinguish them at the tested precision. Changing the mock release time ±10 Myr brackets 34/36 individual coordinates in each preparation; a single shared date cannot erase every coordinate. With the [17 RR Lyrae](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.00595) and the specified noise model the optimal worst-state direction accuracy is essentially a coin toss if the wave preparation is unknown. None of these are fits to held-out stream measurements.

**Separate dynamical result.** Does the actual late simulated environment drive enough wave *inside* the GD-1 probe to fix the older force excess? Across 88.968 Myr of five FIRE-2 m12i epochs, the fixed late tide acts in the helpful direction in the retained axisymmetric calculation, but nowhere near the required size. Table: 280-step results. All force columns use equivalent solar masses; the mass column is actual model mass integrated inside the radius.

| Ordinary history / preparation | Induced radial force at 13.96 kpc | Change in mass inside 16.6 kpc | Upper bound on other linear channels, host-relative |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Straight / reset | −25,921 | −16,988 | 216.03 million |
| Straight / inherited | −26,423 | −17,232 | 394.97 million |
| Counted intermediate source / reset | −26,009 | −17,111 | 216.63 million |
| Counted intermediate source / inherited | −26,512 | −17,357 | 396.11 million |

The first two columns are **actual nonlinear evolutions** on the retained symmetry, not instantaneous exterior pulls. The last is a deliberately conservative numerical bound on the remaining angular responses **to first order** for any azimuth, including odd vertical and dipole perturbations. It is not a measured displacement or an estimate of their signs. At 590 direct exterior gravity itself changes the host-relative radial readout by −4.52 to +2.62 million solar-mass equivalent on the azimuth grid, with a direction-independent bound of 9.95 million. Even the sum of the directly solved axial derivative, the other first-order upper bounds and the direct force bound is **at most 1.204%** of what is needed on the two frozen stellar foregrounds. This is not an all-amplitude 3D inequality. No frozen flow direction is selected by this calculation.

**Checks and limitations.** Both saved no-tide phases replay to <7×10⁻¹⁴ in relative state norm. Reversing the test tide checks the derivative against the actual nonlinear wave, not against an invented density pattern. All two-schedule comparisons in the verifier pass 0.5%; the independent quadrature at the probe agrees, including the mass and host-centre correction. Between five catalogue samples the true driving is unknown: at the three intermediate saved dates even the potential near GD-1 departs from endpoint-linear interpolation by 1.1–3.7% in angular RMS, so we used all five. Do not interpret this as an error bar on an unobserved Milky Way encounter. The haloes are measured **in a simulation**; smooth conventional host dark matter was not added to the MTS Hamiltonian. Earlier tides, internal nonaxisymmetric sources, a differently occupied state, and nonlinear 3D mode transfer have not been ruled out.
