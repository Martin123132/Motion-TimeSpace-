# The early finite tide: which part can be traced to the field?

**The canonical scalar supplies an independently predictable spatial stress at the transition.** It is not represented by the bulk velocity alone. On the same host phases a bulk-only prediction misses 25.90% of the projected mixed field in rms. With the derived density-gradient term included the discrepancy is **0.5272%** against a canonical edge estimate. That narrowly misses the existing 0.5% test: do not round it down to a pass. Changing the edge estimate from 1/2 to 2/4 cycles changes the field by 0.00281%; at the pair level halving the cycle quadrature resolution changes the one-cycle integral by 0.0593%. The two sides are not simultaneous: a further 0.0667% in scale factor separates the last canonical output from the first post-transition one. Those are different error diagnostics and not a statistical uncertainty. Fourier contractions summed directly on four modes agree with the spatial implementation to better than 5e−12 relative.

**Can the microscopic field predict a cycle it did not see?** Yes, on the canonical side. Freeze a constant or linear forecast using demodulated quadratures from two and three cycles earlier, supply only the homogeneous carrier in the held-out cycle. The constant prediction misses the spatially projected last-cycle stress by **0.9866%**. Linear evolution of those same quadratures, with no tuned coefficients, reduces this to **0.00883%** (mode-pair tensor: 0.00952%). This passes 0.5% but not 0.002%; it does not determine the later lifetime of this stress. The cold control has no heavy scalar to which one could assign this tensor.

**Effect on the subsequent inner material-share change.** Generate only the fixed long–short products during a=0.0002251→0.003. On the 13.96 kpc tag patch, carrying that particular response into the z99→19 difference gives +0.000002449 percentage points in wave relative to same-metric CDM. The corresponding paired cold baryon-minus-CDM calculation gives +0.000217530 points. Both are numerical sensitivities of the stated material equations rather than bound halo predictions. The heavy terms nearly cancel; 16/32/64 panels give +2.097/+2.371/+2.449 millionths of a point. The last change is 3.19% of that tiny net: the unchanged 0.5% relative target is **not met**. Holding early baryon compression fixed instead gives +0.00002826 points (cold: −0.00010520), so physical closure, not just step size, matters. Neither of these heavy examples erases the previous −0.03292-point conditional drift. They are not upper bounds on omitted common gravity, intrinsic stress or incoming states. Independent mixed-mode sums verify the implemented signs and amplitudes for 44 components; a uniform common acceleration has no differential signal.

**Strict validity check:** at the switch the small relativistic correction requires k>2.872/Mpc, while the retained slow-wave criterion needs k<0.3018/Mpc. No k satisfies both. The first stored short modes pass at a=0.00085570; **none of the fixed long inputs** pass both throughout or even at one of the 129 dates. Masking the output by the previous late-epoch criterion does not make the early long modes valid. This is why the Newtonian tide cannot settle the earlier relativistic formation response, however accurately it is integrated. The measured pre-transition scalar stress can be taken forward without pretending those long modes are dust.
