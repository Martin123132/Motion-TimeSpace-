Use the two saved anchorA three-knot wave evolutions (32 and 64 steps per native time unit) from snapshots 544 to 558 to 572. Replay only these two segments with the original Hamiltonian and verify complex endpoints without phase alignment. No new occupation, coupling, halo fit or imposed external wave. Keep original baryon driving: the 3D catalogue field defines a diagnostic boundary, not a new force inserted into the evolution.

Calibrate outward probability current on stationary physical spheres (25, 50, 100, 120 kpc). Report direct enclosed-mass change via its exterior complement to avoid cancellation in the nearly empty tail; check total unscaled norm drift separately. Then use host-centred level surfaces below the embedded companion saddle: subtract 0.1, 0.5 and 1.0 times G M_c(<r_s)/r_s at each catalogue epoch. Find the first outward crossing on each direction from the centre, check radial gradients and branch connectivity. The actual surface evolution between the three force reconstructions is unknown. Use straight interpolation of the ray radii as an explicit history, check an alternative geometrical continuation and endpoint-only sweep/flow decomposition, rather than treating interpolation as data. Compare ordinary, wave and conventional geometry without attributing any actual cold flow to a missing particle history.

Check both spherical and transverse current, sweep, mass closure, angular and temporal refinement, source and no-current controls. Report existing 0.5% and 2e-5 fractional gates separately, with a cancellation-aware denominator and also residual in solar masses; do not promote tiny signals just because errors are small compared with the whole halo. Timesteps and angular quadratures need not be the dominant error: in a finite-element wave the continuum surface current is not necessarily the exact conserved discrete current. Separate representation effects from physical transport before interpreting a mismatch. Raw states reused; private, no network or public repository changes.
