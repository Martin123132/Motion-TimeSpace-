# The tidal boundary overtakes an outward wave; GD-1 requires an interior change

We separated **mass crossing the surface** from **the surface moving over mass** using the retained complex Galactic wave. On the first 322 Myr interval the deepest tested surface gained 12,605 solar masses, even though **17,223 solar masses flowed out** through it. Its expansion swept in 29,828. Counting the increase as gravitational capture would get the direction of transport wrong. No wave, stellar mass, G, stream data or coupling was refitted.

For example, imposing zero endpoint speeds changes the early deep split to −16,027/+28,632, with the same direct net gain. ### What difference could the outer wave make to a real stream?

Use the two *actual snapshot-590* preparations, not an extrapolation of the earlier boundary. Against the [published GD-1 radial force at 13.96 kpc](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07187v1), the two fixed stellar models require a reduction of 33.65–43.91 **billion** solar masses in radial-force equivalent. The saved wave at that epoch contains just 143,000–174,000 solar masses beyond 70 kpc. We gave an exterior-only repair the most generous possible freedom: all that mass can be placed anywhere **outside** 70 kpc without paying an energy penalty. Even concentrating it in the best direction can reduce the proxy by at most **10,804 solar masses** in a fixed frame. With the retained axis and reflection symmetry the limit is **106**. These are bounds on the instantaneous gravitational effect of the existing outer material, not on an interior dynamical response to a tide. The 70-kpc sphere is a diagnostic, not an assumed boundary at 590.

More usefully, solve for **how far in a change has to reach**. Leave everything inside radius R untouched; permit all existing mass outside R to move to a maximally favorable distribution outside R. The largest R for which that could even in principle repair this one force component is:

| Permitted shape of the exterior wave | Necessary penetration radius, range across the two preparations, four source histories and two Pouliasis foregrounds |
|---|---:|
| Any positive density, absolute force | 23.01–23.73 kpc |
| Any positive density, host-centred differential force | 22.64–23.30 kpc |
| Reflection through the centre, allowing tilted lobes | 21.34–21.97 kpc |
| Retained axial and equatorial symmetry | **16.25–16.62 kpc** |

For the last row even a pair of infinitesimally thin mirrored rings is allowed, more freedom than a smooth finite-energy wave has. These radii are *necessary*, not proposed new cutoffs. If a symmetric change is confined outside 17 kpc, then it cannot supply the missing force on *any* of these frozen cases. The original lighter stellar foreground is included separately in the ledger. For the conventional published spherical haloes, moving spherical shells outside the tracer has exactly zero effect: one conventional stellar+halo combination lies inside the published radial interval, the other above it. We did not multiply the radial and flattening constraints from the same stars or claim a joint fit.
