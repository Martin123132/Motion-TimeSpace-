The 1,024-interval saved-output grid was adequate for the recombination-window RMS. At the shortest wavelength, halving this sampling density moved the nearly vanished instantaneous signal by 0.75–0.78% at the visibility maximum. Retain all these results. Save more output from the same evolution, without altering its physical inputs: 4,096 output intervals; compare the 192-moment answer with 128 moments, 16,384 with 8,192 integration intervals and the complete output with every second sample. Require the same 0.005 threshold for each branch and their signed contrast; do not interpolate a small signal solely from a less-resolved run.

A separate trap: early in the saved native thermal background, visibility/opacity sits at 1e-30 even when the integrated optical depth exceeds 2,600. That floor is irrelevant to the declared z=1500–750 window, but can dominate an all-epochs visibility-weighted RMS for an otherwise erased short wave. For an auxiliary early-tail diagnostic reconstruct exp(-tau) from the native opacity, anchored by visibility/opacity at the late end, and integrate towards earlier times (underflow to zero is fine).
