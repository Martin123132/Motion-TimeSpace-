# How much early photon contrast reaches last scattering?
23 November 2026 · Private, local MTS research. **Use the final-* files**; the other tables retain the coarse-grid tests and their failures.

At the longest of the five tested wavelengths, **3.835%** of the launch MTS-minus-baseline photon contrast survives across the last-scattering window, or **1.404%** at the instant of peak visibility (z≈1089.17). At the next wavelength those figures are **0.00633%** and **0.0000199%**. The three shorter waves are effectively erased in this two-coordinate transfer. These are fractions of each mode's own early contrast, not a percentage change in the observed CMB. The window result is a visibility-weighted RMS amplitude, not a signed average.

**Why carry both quantities?** In the longest wave the outgoing energy and current amplitudes are respectively 3.374% and 1.824% of the joint launch contrast (add in quadrature); current accounts for 22.6% of the outgoing squared contrast. Initial current also feeds *both* outgoing quantities: propagating its contribution alone gives 3.00% of the original pair's amplitude. That last value is a coherent contribution, not an extra 3.00% to add. Exchanging momentum with initially comoving rather than initially untagged baryons gives 3.928% instead of 3.835%. The different background transfer acting on the common signal contributes only about 0.0010% of the longest-wave launch norm; the surviving difference is predominantly inherited.

**What was held fixed.** Both saved 77-bin readouts were reconstructed and checked against their previous seals. Both early complex amplitudes stayed fixed. Redshifts and phases were matched at common scale, including the slightly different launch times; no fitting, late phase shift or substitution of a new gravitational history. The propagated signal is the response to *only* the saved photon energy and current, not all radiation regenerated by gravity and new collision sources after launch. Starting with zero additional baryon or higher-moment perturbations defines the tagged response; it is not a statement that the saved galaxy or cosmology has no other perturbations.

**Numerical checks.** Native opacities give optical depth ≈2687 from launch to the visibility peak; multiplying both coordinates by the single-photon transmission would give the wrong answer. The collision-only tests conserve photon density and total photon–baryon momentum; collision-free streaming reproduces the analytic monopole and dipole. Native and independently opacity-reconstructed visibility weights agree in the declared z=1500–750 window. Its absolute visibility integral is 0.91812 before normalization. The very early native visibility has a floor: corrected full-tail weighting would raise the shortest-wave ratio to 1.06e-18, still negligible. The first angular truncation and later a coarse output interpolation failed on small signals; neither was promoted. Enlarging the hierarchy to 192, keeping 4,097 output samples and comparing time, angular order, medium sampling and output interpolation gives **120/120 RMS and 120/120 peak comparisons within 0.5%**, on cold, MTS and their signed difference, for both initial baryon controls. Worst movements: 0.058% RMS and 0.1075% at peak. These are numerical checks, not observational uncertainty bars for the tiny values.

**Next:** use the longest wavelength first. Reconstruct its post-launch gravitational driving on both backgrounds and compare propagated launch signal alone, newly driven signal alone, and their coherent sum, without refitting the early coefficients. Verify the source coverage before interpreting a late signal as a prediction.
