# Where the six-band agreement comes from — and a test away from the disk

20 November 2026 · private continuation of the retained weak-field MTS branch. **Changing the ordinary disk supplies almost the entire improvement in the inner polar force. Changing wave occupation alone does not.** A second result is more encouraging: without any recalibration, the model gets the direction of the total force right at both high-latitude stellar streams tested here. The force strength is less successful. Nothing was fitted to these targets.

## Disentangling the eight sky bands

A = the measured population/gas foreground with the retained bar redistribution. B = the disk and bulge specified in the satellite simulations. H = either previously retained high wave occupation, L = the lower occupation set by the native host's initial outer dark radial force. All cross combinations use their own self-gravitating equilibria and axisymmetric LMC response, not fields transported from another ground. Every entry below holds across all six archived satellite histories and both representations. Only the common archive accelerating frame is used. Both models always receive the same ordinary and direct LMC fields and are paired at the same outer speed **within each comparison**.

| Ordinary foreground | Wave occupation | MTS | Matched static NFW |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| A | H | 2/8 | 2/8 |
| A | L | 2/8 | 2/8 |
| B | H | 4/8 | 4/8 |
| B | L | 6/8 | 4/8 |

For the inner northern polar component, moving from A/H to B/L changes the total by +0.2079 to +0.2082 in units of 10⁻¹⁰ m/s². **+0.20898 comes directly from ordinary matter.** The change of stellar potential acting through the stationary wave adds ~0.0044 and the lower occupation subtracts ~0.0054; changing the driven response adds at most 0.00011 in magnitude. Radially the cancellation is very different: the direct ordinary change is −0.8663, the wave's response to the new foreground −0.386 to −0.390, and lowering its occupation gives back +0.600 to +0.626. ## How much of that ordinary foreground is independently supported?

The solar-neighborhood [McKee et al. census](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.05334) constrains ordinary mass, not the eight acceleration bins. We included the central E3 and long-bar donor and recipient at the solar meridian; then intersected the midplane density, ±1.1 kpc slab and full-column ranges. A mixture (1−f)A+fB permits f≤0.231 using one quoted width or f≤0.479 with two. The midplane density sets both ceilings. These fractions describe the tested interpolation, not a bound on the fraction of baryons in the Milky Way. The census already informed A; it is separate from the target accelerations but not a new blind validation.

At the old external speed of 224.03 km/s, MTS gets 2/8 on A and at both census ceilings. Static NFW gets 2/8, 4/8, 4/8, respectively. On the *pure* simulation disk at that same speed, both get 6/8. This differs from the 6-versus-4 comparison in the table because the native initial-speed calibration there is about 212 km/s. The measured speed's ±1.91 km/s bootstrap shifts were also propagated on A and both census endpoints: MTS stays at 2/8; the static control gives 2/8 on A, 3–4/8 at the one-width ceiling and 4/8 at the two-width ceiling. These are marginal band checks, not independent detections or a model-selection statistic.

At the inner pair (R=6 kpc, |z|=1.5 kpc) the gravity left after subtracting ordinary matter and the direct satellite must point **at least 2.40° toward the plane from inward radial**, across the whole two-width family and all six histories, even granting an arbitrary opposite-parity contribution. The one-width family requires about 4.0° or more. The wave at the most permissive two-width endpoint supplies **0.61–0.63°**, static spherical NFW 0°. The maximum local effective residual-force axis ratio is 0.921, versus 0.977–0.978 for that wave. A spherical dark field would need about 74% native ordinary foreground on even the most favorable history to supply the polar force from ordinary matter instead; this exceeds the 47.9% census ceiling. This is a constraint on direction, not a global halo density shape, an exclusion of every smooth foreground, or a statistical significance. ## An external check: Pal 5 and GD-1

We evaluated both already frozen stationary potentials at the two stream locations, rather than extrapolating the 6-kpc angle. [Bovy et al. (2016), eqs. 3–7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.01298) give both a force-direction and a weaker force-strength constraint. Using all three census-tested preparations at the central speed:

| Stream | Published total-force q | MTS q | NFW q | Strength discrepancy, MTS / NFW (quoted widths) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Pal 5 | 0.94 ± 0.05 | 0.9792–0.9794 | 0.9853–0.9858 | 1.51–1.66 / 1.79–1.93 |
| GD-1 | 0.95 ± 0.04 | 0.9417–0.9423 | 0.9476–0.9493 | 1.98–2.26 / 1.70–1.87 |

All four directions are within one published width. Both models pull more strongly than the stream central values. MTS is closer on Pal 5's strength, NFW on GD-1's. Increasing the outer normalization would strengthen **both** streams further while it would help MTS's weak radial pull near the disk. The constrained field derivative and independent finite changes agree; there is no local amplitude adjustment in a direction that improves both. This is not a global no-go. At the actual ±1.91 km/s anchor endpoints, both stream directions still lie within one width for both models. The summaries were inferred from stream tracks using stationary models, not direct accelerometer data. We tested the static azimuthal mean; no disturbed-stream likelihood, full covariance, joint sigma, or verified historical holdout is claimed. **A Newtonian consistency check independent of NFW concentration.** Pal 5 is at spherical radius 18.78 kpc, just outside the 17.58-kpc speed anchor. A positive spherical halo cannot have less enclosed mass there. At 224.03 km/s, even an empty shell would give too strong a Pal 5 normalization for its nearest one-width boundary on the census-limited sources. But allowing the speed, census and stream to move together by just **1.079 times their quoted widths** removes this necessary obstruction. It is mild tension, not evidence against spherical gravity. For the actual wave at the two-width census endpoint, the shell contains 8.22 billion M☉ and the combined aspherical correction to the projected strength is +0.0414 km/s/Myr, versus +0.3476 needed for the nearest one-width edge with that shell. ## Confidence and next test

The saved input grid, six [satellite histories](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04837), priors and tolerance were not changed. Of 288 new component/time checks, three tiny induced polar components exceed the 2×10⁻⁵ relative target, by up to 8.41×10⁻⁵; the largest absolute difference is 9.30×10⁻¹⁰ **in the table units**, with no band flip. All sampled basis refinements pass. Independent stream-force comparisons differ by ≤5.4×10⁻⁷ relative. These are finite numerical checks; actual stellar geometry, stream modeling and rotation systematics are much bigger uncertainties. See [derivation](DERIVATION.md), [replay](REPLAY.md) and [verification](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `checks.json`).

**Next:** test the explicitly forced, vertically even m=1 (lopsided) mode of the same wave on the census-allowed foregrounds. Remove common free fall before interpreting it as a density distortion; distinguish displacement relative to the stars from true shape change. Use the six physical satellite histories, not a fitted displacement or a chosen halo flattening. Its job is quantitative: can it supply the inner force tilt without worsening the already reasonable off-disk force directions? Compare to matched halo controls. If it is too small, move on to formation-supported anisotropic populations rather than retuning the total wave mass.
