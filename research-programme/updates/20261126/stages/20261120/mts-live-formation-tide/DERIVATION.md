# Common gravity, occupation and the origin of the shape difference

Use conformal time η and comoving radius r. The tracer is the axially tagged cold matter or the retained heavy wave before a density node. For species s write n=1+D, peculiar velocity v=∇V, with phases normalized by S=a m V for the wave. From the single-potential Newtonian action and continuity, on the retained subfamily,

    ∂η D = −n ΔV − ∇D·∇V
    ∂η V = −ℋV − U − |∇V|²/2 + Q
    Q_wave = [ΔD/(2n) − |∇D|²/(4n²)]/(2a²m²),    Q_dust=0.
    ΔU = Σ_s C_s D_s/(2a)   (C_s = 8πG a³ ρ̄_s).

No potential gradient distinguishes two species at the same point. The matter differences enter through the prepared current, the sources of gravity and the gradient stress of the wave. This is the nonrelativistic weak-field branch, not an assertion about gases after shocks or fluids after crossing. Cold CDM and baryons are pressureless in this retained interior. The expansion history is the original one separately for each branch.

**Boundaries and evolution.** Regular even Legendre modes, radial spherical Bessel modes, angular quadrature and explicit Runge–Kutta evolve density and velocity together with their common gravitational field. The spherical solution is evolved, not frozen. The first implementation evolves modal deviations from smooth native linear reference functions, subtracting their derivatives in the equations of motion (a change of representation). It matches deviations to the environment in the outermost 30% of a finite box; this is a conditional finite-domain calculation, not a new physical force. Poisson for an added density coefficient c j_l(kr) gives −c j_l/k² plus a regular harmonic to enforce the exterior r^(−l−1) decay. A constant monopole potential is removed as a velocity-potential gauge. The native metric option includes the native linear difference from the nonrelativistic inventory. The separate **matter-interior** option removes it: its interior gravity is exactly that of the live ordinary, cold and wave matter in the retained representation. Only the external quadrupole coefficient A=[U₂′(L)+3U₂(L)/L]/(5L) is supplied by the original large-scale metric. The live density is not a stationary fitted halo.

An independent solver forms the potential from two radial integrals over the current physical density at every step, not by subtracting two metric histories. For angular degree l, the radial force of the density inside the box is

    g_r = [−(l+1)r^(−l−2)∫₀ʳ s_l(u)u^(l+2)du
           + l r^(l−1)∫ᵣᴸ s_l(u)u^(1−l)du]/(2l+1).

Here s_l is the degree-l component of the Poisson source. This also resolves which shells provide the remaining tidal force. The separate exterior potential is A r²P₂. The direct solver can use time-independent initial density/velocity basis lifts throughout r<0.4L while the physical fields are free to change; only the matching annulus and exterior require the time-dependent environment. This directly tests whether borrowing a native reference curve inside the patch caused the effect.

**Material test.** Mark a sphere of each target species at a_i=0.01. At pole and equator reflection symmetry makes ∂θV vanish. The actual radial marker follows dr/dη=∂rV. Thus (a dr/dη)′=a[−∂rU+∂rQ]. With J=∫dη/a, the predicted final difference of radii is the initial velocity contrast times a_i J plus ∫a(F_axis−F_equator)[J_final−J(η)]dη. The gradient term in this expression is evaluated from the differentiated physical density, not the time derivative of V or the marker speed. For the infinitesimal response one must also include the spherical force gradient sampled by displaced markers. Ignoring it confuses moving a tracer with changing gravity. The full angular equations also include lateral mass motion: a 10% change in the local quadrupole density-rate magnitude need not be a 10% change in the shape of a material surface.

The finite surface r=B(μ,η), μ=cosθ, obeys ∂ηB=∂rV−(1−μ²)(∂μV)(∂μB)/B². Integrating n r² dr dμ/2 inside the moving surface checks its occupation. The mass in the two fixed-angle polar caps within this *material volume* can change only by crossing the cones. Reflection symmetry gives the signed rate ∫₀ᴮ⁽¹⁄²⁾ n(1−μ²)∂μV dr at μ=1/2, in units of 4π times the homogeneous comoving density factored out. This tests the physical cap mass, not just its l=2 proxy. A changing mass in a fixed sphere is not evidence that the material gained particles.

**Counterfactual, with a forecast made first.** Exchange only the initial target-species quadrupolar velocity potential. The density, spherical flow, other species, couplings and external tide of the recipient are untouched. For the wave this is a phase intervention. Project the donor-minus-recipient velocity potential onto the same regular basis, matching the original outside the patch. For each marker retain the actual achieved initial speed; an imperfect representation is not held out as a physical discovery.

The leading shape shift is the redshifting initial peculiar velocity. The first change in force starts one derivative later: the density-rate perturbation is s=−n₀Δ₂δV₂−n₀′δV₂′. Its material force-rate shift (axis minus equator) is 3/2 times the sum of (i) δV₂′ times the spherical force gradient, (ii) the Poisson force of C_target s/(2a), and (iii) the wave's gradient-force derivative obtained by varying Q with δn=sP₂. The jerk-only forecast holds this initial rate fixed along conformal time and integrates it with the above Green function. This approximation is tested, not assumed constant in the measured trajectories. The separate higher-order linear-transfer forecast evolves all the Fourier currents and densities, including gravity and heavy pressure, on the homogeneous background. It still omits nonlinear monopole evolution and finite-box mode couplings.
