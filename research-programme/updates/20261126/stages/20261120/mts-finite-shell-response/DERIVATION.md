# From a local mass impulse to a common-gravity response

This is a controlled forward experiment on the two post-crossing spherical preparations at a₀=0.65. The cold control contains two positive collisionless mass sheets; the heavy branch adds its retained coherent wave. They do not have identical density fields: each uses its own saved evolution of the same specified primordial patch. No change to cosmological abundance, G, occupation, or the Galactic band data. See the previous impulse derivation (not bundled here) for normalization and the scalar momentum constraint (not bundled here) for its weak-field interpretation.

## The observable and the actual perturbation

For a pair of comoving radii the observable is D=a(Φ_outer−Φ_inner), not an absolute central potential. The common Poisson action gives D=Σ W_i S_i, where S_i is the nodal mass excess including C_s=8πG a³ρ̄_s. The drive is an instantaneous common unit-mass potential impulse: p→p+ε∂_X W_A on the actual stream, u→exp(i m ε W_A)u for u=χψ. A spans 100–200 kpc comoving, B spans 150–300, C spans 250–350. The gradient, not the value of W, is compact. The wave picks up a constant phase throughout the interior. An unobservable global phase cannot create density by itself. Neither an extra gravitational mass nor a force term for the test observable was inserted.

For zero-time response the continuum expression is proportional to ∫ρ ∇W_A·∇W_R. C therefore has an instantaneous null. At a later time equal mass can be redistributed within a shell; the observable need not stay zero. We normalize dD/dε by Δln(a) times the geometric mean of the initial diagonal response coefficients. For comparisons across all four spatial grids we use *the same* native two diagonals; the raw per-unit values are also retained. This normalization measures an absolute error on a nonzero drive scale, not the fractional accuracy of a signal close to zero.

## Evolving and differentiating the matter, not the readout

Inside each native gravity cell Φ is affine in X²; its derivative in signed radial position is linear in X. Cut the oriented mass stream at all preimages of the cell knots. Carry both endpoint momenta and positions of each piece. During each kick p←p−δt ∂_XΦ; during free streaming X←X+δt p/a². Integrate the original positive mass exactly on each label interval with its q² weight. The radial mass shape for the right node is (X²−r_i²)/(r_{i+1}²−r_i²); the complement goes to the left node. The force has distinct cell-side derivatives at a knot: each adjacent material piece follows its own side. This is exact for these split sub-operators on their chosen finite-element representation, not an exact continuum trajectory. The initial states, freezing and conservation checks are in [the protocol](PROTOCOL.md).

Differentiate the unforced trajectory. If X_left+f(X_right−X_left) is a fixed spatial knot, its *label* moves by δf=−[(1−f)δX_left+fδX_right]/(X_right−X_left) for an interior split. Include this variation in both momenta and the mass integrated over that piece. At a cut take the displacement of the cut position itself to be zero. Reassemble the varied metric from all streams and 2 Re(u*δu). This avoids dividing a small signal by a difference of large perturbed forces to generate a forecast. Test the forecast independently with positive, negative and zero actual drives.

## A local witness in the separated shell

The compact kinetic operator has links of lengths 1,2,3 and coefficients b=(3/2,−3/20,1/90). Summing the antisymmetric pair terms of the wave equation gives, for each real kernel W,
    
    dD_wave/dt = C_h/(ma²h) Σ_{i,d} b_d (W_{i+d}−W_i) Im(u_i* u_{i+d}).

For the linear response replace the imaginary product by Im(v_i* u_{i+d}+u_i* v_{i+d}), with v=δu. **Only links across unequal W contribute**: a uniform phase, a uniform potential shift, or inner-shell density cancellation cannot fake an outer current through this formula. The radial ramp enforces the saved outer boundary; W vanishes near it, and the origin terms cancel because W is constant there. The total wave-norm check accounts separately for exchange with the inhomogeneous outer boundary. An impulse at a distance need not have a sharp light cone in the nonrelativistic wave equation.

To isolate feedback, evolve a second tangent v₀ with the *same initial phase* and *same live unperturbed potential* as v, but omit −im δΦ u from its potential kicks. Then v−v₀ is sourced only by the varied common metric. Their currents add linearly; neither diagnostic tangent is added again to the gravitational density. Integrate the current on each exact discrete free step with independent 8- and 16-node time quadratures; compare the result with the varied endpoint densities and actual opposite-sign runs. This is also a sign-sensitive normalization test of the coupling.

## Matched original control

The original comparison retains the continuous nodal stream momenta and spectral wave kinetic energy; projected impulses come from the consistent mass-matrix solve, not the repaired gradients. It is tested on the same positive and negative kicks, unforced initial state, epochs, Poisson source and readouts. This isolates the effect of the whole changed representation, not solely that of one term. The explicit dust tags track positively weighted material that was in A when driven. Counting a tag at C is not by itself a perturbation signal: its unforced trajectory already moves.
