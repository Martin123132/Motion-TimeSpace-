# A relative current can be hidden from the exterior briefly, not from every interior probe

On the retained nonuniform z=99 formation preparations, we can now separate **ordinary streaming** from the part of the common Newtonian field generated by **wave dispersion**. The two mechanisms do not start at the same power of time. More importantly, the internal response and the field visible outside the source obey different constraints.

## Saved formation states: which source turns on first?

We kept the original n_s, spherical flows, 99-mode launch, mass, countercurrent prescription, geometry and 20 ppm threshold. A weighted aperture has essentially zero net initial density-source slope after balancing the currents. Its curvature need not vanish. The independently evaluated divergence of the matter momentum flux gives the quadratic response for **both** heavy-wave and cold hosts. At the 1.353-comoving-Mpc reference aperture the frozen stress coefficients were +1.72381e−19 (wave host) and +1.27868e−18 (cold host), in comoving Mpc⁻⁴ for this normalized source moment. With vs without dispersion in the perturbation of the same heavy host, the first difference is cubic: +3.93197e−20 Mpc⁻⁵ there. The corresponding all-cold difference is identically zero; its streaming response is not. All nonzero stress and wave startup tests, across two spectral cutoffs, two continuity formulations, both time resolutions and the applicable controls, agree to at most 1.32 ppm and 0.122 ppm respectively. The balanced current is divergence-suppressed on these apertures, not pointwise zero.

## A bound, and exactly what evades it

For a nonzero compact quadrupolar launch W on positive n_h, the extra gravitational source always has a nonzero overlap with W at cubic order, of fixed negative sign. The [derivation](DERIVATION.md) gives an integral of squares and a lower bound in terms of the support radius, without adding a coupling or specifying a galaxy from its forces. It **doesn't** say that every force probe sees every state. Among three smooth test profiles and their linear combinations, the smallest and largest derivative energies per unit weighted norm were 0.67589 and 1344.19 Mpc⁻⁶. Both were predicted before evolution; the independent self-probe tests of all three eigenvectors agree within 0.037 ppm. These are spatial response scales, not quantum-particle masses.

Outside the material perturbation only its ordinary Newtonian multipole remains in this sector. Its cubic coefficient samples a different functional, proportional to the **radial gradient of background density**. On a uniform host any compact phase of this type gives zero exterior wave-only quadrupole at cubic order. On the nonuniform host we built one unit-norm phase that maximizes that exterior wave term within the three-profile family and one unit-norm phase that makes it vanish. Their internal cubic overlaps are **both nonzero**, −2.19132e−20 and −7.54656e−20 in the declared test normalization. These are theoretical interventions, not inferred Milky Way occupations or evidence for generic screening. Two explicit states and force coefficients (not bundled here).

## Is the exterior zero physical or a numerical artifact?

- **A real numerical failure:** the old global basis gives a false exterior term for the designed null more than 200 times the visible control on the higher cutoff. Smooth interior apertures had not detected the failure. Windowing the exterior integral reduces it but is not a force prediction: the true moment weights density by r⁴ all the way to the exterior. We kept [those failed comparisons](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `external-multipole-tests.csv`).
- **A repair at launch:** local radial elements preserve the compact initial phase without projecting its small tails across the whole box. The wave, ordinary streams, live monopole, and vacuum-matched Newton potential all evolve. Reading the full quadrupole directly **or from the actual boundary potential** gives worst errors of 1049, 5.75, 0.351 and 0.048 ppm of the visible launch coefficient over 20, 32, 48 and 64 elements. The last three meet the original 20 ppm threshold; the coarsest does not. This includes the homogeneous and exterior-null controls. Local-cell refinement (not bundled here).
- **The stratified exterior zero lasts only through cubic order for this phase.** Differentiating the coupled operators predicts a signed **fourth** exterior-moment derivative of −7.7661e−26 on the 64-element grid. Forward-only extraction missed the 20 ppm target in 18 of 36 non-null comparisons. Independent evolution to equal positive and negative conformal times isolates the even part; all 36 symmetric tests pass within 0.615 ppm, and the finest spatial/time run within 0.020 ppm for its nine readouts. This checks the derivative, including the metric's extra 1/a, without assuming the previous cubic null is an all-time one. Frozen derivative (not bundled here) · [raw forward checks](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `quartic-release-checks.csv`) · [symmetric checks](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `quartic-symmetric-checks.csv`).
- **Finite-time exterior force remains unresolved.** At one saved log-a window after launch, the null phase's extra exterior quadrupole is opposite in sign to the visible phase's extra field; its magnitude ranges from **1.24% to 3.15%** of that reference at 48 vs 64 cells. That is not converged to 20 ppm. The pressure-disabled ordinary field is also nonzero. Neither the two-significance-digit range nor the infinitesimal cancellation should be used as a force law for an actual halo. Total, pressure-disabled and wave-only histories (not bundled here).

**Next physical test:** match the matter velocities as well as the metric at the outer boundary. In an unperturbed homogeneous exterior a decaying quadrupolar potential drives a harmonic velocity potential proportional to r⁻³, even when the interior source is compact. For the pressureless exterior amplitude this implies v̇+ℋv=−φ_ext, not v=0 at a finite wall. This is an analytic exterior control, not a justification to set the exterior wave density to zero: test it against enlarged homogeneous buffers including propagating wave density. Keep the same three compact states, full-force readout and cold/null controls, and test convergence of the finite-time field before interpreting it. This entire release is local.
