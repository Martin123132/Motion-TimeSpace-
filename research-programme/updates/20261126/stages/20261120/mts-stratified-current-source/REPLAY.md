# Reproduce the stratified source and exterior test

Use the retained Python 3.13 with `-B`. One numerical thread. No download, CAMB production run, new fit or GitHub access. The prior 198 files are pinned, including both native spectral banks. Repeating an output compares its bytes rather than overwriting it. Start with `python -B verify.py`, not a new evolution.

1. `predict.py` constructs the compact probes, compensated and uncompensated currents, canonical heavy/cold hosts, quadrature, continuum weak stress and cubic pressure derivative. SHA-256 of the initial forecast is recorded in evolution.py. No achieved shape/source result goes into that step. `origin.py` handles initial-state assembly, masks and local-only outputs.
2. For each heavy/cold model, each strong/weak radial operator, and `--sub 2` or `--sub 4`, run `evolution.py MODEL METHOD --sub N`. On each of the two original radial cutoffs it runs the saved stratified drive, its uniform host control, and an independent compact probe launch. The retained mass and monopole are unchanged. The alternative operator integrates continuity and wave pressure by parts in space; the derivative of the taper is included. For every joint evolution the last state evolves the on–off difference from zero directly, sourcing its pressure with the sum of pressure-off and difference densities. Subtraction of the two independently evolved original states is an explicit cross-check. `assess.py` compares the conformal-time derivatives, raw short histories and the available sealed first-epoch pointwise sources. Each frozen cubic entry for a balanced drive also applies to the same target velocity without its compensator; only the balanced on/off difference is tested directly.
3. `scale_forecast.py` freezes the crossing-time estimates from the initial coefficients before `gravity_clock.py` evolves and measures the crossings on a dense separate time grid. The radius-integrated force uses inner and outer quadrupole moments and spherical radial and polar components, not a spherical enclosed-mass shortcut. The root compares the signed magnitudes of the measured wave-only and pressure-off terms in their anticipated directions, excluding the trivial t=0 root. Outputs include brackets and comparisons for both time resolutions and spatial methods; the box ends at 24 comoving Mpc.
4. `invisible_bound.py` constructs the norm and derivative Gram matrices from independent analytic compact probes and freezes three normalized eigenvectors. `test_bound.py --calculate` runs these perturbations independently at both cutoffs and checks the achieved overlaps on three basis profiles and on the initial phase itself. Omit `--calculate` for the read-only replay using the saved evolution packets.
5. `outside_forecast.py` freezes the signed exterior functional, its most visible direction, an orthogonal direction and a reference. `outside_test.py` tests them using the complete r⁴ density moment, a separately labelled smooth interior diagnostic, and the self-overlap. It does not truncate the physical exterior readout to make it pass. The quantities are coefficients of P₂, not independent rotational velocities or real sky measurements.
6. `local_elements.py` runs an independent local finite-element evolution of the same smooth compact phases, a density- and velocity-dependent compensator, pressure-off state, wave-only difference and time-dependent monopole. Lobatto mass quadrature, continuous order-six shape functions, weak angular+radial stiffness and a Poisson Robin boundary (+3L in the stiffness) determine the potential. Matter perturbations have homogeneous Dirichlet endpoints in this **finite-domain numerical branch**, with no global taper operation on the local representation. This changes the spatial implementation and is not an all-time physical exterior condition. The gravitational readout is from the boundary potential as well as from the integrated source. The homogeneous exterior control refers to uniform background within the computation, not proof of a cosmological vacuum beyond it.
7. `fourth_forecast.py` uses the original native background and **its equation** at launch to predict the next derivative. `check_fourth.py` tests it in positive-time coupled evolutions. In exterior-finite-time.csv the density readouts are Q or a self-overlap; **outer_metric** is the actual boundary potential multiplied by the constant −10 a_i L³, i.e. (a_i/a)Q. For a physical radial acceleration at 36 comoving Mpc along the polar axis multiply that metric readout by −3/(10 a_i 36⁴). All are comoving coefficients with c=1. `symmetric_check.py` starts again at the same launch and evolves to equal positive and negative conformal durations, cancelling odd powers before extracting the fourth derivative. Negative duration is only a numerical derivative test, not revised formation data.

The five frozen products and original unpromoted numerical failures are part of the seal. `verify.py --seal` is a one-time exclusive creation; run without that argument to independently check the files and scientific gates. No protected services or continuing workers are needed.
