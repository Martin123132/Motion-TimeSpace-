# What the extended bar does to the wave at fixed occupation

20 November 2026 · private MTS parent calculation. **The mean stellar transfer pulls both waves inward. The density changes in the direction the inner data require, but the force is far too small.** Six saved Large Magellanic Cloud (LMC) histories were evolved on each new ground rather than copied from the old one. No rotation curve, sky acceleration, mass or pattern speed was fitted in this step.

## Can the timing reverse the conclusion?

We derived a bound rather than trying a few favorable dates. Starting on either old ground and growing just this axisymmetric source monotonically to its full admissible strength:

- In linear theory the fine-grid inner polar change can never exceed +0.00005284 (occupation 0) or +0.00005190 (occupation 1). No adiabatic assumption. On the preceding frozen-field force box even allowing an arbitrary opposite-parity force, the necessary linear total variation is over 5,600 versus 1 for a one-way turn-on. This is not a proposed physical oscillation cycle.
- Including nonlinear self-gravity in the same isolated finite wave equations gives the looser **absolute** inner polar ceiling 0.00018268 or 0.00017979, and inner radial ceiling 0.00072736 or 0.00071548. By comparison, with the newly calculated LMC term included the remaining even polar contribution is at least +0.14584, and the remaining inward radial magnitude at least 0.54214. The polar ceiling is below **0.13%** of the shortfall. The bound applies to stellar assembly alone; the satellite was propagated separately in linear response, not jointly in that nonlinear inequality.

## Accuracy and next discriminating test

Independent increments reproduce the first and second force derivatives to 8.0×10⁻¹⁰ and 2.0×10⁻⁸ relative. Subtracting absolute equilibria directly loses digits in the smallest curvature (up to 2.33%); both raw and repaired diagnostics remain available. Source quadrature changes the stationary forces by ≤8.3×10⁻⁶ relative. Satellite time refinement and increasing the fine basis from 64 to 96 change each force by ≤4.93×10⁻⁶ and ≤1.11×10⁻⁵ relative, respectively. On the two *spatial* setups the tiny stationary polar changes differ by up to 8.46×10⁻⁵ relative and the coarsest-to-finest satellite change reaches 8.71×10⁻⁵: not every component meets the original 2×10⁻⁵ precision target on the coarse setup. The direct local gradient of the angularly truncated source is not the full direct stellar force; the independent Newton ring check and the original full direct bar are retained. No band or tolerance was widened.
