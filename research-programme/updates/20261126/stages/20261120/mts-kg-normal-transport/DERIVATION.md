## Derive the carrier, rather than minimizing its energy

Use proper normal time (dot), signature −+++, and the parent's convention K = −3H/a, with H the conformal Hubble rate. The real free massive scalar satisfies ḧ−Kḣ+(m²−∆γ)h=0. Write its complex carrier whose real part is h, and let J=(det γ)^(1/4). Since Ĵ/J=−K/2, y=Jh satisfies

    ÿ+(m²+L)y=0,    L=−J ∆γ J⁻¹ + K̇/2−K²/4.

The spatial term is an operator, not a local squared frequency. Seek the slow positive-frequency graph ẏ=Ay in the operator Riccati equation Ȧ+A²+m²+L=0. Its leading terms are −im−iL/(2m)−L̇/(4m²); the frozen next spatial term is +iL²/(8m³). This is an adiabatic preparation, not a proof of uniqueness of the exact graph or convergence of its series. Restoring the field divides y by J, hence adds K/2 to its logarithmic derivative. Omitting that term would miss homogeneous amplitude drift even at zero spatial momentum.

Let µ=am, p=∇V, ∇θ=µp and write the logarithmic normal derivative in units of m as ζ−iν. At fixed local phase-ellipse area, m²νA²/2 = ρ̄ n/u⁶. To leading order log A = D + constant, D=(log n)/2−3 log u. On the initial slice γᵢⱼ=a²u⁴δᵢⱼ, the real and imaginary parts of the spatial KG operator on A exp(iθ) give

    δ≡ν−1 = {p²/u⁴ + [C−u⁻⁴(∆D+|∇D|²+2∇log u·∇D)]/µ²}/2
    ζ = −[3H+u⁻⁴(∆V+(∇log n−4∇log u)·p)]/(2µ)
    C=a²(K̇/2−K²/4)=(E+S−3H²)/4 + (B:B)/(2u¹²).

Here ∆ is the reference flat 3D Laplacian, S is the trace of spatial stress scaled by 8πGa², E is energy in the same units. Raychaudhuri for the chosen normal gives a²K̇=3H²+B:B/u¹²+(E+S)/2. A homogeneous dust background therefore has C=0, not −9H²/4; for a general flat FLRW background C=3P/4 with P the rescaled pressure. In the EdS case h ∝ cos(mt+φ)/t solves the homogeneous equation exactly and has ζ=−1/(mt). In flat space a single travelling mode gives ν²=1+p² exactly. The coefficient of p² in the equation above is its Taylor coefficient, not an amplitude chosen from the tide.

The spatial gradient of the reconstructed amplitude **in the stress** includes −∇log ν: g=∇log n−6∇log u−∇log ν. Our retained stress is the actual phase integral of this prepared pair of quadratures, not a Taylor series in δ in which the drift-squared contribution could disappear. The higher-order change −(log ν)/2 in D inside the graph equation itself is not solved to all orders. The density is kept positive, and only the first quadrupole variation about each spherical preparation is taken; contractions are angularly differentiated before the axis is evaluated. There is no angular averaging of the signed tidal observable.

## What is controlled in the normal graph?

Nine exact flat-space interfering two-mode preparations check phase and amplitude together. A uniform single-mode dispersion test alone would miss amplitude drift. The second-order Taylor remainder of √(1+s), s≥0, is bounded by s³/16; apply this to each mode and divide by the actual complex field amplitude at the probe. This fixture has no nodes there. The saved inhomogeneous comparison evaluates the frozen L² term pointwise: for w=Ly/(m²y), L²y/(m⁴y)=w²−[∆w+2(∇D+∇log u+iµp)·∇w]/(µ²u⁴). The real and imaginary parts are carried separately from the angular variation. Multiple differentiation widths and two grids are saved. These are next terms, not an upper bound on the full asymptotic series.

On the sampled FLRW case use q=iẏ/(my)−1, l=(k²+3P/4)/µ². With q₀=√(1+l)−1, the exact time-dependent mode equation is (q−q₀)′=iµ[2(1+q₀)(q−q₀)+(q−q₀)²]−q₀′. The first transport term is −i l′/[4µ(1+l)]. Integrate this small difference rather than subtracting the carrier; compare two initial preparations, time-step sizes and sampling densities on the same interval. This tests the time derivative in homogeneous geometry; it does **not** measure the full inhomogeneous L̇, nor exclude other microscopic momenta on these clouds.
