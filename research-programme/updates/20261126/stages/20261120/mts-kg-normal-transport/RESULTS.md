# Does fixing the real wave's initial momentum change the predicted tide?

**Yes, but very little on the retained slow branch.** At redshift 86.11, compare the same 15.532 kpc proper axial ruler on the positive and negative seed and on the unhosted control. Solving for the field's amplitude drift and frequency rather than assigning the leading local oscillator quadrature changes the host-only axial tide by **8.85 and 8.46 parts per billion of its earlier magnitude**, respectively. It weakens both signs. It does not explain the ~361–363 ppm GR/Newton difference measured on those same initial data. These are predictions for prepared matter clouds, not measurements of the present Galaxy.

Thus the current supplies 8.15% and 5.73% of the small correction. It cannot be dropped on the grounds that the rest energy is stationary with respect to frequency. Against the *previous GR-minus-Newton difference*, the correction is +0.00245% and +0.00233% of that difference, not 0.00245% of the entire tide. Restoring the same corrected scalar quadratures on the pressure-off comparator too leaves only +1.63e−26 and −1.58e−26 in the correction to the host's pressure-on minus pressure-off contrast. **Where the result comes from.** The real Klein–Gordon equation fixes a leading logarithmic derivative on the specified slow, positive-frequency branch. At fixed phase-ellipse area, amplitude drift and frequency shift alter rest energy quadratically; drift times the density gradient alters current linearly. For the spherical part of the two clouds at the central probe, the fractional frequency shifts are +1.041e−9 and −6.604e−10; drift is −1.0450e−6 and −1.0705e−6, straddling homogeneous dilution −1.05775e−6. The fixed-area homogeneous energy change is 1.20827e−18 conformal Mpc⁻² and is included in the expansion constraint. These are not new coupling constants. Across the 5 probes, both signs, both meshes and both differencing choices, the largest disagreement is below 0.1 ppm.
