# A finite-ruler signal survives, and the local matter part is predictable

**In this prepared cosmological cloud, the finite-width tidal measurement separates a small but reproducible excess over Newtonian gravity. Roughly two thirds is the explicit local energy/stress of the moving matter. A calculation using only the Newtonian histories and the retained scalar action predicts that part to within 0.45%.** This is a conditional simulation result, not an observational detection or a theory-wide error bound.

Same starting redshift 86.11, signed overdense/underdense hosts, 273.43 proper years, pressure-on minus matched pressure-off with the unhosted signal removed. The quantity is the *change in that additional tidal signal*, not the total gravitational force. Preserve the 15.532 kpc point ruler; the two additional ideal detectors average over proper axial distance with compact weights fixed before calculating their outputs. Their half-widths are 2.33 and 4.66 proper kpc. No MTS coupling, gravitational normalization, force band or matter preparation was fitted.

## What supplies the two thirds?

For the real, phase-averaged massive field at the angular order used here, the gradient contribution to radial trace-free stress is exactly −4 times its contribution to the tidal term −E/3. Combining the two leaves a fixed coefficient +½ multiplying density times the squared radial gradients of wave phase and log amplitude (in carrier units). That coefficient comes from the canonical stress tensor. It is not inferred from the 261–284 ppm offsets. In the four measurements the amplitude gradients supply **110–119 ppm**, the heavy bulk phase **66–71 ppm**, and the normal amplitude/frequency correction about **−0.004 ppm**. Ordinary dust is about −0.1 ppm after the host and switch subtraction. The largest explicit matter term is the spatially changing **amplitude**, not an anomalous clock frequency. See [derivation](DERIVATION.md) and microscopic terms (not bundled here).

The rest is not a single missing force. In the narrow detector, changing from Newtonian coordinate rest mass to the local relativistic proper rest-density term gives −434/−422 ppm on the positive/negative hosts. The intrinsic-plus-extrinsic geometry relative to the Newtonian nonlocal field gives +516/+513 ppm. These leave approximately +82/+91 ppm together; the direct surrounding species' energy and stress add less than 0.03 ppm in magnitude. Across the two widths the rest-plus-geometry remainder is about **82–98 ppm**. These are terms in the same solution and ruler, not independent interventions. The fully decomposed secular rate closes to within 0.00066 ppm of the Newtonian change. Source budget with both input meshes (not bundled here). The saved Newtonian dynamics already include wave dispersion: this is a comparison of how the evolving matter *gravitates*, not of wave against no wave.

## Which numerical issues were actually resolved?

- **Finite detectors:** all 44 default-frame width/sign/configuration comparisons keep the same sign of the GR excess. The two curvature readouts differ by at most **6.14 ppm of the whole tidal change**, below the unchanged 20 ppm threshold. Four/eight-point spatial quadrature within cells and halving time samples change the totals by at most 0.064 ppm. Across both curvature readings, both signs and the alternative incident velocity/clock prescriptions tested, the finite offsets range from **260 to 315 ppm**. These are numerical variations, not probabilistic confidence intervals. All frame/curvature pairs (not bundled here).
- **Point detectors:** using a shared radial derivative can bring the central point readings within 20 ppm on one mesh, but it does **not** survive all refinements or changing to free radial shear evolution. The staggered method eliminates the spurious alternating null mode, but does not by itself fix pointwise second derivatives of the saved profiles. The original 12 signed tide values on the matched replay agree exactly; the buffer check had zero effect at equal resolution. The finite measurement is a second observable, not a retroactive pointwise pass. [Point results](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `point-comparison.csv`) · matched replay/buffer (not bundled here).
- **Independent constraint transport:** integration by parts lets the momentum defect predict its contribution without differentiating a noisy measured momentum profile. Its error is at most **0.045 ppm of the full finite tidal change**, but up to **4.51% of the small constraint signal itself**. It does not pass a uniform 20-ppm-on-the-defect test. The separate diagnostics retain that failure. [Finite constraint budget](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `finite-ruler-comparison.csv`) · [explicit gates](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `checks.csv`).
- **Controls:** non-polynomial off-constraint profiles at three widths converge with radial refinement; some narrow-profile gates fail at coarse resolution. The moving proper-detector check uses an independent analytic tensor and momentum flux. On evolving analytic dust spacetime both curvature readings agree with exact tides to within 0.53 ppm at 80 cells/Mpc across tested methods. The two independently initialized native all-cold evolutions give byte-identical complete point and finite-width tables. No observational data were added. Analytic dust (not bundled here) · [proper window controls](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `proper-window-controls.csv`) · staggered profile tests (not bundled here).
