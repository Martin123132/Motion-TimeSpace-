# Predicting the gravity of an overdensity and an underdensity from the same equations

**New result:** a second-order calculation of the spherical environment's effect on the same native cosmological quadrupole predicts the force for **both signs of the starting cloud**. The negative sign was frozen before its finite-amplitude evolution. The cosmological axis, 99 Fourier modes, heavy mass, common gravity normalization and all starting conditions other than the sign of the spherical excess were held fixed. No force or galaxy datum set a coefficient.

We extended the coupled evolution from z≈97.29 to **z=86.11**. Here is the conditional environmental contribution at **1.353 comoving Mpc** at the last epoch. For the wave, this is the difference between separately evolved pressure-on and pressure-off perturbations on the same heavy background, after removing each one's spatially unperturbed Fourier reference. For the cold baseline the pressure switch makes exactly zero difference; its nonzero **absolute** environmental force is tested instead. Errors are |prediction−evolution|/|evolution|, not observational uncertainties.

The wave's environmental radial force is −1.0758×10⁻¹⁴ Mpc⁻¹ for the overdensity and +1.1574×10⁻¹⁴ for the underdensity. This is not an exact sign flip: the even term has the same sign for both. For the reversed wave seed the second-order term reduces the endpoint error at **all nine designated physical radii** for both components. Across those probes the largest remaining radial error is about 0.46%. Improving a coefficient is not the same as meeting the original 20 ppm force target. ## What actually supplies the exterior tide?

- **First, a boundary test with no fitted inputs.** At 36 Mpc the standalone small box misses the heavy environmental radial correction by 3.98% against the 96-Mpc run. Adding the omitted exterior gravity after the fact leaves 3.66%. Feeding the true exterior monopole and quadrupole potentials to the dynamics still leaves 4.02% on a common restarted state. Transmitting the matter state as well leaves 0.0186% (186 ppm); halving the time step retains that residual. For the cold absolute source, the corresponding full transfer leaves only 0.0284 ppm. The shell gravity itself agrees with an independent elliptic solve; it is not the main missing wave information. Rate identity (not bundled here) · signed force breakdown (not bundled here).
- **Then a prediction without any transmitted exterior measurement.** The conserved-current and momentum equations give ordinary exterior density proportional initially to elapsed conformal time squared. Pressure-on minus pressure-off *local* density starts at **fourth order** outside the initially populated collar: the prepared spherical gravity must distort the cosmological tide before a locally changed dispersive density appears there. This differs from the **quadratic** law for the total wave force with nonzero primordial density. On the original implementation the supposedly constant density had a numerically nonzero derivative; the resulting false quadratic term was calculated from the discrete generator, not fitted. Computing with density excesses eliminates this term. Time doubling then gives approximately ×16 rather than about ×4. The frozen fourth derivatives in the 42–48 and 48–60 Mpc shells agree within 20 ppm on the 0.25-Mpc mesh in both tested boxes; the nearer 38–42 coefficient and the finest-mesh early estimates do not all meet that target. [Launch checks](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `balanced-startup-checks.csv`) · [false term and control](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `constant-gradient-control.csv`).
- **The calculation found two physically distinct regions.** In a 120-Mpc domain, the simple exterior-flow prediction gets the final wave-only sources in 42–48, 48–60 and 72–96 Mpc to about **11, 2.4 and 2 ppm**, respectively. At 72–96 the apparent error was 31% when the numerical box ended at 96. Moving that wall does **not** fix 36–38, immediately outside the original preparation: the simple prediction there is −1.123×10⁻²³ versus −3.067×10⁻²³ in the live calculation (weighted density integral, Mpc⁻²). The near-collar difference accounts for essentially the entire 36–96 mismatch once the outer wall is moved. Predicted vs live shells (not bundled here).
- **Deriving the collar response recovers the missing term.** The first variation with the actual spatial density and velocity taper predicts the whole 36–96 integral to **13 ppm**, instead of a 73.8% miss from the purely exterior model. The second variation predicts −2.6341×10⁻²³ Mpc⁻² (positive seed). Both signs agree with separately evolved 0.20-Mpc meshes to about **3.1–3.3 ppm**, with the original forecast fixed on the 0.25-Mpc mesh. This is a finite-shell convergence result. Attempts to assign a percentage to transport from inside versus outside the cut separately encounter opposite terms over 300,000 times the net: do not interpret those arbitrary split percentages as a physical partition. - **Physical meaning.** This additional shell potential is an ordinary trace-free tide in the common metric. At the endpoint the overdense wave-minus-disabled 36–96 contribution gives about **−1.01×10⁻²⁶ m/s² per physical kpc of separation along the tidal axis**; the total on-branch *environmental contribution from this shell* gives +8.94×10⁻²² in the same units. The two transverse effects are each minus half the axial value. These are predictions for an ideal freely falling pair in this simulated environment, not the acceleration of the whole host or a Milky Way measurement. Eigenvalues and units (not bundled here).
