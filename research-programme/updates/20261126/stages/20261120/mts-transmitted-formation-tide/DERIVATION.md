## The metric and the matter carry different information

A density multipole outside a spherical cut R gives a homogeneous interior solution of the Poisson equation, regular at the origin. For the retained P₂ angular component it is Φ_ext=−r²P₂ O/(10a), where O=∫_R^L S(s)ds/s and S=Σ C_s e_s. Four measurements distinguish it from a change of the interior matter: (i) its radial acceleration along the axis is linear in r; (ii) its polar acceleration at π/4 is −3/4 of that radial value; (iii) on an unchanged wide state the narrow and wide elliptic solves differ by this harmonic, also below the cut; (iv) its addition to the force after an evolution changes no density. The source includes local deviations from each reference. Noncompact Fourier fields already contribute through the reference and must not be counted again. The spherical exterior potential is separately a spatial constant, −∫_R^L Σ C_s s(n_s−1) ds/(2a); it matters for phase matching, not direct differential acceleration.

On the same mesh the evolved response can be coupled by supplying exterior monopole/quadruple potentials in its Euler equations and matter endpoint histories in its evolution. Those histories were sampled independently in a 96-Mpc solve. This measures what was missing, not the ability to predict an unobserved environment. An endpoint-only potential condition does not generally give the exterior matter speed. The volume on either side of a shared node contributes to its numerical velocity gradient: the leftover discrete continuity-rate difference on the last inner element contains (U_wide−U_left)(e+D)R² w_end times the endpoint derivative of the test function, divided by its mass and ℋ. The generator test (not bundled here) distinguishes this term from the larger physical effects. We do not claim exact dynamical transparency from a boundary transfer.

## A locally interpretable observable

For the dimensionless Newtonian potential of this external shell, r²P₂=(2z²−x²−y²)/2 in comoving Cartesian coordinates. For nearby freely falling slow bodies, subtract the FLRW homogeneous acceleration; the peculiar relative acceleration is minus the Hessian of c²Φ with respect to physical separation. Thus the axial acceleration per metre of axial separation is c²O/(5a³ Mpc²), and each transverse eigenvalue is minus one half of it (in s⁻² when Mpc is converted to metres). The trace vanishes in the source-free interior contribution. An additive constant, common acceleration, or choice of origin in this quadratic potential does not affect that readout. This uses the inherited **Newtonian limit of the common metric**; it is a quadrupolar source prediction, not a relativistic closure inferred from the Newtonian simulation. The wave fluid itself has a pressure force; it is the test bodies that follow geodesics. For the distinction between a scalar weak-field perturbation in FLRW and a full Einstein solution see [Ma & Bertschinger](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9506072). The result is conditional on the finite shell and the chosen initial tide, not an observed clock, local gravity measurement or dark-matter detection.
