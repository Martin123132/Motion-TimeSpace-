# Closing the kinetic-stress hypothesis on a retained fixed-occupation branch

This is the positive quadratic, finite-mass, nonrelativistic Schrödinger–Poisson branch already used in the heavy-bath galactic calculation (not bundled here), not a new matter action. It yields a sufficient physical condition for the smooth-detector bound in [the derivation](DERIVATION.md). No galaxy normalization, radial force, or force band is used to determine a coefficient.

Use mass-normalized u (or a list of occupied uⱼ with weights absorbed) with ∫Σ|uⱼ|²=M, κ=ℏ/m. K=(κ²/2)∫Σ|∇uⱼ|², and W=−(G/2)∫ρ(x)ρ(y)/|x−y| dx dy. For each centre y the translated three-dimensional Hardy inequality gives ∫|u|²/|x−y|²≤4∫|∇u|². It follows by integrating div[(x−y)/|x−y|²]=1/|x−y|² against |u|² and applying Cauchy–Schwarz, first on compact smooth states and then in H¹. Another Cauchy–Schwarz and summation over modes bounds the potential integral ∫ρ(x)/|x−y|dx by 2√M (Σ||∇uⱼ||²)¹ᐟ², uniformly in y. In specific units k=K/M, w=W/M, v_B=GM/κ this proves w≥−b√k, **b=√2 v_B**, without assuming a stationary state or spherical symmetry.

If isolated and h=k+w is conserved, then √k≤[b+√(b²+4h)]/2. Thus fixed initial finite energy and fixed physical mass bound the anisotropic stress at *all* times of the regular evolution. It does not require knowing the instantaneous profile. For an external force use internal energy instead of requiring a choice of zero of potential: the wave continuity and Hamilton equations give M h′=−∫j·∇Φ_ext and ||j||₁≤√(2MK). With I(T)=∫₀ᵀ||∇Φ_ext||∞dt, set Y=h+b²/2≥k/2. Then |Y′|≤2||∇Φ_ext||∞√Y (use Y+ε at zero). Consequently

    k(t) ≤ 2 [√(h(0)+b²/2) + I(T)]²   for 0≤t≤T.

The force integral has units of velocity, like √h and b. Uniform inertial accelerations need not have a bounded *potential*. Spatially unbounded quadratic tidal approximations, open-boundary mass or energy flux, and an infinite homogeneous density cannot be silently included in this isolated formula.

The positive canonical gradient stress satisfies ∫|Sᵀᶠ|_F≤2K. Substituting the bound above into the smooth detector inequality gives an O(q⁻¹ᐟ²) TT curvature response for this finite-energy channel even if it oscillates arbitrarily fast in physical time, with explicit coefficient no larger than 4 M T C_W [√(h(0)+b²/2)+I(T)]² in the same stress units (apply the original Einstein stress conversion when using SI). For a difference of two solutions sum the bounds; for a tangent perturbation use a bound on its derivative/perturbation energy, not division of a finite-difference bound by an arbitrarily small amplitude. Occupation alone permits arbitrarily energetic phases; it is **occupation plus finite initial energy and controlled external work** that gives the result. This is a statement about the retained SP subfamily and its leading stress-to-metric coupling, not global regularity of Einstein–Klein–Gordon, a mass gap, or a bound on a pressureless self-gravitating fluid.

For a changing source distinguish the spatial-metric response Δh/2 from the **full linear TT tidal acceleration**, h″/2=Δh/2+η⁴Sᵀᵀ in the parent's positive-acceleration convention. Its normalized *secular change* also includes q⁻¹〈P_TT w,S(T)−S(0)〉. This vanishes identically in the frozen calculation. Without time differentiability it is bounded by C₀[||S(T)||₁+||S(0)||₁]/q, C₀=||(1−ℓ²Δ)w||₂/√(8πℓ³). Thus the energy-bounded metric-plus-local TT term is no greater than 2Kmax T C_W/√q + 4Kmax C₀/q in the stated source units. Endpoint coefficients (not bundled here) use the same fixed ruler. This is the *TT* part of the ordinary measured tide; longitudinal gravity, moving-ruler terms and relativistic remainders are not silently included.
