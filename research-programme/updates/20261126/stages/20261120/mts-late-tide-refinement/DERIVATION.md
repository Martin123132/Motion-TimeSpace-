# Where the numerical centre enters, and why the true stress transient need not survive the Newton limit

## 1. Two independent routes to a signal at the ruler

The observable is an integral of radial curvature at fixed *proper* radius from the centre, not a trace at fixed coordinate radius. Write the monopole/quadrupole parts of the radial metric factor as m₀,m₂, with dᵢ(r)=∫₀ʳmᵢdr. If the local tidal components are E₀,E₂, the detector quadrupole integrates
`W(d₀)(m₀ E₂+m₂ E₀)+W′(d₀)m₀d₂E₀`.
Thus even an error wholly inside the aperture's *inner edge* can change its physical location. To isolate the effect, hold only the change in d₀,d₂ accumulated below a fixed interior coordinate radius at its launch value. If those changes are a,b, respectively, the full-minus-frozen-path integrand is the integral from u=0 to 1 of
`a W′(d)(m₀ E₂+m₂E₀) + [a W″(d)(d₂−(1−u)b)+b W′(d)]m₀E₀`
with d=d₀−(1−u)a. Keep all local metric/curvature fields unchanged. This computes a small difference directly, including the mixture with the monopole. The length change uses expm1 on the evolved change, not a subtraction of two nearly equal lengths. Remove the corresponding launch slope in each history before comparing *curvatures*. The genuine primary measurement always uses the full origin-to-aperture path. This diagnostic is not a regulator.

The centre growth operator and regular spherical/tensor Taylor conditions are in [the pre-test note](CENTRE-FORECAST.md). In cell coordinates its largest exponents λ result from linearizing the actual constrained Einstein evolution at a flat, source-free centre. Changing the cell spacing h multiplies the rate by 1/h. A pure regular constant spatial quadrupole has v=t=2s/3. Independent component-wise even parity allows additional, nonsmooth centre modes. An off-constraint failure amplified by that operator is not physical collapse. The eigenvectors and the projected physical histories are tested separately.

A local numerical treatment of the centre restricts the **metric accelerations** to Cartesian tensors smooth through the origin. On the innermost stencil of six or eight cells the fixed volume-weighted projection reproduces all allowed r⁰,r²,r⁴,r⁶ monomials exactly. It operates in physical tensor components: for A=exp(2ell₀), B=exp(2bend₀), use x=Aell₂, y=Bbend₂, v̂=Bv, t̂=Bt. Then t̂, v̂−t̂, x−y−2v̂+t̂/2 have orbital degrees 0,2,4. A−B is a spherical metric anisotropy of order r². This is not a new origin cutoff in physical units: the width shrinks with spacing. The initial data aren't sampled again or changed. A projection can suppress rather than eliminate a growing residual; calculate the remaining eigenvalues and do not infer unrestricted convergence from two grids.

There are two implementation tests. The all-metric version projects also the scalar trace; it is a comparison only. The trace-preserving version restricts the **trace-free** accelerations and solves the trace/anisotropy pair with the *unmodified physical Raychaudhuri* K′. That leaves the Klein–Gordon rest-normal transformation, all matter right-hand sides and the entire exterior stencil unchanged. In particular the canonical variable includes −K/(2μ): changing K′ alone while reusing its old value in the matter equation would be an inconsistent off-constraint evolution. No such substitution is made in the trace-preserving test.

## 2. Causal mechanical stress, not an adjustable Newtonian constant

This is a leading linearized calculation in local Cartesian units, not a replacement for the Einstein evolution. Projection onto its transverse-traceless part removes longitudinal terms and all isotropic stresses. ## 3. A bound that allows time dependence and resonant forcing

For a time-dependent physical stress, Duhamel integration by parts replaces the square bracket on S by
`−S(T)+cos(T√(qA))S(0)+∫cos((T−t)√(qA)) S′(t) dt`.
The TT projection is implicit. For uniformly bounded L² endpoints and total time variation this proves O(1/q) after pairing with a fixed detector. It is not sufficient to invoke this for fast oscillators whose variation grows with q.

There is a weaker but more general *finite-energy* bound. Without differentiating the source at all, the detector response is
`−q⁻¹ᐟ²∫〈A¹ᐟ² sin((T−t)√(qA))P_TT w,S(t)〉dt`.
For P₂ normalized to Cᵧ=∫P₂²dΩ=4π/5, w=(W/(Cᵧ r²))P₂(nn−I/3) extracts exactly ∫W B dr. Hence its magnitude is at most q⁻¹ᐟ² ||∇w||₂ ∫||S(t)||₂dt. A smooth finite-width detector even permits only *spatial L¹* of the stress: with any fixed ℓ>0, the coefficient is
`||∇(1−ℓ²Δ)w||₂ / √(8πℓ³) × ∫ ||S(t)||₁ dt`.
Indeed spatial Fourier Cauchy–Schwarz gives ||f||∞≤||(1−ℓ²Δ)f||₂/√(8πℓ³); the TT projector and sine are contractions and commute with the derivatives. The compact W is C³ and its support avoids r=0. For a linear perturbation the mixed products are bounded by the product of the two gradient L² norms. Dust kinetic stress admits the corresponding velocity-energy bound.
