# A tidal initial state on the retained branch

## Specify the environment without a Galactic force
Use the original spherical +2σ scalar selector and physical transfers on the anchorA 1.3530776523 comoving Mpc aperture. The cold branch is evaluated on exactly the same primordial modes, not on a new cold-selected peak. A spherical overdensity does not fix a traceless local tide. For a predeclared axis z introduce the Gaussian variable Q with Fourier kernel S(k)P₂(k̂·z), where S(k)=−k²U_i(k)W(kR), W is the original spherical top hat and U_i is the heavy-branch native Newtonian lapse at a_i=0.01. The trace-free axial Hessian of the smoothed physical potential in c=1 units is 2Q/(3a_i²). Positive means extra compression along z. This definition does not assume the axis is the Galactic pole or the principal eigenvector.

Use the retained log-k integration weights w = Δln k times the primordial dimensionless power with trapezoidal endpoints. Angle averages give 〈P₂〉=0 and 〈P₂²〉=1/5. Thus Q is independent of the initial scalar selection **in this isotropic Gaussian linear model**; σ_Q²=Σw S²/5. A linear observable with Fourier kernel K(k)P₂(k̂·z) has mean Σw KS/(5σ_Q) conditional on Q=+σ_Q, and conditional variance Σw(K−S mean/σ_Q)²/5. Importantly, model differences use K_heavy−K_cold before taking this variance. A spherical condition alone gives zero mean quadrupole, not zero realization scatter. This applies the [constrained Gaussian field method](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9507024); that method is not an MTS derivation. The new calculation is the projection through these retained paired transfer functions. Metric and velocity conventions are detailed in the upstream derivation (not bundled here) and agree with the scalar-gauge framework of [Ma & Bertschinger](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9506072).

## Density, peculiar velocities and actual material separation
Put b=wS/σ_Q. In the axisymmetric mean, a field X=X₀+X₂P₂ has quadrupole X₂(r)=−Σ b T_X j₂(kr): the minus is the i² angular Fourier factor. For conserved coordinate number D, use its native transfer, not the bare synchronous density. The irrotational Newtonian peculiar velocity has potential V_k=−θ_k/k², so V₂=Σ b θ j₂/k², v_r,2=∂r V₂, v_θ=(V₂/r)∂θP₂. The wave phase perturbation is a_i m V₂ at preparation. The lapse also gives a signed independent polar acceleration at 45°: +(3/2)c² U₂/(a r); radial axis-minus-equatorial acceleration is −(3/2)c² U₂′/a. These are early common-gravity accelerations in a comoving patch, not the late Milky Way force bands.

Initially place a sphere of tagged material at comoving radius r. To first order its difference in axial versus equatorial radial displacement divided by r is (3/2r)∫ ∂rV₂ dη. With D′=−θ and η conformal time this is −(3/2r)Σ b [D(a)−D_i]j₂′(kr)/k. The first formula was independently integrated from actual native velocities; the second used only the two density endpoints. Use dη=d ln a/ℋ with native conformal Hubble rate ℋ. For a general realization these formulas refer to the l=2 projection, not to differences including higher multipoles.

The equal-volume polar caps |cosθ|>1/2 and equatorial belt have a mean P₂ difference of 3/4. Integrating the continuity equation for the fixed ball gives (∫r²D₂ dr)′=−R²∂rV₂(R)+6∫V₂ dr. The second term is flux through the angular boundaries and cannot be dropped even when total first-order occupation is conserved. The independent density-to-current test compares this net with the actual angle-projected evolution.

## Let the initial tide supply its own axes
A random traceless symmetric 3×3 matrix T represents the initial tide with Var(nᵀTn)=1 for any fixed unit n. Let X represent the corresponding random fixed-axis shape projection. Isotropy gives X=βT+E, where β is the positive-unit-tide response and E is an independent traceless Gaussian with axial rms s from the residual calculation above. Radius displacement along n relative to its spherical average is (2/3)nᵀXn. Label the initial most and least compressive eigenvectors by +,−. The signed material-radius contrast along them has conditional mean (2/3)β(λ₊−λ₋) and conditional scatter 2s/√3, since two orthogonal projections of E have correlation P₂(0)=−1/2.

In any Frobenius-orthonormal basis of the five traceless matrices the normalized T has coefficient variance 3/2. Diagonalizing rotates off-diagonal variations by eigenvalue differences, giving the product of the three differences as eigenvalue Jacobian. At fixed trace and Frobenius length the eigenvalues are therefore χ₅ cos(θ+2πj/3) in distribution. On the ordered sector θ∈[0,π/3] the normalized angular density is (3/2)sin(3θ), independent of χ₅. The mean eigenvalue gap is (9/2)√(2/π), giving a mean material contrast 3√(2/π)β. Integrating the remaining Gaussian scatter gives the probability of a positive contrast (independent radial integral and Student-t check). Apply this to the *paired* heavy-minus-cold tensor, not to two independent halo draws. Eigenvectors here belong to the initial common tide; they are not the eventual axes of either halo or the stellar disk.

## Initial state, exterior and finite-background tangent
Use the inherited nonlinear seed convention ψ₀=√n₀ exp(iS₀). At unit tidal variation, δψ₂=ψ₀[D₂/(2n₀)+ia_i m V₂]. Angular averaging 2Re(ψ₀*δψ₂)P₂ gives zero at each radius. Both signs give positive densities at preparation. The saved arrays are unwindowed regular profiles on 0–24 Mpc, not hard-wall boundary conditions. They specify an ensemble-mean initial state, not a single drawn realization including its residual.

For the linear quadrupole potential U₂ inside a spherical domain of radius L, the exterior contribution is A r² with A=[U₂′(L)+3 U₂(L)/L]/(5L). All interior sources give an exterior vacuum profile proportional to r⁻³; annihilating that profile at L gives A. The spherical Green integral for the actual interior is −[r⁻³∫₀ʳu⁴s₂du+r²∫ᵣᴸs₂du/u]/5 with s₂ the l=2 coefficient of ∇²U. Test it first on the exact lapse-equivalent source, then on Σ C_species D_species,2/(2a). The latter is only the nonrelativistic matter inventory: boundary matching cannot also repair missing relativistic interior source terms. Do not add A twice or set it by a Galactic band.

On the finite spherical seed n₀=1+D₀, perturb its density by D₂P₂ and velocity potential by V₂P₂. Define L₂=∂r²+(2/r)∂r−6/r². The coefficient equations on this common weak-field branch are

    ∂ηD₂ = −r⁻² ∂r[r²(n₀ V₂′ + D₂ V₀′)] + 6n₀ V₂/r²
    ∂ηV₂ + ℋV₂ = −U₂ − V₀′ V₂′ + (L₂ + (ln n₀)′∂r)(D₂/n₀)/(4a²m²).

In these two lines primes are radial derivatives. Drop only the last term for a pressureless species. For the wave it follows by differentiating ∇²√n/√n; it is not phenomenological pressure. The finite-seed check perturbs the complete nonlinear angular rates in opposite directions and projects them onto P₂. Its numerical tangent is independent of this analytic differentiation. The retained lapse at the initial slice is the same on either side of the finite-monopole comparison. This is an instantaneous derivative before crossing, not a late nonlinear simulation.
