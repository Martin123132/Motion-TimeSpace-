# A host-shape prediction before the satellite acts

**The retained wave patch is less anisotropically compressed than a matched cold patch in the same primordial tidal environment.** This follows from the original early density, velocity and lapse transfers; no Milky Way acceleration, disk orientation or halo amplitude entered the calculation. It is a conditional linear formation result, not a new fit to the eight Galactic bands.

### How much, and on what scale?

An initially spherical surface tags the matter at the existing 1.3531 comoving Mpc preparation radius at redshift 99. At each later epoch we measure **differences in its radial displacement divided by its initial radius**. These are not final axis ratios. For a predeclared axis with one rms of compressive initial tide, at z=10 the axial-minus-equatorial displacement is **−5.76% for the wave, −8.66% for cold**. The respective radial peculiar-velocity contrasts are −11.20 and −16.80 km/s. The matching direct peculiar-velocity integrations and endpoint density changes give the same material displacement. The ordinary matter and lapse are not held equal between the two model histories; it is the primordial realization and environment selector that are held equal.

There is no independently known Galactic disk orientation at z=99. A second calculation therefore uses the initial tide's own most- and least-compressive axes and integrates over all its Gaussian amplitudes and orientations. The ensemble mean material-radius difference between those axes is **−13.79% (wave) vs −20.72% (cold)** by z=10 at the reference radius. In the paired comparison the mean shift is **+6.94 percentage points**, with a **6.33-point ensemble standard deviation**. The wave has the less negative contrast in **86.38%** of these finite-band linear realizations; a separate calculation drawing and directly diagonalizing 131,072 paired Gaussian tensors gave **86.43%**. These are model probabilities, not a preference measured in galaxy data or a probability that MTS is true. The matched difference is already +1.74 points at a=0.03015, when the spherical mean contrasts are 0.100 and 0.149. At z=10 they reach 0.299 and 0.442; nonlinear evolution becomes increasingly important. z=2 rows are linear-transfer diagnostics only (the reference wave contrast exceeds unity).

### What is checked, and what changes next?

- **Actual transport:** 129 native time samples, 99 original common modes, 14 small saved packets. In all 18 branch/radius/time tests the density-forecast vs integrated-current shape difference is below **0.065 ppm**, and the fixed-ball angular mass difference from surface and lateral currents below **0.168 ppm**, both relative to the respective signed signal. Halving the time sampling changes the shape by at most 0.009 ppm. These are internal consistency checks, not physical error bars. The independent reduced Newtonian evolution misses the inherited 20 ppm on the reference z=10 shape by giving 36.3 and 29.2 ppm errors for wave and cold; no tolerance change.
- **An initial wave, not just a statistic:** supplied the regular density, potential-flow velocity and wave phase perturbations on the 0–24 Mpc grid, for both branches where applicable. The signed wave tangent recovers the density derivative, and its net occupation change vanishes at each radius after angular integration. These unwindowed regular profiles include a nonzero environmental tide; they must not simply be placed against a vacuum wall.
- **Exterior versus local gravity:** on the 24-Mpc box, setting the exterior tide to zero misses the initial quadrupole potential at the patch radius by **0.465% (wave) and 0.313% (cold)**. The independent interior-plus-exterior integral reproduces the native metric there. Even after matching the exterior, nonrelativistic matter alone misses the initial potential by **62 and 48 ppm** respectively; at z=10 this shrinks to **4.77 and 3.65 ppm**. The native exterior is known only conditional on these transfers, not from a measurement of our Galaxy.
- **First nonlinear test:** on the finite +2σ spherical seed, the initial local quadrupole density-rate magnitude at the reference radius is **10.20% greater for the wave and 14.61% greater for cold** than linearizing about the homogeneous state at the same tide. The respective velocity-potential rate plus expansion drag changes by 1.46% and 2.00%. Opposite angular perturbations of the full equations independently recover all 15 analytic local tangents. This derivative is not a measured correction at z=10. It explains why excellent numerical agreement within linear theory is not enough.

**Next calculation:** propagate this quadrupole tangent on the *evolving spherical formation background*, first across a short interval prior to shell crossing. Retain radial and polar mass currents, use the nonzero environmental potential at the outer boundary, and run the same cold control. Test a finite ±tidal amplitude against the tangent before extrapolating to a flattened Galactic halo. The old LMC result stays intact: on its two original backgrounds both MTS and static NFW reach only the two outer polar bands. This formation seed selects neither the actual Milky Way mass nor its orientation.
