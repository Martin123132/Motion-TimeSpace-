## 1. An exact exterior split

For the flat radial quadrupole operator Δ₂ and the coefficient u of P₂ define B=u′/r+3u/r², where primes here are comoving radial derivatives. Then B′=Δ₂u/r and (r³u)′=r⁴B. For −Δ₂u=s, the exterior prediction is

    B(R) = B(L) + ∫[R,L] s(r) dr/r .

An interior decaying exterior solution u=d/r³ drops out of B; a regular external u=b r² gives B=5b. The retained elliptic launch at L prescribes u′+3u/L from its native cosmological field, not u=0. If u_native=Σ w_k χ_k j₂(kr), then B_native(L)=Σ w_k χ_k k j₁(kL)/L. This follows from j₂′(x)+3j₂(x)/x=j₁(x); equivalently ∫[z,∞] j₂(x) dx/x=j₁(z)/z. The tail is evaluated to infinity for these known modes, not cut at a convenient radius. A separate oscillatory integration checks the identity. Solving the nonlinear initial constraint gives its actual s including the metric factors, graph matter and shear. We integrate only the exterior cells to predict the interior face and compare against left and right metric derivatives. The native contribution is nonzero per branch and cancels in the matched signed-host difference, not by setting it to zero.

## 2. Evolving the scalar boundary without measuring a slope there

    |M[L,U]| ≤ ||f||_(L,U) sqrt((L⁻³−U⁻³)/3)/5.
