# What the common coupling tests here

For the retained Newtonian wave sector, let rho_w be wave mass density, rho_b=rho_star+rho_gas the projected ordinary rest-mass density, and K(x,y)=−G/|x−y| with the specified isolated boundary. The mutual term in the energy is E_bw=∬rho_b(x) K(x,y) rho_w(y) dx dy, with **no 1/2** for the cross interaction: expanding (1/2)∫rho_total Φ_total gives two equal cross terms. Varying the baryon density gives Φ_w; varying the wave density gives Φ_b. Along a prescribed source history the explicit power into the wave energy (including Φ_b) is ∫rho_w ∂tΦ_b=∫Φ_w ∂t rho_b. This compares the matter-to-wave and wave-to-matter matrix elements using the *same* parent G, rather than fitting a second coefficient. It does not determine the numerical value of G or verify that the external CDM simulation responds dynamically to an MTS wave.

If the stellar and gaseous mass balances have transfer terms +S and −S at the same spacetime point, then ∂t rho_b=−div(J_star+J_gas) at leading, constant rest-mass order. Thus the work on a bounded region comes from ∫(J_star+J_gas)·∇Φ_w−∮Φ_w (J_star+J_gas)·dA, not from S. Conversely, moving the gas to another radius before forming stars can do work. Even a perfect mass history does not fix the velocity field: its divergence is constrained, its solenoidal part is not. Escape, winds or an omitted component can contribute a flux term; a spatially weighted transfer into a Milky Way analogue is not a measured flux. Source-work tests here do not close the total energy budget of the external hydrodynamic simulation.

In the metric weak-field benchmark, with signature (−,+,+,+), linearized harmonic-gauge Einstein equations read □hbar_μν=−16πG T_μν/c⁴. The quasi-static 00 equation with h00=−2Φ/c² and trace reversal gives ∇²Φ=4πG(T00+Σ_i Tii)/c². For slow cold matter the leading source is the rest-mass density used here. Thermal, velocity-stress, radiation, retardation and gravitomagnetic terms are not tabulated in this rest-mass calculation. Locally radiated binding energy is not an exact rest-mass-preserving conversion at that order: the escaping energy and its stress belong to the total balance. A Maxwell field has traceless stress, so T00+ΣTii=2u_EM in an orthonormal frame; the stresses of confining matter cannot be dropped from an isolated-system mass. This is a benchmark for a subsequent limit of the retained total stress, not a claim that Newtonian source reciprocity alone establishes the metric field equations. No second coupling is justified by a change in chemical composition.
