# What ordinary matter did in the independent simulation

Five snapshots from a single FIRE-2 Milky Way analogue, not five independent galaxies and not an observational validation set. Within 8.3 spherical kpc between snapshots 572 and 600 the gas mass falls from 7.89 to 7.39 billion solar masses while stellar mass rises from 58.58 to 59.68 billion. Net increase: about **0.60 billion**, not the **1.10 billion** one would infer from stars alone with fixed gas. At 30 kpc both phases increase. These are Eulerian mass budgets; no inference that a specific star was formed out of the missing gas. Full gas and star counts (including material outside the science aperture) sum to 355,127,083 particle instances across five times. Encoded byte-range traffic from the 20 source files was 6.307 GB, not 20 raw snapshot files stored on disk. The same particles may appear at multiple times. Project gas and stars first, before importing any halo potential.

The retained observed-source transfer varies roughly 38.94 billion solar masses of tapered terminal ordinary matter; outside the aperture and in any unclassified native census fraction the original gravity remains fixed. The midpoint vs two-point-per-axis integration of that tapered mass differs by 0.0141%. No negative matter density was introduced and unmeasured Milky Way history was not declared observed. For the original three source times, the potential-energy change evaluated on the fixed terminal ground, calculated from matter acting on the wave versus the wave acting on the same matter, agrees to 1.2×10⁻¹² (km/s)² or better. A stress test that relabels up to half the terminal gas as exactly co-spatial stars over the same history changes the final wave acceleration by less than 1.3×10⁻¹² in units of 10⁻¹⁰ m/s². The sectors separately change, but their mutual gravitational drive cancels. The field energy evaluated by subtracting two large absolute numbers has a higher noise floor in this null; the small-excess bound instead expands the energy around the terminal ground before subtracting.

The measured disk normal changes by 4.70 degrees over this window **in the simulation**. A physical nonrotating final-axis projection is not the same as stacking each snapshot face-on. For an exactly axisymmetric source rotated by β, the projected m=0 coefficient of degree l changes by the known factor P_l(cos β), by the spherical harmonic addition theorem. The earlier fixed-axis factors for l=2,4,8,16 are 0.990, 0.967, 0.883 and 0.593: the thin structure is more sensitive than its quadrupole. The saved audit checks this prediction against independent particle moments in both frames with explicit angular-bin bounds; the factor itself is not assumed for a nonaxisymmetric disk. The face-on source run is a sensitivity check, **not** a covariant transformation of the wave into a rotating frame without its inertial terms.
