# Derive the finite-geometry correction, rather than fitting a 1/q trend

At fixed canonical launch number n, a₀=log(n)/2 and δa=(Im z−ℓ−2b)/2 at first order. Let L₀f=D_b[r_f² D_f f]/r². Differentiating the same discrete amplitude operator as in the coupled evolution gives, before division by 2M², the first change in its mechanically rescaled pressure:

    L₀ δa + 2(D_c a₀)(D_c δa)
    + D_b[r_f² I_f(2b−ℓ) D_f a₀]/r² − (ℓ+2b)L₀ a₀ − 2ℓ(D_c a₀)².

The first line separates the volume and normal-frequency contributions to amplitude. The second changes the radial Laplacian and gradient norm. All fields come from the constrained launch, not the pressure readings. As a finite-speed proxy for the coefficient of 1/q, multiply each linear term by the source q, divide by the target q, and compare with the *actual curved-minus-flat pressure* of the target. This is a retrospective source transfer, not an independent blind validation. The first-order approximation is not imposed on either solution.

Both q=64 and q=128 source transfers reproduce the q=1024 correction, within 0.0165% **of the correction** in every signed width and readout (at worst 2.18 × 10⁻¹² of the native weak pressure). For the q=1024 target the volume change inside the wave amplitude supplies a signed fraction 0.911–1.099 of the measured correction. The geometry of the operator contributes −0.122 to +0.089, and the normal-frequency contribution is smaller here. These are signed contributions to a small difference; they need not be positive. For the independent native solver, let A be its fixed reduced-amplitude polynomial on each element. The mass-lumped nodal pressure measurement equals the weak stiffness contracted with the interpolated test I_h[W/(r+A)] and the lumped normalization. For the force-gradient reading substitute −W′ for W in the numerator. Holding A fixed but interpolating the test on 1, 2, 4 and 8 subdivisions reduces the maximum discrepancy from the continuous test to 1.681 × 10⁻³, 1.947 × 10⁻⁴, 1.75 × 10⁻⁷ and 1.37 × 10⁻⁸ respectively. With one subdivision the original native result is independently reproduced. This identifies a detector discretization effect, without modifying either force law or the parent mesh.
