# A prospective response coefficient and a disjoint-shell null

A recording of a successful Poisson solve does not by itself show how the metric will respond to a new disturbance. Here the perturbation, its sign, the three readouts and two amplitudes are fixed before the response test. The tested quantity is an **instantaneous derivative of the induced clustering potential after a weak potential impulse**, not the static force of externally added matter.

**Continuum derivation.** The shell contrast D_A=a[Φ(r₂)−Φ(r₁)] has source weight W_A(χ)=½[1/max(χ,r₁)−1/r₂] for χ<r₂, zero outside. Its derivative is −1/(2χ²) on the shell and zero elsewhere. In spherical symmetry it responds to all the retained clustering matter with the same metric coupling, C_s=8πG a³ρ̄_s. Apply a short *negative* external potential impulse −ε W_B in cosmic time. Dust receives Δp=+ε ∂_X W_B; the heavy wave receives a local phase exp(+im ε W_B). Both leave the density unchanged at the initial instant. Differentiating the subsequent currents gives

    d/dε [dD_A/dℓ] at ε=0 = (1/(Ha²)) Σ_s C_s ∫ W′_A W′_B dI_s.

dI_s is the actual positive radial mass measure of each species, not a stationary occupation fitted to get the answer. The same result follows by differentiating the kinetic energy under two independent phase/momentum impulses. The wave mass in the phase cancels the inverse kinetic mass. No adjustable source coefficient is left once the retained common-metric Newton normalization is specified.

The matrix is symmetric and positive semidefinite in the continuum. A and B overlapping gives a nonnegative cross response; A and C with disjoint gradients give **exactly zero first-order instantaneous cross response**, irrespective of the detailed mass profile. Gravity is not being made local by fiat: the *potential difference* is nonlocal in density, but its change after a potential impulse depends on the product of the two kernel gradients. The null does not apply after subsequent finite-time propagation, to an imposed external mass contribution counted in the readout, or to arbitrary relativistic perturbations.

**Exact discrete prediction used in the blind comparison.** Use the actual finite-element W from the common-metric action, not the analytic continuum W substituted for it. For a particle sheet with consistent mass matrix M and g_A=∫ L(q) ∂_X W_A dM, its contribution to the response is C_s g_Aᵀ M⁻¹ g_B/(Ha²) with prescribed endpoint momenta held fixed. For the wave, u=χψ, u−χ has zero endpoint values, K is the positive sine kinetic operator with eigenvalues k². The wave contribution is

    (C_h h/(Ha²)) Re{〈W_A u,K(W_B u)〉 − Σ W_A W_B u* K(u−χ)}.

These are second variations of the retained kinetic actions. The first variation with weight A recovers dD_A/dℓ from the variational current. Although the local continuum null is exact, the spectral kinetic operator and projection of forces onto a finite folded sheet can be nonlocal on the numerical grid. An off-diagonal residual is therefore **predicted and measured**, not suppressed by construction. In the continuum this expression reduces to the gradient product above.

**Independent experiment.** Save the matrix, then at the two actual saved epochs of both models apply the frozen plus and minus impulses to the original wave and all streams. Measure the potentials from a new self-gravitating evolution for each sign, at small positive and negative log-time offsets. The cross-difference divided by 4εΔℓ tests both the response sign and normalization without using the predicted matrix to calculate that readout. Two meshes share the same physical initial state at each probe. Two amplitudes correspond to at most 0.03 or 0.12 km/s nominal peculiar speed on the tested spatial generator. The output includes all temporal refinements, discrepancies rescaled by diagonal responses, and the unapproximated continuum null separately. These are synthetic impulse experiments on formed states, not observations of a pulse in the Milky Way.
