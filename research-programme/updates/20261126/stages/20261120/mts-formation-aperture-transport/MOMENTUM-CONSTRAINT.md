# A weak-field gravitational momentum constraint on the actual history

The quantity tested here is a **difference of metric potentials driven by momentum**, not a halo fit. Work in c=1 units and cosmic time, with χ comoving, weak potentials and nonrelativistic peculiar motions. Use the scalar longitudinal gauge with lapse perturbation Ψ and spatial perturbation Φ. Retain their common Newtonian value to leading order, not exact equality at all orders. Define D=a[Φ(χ₂)−Φ(χ₁)] for two fixed comoving shells χ₁=0.1, χ₂=0.2 Mpc. Choosing fixed comoving shells removes the moving-aperture term present in the separate proper-radius test. Absolute potential zero and exterior spherical shells cancel.

For each normalized species, I_s(χ) is its positive enclosed mass measure in units where the homogeneous result is χ³/3, and C_s=8πG a³ρ̄_s. The common Poisson equation gives ∂χΦ=Σ C_s(I_s−χ³/3)/(2aχ²). Current conservation gives ∂ℓ I_s=−J_s, for ℓ=ln(a). Consequently

    D = ½ Σ C_s ∫_{χ₁}^{χ₂} (I_s−χ³/3) dχ/χ²,
    dD/dℓ = −½ Σ C_s ∫_{χ₁}^{χ₂} J_s dχ/χ².

This is also the leading mass-current part of the scalar GR momentum constraint: in spherical symmetry ∂χ(Φ̇+HΨ)=−4πG a j_pec, where the dot is a cosmic proper-time derivative and j_pec is the outward *physical* momentum density in c=1 units. For the comparison to the retained branch set Ψ=Φ at leading order and use the total nonlinear nonrelativistic mass current, not background density times a single shell speed after shell crossing. For gauge/sign conventions see the linearized conformal Newtonian equations of [Ma & Bertschinger](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9506072). The nonlinear Newtonian continuity identity above is derived here from the actual sources; the relativistic weak-field interpretation neglects metric-product, slip, pressure/enthalpy, and other post-Newtonian corrections. Homogeneous expansion is already in a and H; only the retained clustering species are tested. These equations do not determine a global lapse gauge or absolute time reference.

**Independent right side.** For the wave, s=χψ gives J=Im(s*∂χs)/(m a²H). For streams the integral is the original positive mass integral of sign(X)p/(a²H X²) on χ₁<|X|<χ₂. Summing signed velocities avoids dividing by density at a caustic; p is the canonical momentum on each sheet. The left side is calculated separately from the solved metric. A second continuum left side uses the directly integrated densities. This distinguishes actual failure of current transport from spatial representation error. The ordinary endpoint quadrature (not bundled here) and step-integrated path calculation (not bundled here) use different methods.

**Discrete identity, including the source that actually gravitates.** In the existing variational Poisson solve the metric is linear in χ² per cell. Let K_j be its original radial stiffness, λ_j(b)=clip[(b²−χ_j²)/(χ_{j+1}²−χ_j²),0,1]. From the saved Poisson solve the nodal kernel for D is W_i=½ Σ_{j≥i}(λ_j(χ₂)−λ_j(χ₁))/K_j. Thus the wave contributes C_h h Σ W_i(|s_i|²−χ_i²); dust contributes C_s ∫ W_h(|X|)dM minus the homogeneous counterpart, where W_h interpolates W in χ². Their sum is compared against a times the actual metric-potential difference. No coupling is adjusted to obtain agreement.

During an individual kick the potential is real. It rotates the wave phase and leaves *each nodal source density* unchanged. In the exact free sine evolution use K_kin s=−∂²χ s, with eigenvalues k² and the homogeneous linear ramp restored. The source derivative is h Σ W_i Im(s_i* K_kin s_i)/(ma²). Evaluate it independently at internal Gauss time points. At fixed **proper** measurement radii the nodal weights change; splitting the product exactly adds Σ ΔW (ρ_before+ρ_after)/2 and uses the averaged W on the transport term. [The proper-radius test](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `action_flux.py`) also checks a pair of ordinary positive aperture masses, the accelerations, and the complete wave, tags and coherent complements. Sharp enclosed-acceleration weights can change abruptly as a cell moves past a probe; the potential difference is continuous.

For each actual half-kicked dust element, integrate along its free drift X(q,t)=X₀(q)+t p_half(q)/a_mid². Split the original mass label where either endpoint crosses a metric knot. Between the two radii the continuum primitive changes by −sign(Δ|X|)(u−l)/(2ul); in the metric cell multiply (u−l)(u+l) by the known kernel difference divided by Δχ². Here l,u delimit the radial portion swept within the shell. This uses trajectories and their positive original mass, **not** a subtraction of the two solved endpoint potentials. An inward and subsequent outward passage on the same path may cancel in the *net* source. Higher-order label quadrature and an independent potential readout test this construction.

**Why check both continuum and discrete currents?** Pointwise wave kicks preserve the nodal source exactly but not the off-grid squared magnitude of its band-limited interpolant. For the continuum density readout, separately measure its change from each of the two kicks and compare its free-drift change to the integrated physical flux. Do not reinterpret an interpolation error as a flux of physical matter or relax the tolerances. A discrete compatibility result does not establish continuum convergence of the formation history.
