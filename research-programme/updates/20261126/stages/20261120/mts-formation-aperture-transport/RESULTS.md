# A derived common-gravity response, a failed null, and its local repair

20 November 2026. Closed local extension of the [previous conditional formation calculation](../mts-collisionless-sheet-infall/RESULTS.md). No new coupling, occupied mass, observed galaxy, fit or LMC band change.

## Which response should vanish?

We derived the immediate change in the rate of the gravitational potential difference between two spherical radii after a brief potential impulse. For readout A and impulse B it is proportional to **the integral of the actual mass density times the two potential gradients**. The same coefficient follows from the kinetic action of the wave and the matter streams. Two overlapping shells must respond; two separated shells must give zero instantaneous cross response in the continuum limit. This is an observable defined on the simulation, not a claim that a detector has supplied that impulse. [Derivation](COMMON-COUPLING.md).

We froze the numerical 3×3 response matrices before applying any impulses, tested both signs and amplitudes at two saved epochs of both branches, and independently solved the perturbed gravity. At Δln a=5×10⁻⁶, 144/144 original readouts match their finite-grid predictions to 2×10⁻⁵ on the diagonal response scale. **That agreement exposed a problem rather than hiding it:** in the end-state cold calculation, the native representation transmits a false 7.91% cross response between separated shells; in MTS it is 3.55%. Doubling only the gravity grid leaves 7.91% and 3.51%, respectively. These are fractions of the geometric mean of diagonal responses, **not** errors of that size in all previous force predictions.

**We repaired the local impulse itself.** A strongly folded element can connect material in both shells to the same momentum coordinates. We split the external kick at each gravity-cell preimage on the *unchanged* positive mass sheet. No new mass or fitted parameter was introduced. Keeping the old wave kinetic term isolates the residual spectral effect; using the compact canonical gradient action on the identical wave gives a strictly local control. The old solution is retained. In the repaired short experiment the disjoint response is zero in the prediction and consistent with zero on evolution, while the overlapping response remains nonzero. Across two epochs, meshes and branches, **288/288** nearest-neighbour response comparisons pass on the two shortest paired time windows. The 5×10⁻⁶ window that was adequate for the native representation is *not* uniformly adequate for the overlapping repaired source: progressively shorter windows were tested without changing the frozen coefficients. [Repair design](NULL-REPAIR.md) · [all refinement scores](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `local-impulse-summary.csv`).

**Is it merely a trick for making zeros?** On the finer end-state mesh, the cold A→A response went from **57.998% to 99.884%** of the independently integrated continuum mass-weighted coefficient. For MTS it went from **79.334% to 92.421%** with the nearest-neighbour gradient. We then kept the same impulse, state, original mass and continuum kinetic coefficient, and replaced only the wave-gradient discretization with the standard fourth- and sixth-order compact versions. The MTS ratio became **98.460% and 99.449%**. Their short evolutions pass all **288/288** fixed-precision response tests, including nonzero overlaps and separated-shell nulls. This validates the short action response, not an already repaired long formation run. The residual 0.55% for this example is larger than the numerical readout tolerance. No continuum force curve or Galactic occupation is inferred from this one state.

## What happened to the originally negative-energy population?

Same starting material at a=0.65, final a=0.925159195. The two gravity grids use exactly the same dust labels, momenta and initial wave tags. These numbers are endpoint fractions of **initial negative tags**, at fixed *proper* radii on the 3.125-comoving-kpc grid and the refined time step. For a wave, “outside” means outside probability mass; it is not a count of individual trajectories.

This decomposition and the outside/inside table answer different questions; **do not add them together**. The continuum open-boundary differential reconstruction of the negative population still misses a few percent on these evolving histories, even though 96/160-mode endpoint fractions agree. ## What gravity can read out of that current

Unlike the negative-energy classification, the leading Newtonian metric responds to the **entire coherent mass density**. Between 100 and 200 *proper* kpc the heavy contribution ΔΦ/c² starts at +2.4773×10⁻⁷ and grows to +(3.394–4.004)×10⁻⁷ across the four native-history runs. It remains positive but is not converged to the last digits. On the finest/time-refined run its final value is 3.4115×10⁻⁷. Decomposing by the *100-kpc tag* yields a signed interference contribution of −7.97×10⁻⁸. Adding tag and complement as if they were independent classical masses would overstate this lapse contrast. The changes from the physical radial current close to 1.19×10⁻⁶ of accumulated absolute work on this run; this tolerance is not met on every grid. [Metric transfer](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `metric-transfer-summary.csv`).

For the leading scalar GR momentum constraint we separately used two fixed **comoving** radii and tested the potential change sourced by the actual wave, CDM and baryon currents together. In six intervals of the time/space-refined trajectories, the maximum discrepancy between the continuous-density potential and the path-integrated physical currents is 4.41×10⁻⁷ of current work for MTS, 1.14×10⁻¹⁴ for cold. The variational metric and its own action-derived currents agree to 1.75×10⁻¹³ and 8.10×10⁻¹⁵, respectively. Crossing the representations—finite-element potential versus continuum current—leaves peak discrepancies **5.26×10⁻⁴ and 2.61×10⁻⁴**; those do not meet 2×10⁻⁵. The coarser heavy continuum test also misses that threshold. The report distinguishes actual nonlocality in the old numerical response, phase-interpolation error, and time sampling from a failure of the retained gravitational coupling.
