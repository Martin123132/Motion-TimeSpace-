# The stress and rapidly oscillating curvature source of the populated heavy field

On a fixed time slice use the orthonormal frame of the cosmological rest observers and proper volume. Let χ be the comoving radius. The retained quadratic real heavy field (not bundled here) has the leading envelope h ∝ a⁻³ᐟ² Re(ψ e⁻ⁱᵐᵗ), where m is an inverse length in c=1 units. Use the evolved cosmological phase; the quadratic phase change used in the *proper-coordinate Hamiltonian* of the binding test is not an extra physical peculiar velocity in this frame.

Canonical scalar stress gives T_rr−T_θθ=(∂_proper h)² in orthonormal axes for a spherical field. The isotropic kinetic and potential terms subtract out. Averaging over the fast carrier and dividing by the leading mean rest energy in the same sphere gives

    (〈T_rr〉−〈T_θθ〉)/(ρ̄_h c²) = ∫χ²|∂χψ|² dχ / [(am)²∫χ²|ψ|² dχ].

A classical radial stream contributes its full peculiar velocity squared divided by c², mass weighted, not just its random dispersion about the mean. The bulk velocity and dispersion separate in the saved stream diagnostics. There is no heavy carrier in the cold control.

For a canonical scalar with potential m²h²/2, the leading source ρ+Σp is proportional to 2 ḣ²−m²h²; spatial-gradient terms cancel in that trace combination. The doubled-frequency term in the aperture average has the complex coefficient

    C₂ = ∫χ²ψ² dχ / ∫χ²|ψ|² dχ,
    c²〈R_hat0hat0〉_sphere = 4πGρ̄_h [1−3 Re(C₂ exp(−2imt))]    (heavy component only).

Here ρ̄_h denotes the instantaneous aperture-averaged heavy mass density; 4πGρ̄_h is not the entire background curvature. Expansion, slowly varying envelope derivatives and relativistic corrections to the oscillator are omitted to this nonrelativistic order. Absolute clock phase changes the complex phase of the coefficient but not its magnitude. For example, in the five final calculations the coherence |C₂| at 100 proper kpc ranges from 0.117 to 0.464, not unity. The dimensionless radial anisotropy is about 2.06–4.22 ×10⁻⁶ there. These are not uncertainties on a measured galaxy.
