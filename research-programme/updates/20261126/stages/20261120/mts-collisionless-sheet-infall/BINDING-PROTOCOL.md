# Frozen-well diagnostic, not a core fit

The high wave densities after crossing justify asking whether the dynamically prepared wave actually occupies low-energy states, not just whether an empty potential well could host a ground state. Freeze the saved spherical total potential at the actual saved epoch. Preselect proper outer radii 100 and 200 kpc and compare Dirichlet and zero-normal-current reflecting (Neumann on the scalar) walls. Test both the <0 subspace and a fixed interior margin E<−5000 (km/s)², with zero potential at the comparison sphere. Include Hubble phase and cosmic acceleration in proper coordinates. Apply the same potential-depth/capacity test to the paired cold potential; calculate actual particle energy weights for the cold streams, never invent a wave occupation in a cold run. No fitted radius, chemical potential or angular state. No spherical exterior assertion is needed for differences of interior gravitational potential; the physical environment beyond the sphere, persistence after the metric changes, and unresolved nonspherical structure remain separate.

Use the measured complex state with its inherited phase. Do not cut it into a stationary profile or normalize its physical mass to a target galaxy. For selected negative trial eigenstates bound positive-spectrum contamination by the strong residual and spectral gap. Compare basis sizes, potential quadrature and time/space refinements. This gives a conditional instantaneous occupancy test for the chosen frozen weak-field problem, not a global escape probability or final virialized occupation.
