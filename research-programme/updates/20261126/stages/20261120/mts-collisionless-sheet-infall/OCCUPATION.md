# From the evolved phase to an occupation, without a galactic chemical potential

**Comparison being made.** An attractive potential with negative eigenvalues would only establish *capacity*. Here the input is the actual complex wave emerging from the fixed primordial conditional realization. The readout below measures its overlap; there is no assignment in order of lowest energy, no replacement by a stationary halo, and no parameter chosen from the force bands.

**Use proper coordinates.** For R=aχ, the wave in proper volume is Ψ(R)=a⁻³ᐟ² exp[iH R²/(2κ)] ψ(R/a), with κ=ħ/m=2 Mpc km/s in the retained example. This phase gives the total velocity H R+v_pec. Differentiating in time at fixed R cancels the coordinate dilation and leaves a local Hamiltonian per unit mass
     
    H_B = −(κ²/2) d²/dR² + V_B(R)   acting on u=R Ψ,
    V_B(R) = Φ_phys(R) − Φ_phys(B) − (ä/2a)(R²−B²).

The last term uses the actual retained expansion and its logarithmic derivative, not a chosen local cosmological constant. A constant shift of Φ cancels. An aperture energy below zero means below the potential at the chosen boundary *at that time*. We do not know a global escape energy. At the origin u=0; compare u(B)=0 to u′(B)=u(B)/B (Neumann on Ψ, not on u). The latter condition includes the constant scalar as the free zero mode. The positive spectrum of a finite box is also discrete.

For each saved metric we calculate negative energies in a free radial basis with kinetic energy κ²k²/2. Dirichlet has kB=nπ. For Neumann the positive roots obey tan(kB)=kB, with the separate regular k=0 mode u∝R. The potential/overlap integrals are resolved at the original gravitational grid knots with independent Gaussian quadrature. The actual spatial phase is reconstructed by sine interpolation. The normalized amplitudes are c_j=⟨v_j,u⟩/‖u‖. In the chosen aperture the conditional density matrix has elements M_B c_i c_j*, not just its diagonal. The [extraction code](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `population.py`) saves signed complex amplitudes in each negative mode with an explicit sign convention. The mass unit is the species’ homogeneous mass in the original target comoving radius, not the full box. Later aperture mass can exceed that unit by infall.

**How much of this can be called negative-energy population?** For a normalized negative Ritz state v_j with energy e_j<0, write r_j=(H_B−e_j)v_j. Kinetic evolution is diagonal in this basis, so r_j is the explicitly evaluated potential multiplication left outside the trial space. By the spectral theorem ‖P_nonnegative v_j‖≤‖r_j‖/|e_j|. For any orthonormal set of these states, the norm of its projection into the nonnegative spectrum is no greater than the square root of the sum of the squared leakage bounds. Applying the triangle inequality to the *actual* wave gives

    ‖P_negative u‖²/‖u‖² ≥ max(0, sqrt(Σ|c_j|²) − sqrt(Σ‖r_j‖²/e_j²))².

We use states below −5000 (km/s)² for the witness so that a level just below the boundary energy cannot make the denominator arbitrarily small. Each nested subset starting at the lowest mode gives a valid inequality; the largest of those bounds is also valid. This strengthens a witness without changing H_B or fitting the wave. This is a theorem for the specified frozen operator; its numerical evaluation uses quadrature/refinements, not interval-certified input gravity. The raw projected negative fraction and the lower bound are reported separately. A harmonic test recovers a known coherent 35:65 occupation split, both admissible walls, and a separate analytic classical energy cut [here](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `binding-checks.json`). The original lower-order quadrature lost orthogonality for one cold panel and was rejected; recomputation splits the radius finely enough to enforce the unchanged Gram test. Only the r2/refined readouts enter interpretation.

**Matched cold control.** Use the same potential and boundary-energy convention, but do not project a nonexistent quantum wave onto a cold halo. On every oriented particle sheet intersect |aX|≤B and E=½(HaX+c p/a)²+V_B(a|X|)<0 (or the deeper fixed threshold). Here H and velocities are in km/s/Mpc and km/s. Within a metric element the potential and squared velocity are quadratic in the Lagrangian interpolation fraction. Splitting at the real roots gives an integral of the original positive cell mass, rather than a count of samples. The same-species initial/final intersections retain particle identity across caustics.

**Two distinct times.** For the wave define a tag by projecting the actual state onto the initial negative Dirichlet modes inside B and zero extending it. Undo the Hubble phase before evolving. Through the next interval this tag obeys the original Schrödinger equation in the exact same time-dependent potential as the unmodified complete wave, but contributes **no additional density** to gravity; its outside boundary is homogeneous zero. Compact Dirichlet tags have a finite gradient energy; a sharp cut of a nonzero Neumann edge would not. At the later epoch measure both (i) how much of the initial tag is still inside B and (ii) how much is in the new negative subspace. For the full final negative projection one must add tagged and untagged amplitudes before squaring, including 2 Re⟨P tag,P complement⟩. Dropping it can falsely suggest a particle-like allocation. A two-time projection quantifies retention/return in this protocol, not a measured particle trajectory, survival at all intervening times, or a cosmological mass gap.
