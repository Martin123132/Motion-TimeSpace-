# Fixed ring mass, a bounded force, and a metric target
Use kpc and M☉; G is the retained Newtonian coupling, not estimated here. Write S_j(R) for each available stellar column after subtracting the retained central excess, and p_j(z) for its normalized measured vertical shape including the inherited tail. Let W_i(R,φ,z) be the candidate component density, B_i(R) its integrated vertical and mean-azimuthal column, and h_i=W_i/B_i the conditional shape (mean angular integral of the vertical column is one). At fixed mass, lawful reallocation is

    δρ = Σ_i a_i W_i − Σ_j d_j(R) p_j(z)
    0 ≤ d_j ≤ S_j,    Σ_j d_j = Σ_i a_i B_i,    a_i ≥ 0.

The forward scenarios remove mass proportionally to S_j. The relaxed bounds do not require proportionality. No sum of satellite mass, angular source normalization or optical data enters the occupation. Restricting the transfer to one annulus multiplies both terms by the same indicator; its edges are a sensitivity experiment, not the physical edge of the observed bar. Linear gravity/dilute optical depth allows this ordinary transfer to be propagated exactly in amplitude on fixed dark fields. It does not make the self-gravitating wave linear in a finite change of this size.

**Ring test.** A necessary and sufficient condition for some nonnegative proportional remainder at each radius is Σ a_i B_i ≤ Σ S_j. For the common multiplier construction the ratio is bounded using min(total census at both endpoints) in each small interval split at every census/radial-cut knot. This is valid for the retained piecewise-linear inner source and monotonically declining exterior. Bound the angular average from above using the smaller major/minor coordinates on each quadrant cell, left radius for the decreasing generalized-ellipse exponential and the favorable endpoint of each monotonic cutoff factor separately. A safety factor covers the tested shape-normalization quadrature; this is not directed interval arithmetic. The table's minimum fraction left uses the upper ratio, not an optimized sample maximum. WGP masses are normalized inside the retained 30-kpc domain (the WGP outer Gaussian tails there are negligible). For S22 the model is likewise used on the census domain. No physical constraint outside the model coverage is inferred.

**Force kernel.** For a probe (R,0,z) and a source ring element (s cosφ,s sinφ,u), the cylindrical force per unit source mass is G(s cosφ−R,u−z)/d³, with d²=(s−R)²+4sR sin²(φ/2)+(u−z)². The tested components are spherical radial and increasing polar angle from the north pole, obtained using (R,z)/r and (z,−R)/r, r=hypot(R,z). The exact azimuthal mean uses complete elliptic integrals K,E; the nonconstant shape is integrated after subtracting its value at the nearest azimuth, then restoring the exact constant part. Split the height quadrature at the force probe, near its separation from the ring, and at the source scale heights or tabulated vertical knots. An independent direct angular and height integration agrees on the near and far sides of both test radii. For even ordinary density the radial response is even in z and the polar response odd. Reflect only this addition, never the saved satellite subtotal.

**Two different relaxations.** (1) At fixed reported radial profiles, the maximum polar contribution per component with arbitrary donating species cannot exceed U_i = ∫2πs B_i(s)[K_i(s)−min_j K_j(s)]ds, where K is the acceleration per unit ring mass. At witness radii let A_ni = B_i(s_n)/Σ S_j(s_n). Physical amplitudes obey Aa≤1. For any y≥0 with Aᵀy≥U, U·a≤Σ y_n. Hence a few rings can rule out a source even though most of the disk is unconstrained. Neither the finite set of witnesses nor the relaxed species allocation can spuriously tighten the mathematical inequalities; their coefficients are numerical force and column integrals. We save the dual weights, signs and residuals.

(2) Without fixed radial profiles put any allowed mass from any species into any candidate shape in each ring. For component k the favorable bound is the integral of Σ_j 2πs S_j max(0,max_i K_ik−K_jk), and similarly for the unfavorable bound using min. Orientations are bracketed continuously by tiles: in each tile bound |cos(φ−α)| and |sin(φ−α)| separately and use monotonicity of the generalized-ellipse exponential; divide by its orientation-independent ring mean. Integrate the signed, vertically averaged angular kernel times the appropriate upper/lower density, then maximize over tiles in that ring. Allowing independent orientations, shapes, and fractions across rings weakens the bounds on purpose. At large radius this even allows a shape where its population fit predicts effectively no stars. Bounds need not be attained. The angle brackets are analytic; the radial/vertical/azimuthal integrals are numerical with the refinement ledger retained.

**Observable and metric.** For each hemisphere subtract its frozen prediction from its original observed interval. Transform the southern residual interval by parity: negate and reverse its endpoints for polar, leave unchanged for radial. A purely even potential perturbation must land in the intersection of those two transformed intervals. If an arbitrary opposite-parity field is allowed as well, the required even contribution must instead lie between the averages of their lower endpoints and of their upper endpoints. Subtract a bound on the direct stellar response to obtain an interval for the remaining even field. With weak metric g₀₀≈−1−2Φ/c², a_theta=−∂θΦ/r and a_r=−∂rΦ. The table reports |∂θΦ| in (km/s)² or |∂rΦ| in (km/s)²/kpc and the corresponding metric derivatives per radian or per kpc. A derivative at a probe is not a measured potential offset or a new fundamental coupling.

**Optical normalization.** For a source-distance window W(t)=E[(1−t/D_s)_+] and the saved sightline x(t), Δτ=(4πG/c²)∫dt t W(t) δρ_compact(x(t)). Report micro-optical-depth. Use only the four retained stellar populations in this differential and absolute compact-lens calculation.
