# What changes when matter can cross the boundary

This is a cosmological formation calculation, not the LMC equilibrium or a Milky Way acceleration in SI. Radii are comoving Mpc, conformal time is in Mpc with c=1, and primes below denote conformal time derivatives. The monopole and its quadrupolar perturbations obey the same parent weak-field system used in the source stage (not bundled here). The pressure-off perturbation control uses the same evolving spherical medium as pressure-on; the separate all-cold control has its own matched background.

## Boundary condition and force

For a localized quadrupole with no density outside its support the decaying potential is proportional to r⁻³. In cold homogeneous exterior matter V′+ℋV=−Φ, so a velocity potential initially proportional to r⁻³ remains of that form. It is not zero, and its radial derivative is −3V/L at a spherical boundary L. Integrating the weak divergence by parts thus adds a positive 3Ln(L) endpoint stiffness; the same surface term is required when preparing a compensating current. Setting the velocity potential to zero instead changes the experiment. The Robin condition is exact for this harmonic exterior, **not** an outgoing dispersive-wave condition. The buffers in this test allow live density, and only the perturbation relative to the noncompact Fourier reference is matched in the cosmological run.

For matter source S=Σ C_s d_s, let I=∫₀ʳs⁴S ds and O=∫ᵣᴸ S ds/s. With the P₂ angular factor, the quadrupole potential coefficient is −(I/r³+r²O)/(10a); radial acceleration on the axis is (−3I/r⁴+2rO)/(10a) and polar acceleration at θ=π/4 is 3Φ/(2r). The inner and outer terms can oppose. The independent current identity is I′=−r⁴J_r(r)+∫₀ʳ[2s³J_r+6s²Σ C_s n_s V_s]ds, with J_r=Σ C_s(n_s∂ᵣV_s+U_s d_s). The boundary term does not vanish just because the exterior density vanishes initially.

## Launch law for a density tide

For the heavy species write n for the spherical background, d for its quadrupolar density perturbation, and L₂=∂ᵣ²+(2/r)∂ᵣ−6/r². The linear pressure operator is K_n f=L₂f+(∂ᵣln n)∂ᵣf. Copy the same density and velocity into the on/off solutions at launch. Subtracting their Euler equations leaves a phase *derivative* difference K_n(d/n)/(4a²m²). Applying the continuity equation once gives ΔS=ΔS′=0 and ΔS″=−C_h div[n grad K_n(d/n)]/(4a²m²). Here the angular derivatives act on P₂. If d=0 at launch the quadratic term vanishes; it need not vanish for the native density tide. For compact smooth test T, integrating twice by parts gives
Δ(∫r²TS dr)″=−C_h∫r²n(K_nT)K_n(d/n)dr/(4a²m²). Choosing T=d/n when compact exhibits a nonpositive self-overlap, not a sign constraint on every observed radial force.

For a noncompact Fourier tide put z=K_n(d/n), p=z∂ᵣn, b=C_h/(8a³m²), I_p=∫₀ʳs³p ds, O_p=∫ᵣ∞p ds/s². Use div(n grad z)=L₂(nz)−div(z grad n) and the all-space regular Newton inverse; p is compact because the reference medium is homogeneous outside its collar. This gives
ΔΦ″=−b[nz−(2I_p/r³−3r²O_p)/5] and Δg_r″=b[n∂ᵣz+6(I_p/r⁴+rO_p)/5]. The homogeneous limit is ΔΦ″=b k²d for a single k. The mode-by-mode prediction and conditional variance were frozen before evolving.

The actual evolution subtracts each control's own homogeneous solution, including the common native external metric forcing. Each k is evolved, not forced to maintain its saved density transfer at later times. Local radial elements then propagate the contrast caused by the live medium. Fourier gravity is added back **once** at every probe. An identically homogeneous patch has zero local correction, not zero total dispersive response. The +2σ spherical and +1σ oriented tidal preparations are specified examples, not a Milky Way initial state.
