# The wave follows the expanding cloud: a 273-year test of the initial momentum

**Result.** We evolved the retained real massive field and the local geometry together rather than imposing a frequency at each later time. At redshift 86.11, over two carrier periods (273.43 proper years), the wave's departure from the instantaneous normal-momentum formula has a predictable oscillation. **The ordinary expansion of the spatial metric supplies almost the entire leading drive.** Local shear was included; it is not the source of a large enhancement. Nothing was normalized to a force measurement.

At the original 15.532 kpc proper axial distance, the change in the signed host-only, pressure-on minus matched pressure-off tidal field over this interval is approximately **−6.66 × 10⁻¹⁹** conformal Mpc⁻² for the overdensity and **+6.85 × 10⁻¹⁹** for the underdensity. The magnitude grows by about **0.011%** on each sign. This is the motion of the **wave-switch tidal signal inside the relativistic calculation**, not a newly measured change in the GR-minus-Newton difference. The prior GR/Newton result describes the launch slice. A separate all-cold cosmology gives identically zero switch effect throughout its independent evolutions.

## Does the error alter gravity?

The field's **actual momentum** sources both real-field energy and stress throughout the run. Independently propagate the linear response of the metric, wave and ruler to replacing that momentum law with its instantaneous graph, matched to the same initial data. On the central 80-cell/384-step run the host-only changes at the end are **−7.69e−27** and **+7.71e−27** conformal Mpc⁻²: about 1.3 parts in 10¹² of the host wave-switch tide. These estimates vary by roughly 2–6% with radial refinement; they are not 20 ppm measurements. Including the launch transport forecast reduces the estimated error further, but the remaining signal does not have a stable relative magnitude across the meshes. No exact nonlinear upper bound is asserted. Numerical resolution of the absolute tidal field is much worse than resolution of this separately propagated response. ## Does the surrounding matter balance?

Three different questions were tested:

- **Field current:** on both signed clouds the change of KG symplectic number in the central aperture agrees with integrated flux to better than 1.4e−9 relative for the spherical and quadrupolar coefficients. The angular coefficient includes through-angle flux, not just flux through the sphere. The near-zero unhosted monopole has an absolute imbalance of order 1e−20 and no meaningful relative pass. This is a conserved quadrature current, not a new fundamental particle number for a single real field. Flux (not bundled here).
- **Surrounding matter:** the fully sampled external energy/pressure history is one preparation. In another, only its spatial stress is prescribed; its energy and momentum respond by local covariant conservation on the new metric. Switching between these changes the central integrated tide change by ~0.021 and ~0.052 ppm on the two signs on the matched 80-cell constrained calculation. It does **not** specify the full nonlinear radiation stress. The radial and polar momentum and Hamiltonian defects are retained rather than being set to zero. Conservation and constraint readouts (not bundled here).
- **Secular gravity:** using the spatial Gauss relation in the evolution and integrating the proper-radius tidal rate avoids differencing large endpoint curvatures. On that formulation the 80→120 radial change is 0.055 and 0.196 ppm; doubling time resolution changes the result by <0.05 ppm. However, changing the *input preparation mesh* changes it by **58 and 54 ppm**, and reading the unconstrained spatial Ricci instead changes it by **143 and 105 ppm** at the central reference setting. The negative cloud also misses the target when freeing the radial shear equation. Both signs and the approximate size survive; the total numerical precision is not established at 20 ppm.
