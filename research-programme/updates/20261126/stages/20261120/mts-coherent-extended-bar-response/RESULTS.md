# The rotating bar: a small polar force and a new azimuthal test

20 November 2026 · Private, local MTS parent calculation. **The rotating Galactic bar does drive the wave, but not strongly enough to recover the missing inner force. It also predicts a signed azimuthal pattern which can be tested separately from that failure.** No fit, changed occupation, new coupling, or enlargement of a measurement band. All accelerations in this note are in 10⁻¹⁰ m/s² unless specified.

## What the saved force bands say

On both previously prepared wave states we solved the response to the central bar and both extended stellar components. They share the independently measured [fiducial pattern speed](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.10875); each retains its own orientation. We use the admissible fraction of the [extended source model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.13114), not a second copy of those stars. The six saved Vasiliev satellite histories are alternative simulations, not six observed or independently fitted galaxies. Their direct fields and freshly prepared axisymmetric wave responses are retained.

## A more useful target than the single-site force

Measure the **twofold cosine component of azimuthal acceleration** on rings, referenced to the central bar axis. It cannot be obtained from a single circular-speed measurement. The central bar's own direct cosine is zero by symmetry. A two-ring difference with weights 1 and −3.29895 also cancels the direct extended stellar quadrupole at fixed shape. After subtracting the direct satellite quadrupole it predicts **+0.001496 to +0.001521** for MTS versus zero for static NFW. The unsubtracted two-ring satellite contribution is −0.03282 to −0.02087: omitting it would swamp the prediction. The definition was frozen before evaluating this contrast. [Protocol](TWO-RING-PROTOCOL.md) · raw and corrected values (not bundled here).

A secondary test uses the saved 17.58-kpc calibration radius as a third **azimuthal** measurement, not its previously used mean circular speed. Weights **(1, −3.36270, +1.24633)** cancel the direct extended bar and *any spatially constant tidal tensor*. Take the plane-even component. Residual direct satellite force is −0.002279 to −0.001129. After subtracting that residual the prediction is **+0.0013225 or +0.0013455**; static NFW is zero. Without subtraction, even the raw model ranges remain separated by 0.000173 on the six histories at the fiducial speed; at the two independently specified speed endpoints the smallest sampled separation is 0.000138. These are ranges of calculations, **not observational confidence intervals**. Weights (not bundled here) · history-by-history predictions (not bundled here) · [speed controls](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `speed-controls.csv`).

This test suppresses two specific foregrounds, not all Galactic uncertainties. Equal independent errors on the three even ring coefficients are amplified by 3.72 (measurement covariance is unknown). The underlying stellar templates, their shapes and the chosen preparations are conditional. Independently changing the relative short/long stellar contributions introduces leakage; making their cancellation exact instead costs a large noise gain of 66.8. A shift in reference axis leaks about 0.0000156 per degree from the direct central shape at nominal geometry. These are sensitivity calculations, not assigned observational errors.

**Fair live-halo control:** the [archived collisionless MW–LMC models](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.8015660) do not predict zero. Applying the identical statistic to initial and final halo fields in both published representations gives a *change* between −0.01321 and +0.000827. Several changes are larger in magnitude than the wave signal. Their baryonic environments differ from the retained MTS foreground, and some representation differences matter at this precision. This establishes that a nonzero contrast is **not unique to a wave**; it does not establish either live model's superiority. [Initial, final and differenced control](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `live-halo-contrast.csv`). No archived observed ring data have been scored.

## Energy exchange and numerical controls

- Interference matters. The steady outgoing wave corresponds to **2.39–2.44 million M☉/Gyr**, versus 1.24–1.27 million if the separate stellar powers are added incoherently. The fractional rate is only about 1.1×10⁻⁵/Gyr of the retained wave mass. Predicted positive outgoing angular-momentum flux is 9.58–9.76×10⁹ M☉ kpc km/s per Gyr. This is conditional leading-order radiation on a fixed ground, not an observed loss rate, torque on a rigid material bar, or secular evolution over gigayears. Mode-by-mode flux and source work (not bundled here).
- At high m the raw work integral can lose the tiny outgoing flux in the difference of much larger reactive torques. A reciprocity derivation separates reversible work from dissipation; the independently measured complex outgoing-flux cross term agrees with the resulting damping cross term to better than 8.0×10⁻⁸ of the geometric flux scale on the high grid, all 32 occupation/mode cases. Both estimates and the failed raw integrals remain on disk. No high-m raw relative precision pass is claimed.
- Reproduced the central-only control on its original ground to 4.49×10⁻¹⁶ absolute at the three replay harmonics for both occupations. For all nonzero **summed** force components the last source/channel/radial refinement changes the result by at most 1.82×10⁻⁵ relative; the external boundary test by 4.11×10⁻⁶. Both pass the original 2×10⁻⁵ target. The coarsest sums and some tiny individual harmonics do not all meet it. The truncated source's local polar derivative differs from the direct stellar Newton integral by up to 3.51×10⁻⁴ relative, so the sky panels use the direct stellar force, not that approximation.
