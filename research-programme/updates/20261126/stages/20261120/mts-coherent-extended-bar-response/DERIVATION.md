## Retarded response and force

In the parent weak-field Schrödinger–Poisson normalization, writing u,v for upper/conjugate-lower sidebands and w for the induced gravitational potential radial coefficient, the projected equations have diagonal wave blocks ℓ(ℓ+1)/r²+2[V−(μ±mΩ)] and couplings 2Bw into each wave, B(u+v) into the Poisson equation. B is the angular product with the real ground divided by radius. The forcing per sideband is 2 times the angular projection of the ground multiplied by the stellar potential. Use the outgoing Coulomb solution in the positive-energy branch, the decaying solution in the negative-energy branch, and vacuum Poisson matching. No incident free wave is added. This is steady retarded linear response on the nonlinear stationary background, not a nonlinear history of bar and satellite formation.

Solve once for each of the two *real* source shapes with a common operator. In central-bar coordinates the actual response is X₀ + exp[im(α₀−α₁)] X₁. At the observer include the common factor exp[im(π−α₀)] and take twice the real part, never add magnitudes. Radial force is minus the radial derivative of the potential; polar is sinθ ∂μΦ/r; azimuthal is −imΦ/(r sinθ). All three are independently recovered from the wave-density Poisson integral. The local azimuthal stellar pull is also computed by direct cylindrical Newton integration, pairing opposite source azimuths to integrate the odd force without subtracting two large accelerations after the integral. Reflection across the plane changes the polar sign only. Original six axisymmetric satellite waves and their direct fields are already in the incoming stage on precisely these grounds.

## Energy and angular momentum

The retarded linear operator is symmetric under the unconjugated reciprocity pairing (the Poisson block has relative weight two; regular and like outgoing/decaying boundary conditions have zero mutual boundary Wronskian). For source coefficients c=(1,exp[im(α₀−α₁)]), total work is −Im(c†Sc). Evaluate its dissipative part as c†[−Im((S+Sᵀ)/2)]c rather than subtracting enormous, nearly opposite real reactive terms. Raw unsymmetrized quadrature is intentionally retained: its relative error is enormous on some almost-dark high modes. Symmetry is an analytic identity; the radiative matrix is independent evidence that enforcing it recovered the right small answer.

With wave mass M, discrete normalization N, length L and speed V, the physical mass flux is (M/N)F/(L/V), angular momentum flux m(LV) times that flux, and pattern work Ω times the angular momentum flux. These are leading quadratic outgoing fluxes during steady forcing, not an observational measurement of bar slowdown or a formation prescription. Fixed ground occupation in a linear force calculation does not mean its open channel has identically zero flux at second order.
