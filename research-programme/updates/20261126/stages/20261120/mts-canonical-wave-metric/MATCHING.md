# A boundary derivative fixed by the trace equation

In the existing box experiment h(L,t)=H(t), b(L,t)=N(L,t)=1 at a fixed comoving L. The three conditions are part of that matching *model*, not a measured exterior. If this choice holds over time, ∂η(h−H) at that boundary must be zero. On substituting in the independently derived trace law one obtains, without choosing a slope to improve any clock result,

    D_L ≡ U'(L)−V'(L) = [4πG a² (P_r(L)−P_bg) + V'(L)²/2] / [1/L−V'(L)] .

This holds at nondegenerate boundaries (1−L V'≠0). Thus at such a surface zero relative slope is **not** generally compatible with all the time equations, even if the boundary is pressureless. A finite enclosed perturbation can give V'≠0. In the material calculation the box is normalized to b=1; for a static exterior in general isotropic coordinates use a²b⁴ in place of a² in the pressure term. Schwarzschild and Reissner–Nordström provide independent analytic tests, including the extremal cancellation where the right-hand side vanishes despite nonzero field and stress.

The additive lapse normalization and the spatial/expansion matching itself remain boundary inputs. This is a necessary *derivative compatibility* condition at the surface. It does not specify a galaxy's formation environment or prove that moving to a different box gives the same physical clock transfer. The two-box check uses the same starting matter, projects its actual radial pressure, and compares both the old and predicted exterior derivative without refitting. It is a slice test; a finite history whose trajectories were generated with the old slope must not be relabelled as evolved with the new condition.
