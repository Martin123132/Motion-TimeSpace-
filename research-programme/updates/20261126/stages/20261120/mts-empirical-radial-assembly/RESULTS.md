# Can fixed-occupation wave redistribution produce both force components?

20 November 2026 · Local only. The LMC response and the stellar-assembly control below are two **separate** calculations. No fitted acceleration, selected satellite history, new halo normalization or independently predicted occupation.

**Same LMC histories, same bands.** Both original preparations and all six retarded LMC drives give MTS and the static NFW control the same 2/8: the north and south polar components at R=8.3 kpc. The extra wave mass inside 6.185 kpc is compensated outside. At R=6 kpc the induced radial force closes 1.08–2.44% of the earlier deficit. Why simply amplifying the wave fails: at the northern site the radial component would require multiplying its signed response by **at least +41 to +86**, depending on the original ground/history; the polar component would require **at most −224 to −596**. The two acceptable intervals never intersect, including on both extended boxes [complete signed test](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `original-LMC-sign-test.csv`). The direct LMC force is already in the frozen subtotal. This argument tests a shape, not the validity of linear dynamics at giant amplitudes. See the original replay (not bundled here) for both signs of height, units, retarded integral and resolution limits; domain changes affect the tiny induced polar term at up to 0.2264% without changing its sign or a decision.

**Independent sources for changing the shape without changing the number.** [Van Dokkum et al., Figs. 3–4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1304.2391) give stacked progenitor stellar profiles at six redshifts; not six observations of a single Milky Way. We recovered 276 radial ordinates, checking the figures independently. Direct circular integration of the plotted profiles produces almost twice the published aperture masses. Rather than assigning that unexplained offset to the Galaxy, use the Fig. 4 inner aperture to normalize relative profile growth at each epoch (factor 0.503–0.507). Inner plus outer aperture masses agree with their mass evolution to 0.28%; the central z≈2.4 to 1 mass ratio is 3.25, against the published 3.2±0.8. One outer clipped segment differs by 0.058 dex between panels; the forcing is tapered before each clipped end. Where the empirical radial extent ends we retain the unmeasured component, not an assumed absence of stars. The retained Λ=0 parent-scalar clock puts 10.406 Gyr between the first and last measured bins, compared to 10.554 Gyr under the flat comparison clock. Neither is a new fit.

[Papercopy of Ratcliffe et al., Table A.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.02691) provides an independent Galactic archaeology reconstruction from stellar data. Ten epochs span 12.25 to 0.7 Gyr lookback. Its two model-dependent radius histories (birth and present radii) and stellar-mass evolution are applied separately as homologous scalings, not direct observations of past 3-D density. Both source families are anchored to the retained, independently normalized terminal stellar mass; the terminal tabulated times do not purport to be exactly today. No stellar trend is applied to the entire ordinary mass. If stars form out of co-spatial gas already providing the same gravity, that conversion alone gives no new gravitational drive; keeping gas fixed is an explicit *net mass assembly* control.

**Work budget from the field equations.** The independent positive stellar source evolves first in spherical average; the disk angular forcing follows with the complete complex state. Ordinary source work in the primary linear interpolation is −7993, −6353, −4452 (km/s)² per unit wave mass on the three preparations. These are mostly changes in binding energy. After contraction, only **0.300, 0.223 and 0.083 (km/s)²** remain above the terminal spherical equilibria. Both archaeological controls give 0.010–0.094; smoothstep interpolation of the stacked profiles gives 0.44–2.55. The conclusions apply to the time histories specified, not to every history compatible with the snapshots.

The existing angular-energy inequality has been **rederived for each new starting state**, including loss of overlap and the extra energy. The certificate excludes the required vertical change for all smooth cases, all five separate one-bin impulses and most of the coherent multi-impulse trials. But holding the same six observations fixed and placing each intervening change over the retained 82-Myr wave time can accumulate **469 (km/s)²** (all early-quarter impulses, measured foreground); late-quarter gives 149. Neither admits the previous energy exclusion. These are deliberately unmeasured timing controls; there is no measured energy surplus at those values. An inconclusive bound is why the signed force was actually evolved.

**Same-band test after the change in preparation.** Across 65 equally spaced post-assembly samples over 328 Myr and six direct LMC alternatives, MTS stays at 2/8 on all three foregrounds. Static NFW remains 2/8, 4/8 and 4/8 respectively. No decision changes between the 32- and 64-step integrations. Even the 469-energy example supplies at most **0.0374** extra inward vertical force at the sampled times, short of the approximately **0.282** needed on that foreground (units 10⁻¹⁰ m/s²). The necessary eight-band condition is not met, even though at two of its seven preselected stream readouts (82 and 164 Myr) **both Pal 5 and GD-1 strengths** fall within one quoted width, with directions also within a width. These are instantaneous force summaries, not integrated stream tracks or an inferred time for the Milky Way. Both early and late high-energy scenarios are reported, not chosen afterwards as a preferred model.

**Numerical audit.** The shell work identity closes to less than 10⁻⁷ (km/s)²; upstream radial energy/work closure is better than 1.6×10⁻⁷ (km/s)² on all 84 runs. The largest relative particle-number error anywhere in the primary radial and post-assembly runs is below 3.4×10⁻¹². Coarse/fine comparisons: **3,532 of 112,320** signed totals and **5,589 of 5,850** individual induced/anchor changes miss the original relative tolerance of 2×10⁻⁵. No tolerance was relaxed. The totals count includes the static controls and both hemispheres, not independent observations. Per-case refinement (not bundled here) and extra 128-step comparison of both uncertified physical scenarios (not bundled here) keep precision and classification distinct.

For the two distinct fixed-input histories without a monotone certificate, halving the post-assembly time step again reduces the signed totals outside tolerance from 1,104 to 156 out of 12,480, with zero changed decisions. Both transient stream-strength entries at both times also retain their classification.

**Next test with discriminatory power:** supply a separately constrained time-dependent *total* gas-plus-stellar potential and evolve its radial and angular parts together, preserving the LMC and static-control conventions. This would test a change of wave shape capable of affecting vertical and radial forces differently. Merely increasing the original satellite amplitude or choosing a burst time by its sky score is not that test.
