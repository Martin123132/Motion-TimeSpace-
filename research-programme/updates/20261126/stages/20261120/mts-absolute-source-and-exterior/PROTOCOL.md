# Absolute gravity: independent source and boundary forecast

The previous four relative-radius tests do not certify the absolute normal expansion. Preserve its fourteen raw runs and scores. Test the forecast separately on all seven original sites, both formation epochs and both branches: no fitted constant, no chosen best radius, no change to 20 ppm. Time runs backward on the later slice; signed integrals must follow it. The alternatives are diagnostic simulations, not independent observational data.

In units 8πG=1 the radial momentum constraint has h′=j/2, h(L)=H, N(L)=1. A homogeneous component omitted from the radial momentum evolution contributes to the old error (measured minus predicted) by +½∫a(ρ+p)[N(r)−1]dη. For the original zero relative outer slope, the offset from the edge equation is +½∫[a δP_r(L)+V′(L)²/a]dη. Compute both from source and geometry rather than residuals. Separate physical photons, relativistic neutrinos, thermal massive neutrinos, the retained scalar and any disagreement between the assumed background derivative and the explicit inventory. Native densities are 8πG a⁴ρ. Use dρ/d ln a=−3(ρ+p) only for independently conserved species, not to re-label a bookkeeping mismatch as a real neutrino.

Independently propagate an initially isotropic thermal distribution on the original metric on this short interval. This is a conditional initial state, not a primordial phase-space measurement. Compare its momentum, pressure, anisotropy and their contribution to the local trace with the independent forcing forecast. Keep source-only, test-field and fully gravitating results distinct. Assess temporal, angular, spatial and momentum quadrature where they affect the conclusion. Do not call subtracting a forecast a new coupled trajectory.
