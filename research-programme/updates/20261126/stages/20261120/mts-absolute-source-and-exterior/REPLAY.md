# Local verification and reproduction

This folder is independent of GitHub and of Galaxy Lab. Use the retained Python 3.13 environment, NumPy/SciPy, and the project's own CAMB library. Source phases and data are local. The scientific tolerance is the inherited 2×10⁻⁵; the usual table unit is parts per million of the indicated quantity. A geometry-wide residual and a thermal-only residual have different denominators.

1. `python -B check_release.py` verifies the final manifest against the complete stage, its 116-file predecessor, the intended run matrix, signed trace and metric, and canonical wave energy/charge. Passing that verifier does not certify uncovered thermal-only component tolerances; those are reported separately.
2. The four `*-full.npz` packets are the original finite trajectories with the metric also sampled through the outer matching surface. `capture.py cold start end` and the heavy counterpart perform read-only replays against the sealed parent, compare its original input and result arrays, and write their additional arrays here. No SPARC input, sky band, cosmological likelihood or local charge measurement is used. Do not overwrite an existing seal to conduct a different run.
3. `inventory.py cold heavy` obtains the source densities and mass from the retained native backgrounds; `budget.py` predicts the absolute-site offset and exterior term. `field_calendar.py` independently checks the physical enthalpy evolution against the native expansion, before permitting the source-based calendar in a local run.
4. `kernel_checks.py` tests angular and volume reductions on analytic sources. `hot_characteristics.py cold,heavy start,end base fine time space flat kin` produces the direct causal readouts. `ball_mass.py` checks the translated-volume quadrature. These do not fit or solve the common metric. The direct calculation has its own angular, momentum, radial, time and outer-geometry checks; see `passive-controls.json`.
5. Example live calculation: `python -B feedback.py heavy start 3 source 8`. Arguments: branch, epoch, thermal discretization, background calendar and number of outer steps. Level 1 is the direct interpolation control; level 2 increases its radial/angular/momentum grid. Level 3 separates the analytically transported initial-potential response and measures it with 48 angular points in addition to the residual distribution. Level 5 additionally uses the conserved-volume spatial representation of the static monopole and uses 80 angular points. The original positive material labels, core, wave, slow-field grids, scalar action and fixed matching conditions are unchanged. No late-time closure of the radiation hierarchy or new coupling constant is substituted for the resolved local particles. The first four coarse runs predate the additional shell diagnostic and are not represented as having it. Later cases save the actual shell flux and work terms. The final states include thermal angular distributions on the proper-momentum grid, or both the full and residual state for the reference representation.
6. `compare_sources.py` compares each raw trajectory with its control and with the tighter causal calculation. `precision.py` reports the independent shell energy test and direct-solver discretization controls. The prediction for four previously uninspected radii was frozen separately in `four-new-site-predictions.json` (SHA-256 starts `65c05417`); `unseen_test.py` tests it against actual on/off expansion obtained from the lapse, spatial potential and its kinematic rate, not from a trace score. Origin and exterior matching, gauge, finite duration, conditional initial state and numerical ordering remain part of every comparison.

7. For the kinetic energy cross-check use `causal_energy.py heavy start extra` on the retained extra direct transport. The early level 6 live comparison evolves the residual massive monopole from the angle-averaged equation. `shell_work.py` rebuilds the corrected massive metric-work readout from the frozen raw packets without integrating another trajectory. Run it before `precision.py --save`; `shell_comparison.py --save` instead uses independent line-of-sight energy, not the corrected work, as its reference. See RESULTS.md for the diagnostic erratum. `tables.py` collects the distinct readouts without combining their denominators.

No live evolution needs to be launched to read these results. Each numerical worker restricts its libraries to one thread; the finished batches have no server, preview or app dependency.
