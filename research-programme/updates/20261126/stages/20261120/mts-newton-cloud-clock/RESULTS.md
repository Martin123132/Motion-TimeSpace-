# How much of the evolving cloud tide is Newtonian?

**On the fixed central ruler, Newton predicts the direction and about 99.97% of the relativistic change on the retained constrained readout.** Both signed clouds were evolved for 273.43 proper years starting at z=86.11; the central ruler was held at 15.532 proper kpc. The observable is the change of the additional host wave tide (pressure on minus pressure off, with the unhosted signal removed). It is not the total gravitational force, a confidence level, or a present-day Galactic prediction.

| Prepared host | Newtonian change | Relativistic change | GR / Newton − 1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Overdensity | −6.65538 × 10⁻¹⁹ | −6.65710 × 10⁻¹⁹ | +258 ppm |
| Underdensity | +6.84973 × 10⁻¹⁹ | +6.85157 × 10⁻¹⁹ | +269 ppm |

Units: launch-conformal Mpc⁻². Newton uses the native normal-velocity transplant and independent branches after launch; GR uses the conserved surrounding matter, constrained radial evolution and first-derivative curvature readout, native mesh 5, 80 cells/Mpc and 192 steps per carrier period. Across the other **Newtonian** incident prescriptions the central offsets are +258 to +287 and +269 to +297 ppm on that same GR run. On the innermost ruler the primary offsets are +767/+600 ppm and on the outermost −1,076/−1,112 ppm. There is no universal offset of one sign. Both cold histories evolve; the cold pressure switch is exactly zero on both signs, grids and all three Newtonian preparations. **Which material changes the tide?** The evolving exterior quadrupole supplies −6.329/+7.416 × 10⁻¹⁹ in the two clouds, versus total changes of −6.655/+6.850 in those units. In particular the shell between 2.706 and 6 comoving Mpc supplies −4.165/+4.330 × 10⁻¹⁹. The exterior between the central ruler and 2.706 Mpc is next; 6–12 Mpc partly cancels both. Contributions are signed and need not individually lie between zero and the total. These are source terms, not the crossing of literal mass shells or an inferred Milky Way population. Simply using the mass inside the probe would miss the dominant term. Dilution and the motion of the ruler individually matter. Shell breakdown on both meshes (not bundled here).

**Was the same flow compared?** Both descriptions start from the previously saved density and phase gradients. We also reconstructed the *actual* number flux of curved-space dust and of the two real wave quadratures over the full 120 Mpc launch domain. At unchanged phases, its difference from flat transport, projected into the central tidal rate and multiplied by the interval, is +8.08/−6.68 × 10⁻²⁴, or **−12.1/−9.75 ppm of the respective Newtonian changes**. The launch correction reduces the magnitude, not the direction needed to explain the extra relativistic change on the preferred readout. This is a measured launch slope, not its integral through the whole history; no rephasing made these two spatial metrics identical. **What limits the small difference?** The two GR curvature readouts differ on the *same geometry* by one-sixth of the Hamiltonian constraint defect. A newly derived independent proper-time integral of that defect accounts for the readout split across 18 signed probe/configuration comparisons: at worst 0.0324% of the small split, or 0.032 ppm of the full tidal change. Only 14/18 pass 20 ppm **on the split itself**. The old endpoint subtraction of the defect is not precise enough. On the 80-cell central constrained runs the split alone amounts to about 143/105 ppm of the total increments. On the free evolution/direct-curvature combination some offsets reverse sign, and some errors grow under refinement. The small GR-minus-Newton remainder therefore has **no readout-independent sign determination** here; the data support a common leading change, not a measured positive excess. Constraint identity results (not bundled here).

We also tested whether the momentum-constraint divergence and motion of the ruler independently predict that defect. The derived continuum transport law passes an off-constraint analytic test to better than 10⁻¹¹ in both harmonics, for both evolution choices. On the central **physical** constrained runs it misses by −28.7%/+6.64% of the defect integral at 80 cells/Mpc and −19.2%/−109% at 120. Those percentages refer to the tiny constraint signal, not to gravity as a whole. The ruler and the momentum contribution can be individually larger than their sum. Mesh refinement by itself is not the remedy demonstrated here. 80-cell results (not bundled here) · 120-cell results (not bundled here) · analytic control (not bundled here).

**Numerical checks.** The initial Newtonian tide agrees with the sealed CMC comparison on both archived preparation meshes. Polynomial-density, signed radial/angular flux, derivative and moving-ruler fixtures are independent of the clouds. On the heavy runs: endpoint versus integrated changes agree within 0.30 ppm; independently evolving the archive's on-minus-off channel agrees within 0.040 ppm; doubling the number of fine-mesh Newtonian steps changes the result by less than 0.00050 ppm. Numerical agreement of the Newtonian solver is not an error estimate for the GR result. An initial cold run caught a one-roundoff-unit offset at the interpolation origin; we made the first stored background sample exactly its own reference (no extrapolation or tolerance change), then replayed both models. No new coupling or observational tuning is called for by this result.
