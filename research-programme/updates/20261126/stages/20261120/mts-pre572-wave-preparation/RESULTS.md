# Evolving the wave into 572 instead of resetting it

**Outcome.** Starting from the same-number stationary state one block earlier produces a genuine inward wave current, but not enough excitation to account for the missing inner force. For all three foregrounds, changing only the timing on the specified source paths cannot supply that force either. This identifies a concrete limit on *preparing the halo in equilibrium at 544*. It does not rule out an already excited or accreting halo.

Used the all-gas and stellar masses and positions from two earlier [FIRE-2 m12i](https://astrosims.flatironinstitute.org/fire) snapshots: 70,961,229 and 70,984,632 gas-plus-star particle instances before the aperture cut. Eight source files read by range (2,522,916,882 bytes including acquisition overhead), two new compact mass banks per frame/species distributed across their original pieces; no raw dark halo or gas velocities substituted for the wave. The earlier window covers 640.33 Myr up to 572. Then the same full wave, including its phase, continues for another 425.18 Myr to 600. Neither the midpoint nor the date convention was chosen from the sky forces. One nominal scale-factor metadata check failed at 544; the separate redshift-column and particle-header check, without changing the physical coordinates, times or tolerance, is recorded in the method (not bundled here).

This is on the transferred local foregrounds, not a claim to measure the history of the Milky Way.

**Motion, not just density.** At the inner shell near 6.185 kpc, respectively 2.16%, 1.73%, 1.20% of the *total fixed occupation per Gyr* is flowing inward instantaneously at 572 on the midpoint histories. These are rates, not fractions of the galaxy added. The integrated surface currents match the changes inside the apertures to better than 2.5×10⁻¹² of occupation. In the first case 0.879 of 0.906 (km/s)² appears in the imaginary, current-carrying wave quadrature relative to the matching ground. This quadrature is *not* synonymous with gas speed or pure bulk kinetic energy. Conjugating the wave reverses the calculated gravitational force slope at unchanged density and energy. Across 30 force-slope readouts the potential-projection contribution is at most 6.95×10⁻⁷ relative. [Current-to-force identity](CURRENT-TO-FORCE.md).

**Does unresolved timing change the conclusion?** For the same two specified formation paths and the same late knots, the answer is no within this discretized branch. On cut1 and cut2 the prefix work envelopes are at most 83.88 and 53.22 (km/s)², below nonlinear energy caps of 98.71 and 99.18, with their overlap traps also verified. The first calculation on anchorA was inconclusive. Connecting the early source-work bound to the later force bound, then recomputing both from the operators, gives **at most 77.102 versus at least 77.601 (km/s)²** in the same nonlinear energy at 572 and compatible wave overlap. The latter number is a *small-loss* threshold; it does not replace 46.65 for arbitrary unknown initial states. No assumption of slow forcing or favorable free phase is needed.
