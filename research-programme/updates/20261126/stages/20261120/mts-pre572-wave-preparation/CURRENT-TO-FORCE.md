# From wave current to gravity at a fixed probe

The even response is a change of mass distribution, not a force proportional to the amount of gas moving past the probe. For the Newtonian common-metric term, write a measured force component F=∫W(x)ρ_w(x)d³x, where W is the corresponding component of the gradient of the stationary Green kernel, including its sign and the same G as in the potential. The time derivative at a *fixed* probe is

    ∂t F = ∫ ∇W · j_w d³x − ∮ W j_w · dS ,

by the wave's own number continuity equation, with j here in mass-current units. If W is singular at the probe, understand the identity weakly (or at the finite force resolution used here); one may not just drop an interior flux surface. This relation contains no extra coupling from current to gravity. A moving tracer also sees (v_tracer · ∇)F, and an actual Milky Way prediction would include the ordinary-source and satellite variations. The tables report only the wave contribution; they are not an observed jerk or a constraint on a post-Newtonian vector coupling.

For a finite basis build the exact Hermitian force observable A and the represented Hamiltonian H=K+V_ord+V_wave. In units with iψ′=Hψ, F′=2 Im〈Aψ,Hψ〉. Evaluate independently (1) differentiating the density multipoles along the equations of motion, (2) the two-sided directional difference of force along this same tangent, and (3) the matrix commutator, separated into K, V_ord and V_wave. In continuum the multiplicative potentials commute with W. Finite angular projections need not commute; a spurious current-to-force contribution must be measured, not set to zero analytically on the grid. The separate file for each foreground gives all three terms and tests the kinetic-only approximation at the original relative tolerance. A tiny relative error on near-zero jerk is not guaranteed by an accurate force, nor does closing the discrete commutator alone prove continuum accuracy.

The noncommuting part can itself be cross-checked without an extra evolution. For the finite angular projector P and Q=1−P, split the dot product of Wψ and Vψ into its P and Q parts. The full quadrature product is real since both spatial functions are real. Therefore the imaginary part from the discarded Q products must cancel the projected potential commutator with the *opposite sign*. The implementation tests this with the actual unresolved products, not by setting their sum to zero by definition.

For a radial *number* cut the projector is block identity inside each included radial shell, so all angular potential blocks commute exactly. The independent shell-flux test is consequently insensitive to that angular projection issue. Time reversal at a snapshot preserves F, nonlinear energy, self-potential and occupation but reverses F′; this makes a signed current prediction distinguishable from a change of instantaneous density. Here F′ is per Myr in units of 10⁻¹⁰ m/s²; multiply by 3.16881×10⁻²⁴ for m/s³.
