# From conserved occupation to the force at a tracer

## Axisymmetric wave (the requested calculation)

Take a real, stationary wave g in the rotating phase frame of its chemical potential μ. In the discrete mass inner product N=gᵀg. For ψ=g+x+iy the linear density is δρ=2 Re(g*δψ), not |δψ|². If H is the stationary self-consistent Hamiltonian minus μ, then Hg=0; projecting off g removes the unobservable common phase. Differentiate the Poisson self-potential, not just the external stellar field: with the positive vacuum Poisson operator L and the product with the ground encoded by B, the stiffness is M=H−2BL⁻¹Bᵀ. In the retained azimuthal-mean, plane-even sector the dimensionless evolution on the tangent space is

    x′ = H y,   y′ = −M x − P⊥[δΦ_LMC(t) g],   δN = 2〈g,x〉.
    δN′ = 2〈Hg,y〉 = 0.

The saved external field is a potential per unit mass; it enters with its physical amplitude, not inferred from the target acceleration. The finite radial basis uses a reduced wave with a factor of radius: convert back to the angular wave and divide the product by the square of the dimensionless radius to form physical-space density in solver units. For the native-host files see matched_sky.py: delta_density is this signed change in dimensionless density multipoles (not solar masses per cubic kpc). Reintegrating the density via the saved Newton kernel gives independent radial and polar accelerations. Only the induced terms have radial-even and polar-odd parity across the plane. The direct satellite contribution must be taken separately at each site and in the same accelerating frame as its control.

On a positive tangent-space mode with frequency σ, the zero-initial-state solution is −∫F(t)sin[σ(T−t)]dt/σ. The alternative displacement and velocity at the first epoch follow the initial source and its saved slope; they are not free fit parameters. No later observation sets an initial condition. Radii quoted are cylindrical; the two reported acceleration components are spherical radial and polar. Counts refer to the original signed intervals, not a likelihood, nor eight independent observations. See also the original analytic replay (not bundled here).

## Separate m=2 prediction

Use the same ground but the distinct complex m=2 sector X=u₊+u₋*. Its azimuthal integral vanishes, so a first-order number correction is unnecessary. Combining the two conjugate equations gives X″+H M X=−2H F(t). The factors of two differ from those in the real m=0 convention. In the canonical basis of the positive operator H¹ᐟ² M H¹ᐟ² integrate the real and imaginary parts of F independently. A real m=2 force is obtained from both conjugate Fourier coefficients; the azimuthal gradient carries −2i/R. The full bar-axis cosine readout retains that phase. Recovering the earlier saved complex sky on its own background checks the Fourier convention and sign. None of these m=2 terms is used to calculate the axisymmetric-only scores.

The previously fixed three-ring weights cancel a direct uniform tidal Hessian because its azimuthal acceleration is linear in R. They do not cancel the wave's gravitational response to that Hessian: Poisson gravity depends on the redistributed density at other radii as well. With normalized Y₂₂=√(15/8)(1−μ²), the uniform part of the incident potential is T(t) r² Y₂₂, where T=(Φxx−Φyy−2iΦxy)/(8√(15/8)). local_tide.py evaluates the analytic Hessian of the actual archived potential at the origin; Cartesian differences independently check it. susceptibility.py propagates this source and the difference from the full field. Subtracting at input or after propagation yields the same wave. The causal response is a sum of sine kernels divided by frequency; the independently solved static ring response divided by the direct tidal gradient is a signed length. It is specific to these weights and grounds, not a new universal coupling.

## What early preparation could change

The conserved free quadratic energy is E=(X†MX+Ẋ†H⁻¹Ẋ)/2. For the complex azimuthal readout and full-mesh static Gram scalar G, the minimum free-mode energy capable of shifting that readout by d is d²/(8G). For the further constraint of zero change to all meridian radial/polar forces, only the real part of X is constrained (the imaginary quadrature is invisible on the chosen meridian); eliminate the four independent real readouts by their Gram Schur complement. Both limiting initial states are constructed by evolving a terminal displacement backwards, then checked by forward propagation.

For a narrower physical preparation, suppose the pre-archive satellite forcing has its first saved spatial shape F₀, starts at zero with an unexcited wave, and grows as a scalar a(t) to final value 1. The exact static state for F₀ is Xₛ=−2M⁻¹F₀ and its free energy is Eₛ=2F₀†M⁻¹F₀. In energy coordinates the freely propagating state is unitary. Superpose the step responses against da: at the start of the archive the total state is the final static vector minus the sum of freely propagated static vectors weighted by da. Hence √E ≤ [1+TV(a)]√Eₛ. Any monotone turn-on (including jumps) has E≤4Eₛ, independent of duration. After the archive start the difference from the zero-start driven solution propagates freely, so this same bound applies to that additional energy at the present observation. prehistory_bound.py computes Eₛ using the full sparse mesh, not just the retained modal truncation. For a rotating scalar coefficient a exp(−2iφ), nondecreasing amplitude, TV≤1+2∫a|dφ|; this is not a bound on arbitrary changes of spatial shape or other sources. The finite-grid energy result does not impose a cosmological state or constrain a separately excited halo.
