# The fixed-occupation axisymmetric test, with an independent source check

**Direct result:** on both original backgrounds and all six saved Large Magellanic Cloud (LMC) histories, the reconstructed wave draws mass inward. The ground/displacement interference redistributes 1.67–3.48 × 10⁸ solar masses into the sphere of radius 6.185 kpc, balanced outside. At the northern 6-kpc probe the added radial acceleration is −0.01314 to −0.00645 and the added polar acceleration is −0.000558 to −0.000235, in 10⁻¹⁰ m/s². The radial term helps (1.08–2.44% of the inner radial deficit across both hemispheres), but the northern polar band requires a positive shift. The static derivative would have predicted the wrong sign for the *actual evolving response*. Both MTS and static NFW pass the same 2/8 original bands: outer polar at both heights. Six histories are alternatives, not independent trials. A fresh read-only time-dependent replay rebuilt the density and both forces and verified all 192 signed comparisons. The tiny induced polar term changes by up to 0.2264% on box enlargement; no pass/fail decision changes. See [derivation](DERIVATION.md) and original force tables (not bundled here).

## Does improving the preparation change that verdict?

This is a **separate source test**, not an extra wave added to the first one. Two previously frozen predictions were opened against full FIRE-2 m12i snapshot 581, selected for a large deviation in the *cold* archive before seeing its all-gas source, not selected by Galactic acceleration. Four saved pieces include 57,213,956 gas and 13,807,731 stellar particle records before the common spatial cut. Subtract the physically irrelevant constant potential using the frozen wave weights. The five-knot linear interpolation misses the actual potential by 83.0633 (km/s)² RMS; including the predicted cold-disk change misses by 15.1328, **81.78% smaller**. The tangent driving the density response gives 83.0619 versus 15.1240. These compare predictions of one simulated time slice, not observational likelihoods.

Taking the *simulated host as itself* with its proper wave reference, the fine actual inner radial change is +0.002737; plain interpolation predicts −0.000175, cold-informed +0.002620 (same acceleration units). The phase-aligned final wave error falls from 0.001324 to 0.0000729 of the norm. The improvement is not universal: the tiny outer polar error grows rather than shrinking. No Milky Way force score is assigned to the untransferred simulation.

Transferring the measured simulation changes onto each retained ordinary foreground, carrying the same complex wave from 579 and preserving occupation, gives:

Here the different drive gives the right signs for both inner components but is much too weak. For each foreground the counts hold for every direct LMC alternative. All 288 terminal coarse/fine signed totals pass the original relative tolerance; **none of the 15 isolated small force changes** does at that tolerance (five readouts on three foregrounds). No band flips. The five-knot restart reproduces its predecessor exactly. Number error stays below 3.64 × 10⁻¹²; the largest work-balance error is 2.88 × 10⁻⁷ (km/s)². The smallest required inward force is more than 46 times the matching upper bound on any subsequent free response of the tested terminal states, without choosing a phase.

## A quantitative target for formation

For the six measured source knots joined along the specified paths, one can also allow an *unknown* initial wave. To attain even the necessary inner vertical force after assembly, its specific excitation in the frozen initial one-particle Hamiltonian must be at least **46.64, 98.71, 99.18 (km/s)²**, respectively (quoted downward). This is nonlinear excitation plus a nonnegative density self-energy correction: it is **not** a lower bound on uncompensated nonlinear energy for arbitrary density change. For pure phase motion at the matching ground density, the two energies coincide. The equivalent minimum RMS coherent speeds are about **9.66, 14.05 and 14.08 km/s**. These constrained matrix results are specific to the retained finite-axisymmetric wave spaces, fixed occupations, source paths, and terminal foregrounds. Neither just attaining this energy nor using one of these speeds as a tunable input predicts the data.

**Next discriminating test:** take the wave from an independently specified earlier formation history and calculate its density and current at 572. Compare its inherited specific energy and then its signed forces to the thresholds. Do not set the wave speed equal to the gas velocity, choose a phase against the force data, or transfer an occupation between galaxies. If earlier wave information is not available, evolving an explicitly prepared earlier state is a conditional test, not a measured initial condition.
