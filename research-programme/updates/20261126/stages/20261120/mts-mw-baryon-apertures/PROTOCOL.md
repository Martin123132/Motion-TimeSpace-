# Solar-neighborhood and inner-disk check (no kinematic fit)

Compare the sealed Vasiliev disk/bulge and both retained ordinary sources with independently observed kinds of mass: source counts, ISM inventory and vertical distributions. Test local midplane density, full column, ±1.1-kpc slab, bounded ±1.5-kpc slab, and stellar mass in a 100-pc sphere. Keep aperture and helium, stellar-remnant, survey radius and phase conventions explicit. At R≈6 kpc distinguish measured ring averages, interpolation and adopted radial shape. A missing phase is not zero. These data are separate observables from the force bands, not necessarily historical holdouts.

Before scoring any new inner force, construct two external fields from the same local ordinary counts. Use the archived 3-kpc exponential radial scale for the controlled radial extrapolation and retain the native bulge. (A) A single disk with its column and scale height determined by the total baryon column and midplane density. (B) A stellar and gas disk each determined by its own column and midplane density. Preserve the radial column in A vs B and assign no force data to either mass or height. Compare to the original unaltered foregrounds. Use these as conditional source tests, not as measured global densities. Freeze both files before solving for the wave or reading their force scores. If local evidence disfavors the original heavy single disk, do not pursue its extra modes as a Milky Way prediction. Test the stationary weak-field response for the old fixed occupations and the original outer circular speed, and give NFW the same foreground and outer datum. Do not copy a previously driven response onto a different ground.
