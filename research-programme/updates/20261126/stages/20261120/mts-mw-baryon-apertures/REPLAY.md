# Evidence, checks and offline replay

Primary result: RESULTS.md. Frozen choices: PROTOCOL.md and frozen-inputs.json. All calculations are local, single numerical thread; no service or ongoing calculation. The earlier common-disk stage, original LMC banks and existing papers/data were not edited. Three small primary-source papers were acquired; saved releases are identified by version and file checksum in source-receipts.csv. This is research output, not a public repackaging of the source PDFs.

**What the evidence counts.** McKee, Parravano & Hollenbach (arXiv:1509.05334v1; Tables 1–3, Eq. 46) combine heterogeneous local counts and gas observations. Table 1 includes brown dwarfs and stellar remnants, Table 2 already includes helium and metals. For finite-height synthesis normalize each gas phase to its tabulated full surface density; Gaussian laws use exp[−(z/h)²], not a Gaussian standard deviation h. Separate 0.11 M☉/pc² in the Gum Nebula from the extended exponential H II distribution; bracket its placement instead of assigning its full mass to every slab. Tabulated rounding means the phase sum need not exactly equal the printed total. Brown dwarfs use the assumed 400-pc exponential effective scale in cumulative-profile diagnostics, not an additional population inside a 33.4 total already including them.

Lutsenko et al. (arXiv:2507.06052v1) estimate a **sphere** mean, not a midplane value. Integrate the disk+bulge over the entire sphere including radial gradients; solar offsets 0/20/30 pc are sensitivity cases. Low-mass IMF extrapolation and WD/NS estimates are not identical to the older star census; report the substellar inclusion alternatives, not a common precision likelihood. The Lian stellar data here are the specific pinned author revision in the previous population-resolved stage (see verify_apertures.py), not the differently rounded values in every preprint version. Count north and south separately, then sum. Shared Gaia material and earlier use of Lian explicitly prevent a blanket independent-holdout claim.

Marasco et al. (arXiv:1707.00743v1) Fig. 11b: markers and errors are independent vector paths, calibrated by nine x and six y grid lines. Use only the declared interval around 6 kpc. Input is azimuthally inferred atomic hydrogen and CO-bright molecular hydrogen; multiply their already mass-based sum by 1.4 exactly once for helium/metals. Do **not** multiply the molecular column by two again. The final low H₂ error at 6.81 kpc is clipped by the plotting axis and is left missing. The paper estimates gas geometry/kinematics using line-profile modeling; uncertainty intervals include quadrant/kinematic and molecular-conversion assumptions. Neighboring smoothed points are not independent detections. For a generous sensitivity check all available printed high errors (and mass-scaled stellar marginal errors) move up together and the stellar radial registration shifts ±0.1 kpc. This is not an upper confidence bound on missing material nor on a common stellar-mass calibration.

**Gravity solver.** Use the unchanged κ, Newtonian coupling, Poisson boundary and fixed occupation/outer-speed constraints. In a normalized sech² layer, Σ(<|z|)=Σ∞ tanh(z/2h); ρ₀=Σ∞/(4h). The two new source files are determined before solving the stationary waves. At equal outer circular speed the NFW control may change its *amplitude only*, never its 16-kpc scale radius or any inner-force coefficient. An independent thin-disk check uses the Hankel transform of the exponential radial profile: S(k)=M[1+(kRd)²]^(−3/2)/(2π). Convolve the normalized vertical density with exp(−k|z−u|); the radial derivative uses kJ₁, the vertical one kJ₀ times sign(z−u). The bulge uses exact spherical shell integrals. The wave force is independently reconstructed from its squared radial-angular eigenfunction, integrated through the Newton kernel. The physical G used to express forces in surface-density units and the archive's rounded 232500-M☉ code mass are kept explicit.

Archive evaluator default harmonic order 32 alone was not sufficiently precise for the thinner sources. We retained its values, recomputed the actual potentials **and** stationary waves at 64, 96 and 160, and checked vs the independent integral. Only the last full-sky refinement attains the inherited 2×10⁻⁵ maximum relative target (1.74×10⁻⁵). All classifications survive the refinements. Independent orders, radial-flux closures and force controls are in sky-resolution.csv, gravity-evaluator-refinement.csv, poisson-radial-flux.csv and vertical-gravity-refinement.csv. The force difference at the RAVE sites is below 0.001 M☉/pc² for the final independent check. These are numerical checks, not error estimates for the astrophysical inputs.

**Offline check:** use the installed Python 3.13 with NumPy/SciPy, requests, PyMuPDF and the previous stage's native Agama evaluator. From this directory, `python -B verify_apertures.py` reads saved inputs/states and rechecks the seal. Reproduction order: aperture_io.py (offline if cached), inner_gas.py, source_models.py, freeze_input.py, census_profile.py, matched_sky.py (no arguments, then 64 96 160), independent_disk.py, vertical_gravity.py, radial_term.py, height_contrast.py, shell_requirement.py, verify_apertures.py. Run serially with -B. No output writer replaces a differing file. Fine primary force tables use the filename suffix ell96; the ell160 run is an explicit convergence test. The RAVE contrast is an external tracer diagnostic from Bienaymé et al., A&A 571 A92, not independent of all prior dynamical literature. At no point was the inferred RAVE local dark density used to set wave mass or to substitute for a measured acceleration.
