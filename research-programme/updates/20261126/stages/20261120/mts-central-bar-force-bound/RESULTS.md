# Where the Milky Way's central stars cannot supply the missing force

**The Galactic bar helps in the right direction but not nearly enough.** At R=6 kpc, z=1.5 kpc, the primary photometric shape adds +0.00509 in polar acceleration relative to the equal-mass sphere. All nine published shapes give +0.00460 to +0.00926. A version that also preserves every original central radial column gives +0.00793. Even allowing a different arbitrary height and angle distribution in each central ring, the direct gravity of that central excess cannot supply the paired force requirement.

Polar here means the angular component in spherical coordinates, positive toward the plane at the northern probe; accelerations are in 10⁻¹⁰ m/s². These are marginal force bands, not a likelihood. Ranges over six satellite histories are scenarios, not error bars.

## What was measured, assumed and predicted

The observed inputs are [Cao et al.'s OGLE-III red-clump fits, Tables 1 and 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1303.6430) and the four stellar mass columns retained in the previous stage. The three density laws and two sightline restrictions are alternatives using overlapping data, not nine independent measurements. We held each stellar population's integrated central excess fixed; the total was **7.354 billion M☉**. Assigning that mass the red-clump shape is an assumption, not a measurement of all stars and remnants. The nominal E3 places 0.85% of its mass outside the old 4-kpc cylinder; we did not truncate it in the forward calculation. Its adopted pattern speed is the existing [Clarke–Gerhard value, 33.29 km/s/kpc](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.10875). No extra dark mass, force coupling, bar amplitude or orientation was fitted to the eight sky components.

We re-prepared three stationary backgrounds with the E3 azimuthal mean. The retained occupations give outer mean speeds **225.30 and 224.22 km/s** without recalibration. The third gives **224.03 km/s** by construction. Only the mean is a circular reference; the instantaneous radial-force speed proxy along the bar-test meridian is 224.064 km/s for the third wave. The spherical NFW halo is matched to the same mean speed on each foreground. It receives the same direct stellar and satellite forces, no wave or live collisionless wake.

For each wave, six unchanged [LMC histories](https://zenodo.org/records/8015660) were evolved afresh in the fixed-number even axisymmetric sector. The rotating bar's induced force was separately recomputed for m=2,4,…,32 on this ground, with outgoing/closed wave boundaries. The **whole rotating-wave increment** at the inner north probe is only +0.000105–0.000108 polar. This is first-order superposition, not a simulation of nonlinear interference, a fit to the data, or an all-harmonic theorem.

## A stronger result than trying several shapes

The original north and south [Gaia force bands](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13358) restrict the even polar component. After subtracting the noncentral ordinary field and direct satellite, the two sites need **at least 0.16493–0.16615**, depending on frame/history, even allowing arbitrary reflection-odd assistance. This is the requirement for the **central stars plus everything else not yet subtracted**, not a direct measurement of the dark force.

- **Central excess only, fixed retained radial columns:** no nonnegative redistribution in height or azimuth can give more than **0.06969** at the inner probe; imposing half-turn symmetry tightens this to **0.03100**. Thus at least **0.09525** (or **0.13394** with half-turn symmetry, rounded down) must come from elsewhere, including the dark component. This follows from the common Newtonian kernel, independent of a halo profile. See DERIVATION.md and exact-rounded-upper.csv for the outward-rounded certificate. It does not constrain an arbitrary extra mass component or a bar with unconstrained radial columns.
- **All inner stars, not just the central excess:** let all four mapped stellar populations change height and angle, keeping the mass in every ring and the other sources fixed. Even the generously maximized half-turn-symmetric distribution limited to **R′≤4.5 kpc** falls short at 6 kpc by at least **0.01594 for the previous wave** and **0.02455 for NFW**, with their respective dark fields held. For NFW the bound still excludes R′≤4.625 kpc. The wave enclosure opens at 4.625 kpc; that is *permission to test*, not a successful stellar model. If all disk stars may also become lopsided, this particular all-stars test is already inconclusive at 4 kpc. It does not invalidate the stronger central-excess-only result.
- Attribution matters: a halo that dynamically responds to an arbitrary new disk is not held fixed. The all-stars figures above do not rule out that response. Only for the primary measured bar have the wave dynamics here actually been recomputed. The same mass-field calculation and observable definitions are applied to the comparison model.

## Numerical checks and next physical test

The nine direct models, the ring-preserving case, and available independent force implementations pass the original relative target on resolved nonzero direct components. The fine projected LMC density reproduces the independently read force and conserves linear number. All time refinements pass 2 × 10⁻⁵; some small induced spatial components do not. For the rotating wave, 225/384 non-negligible component comparisons between spatial settings miss the relative target, but the maximum absolute change is below 4.8 × 10⁻¹⁰ in sky units. Across the 576 final signed totals the largest tested change is below 7.9 × 10⁻⁷, with no changed band decision. This is comparison under finite refinement, not an infinite-domain error guarantee.

**Next:** test the extended 4.5–6 kpc stellar disk, where this geometric enclosure first stops excluding enough polar pull, and distinguish lopsided from half-turn-symmetric structure. Use measured longitudinal mass contrasts or conservative independent bounds on them, not masses or viewing angles chosen to hit the inner band. Test radial and polar forces at both radii together, alongside the existing optical-depth constraints on compact matter. A globally normalized stellar column or a tracer-count enhancement alone is not that azimuth-resolved mass measurement. If the allowed contrasts are insufficient, carry the residual into a bound on formation-driven wave stress rather than fitting a halo flattening.
