# What can assembling the Galactic disk actually do to this wave?

20 November 2026 · Private · Three saved census foregrounds and occupations, six archived direct LMC alternatives, the original signed bands and static NFW controls. No fitted assembly time or new source coupling.

**Answer:** on the retained even, axisymmetric single-wave mesh, a disk becoming flatter at fixed spherical mass profile can make the inner vertical gravity stronger for a time. It cannot make it strong enough from these spherical preparations. This is not just a null result at a particular elapsed time: we derived an energy–force bound that covers any monotone build-up of the saved angular potential, including jumps, pauses and subsequent free evolution. It does not cover every possible form of Galactic assembly.

| Ordinary foreground | Extra inward vertical acceleration needed | Upper bound, any monotone angular assembly | Minimum additional specific work for another source to have a chance |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Measured (anchorA) | about 0.282 | 0.01927 | 110.7 (km/s)² |
| One-width census endpoint (cut1) | about 0.188 | 0.01950 | 124.4 (km/s)² |
| Two-width census endpoint (cut2) | about 0.087 | 0.01973 | 83.6 (km/s)² |

First two numerical columns are in 10⁻¹⁰ m/s² at cylindrical R=6 kpc and mirrored heights ±1.5 kpc. Bounds are rounded outward. The data require **4.40–14.64 times** the allowed angular-only response across preparations and histories. This is a necessary condition from the original marginal intervals, not a fit or a χ². The additional-work column bounds the maximum cumulative net work reached at some time, per unit wave mass, relative to the specified angular path. It is *not* a rotation speed, an observed energy supply or a sufficient condition. Work bounds are demonstrated points, not optimized physical thresholds. Source tables: [anchorA](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `extra-work-bound-anchorA.csv`), [cut1](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `extra-work-bound-cut1.csv`), [cut2](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `extra-work-bound-cut2.csv`).

**What actually happened in time.** A sudden spherical-to-disk change supplied only 7.63–9.65 (km/s)² of energy above the final compact equilibrium per unit wave mass. Its upper limit on extra inward vertical force is 0.01512–0.01559 in the table units. We propagated the full nonlinear self-field at fixed N for 327.8 Myr and also tried a smooth turn-on lasting 163.9 Myr. At the fixed saved times the sudden case briefly strengthened the inner vertical pull by up to 0.00102. The smooth case retained 12.1–16.2% of the sudden excitation energy after assembly. The stationary branch served as the slow benchmark; no Galactic disk age was inferred.

**Same sky comparison.** All post-assembly snapshots and the stationary benchmark give MTS 2/8 signed components, versus static NFW 2/8, 4/8 and 4/8 respectively, on all six direct satellite histories. Both models use the same final ordinary and direct satellite fields; the static reference has no wave and is not an evolving live halo. The six orbits are alternatives, not independent data. MTS force directions at Pal 5 and GD-1 remain within one printed width; the total strengths remain too large (1.49–1.70 and 1.97–2.26 widths, respectively). No present-day stationary force was converted to a stream-track likelihood. The disk assembly experiment isolates the wave driven by ordinary matter; it does not superpose the previous LMC-driven wave.

**Checks and limits.** The final nonlinear energy-work error was below 2×10⁻⁸ of the sudden excitation on all fine ramps. All 3,168 signed totals satisfy the original 2×10⁻⁵ relative time-refinement target; 133 of 165 individual small force-change entries (including the zero slow-reference entries in the denominator) do not. We retain both results. The independent short-step density curvature passes that target on every preparation at the two finest steps. For arbitrary states of this mesh the vertical readout changes by less than 9×10⁻¹³ in force units on raising the direct Green quadrature order from 14 to 20 or 28. Twelve of 57 secondary lumped-readout checks miss 10⁻¹⁰ for deliberately rough stress states; the archived trajectories and the original, non-lumped force operator pass. The work argument uses the original operator, with the exact nonlinear self-gravity term, floating-point matrix inertia tests and explicit margins. This is not a continuum, different-domain or multiple-occupation theorem.

**Next physical target.** Test an independently sourced *radial* ordinary mass history or other time-dependent environmental force against the extra-work threshold, before predicting a signed change. For a spherical extra potential U, the work per unit wave mass is −∫U ∂t(dm_wave/N)dt. With vanishing endpoint flux, radial integration by parts gives ∫dt dr [G δM_ord(<r,t)/r²] ∂t[M_wave(<r,t)/N]. Only changing enclosed wave mass contributes. The discrete shell identity and constant-offset check are verified on 36 saved intervals in [the radial-work control](evidence-001.zip) (archive member: `radial-work-control.csv`). Its ordinary-monopole column is a test probe of a field already present, **not** extra energy to spend a second time. If the physical starting occupation or radial profile changes, recompute the energy offset; do not transplant the threshold.
