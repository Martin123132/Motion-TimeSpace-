# Does the satellite move the wave enough relative to the stars?

20 November 2026 · Private · No foreground change, fitted displacement, new observations, or public release.

**No, on the three census-supported preparations tested.** The Large Magellanic Cloud produces a real relative displacement and a distinguishable deformation. Neither closes the original force bands. Even if the wave could spend *all* its driven energy in the most helpful planar shape, the two measured vertical-force requirements alone would cost **257–3,446 times** the energy it actually has in this mode. This is more informative than the unchanged band count: simply rotating or reshaping that same excitation energy cannot repair the deficit.

The three preparations each retain their own occupation; their normalizations were set at the previously adopted 224.03 km/s outer speed, not selected on the eight bands. The six histories are scenarios, not independent measurements. Sign conventions, frames and units are in [the derivation](DERIVATION.md). In units of 10⁻¹⁰ m/s² the largest absolute combined wave-and-planar-frame increment relative to the point-subtracted prediction is 0.000141. Do not confuse the larger change between the original archived frame and the point frame with a physical wave signal.

## What was actually tested

- **Mutual acceleration:** the disk/bulge and wave feel differently weighted versions of the same external field. The ordinary matter also responds to the wave's restoring force. Uniform external acceleration cancels, including both phase quadratures; inertial point motion is not counted as an offset. Reconstructing the wave pull independently from its density and comparing the relative equation with the independent ordinary-potential derivative gives defects below 1e−6 of the respective peak responses. The ordinary density is translated as a body, not internally evolved.
- **A shape-only prediction:** take the Sun-facing azimuthal cosine of the north–south vertical-force difference, then difference the 6 and 8.3 kpc rings with a weight set by the stationary wave gradient. Both uniform acceleration and rigid wave translation cancel. After subtracting the direct LMC, the predicted signal is **−7.62 to −4.82 × 10⁻¹⁶ m/s²**. The translation leakage is below 1e−19 m/s². The static axisymmetric reference gives zero after that same subtraction. The live archived halo changes span both signs and much larger amplitudes; they do not establish this is an MTS-unique signal. A separate direct-satellite gravity implementation changes the subtraction by at most 1.94% of the predicted wave signal across these scenarios. Existing single-meridian bands cannot measure this two-ring azimuthal coefficient.
- **Energy bound:** the lower bound minimizes over the full retained spatial mesh, not over a set of fitted shapes or over the smaller evolution basis. The test relaxes the original eight components to just two vertical intervals and counts both wave quadratures and kinetic energy as available. Even an extra coherently aligned initial perturbation would need at least 226 times the actual driven energy in the easiest case. This is a conditional linear energy comparison, not a bound on galaxy formation, the satellite's entire orbital energy, or the energy of a different mode. NFW is compared to the same vertical intervals; no fictitious wave energy is assigned to it.
- **Off the disk:** at the Pal 5 and GD-1 sites the maximum change in the saved strength observable from this planar wave plus material-reference correction is respectively 0.010 and 0.018 of one printed width over all azimuths. These are bounds on that *increment*, not the whole external tide. The separate 256-azimuth calculation includes the full direct LMC, m=0 radiation-free density response and m=1. Both models still pull too strongly compared with the central stream strengths. The direct tide alone makes the total Pal 5 direction sensitive to azimuth. These are instantaneous force comparisons, not a stationary-stream likelihood.

## What survived refinement

The 40-to-64 basis check exceeded the unchanged 2e−5 relative target in five of 18 small shape signals. At 64-to-88 **all 18 pass**, maximum 8.83e−6. The 8-to-16 time check narrowly missed the target for the small L2M11 offset on the one-width endpoint; inserting the original saved physical time midpoints brings its 16-to-32 relative vector difference to 5.30e−7. These checks do not bound the entire physical source error.

Recomputing the extended wave-minus-ordinary external force at three actual epochs per history using 384 rather than 192 radial intervals and angular degree 65 rather than 25 changes the result by up to **0.312%**. The change is predominantly angular. Some higher-degree mass quadrature comparisons also exceed the relative precision target. No continuous-in-time error bound follows from three epochs; the evolved offsets and energy ratios use the retained full-time source. The box and nonlinear uncertainty of this m=1 calculation have not been established by modal convergence.

**Next physical question:** can an independently prepared anisotropic population supply vertical stress in the inner galaxy without increasing the already excessive off-disk pull? Use actual formation inputs or a formation budget, not another fitted translation, halo mass, or tilt. The earlier 5152 and 5173 checkpoints are relevant because they separate primordial occupation from local state preparation and compare the same formation pipeline with a collisionless control. The source-resolution findings are a precision task if a quantitative two-ring measurement is pursued, not evidence to retune the source.
