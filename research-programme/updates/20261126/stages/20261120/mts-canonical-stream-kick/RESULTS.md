# Resolving the force, and identifying which matter the local expansion was missing

20 November 2026 · private paired spherical formation calculation · no fit

The metric's geodesic force can be carried inside a folded stream instead of being averaged onto two labels. On the same mass distribution, with the same heavy wave and no new coefficient, this removes most of the percent-level error in the Einstein trace (the independent time-evolution law for the local normal expansion). It also exposes a separate transport error. The smooth part of the error is no longer an unidentified effective component: the retained slow scalar dominates it, and its own finite-time, pressure-carrying response is computable.

The ppm discrepancies remaining after accounting are diagnostics, not passing new metric histories.

## Which source produced the remaining trace signal?

Taking a radial derivative of the trace equation and imposing the spatial constraints exposes precisely the Euler force that was omitted when a spatially homogeneous component with nonzero enthalpy was assigned zero local momentum. Its time-integrated contribution is proportional to a(E_s+P_s) times the lapse difference, with no free coefficient. A separate estimate integrates the projected-minus-local stream force, drift and moving-face terms at the first and last endpoints. Together with the measured metric kinematic term, these account for all 16 primary repaired trace residuals to within 1.41 ppm on their original scales; that agreement is not a bound on other preparations. On the late outer wave span the observed −128.10 ppm separates into −115.72 ppm smooth background and −12.42 ppm estimated stream contribution. Replacing the entire smooth background by a cosmological constant would give zero enthalpy and the wrong prediction.

On the actual source tables the canonical slow scalar accounts for **86.15%** of that instantaneous untracked enthalpy at a=0.65 and **97.56%** at a=0.925159. These percentages refer to the H derivative actually used, not a fraction of total cosmic energy or of the total trace discrepancy. The remainder includes measured model inputs for massive neutrinos and radiation, and an identifiable tabular derivative effect: the log-linear H slope differs from a smooth derivative, especially at the early epoch (1.32×10⁻¹⁰ Mpc⁻²). The independently summed physical species agree with the cubic derivative's remainder to ~2.3×10⁻⁹ relative at both anchors. The cold and wave examples share their expansion preparation here; the two fractions are not independent observational determinations.

## Let the retained slow field respond

We evolved its two canonical variables with its tabulated mass and starting normal velocity in both late metric histories, starting locally uniform. No sound speed, exchange function, action term or pressure profile was fitted. The experiment is on the specified metric, not a second evolution of Einstein gravity after adding its source. Across the four spans the finite-time current supplies 86.6–97.7% of its own signed drive in cold and 82.9–99.5% in the wave case; the pressure difference supplies most of the balance. With the other small, derived volume terms included, momentum balance closes within **0.11 ppm of the scalar drive itself**. The independent spherical-wave analytic check gives 2.74 ppm; halving the medium spatial step changes the current by at most 3.86 ppm of the drive. The nearest measurement is outside both artificial boundary light cones by at least 0.014 Mpc. The full 65-slice local field histories are saved. A homogeneous normal velocity at the start is conditional physical input; it is not a uniquely reconstructed cosmological state.
