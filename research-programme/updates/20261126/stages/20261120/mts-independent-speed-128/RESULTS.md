# Second weak-field speed: a frozen tidal prediction survives, and the source turnover is localized

**Primary result.** At q=128, all 64 comparisons pass the original 20-ppm gate. The full Einstein evolutions give a final signed-host wave–cold excess over the independently evolved Newton tide of **57.60–67.87 ppm in magnitude**. The frozen transverse material stress accounts for it to within **9.94 ppm** over all clocks and apertures. No fitted normalization or q=64 curved exterior was inserted. This is a finite-speed numerical test on *one formation family*, not new galaxy data or a new measurement of gravity.

**What doubling q actually tests.** At fixed physical clock the frozen source predicts that the *normalized* stress shift changes by only 0.0186 ppm or less on doubling q. The actual full change relative to q=64 reaches 7.16 ppm, so these trajectories do not resolve that tiny change. They **do** distinguish the signal from halving the *whole* q=64 excess: at the final clock all readings disagree with that null by 27.93–37.52 ppm. Short tensor travel time, not a law that every correction halves, explains why a nearly constant normalized leading signal can coexist with a genuine Newton limit. This does not extrapolate the nonlinear solution from two points.

**A time-dependent matter calculation.** Independently advanced number, phase and surrounding histories supply the evolving tensor stress, without reading either measured curvature. Including expansion, clock and the moving proper aperture changes the final predicted stress by approximately **4.67–4.82 ppm at q=64** and **2.33–2.41 ppm at q=128**, with opposite signs for the two hosts. Of the latter, 1.38–1.44 ppm comes from changing material in the flat operator; roughly 0.95–0.99 ppm comes from expansion and ruler together. The direct live-source calculation reduces the largest residual against pure Newton plus stress from **6.643 to 3.443 ppm** at q=64 and from **9.933 to 7.601 ppm** at q=128. Halving the source time step changes the live readout by less than 0.000001 ppm; doubling the independent spatial resolution changes it by up to 0.00335 ppm. These are numerical comparisons, not confidence intervals. This auxiliary prediction uses a linear tensor equation on the matched expanding background; it does not replace the prospective frozen target or absorb unknown free radiation. **Where the stress turns off.** The divergence-free radial quadrupole satisfies a local wave equation with weight r⁶. A separate conservative radial spatial discretization, with analytic mode propagation, reproduces the Green/moment forecast to **0.01757, 0.00440 and 0.00110 ppm** on 40, 80 and 160 cells/Mpc. At q=262144 the frozen and short-time Taylor forecasts separate by roughly 35–37 ppm; the full-clock tensor response has fallen to roughly 23 ppm in magnitude. At q=1048576 it is about 5.6 ppm. Both of those are *tensor-channel* calculations, **not** high-q full Einstein trajectories. The time-dependent source test changes the final flat-clock stress result by less than 0.000061 ppm at q=262144. A mode-by-mode total-variation bound on the affine source histories limits the changing-material contribution of the flat operator there to **0.00378 ppm**, including arbitrary phase cancellation in the propagator. The bound does not control unsampled rapid variation of a different physical source.

For the frozen, finite spatial model, each source-detector mode contributes cⱼ(cos ωⱼt−1)/q. Summing positive and negative weights separately bounds *all phases*, not just the sampled q values. On the 12-Mpc, 160-cell/Mpc calculation a common sufficient q for magnitude below 20 ppm for all four signed apertures is **1.77 million**. At this full physical clock the earliest outer-wall causal cutoff among the four is q≈1.966 million. There is a nonempty controlled interval; above the cutoff do not treat this particular numerical domain as the unbounded formation. These bounds do not bound the free scalar, vector or tensor data of a different solution. **A way to reach that test without millions of carrier steps.** The original exponential stepper with 124/128/132 full-clock steps was compared to 1024 resolved steps at q=64 and 2048 at q=128. All **480** paired tidal comparisons agree within **3.322 ppm**, including one off-phase clock, both hosts, controls, forms and apertures. This is roughly 8× or 16× fewer time steps (not a measured wall-time speedup). The slow wave envelope agrees far more closely than the fast normal-momentum increment: relative L² error can reach 0.033% in the latter. Passing for tides does not prove microscopic uniform accuracy at all speeds.
