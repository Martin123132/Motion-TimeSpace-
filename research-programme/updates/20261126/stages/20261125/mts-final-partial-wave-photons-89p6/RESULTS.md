# Equation forecast for the unfinished fifth cycle

The phase-resolved forecast from each branch's own left state reproduces the saved photon signal without rounding the interval up to a whole cycle. The fine-grid forecast accounts for **+28.15517% of the signed late discrepancy**; the archive gives +28.15492%. This is a test of the representation difference on the saved simulation, not of observational data.

The interval is 89.52018481110348–89.6 conformal Mpc: 0.07981519 Mpc and 0.86145165 cycles on the saved phase clock. Primary uses four source subdivisions; two were tested separately. The forecast partial vectors differ by 0.001390% of the fine joint result between gravity clocks. Each of the eight launches came from its own pre-existing predicted cycle-4 right state (two branches, two gravity steps, two subdivisions). We did not start from an archived fifth-cycle outcome, extrapolate a cycle mean, adjust its phase, or restart inside the window. Forcing was zero before entry, then evaluated on actual time cells until the saved end. All photon vectors and their source receipts were frozen before scoring.

## Which work reached the photons?

On the fine grid the heavy-plus-metric contribution points **+119.70%** along the archived partial photon vector; ordinary matter plus supplied geometric work points **−19.70%** along it. Those are signed projections, not independent positive signal amplitudes or evidence that either piece can be dropped. The matched branch contributes +84.73% along the partial result and subtracting the native wave contributes +15.27%. The two are heavy-sector descriptions relative to the same cold baseline. The archive gives a combined photon target and *separate raw-work endpoints*; it does not supply independent branch photon targets here. At the fine gravity step the matched raw endpoint error is 0.000955% and the native-wave error is 0.003207% (coarse 0.003437% and 0.010194%).

## With the independently frozen complete-cycle drift

Adding only the new partial forecast to the frozen drift for cycles 2–4 predicts the **late** joint signal to **0.13709% coarse / 0.13707% fine** of its saved vector, already within 0.5%. No first-cycle or archived oscillatory signal enters this comparison. On the fine grid its magnitude is 0.50975097% of the common signal; the saved late magnitude is 0.50973016%. Adding back the separately labelled, saved within-cycle oscillations gives a fine late-vector error of 0.000128%. Also adding the saved first cycle gives a reconstructed fine full magnitude of 0.57636189% versus 0.57636165% archived. This is a hybrid check, not a new forecast of either added term and not a reduction in the full-window discrepancy.

## Checks and continuation

All 12 partial photon comparisons across settings/subdivisions, 18 contextual comparisons, 16 raw-work endpoint/primitive checks and 102 numerical checks pass the unchanged 0.5% criterion. The primary and angular-check photon arrays are byte-identical, not independent evidence. Earlier work and thresholds are unchanged.
