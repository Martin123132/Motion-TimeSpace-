# The gap survives evolving the quadrupoles from the switch

**Replacing the saved photon and ordinary collisionless quadrupoles with evolving angular hierarchies leaves the joint photon gap at 0.576350% (coarse gravity) and 0.576361% (fine).** Both photon components remain above the unchanged 0.5% threshold on this window. No fits, later state resets, new interval, or 77-bin stress update were used. The projected predictions for both clocks and the controls were frozen before scoring.

| Visibility-weighted gap | Coarse | Fine |
|---|---:|---:|
| Earlier single launch with prescribed quadrupoles | 0.576350% | 0.576361% |
| Evolve both quadrupoles: joint | **0.576350%** | **0.576361%** |
| Evolve both: photon energy | 0.576571% | 0.576582% |
| Evolve both: photon current | 0.575581% | 0.575593% |
| Older, per-cell reset comparison: joint | 0.577973% | 0.577985% |

These are percentages of the original small momentum-route correction, not of the full photon launch. The corrected joint magnitude on the fine clock is 0.003648% of the original launch, below its independent 0.163% limit. The fully evolving coarse and fine photon vectors differ by 0.00214% **of the gap**. This is a test of what sets the residual in the saved calculation, not an observational discrepancy.

**What evolved.** On each of the matched and native wave branches, one switch launch now carries 90 variables: the previous 13 lower variables (including the potential), photon intensity and polarization moments of degrees 2–6, and ordinary collisionless moments of degrees 2–68. The live I2 and N2 feed into shear and metric slip, photon–baryon momentum and the collisionless dipole; the updated lower moments feed back into the angular hierarchy. Polarization scattering, higher-degree forcing, the retained upper-moment closure, gravitational forcing and the separate massive 77-bin stress all keep their previous conventions and values. The work impulse and photon projection are unchanged. Saved angular curves are used for the separately labelled prescribed controls, not as post-switch inputs to the live trajectory. Each branch starts from its previous switch convention.

**How we know they really evolved.** The new angular derivatives were checked against each branch's original physical hierarchy for the switch state and an independent perturbation. Replacing prescribed quadrupoles with invalid values leaves the live rates unchanged but invalidates the prescribed controls; changing a live quadrupole affects the appropriate lower rates only in the cases in which it is released. The forcing, stress and angular-drive hashes are invariant under that change. On the fine grid, evolved versus saved quadrupole RMS differences are 2.95×10⁻⁸% (I2) and 2.48×10⁻⁷% (N2) for the matched branch; 7.87×10⁻⁹% and 7.13×10⁻⁸% for the native wave. The two treatments nearly coincide here. That does not mean the quadrupoles themselves are unimportant.

**Precision of the small difference.** All 46 primary photon checks pass. Direct subtraction of the live and prescribed calculations is inadequate for quoting a precise tiny change: 34 small-effect checks fail the unchanged relative tolerance. A separately identified numerical follow-up propagates their difference from zero instead. For an affine system with the quadrupoles in the lower equations replaced by the prescribed histories in the control, the difference obeys a homogeneous coupled evolution plus an explicit driving term proportional to the control's evolved-minus-prescribed quadrupoles. This avoids subtracting two large final histories. It gives joint live-minus-prescribed sizes of 2.70×10⁻¹³ and 1.89×10⁻¹³ percentage points on the common scale on the two grids. Halving substeps changes the differential result by 0.098% and 0.00371% respectively **of that tiny result**, but the signed coarse/fine difference is still 50.8% of it. These are diagnostic finite-grid results, not a converged effect size, proof of exact equality, or basis for a precise sector attribution. The fixed and live threshold decisions do not depend on that tiny number.

**Next discriminator:** the independently supplied massive-bin stress has remained fixed in this test. Use the frozen continuous metric history to drive the linear response of the existing 77 momentum bins once, then propagate that stress change to the same photon readout. Freeze its sign and magnitude before scoring. On the fine grid the reverse triangle inequality requires **at least 0.076361 percentage points** of joint correction on the original common scale, in a sufficiently opposing direction, to reach the 0.5% gate. This gives a concrete target for that feedback test without choosing a fitted correction.
