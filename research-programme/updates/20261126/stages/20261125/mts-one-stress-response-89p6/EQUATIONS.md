# What is varied once
Let n denote the saved ordinary collisionless multipole vector with the potential added to its monopole, and Ψ the lapse. For each branch independently subtract its own archived (n, Ψ) from the continuous, live-angular trajectory evaluated on that clock. With log-momentum q fixed, ε = hypot(q, am), v = q/ε and v−1 computed as −(am)²/[ε(ε+q)], the perturbation to the massive distribution lag satisfies

    ∂η δL = v A δL + (v−1) A δn + e₁ (1−v) k δΨ/(3v),
    (A f)ℓ = k [ℓ fℓ₋₁ − (ℓ+1) fℓ₊₁]/(2ℓ+1),    δL(switch) = 0.

This follows by subtraction of the retained affine transport equation. The two calculations within a branch use identical incoming microscopic sources, shape history, initial distribution and terminal hierarchy condition; those common terms cancel, not the physical occupations. The kinetic feedback is the response of the *already retained* stress to changing the driver, not a correction for the finite stopping tolerance of an older absolute fixed-point iteration. Recomputing an absolute bank from arbitrarily reset initial occupations would test something different.

Integrate all four perturbations to stress (energy, current, shear and trace) over the original log-momentum nodes and weights. Reinjection uses the original background energy conversion; the physical current also carries its original factor of k. Direct shear changes the slip by δS/k²; current changes the potential rate by δJ/(2k²), in addition to the lapse term. The entire 90-variable homogeneous response then propagates from zero with the same angular, ordinary-matter and metric couplings. Trace is recorded; it is not an extra independent force in these retained equations. There is no second return of this new potential to the kinetic bins.

With W(η) the previous weights on the thirteen lower states (including the potential), and D the unchanged energy-constraint coefficient, the source impulse for each cell is

    −Δ[W·δx + δE/2 + 3H δJ/(2k²)] / D_right.

Take matched minus native *complex impulses*, not a difference of positive sizes. Apply the same photon transport and visibility weighting as before, then add the frozen complex correction to the old gap. Keep separate the normalization to the small momentum-route signal and the independent normalization to the original photon launch. A cadence or cross-grid check on the size of the *change* is distinct from convergence of the total gap.
