## 1. Convention and operator

Work initially in an orthonormal electron-bulk-rest tetrad. Set
x=E/(k_B T_*), D=x partial_x, theta_*=k_B T_*/(m_e c^2),
r=T_e/T_*, kappa=a n_e sigma_T. Angular brackets use dOmega/(4pi).
The transverse Hermitian occupation tensor is f_ab(x,n), with projector
P_ab=delta_ab-n_a n_b. Unpolarized occupation n(x) means f=nP, NOT nP/2.
Its trace is twice the occupation per polarization. The parent bolometric
brightness instead has background P/2. These conventions must not be mixed.

For a tensor-valued angular function define

    R[f](n) = (3/2) P(n) <f(n')> P(n),
    S[f](n) = (3/2) P(n) <(1-n.n') f(n')> P(n).

The first map is the existing polarized Thomson gain. The second inserts
the recoil energy-transfer angle. It is computed without a dense angular
pair matrix from the tensor monopole and tensor dipole. This is an exact
factorization of the angular integral, not a moment closure on f.

With U the thermal angular map, the implemented rest collision per kappa is

    C_0[f] = R[f]-f,
    T[f] = (D^2+3D+2) S[f] + U[f],
    J[f] = x ( {f+P/2, (D+2)S[f]} + 2f ),
    C[f]/kappa = C_0[f] + theta_* (r T[f]+J[f]).

Braces denote an anticommutator. U is implemented from the intensity,
electric-polarization and circular multipoles of Pitrou's component
equations. All required tensor moments are integrated from the supplied
distribution; no fitted angular coefficients enter. In particular,
I_i=3<I n_i>, I_ij=(15/2)<I n_<ij>>,
I_ijk=(35/2)<I n_<ijk>>, E_ij=(5/2)<P_linear,ij> and
E_ijk=(21/2)<P_linear,<ij n_k>>>.
These moment normalizations and the complete U tensor are checked by an
independent moving-electron construction, not only by an isotropic test.

Source: Pitrou, arXiv:1902.09456v2, journal publication2021,
PDF equations7.1-7.8, corresponding HTML242-249:
https://arxiv.org/html/1902.09456v2 . Source pages were visually inspected.

### A source inconsistency caught rather than copied

PDF equation7.4 has an additional 1/E^2 before partial_E^2(E^2 S).

## 2. Independent scattering derivations and limits

For a cold electron, E_out/E_in=1/[1+(E_in/m_e c^2)(1-mu)]. At fixed outgoing
energy the incoming energy is E/(1-epsilon(1-mu)), epsilon=E/(m_e c^2).
Photon phase-space density and the energy Jacobian together with the
leading polarized cross-section supply the gain weight
[1-epsilon(1-mu)]^-2. Differentiating this gain gives (D+2)S[f].
The first cross-section correction to loss supplies +2f. Thus the linear
recoil tensor is independently recovered. The finite-energy remapping
test retains only the amplitude needed for this first derivative; it is
not advertised as an all-energy polarized QED solver.

For the thermal term, boost a distribution into each moving electron rest
frame, apply the already-derived polarized Thomson map, and boost the
result back with the correct rate factor 1-v.n. For isotropic electrons,
<v_i v_j>/c^2=theta_e delta_ij, so its leading thermal coefficient is

    (1/2) sum_i partial^2 C_moving / partial(v_i/c)^2 at v=0.

The test uses three spectral powers and a mixture of intensity, linear and
circular polarization, including angular modes beyond those in the gain.
Symmetric velocity steps decrease0.004 ->0.002 ->0.001. The discrepancies
shrink by about4 and finish below3.0e-6. This validates the operator's
thermal tensor and rate transformation independently of U's tabulated form.

For isotropic f=nP, R[f]=S[f]=nP and U[f]=-2nP. Hence

    T[nP] = x^-2 partial_x(x^4 partial_x n) P,
    J[nP] = x^-2 partial_x(x^4 n(1+n)) P.

Planck/Bose-Einstein distributions at electron temperature are stationary.
The full tensor source reduces to the saved Stage70 heat jet, including
opacity perturbations and its boosted isotropic four-force.

## 3. Number, energy, momentum and gravity

Let I=tr f and B(x)=<tr(f S[f])>. Transversality makes S self-adjoint under
the angular trace pairing. The induced contribution cannot be replaced
by a product of monopoles. S preserves the angular trace monopole, while
its trace dipole is -2/5 of the input dipole. The corresponding U moments
are -2 times the monopole and +2/5 times the dipole.

Assume the boundary terms from the following frequency integrations vanish.
Integration by parts then gives

    integral x^2 <tr C[f]> dx = 0,

    Q_gamma/(kappa rho_ref theta_*)
      = [4r integral x^3 <I> dx
         - integral x^4 (<I>+B) dx] / (2 I3),

    I3 = pi^4/15,  rho_ref is the unperturbed Planck energy density.

The normalization is to the reference density, not the perturbed density.
For positive occupation tensors S[f] is positive and B>=0. This gives a
positive Compton equilibrium temperature whenever photon energy is positive.
It does not enforce that temperature as an algebraic matter closure.

For the thermal operator alone the integrated spatial force is
-2 kappa theta_e times the photon flux. The recoil momentum requires its
directional tensor products, not just the scalar heat above. The general
implementation therefore integrates all four components:

    a G_gamma^hat(mu) = kappa rho_ref/(2 I3)
                       integral x^3 <(1,n)^hat(mu) tr C_per_kappa> dx,
    G_matter^hat(mu) = -G_gamma^hat(mu).

The kinetic stress moment identity is obtained by multiplying the Boltzmann
equation by photon momentum and integrating its invariant mass-shell measure;
the Liouville term becomes the covariant divergence of radiation stress.
Thus the local matter energy and momentum equations must receive -G, not
merely a scalar heat correction. For a thermally pressured plasma the energy
goes into its internal-energy equation, and the spatial force into its
momentum equation. It must not be inserted as a sink in a pressureless-dust
equation and called conserved.

Opposite-source cancellation is an algebraic requirement checked here.
The subsequent coupled space-time integration must also propagate those
moments and Einstein constraints; that is a separate numerical requirement.
No new gravitational action or value of G is introduced in this step.

For conversion to the cosmological tetrad boost the whole four-vector:

    Q_lab = gamma(Q_rest+v.F_rest),
    F_lab = F_rest + [(gamma-1)(v.F_rest)/v^2+gamma Q_rest] v.

The quadratic jet in `source_jet.py` includes both heat and rest-frame force,
and reduces to Stage70 when the rest force vanishes. Tests compare with
independent mixed differences of the full nonlinear tensor source and boost.

## 4. Why angular averaging is not an identity

For unpolarized occupation the angular kernel in the scalar recoil map is
(3/4)(1-mu)(1+mu^2). Its Legendre eigenvalues are

    lambda_0=1, lambda_1=-2/5, lambda_2=1/10,
    lambda_3=-3/70, lambda_l=0 for l>=4.

Consider f=n(x)[1+b P_l(n.z)]P with0<|b|<1. Every such spectrum is positive
and has the same angular-mean photon number and energy, but

    B=2 n(x)^2 [1+b^2 lambda_l/(2l+1)].

Dipole and quadrupole patterns therefore change induced heat in opposite
directions without changing those scalar moments. Polarization contributes
through its tensor pairing too. The implemented kernel keeps these terms.

## 5. Actual connection to saved nonparallel modes

The cold, frequency-independent first-order equation preserves the spectral
shape G(x)=-D n_P=x n_P(1+n_P). This is a consequence of its initial state,
redshift forcing and frequency-independent Thomson term. It permits the
first-order spectral lift from the retained Stage69 brightness matrix B_p:

    f_p^rest = G A_p,
    A_p = B_p/2 - (n.v_b,p)P.

The moving-baryon cold collision is recovered after integrating this lift.
The same statement DOES NOT reconstruct the independent second-order
spectrum from bolometric brightness; Stage70 already disproved that closure.

We use the convention f=f0+u fp+v fq+2uv f_pq. The induced p,q source known
without specifying f_pq is

    C_ind,pq/(kappa theta_*)
       = x G(D+2)G N_pq,
    N_pq = [{A_p,S[A_q]}+{A_q,S[A_p]}]/2.

Its spectrum integrates exactly using

    J_G = integral x^4 G^2 dx = 120[zeta(4)-zeta(6)],
    force_ind,pq/(kappa rho_ref theta_*)
       = -J_G <(1,n) tr N_pq>/(4 I3).

The additional mixed Compton-temperature term, relative to keeping the same
second-order spectrum but replacing only first-order angular correlations
by their monopoles, is

    delta r_C,pq = J_G/(8 I3)
       [<tr(A_p S[A_q])> - <tr A_p><tr A_q>/2].

This is a derived additive contribution, not the full unknown r_C,pq.
Both physical Stage69 endpoints, time refinements and angular refinements
are evaluated. Fourier coefficients remain complex; taking absolute values
inside the equations would give the wrong mixed mode. Conjugate modes and
primordial covariance are needed to build a real observable.

## 6. A further derived drag coefficient, with restricted scope

For a Planck temperature dipole in the baryon rest frame, integrating the
linear thermal+recoil source gives

    delta F_gamma = kappa theta_* d_T F_gamma,unperturbed,
    d_T = 2 + 72 zeta(6)/I3 - J_G/(10 I3)
        = 13.159472534785808.

The cold force is -kappa F_gamma,unperturbed. This coefficient is derived
from frequency integrals, not fitted, and only applies to the specified
temperature-dipole spectral shape with T_e=T_*. It must NOT replace the
frequency operator for a general distorted spectrum.

The independently computed first-order heat is

    Q_p/(kappa rho_ref theta_*) = 4(r_p - <Theta_p>),
    <Theta_p> = <tr A_p>/2.

This supplies the electron-temperature relaxation law's coupling. The code
does not set r_p=<Theta_p> by decree. The angular non-monopole collision
source is independent of that undetermined isotropic temperature response,
so that source can be evaluated now without smuggling a temperature closure.
