The first repaired-compatibility assembly stopped on its manufactured positive control, before any scientific case. A floating linear-programming solver reported success but violated a 1.9e-12 sampled error by approximately 2.7e-12. Its primal tolerance was larger than the narrow manufactured feasible region. The independent replay correctly rejected the output. No physics input or validation tolerance is changed to rescue this run.

## Integer vertex theorem

Treat every supplied binary64 B, C, E_B, E_C as its exact rational value. On a fixed sign orthant write `x=diag(sign)*y`, `y>=0`. The eight residual inequalities plus three non-negativity inequalities define eleven closed rational halfspaces in three dimensions.

If their intersection is nonempty, minimize `y0+y1+y2`. A sublevel set below any feasible point is compact; hence the minimum is attained. The minimizing face is a nonempty compact polytope, with a vertex. Because the non-negative orthant contains no line, this is also a vertex of the original feasible polyhedron. In three dimensions each vertex has at least three independent active constraint normals (including non-negativity constraints where applicable).

It therefore suffices to enumerate all `C(11,3)=165` triples per orthant and all eight orthants. Multiply each constraint by a common power-of-two denominator to obtain integer coefficients. Use integer determinants and Cramer's rule; skip determinant zero, normalize its sign and check every inequality by integer cross multiplication. There is no tolerance in feasibility, no discarded small determinant, no required finite amplitude cap, and no floating-point solver status to interpret.

Among feasible vertices choose the smallest sum of column-scaled absolute amplitudes; these positive rational weights only select a reproducible witness and do not change feasibility. Save its common integer numerator and denominator, signs, active constraints and constraint rows, so the rational witness can be rechecked exactly. Its floating rendering is independently substituted for readability and is not the proof authority.

If no feasible vertex exists in any orthant, the recorded independent componentwise-envelope problem is exactly infeasible. If a vertex exists, the problem is exactly feasible. This finite rational statement does not certify that the sampled error arrays bound continuum/parent errors. In particular, independent coefficient errors are an outer relaxation of correlated source errors; a feasible witness need not correspond to an admissible parent perturbation or finite history.
