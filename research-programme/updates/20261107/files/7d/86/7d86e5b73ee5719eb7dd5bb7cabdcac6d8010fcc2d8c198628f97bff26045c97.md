# Physical species and a derived shear-suppression criterion

## Scope and source conventions

This extends, rather than replaces, the retained two-canonical-scalar linear branch. Sources are the unchanged native stress readout in `../mts-derived-wavelength-test-20261107/build-full-momentum/camb_python.f90` (`mts_operator_stress`), the photon/polarization/neutrino equations in `../condensate-origin-20261003/native-heavy-matching-20261007/native/fortran/equations.f90`, and the full G192 extension inherited by `../mts-independent-poisson-evolution-20261107/poisson_driver.py`. Physical weights and hierarchy conventions are in `../mts-coupled-energy-envelope-20261107/physical_energy_map.py`. The preceding observable theorem is `../mts-explicit-current-shear-forcing-20261107/DERIVATION.md`.

Use N=ln a, conformal H=a'/a, k>0, tau>0, c=1, comoving Mpc. H,k have units Mpc^-1; native conformal density weights R, current Q and anisotropic stress Pi have units Mpc^-2. The scalar variables retain their parent's normalization. E below has units Mpc^-1. A is the SAME dimensionless GR CDM history envelope as in the previous stage, not a newly chosen normalization. Massive-particle velocity v_i is distinct from a scalar's homogeneous field velocity.

These are conditional estimates on a specified finite N interval, not an identification of the saved early cosmological history with a local vacuum. The numerical envelopes apply to a qualified quintic force representation; its continuum interpolation remainder has not been certified.

## 1. Species ownership before normalization

The native 7x7 stress readout is species by quantity in Python: CDM, baryon, photon, massless neutrino, massive neutrino, slow scalar, heavy scalar. Columns are D_sync, Q_sync, deltaP_sync, D'_sync, Q'_sync, W=rho+p, Pi. For each species,

    D_s = D_sync,s - 3 H W_s alpha
    Q_s = Q_sync,s + k W_s alpha
    Pi_s = Pi_sync,s.

Sum these physical species FIRST; only then compare with the stored total and its original readout envelope. The massive W_s here is the retained thermal integral, not its momentum-quadrature surrogate.

For the massive momentum nodes let d_i>0 be the monopole density weights and v_i their speed. Define

    Wq_mass = (4/3) sum_i d_i
    DeltaW = Wq_mass - Wthermal_mass.

The normal-frame moment variables obey

    F0n_i = F0sync_i - 4 H alpha
    F1n_i = F1sync_i + 4 k alpha/(3 v_i)
    D_mass = sum_i d_i F0n_i + 3 H DeltaW alpha
    Q_mass = sum_i d_i v_i F1n_i - k DeltaW alpha
    Pi_mass = sum_i d_i v_i^2 F2_i.

The mismatch remains explicit. No division by a total current/shear at a zero crossing is used.

## 2. Canonical scalar shear is second order here

For a minimally coupled canonical scalar on a homogeneous isotropic background, its orthonormal spatial trace-free stress is

    T_ij^TF = a^-2 [partial_i phi partial_j phi
                        - delta_ij |grad phi|^2/3].

The homogeneous background has zero spatial gradient. Therefore delta T_ij^TF=0 at LINEAR order, for either of the two retained scalars. A nonzero scalar density/current still changes the metric and consequently the radiation; zero direct shear is not decoupling.

At quadratic gradient order the Frobenius norm is sqrt(2/3)|grad delta_phi|^2/a^2. For two scalars its norm is bounded by the sum, or 2 sqrt(2/3) rho_gradient. For perturbations band-limited to k<=k_max with positive masses m>=m_min, Parseval gives the integrated estimate

    integral ||T^TF|| <= 2 sqrt(2/3) [k_max/(a m_min)]^2
                           integral rho_mass,pert.

This is an integrated, fixed-background leading-gradient statement. It is NOT a pointwise mass-energy bound at field nodes, not a proof for an inhomogeneous background, and not a treatment of nonlinear metric corrections. It need not suppress the slow field if its mass is too small.

## 3. Photon-baryon energy: do not assume Phi_N is slow

For a full massless hierarchy, normal-frame monopole and dipole equations are

    F0n_N = -(k/H) F1n + 4 Phi_N
    F1n_N = (k/3H)(F0n - 2 F2) + (4k/3H) Psi + collisions.

Set I0=F0n-4Phi and leave higher moments unchanged. This removes Phi_N EXACTLY, however rapidly Phi changes. Use the retained angular weights sqrt[R_gamma(2l+1)/4], its actual terminal weight, and polarization weights sqrt[R_gamma(2l+1)(l+1)(l+2)/(4l(l-1))], with the actual terminal closure. Add baryon normal velocity sqrt(R_b) v_bn to the SAME norm E_gb.

Streaming is skew in these weights. The joint Thomson dipole quadratic form is

    -(4/3) opacity R_gamma (v_bn - 3 F1n/4)^2.

The weighted photon/polarization quadrupole collision block has eigenvalues -opacity and -0.3 opacity. Higher collision terms and outgoing terminal terms dissipate. Expansion supplies -1 to the photon/polarization weights and -3/2 to the baryon velocity. The only external entries after the shift are

    g_b = sqrt(R_b) (k/H) [Psi + delta_p_b,s]
    g_gamma = (2/sqrt(3)) sqrt(R_gamma) (k/H) [Phi+Psi],

where delta_p_b,s is the native pressure perturbation per baryon density. Its native gauge/thermal dependence is retained, not silently set to zero. Pressure is an explicit input here, not assumed independent of the coupled system. Thus

    D^+ E_gb <= -E_gb + sqrt(g_b^2+g_gamma^2)
    |Pi_gamma| <= sqrt(4 R_gamma/5) E_gb.

D^+ handles zero norm without division by zero. Treating Thomson drag as a large independent baryon forcing would destroy this useful cancellation; the joint norm avoids it.

## 4. Massless neutrinos: keep the actual branch

For the full hierarchy (half wavelength case), the same shifted monopole and angular weights give

    D^+ E_r <= -E_r + |(2/sqrt(3))sqrt(R_r)(k/H)(Phi+Psi)|
    |Pi_r| <= sqrt(4 R_r/5) E_r.

The central and double cases instead use the retained high-k-tau approximation. Its normal quadrupole equation is

    F2_N = -3 F2/(H tau) + (k/H)F1n
           -4 Phi_N -4 Psi -4(H_N-H)alpha.

Keep that equation. Set I0=F0n-4Phi, I2=F2+4Phi, and use angular weights 1,3,2. The two nonzero force entries become

    g_r1 = (2/sqrt(3))sqrt(R_r)(k/H)(3Phi+Psi)
    g_r2 = sqrt(R_r/2)[12Phi/(H tau)-4Psi-4(H_N-H)alpha].

The resulting streaming block is skew; the quadrupole has additional damping 3/(H tau). Hence

    D^+ E_r <= -E_r + sqrt(g_r1^2+g_r2^2)
    |Pi_r| <= sqrt(2R_r) E_r + 4R_r |Phi|.

Both the endpoint restoration and alpha source matter. Neither an unshifted quadrupole estimate nor a substituted full hierarchy is legitimate for these cases.

## 5. Massive particles: remove inverse-speed forcing in the shear norm

For every one of the five retained momentum nodes and all G192 multipoles use I0_i=F0n_i-4Phi and Il_i=Fln_i for l>=1. The native quadrature weights satisfy

    (ln d_i)_N = -(1+v_i^2),  (ln v_i)_N = -(1-v_i^2).

Define the shear-specific energy

    E_m^2 = sum_i,l [d_i v_i^2 (2l+1)/4] I_li^2.

The extra v_i factor in each node's energy coordinate preserves streaming skewness, including the exact core/tail link. Its logarithmic weight rate is -(3-v_i^2)/2 <= -1. The only external dipole entry is

    g_mi = (2/sqrt(3)) sqrt(d_i) (k/H) [v_i^2 Phi+Psi].

The inverse speed in the usual gravitational dipole force has CANCELLED; there is no averaging or small-Phi_N assumption. The retained last G192 streaming upper link is zero, so no invented tail injection is needed. Consequently

    D^+ E_m <= -E_m + sqrt(sum_i g_mi^2)
    |Pi_mass| <= sqrt[(4/5) sum_i d_i v_i^2] E_m.

This is an observable-specific cold-shear estimate. As v_i->0 the new norm degenerates on the complete hierarchy; it is NOT by itself an invertible, uniformly equivalent full-state norm. In particular it does not remove the separate v_min>0 hypothesis from the preceding stage's whole-state gain theorem. A uniform cold limit for that gain must still be derived before composing such a limit with this result. At finite positive speeds the displayed kinetic estimate is well defined.

## 6. Explicit sufficient conditions, not an assumed small Pi

For any group a in {gb,r,m}, the comparison solution is

    J_a(N) = exp[-(N-N0)] E_a(N0)
               + integral_N0^N exp[-(N-s)] |g_a(s)| ds
    E_a(N) <= J_a(N).

This exposes both preparation and driving, including potentially fast metric amplitudes, actual pressure, and the UFA alpha remainder. Merely saying that initial energy is finite is NOT enough if the normalized integral grows as a limit is taken.

For the full radiation hierarchy a sufficient derived shear-suppression condition, uniformly over a finite interval, is that each following product tends to zero:

    sqrt(R_gamma/H^2) J_gb/(H A)
    sqrt(R_r/H^2) J_r/(H A)
    sqrt(sum_i d_i v_i^2/H^2) J_m/(H A).

For the UFA branch include R_r |Phi|/(H^2 A)->0 and use its force and sqrt(2) coefficient. These conditions bound the actual |Pi|/(H^2 A) through the kinetic equations and physical density/velocity moments; they are not a postulate that Pi is small. Vanishing radiation fractions and cold massive second moment suffice if the normalized preparation/force integrals stay bounded. Radiation with finite fraction or growing excitation can violate the conditions. The present saved cosmological segment must be checked, not labeled vacuum.

Current can be bounded in the SAME dictionary:

    |Q_c| = R_c k |alpha|
    |Q_b+Q_gamma| <= sqrt(R_b+4R_gamma/3) E_gb
    |Q_r| <= sqrt(4R_r/3) E_r
    |Q_mass| <= sqrt[(4/3)sum_i d_i] E_m + k|DeltaW alpha|
    |Q_scalar,j| <= (k |field_velocity_j|/Omega_j) E_scalar,j.

Here E_scalar,j^2=Omega_j^2 delta_phi_n,j^2+delta_pi_n,j^2 and Omega_j^2=k^2+a^2m_j^2. These follow by Cauchy-Schwarz, with no division by a signed current. The CDM gauge term and quadrature correction are not lost. Together they specify what is required for epsilon Q/(H^2 A) to shrink in the preceding observable theorem.
