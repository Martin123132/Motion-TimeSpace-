## 1. Two expansions, not one

Use the inherited chart ds^2=a^2[-d tau^2+C_ij dx^i dx^j]. Amplitude
expansions have no factorials: C=I+epsilon h+epsilon^2 l. Independently,
count spatial derivatives with a gradient order. The boundary below retains
metric/density/stress through two gradients and momentum through three,
nonlinearly in the seed geometry before amplitude expansion. A regular
long-wavelength boundary is not an exact finite-k initial solution.

Let gamma_ij(x) be the positive primordial spatial metric. Write its mixed
Ricci tensor as Ric, scalar as R and tracefree part as T=Ric-I R/3. All
derivatives D in the seed formulae belong to gamma. Let f_nu be the constant
early ultrarelativistic neutrino fraction and D_nu=15+4 f_nu. The initial
family is adiabatic with no independent homogeneous anisotropic stress,
decaying mode, field-isocurvature mode or primordial vector mode added.
Those exclusions define a basis, not a prediction of their amplitudes.

In leading radiation domination a=beta tau and Hcal=1/tau. With all stress
normalizations as in Stage66, a^2 rho_r=3/tau^2. The regular solution is

    G0 = -(5/D_nu) T - I R/24,
    C = gamma (I+tau^2 G0) + O(grad^4),
    A = Cinv C'/2 = tau G0 + O(grad^4),
    theta = -tau R/8,
    delta_c = delta_b = tau^2 R/16,
    delta_gamma = delta_nu = tau^2 R/12,
    pi_nu/rho_nu = (4 tau^2/(3D_nu)) T.                     (68.1)

The mixed tensor G0 is self-adjoint with respect to gamma, so gamma G0 is
symmetric. It must not be forced to be Euclidean-symmetric after a nonlinear
coordinate map.

### Derivation rather than independent seed assignments

Radiation continuity gives delta_r'=-(4/3)theta at two-gradient order;
the cold conserved-number equation gives delta_c'=-theta. With
theta=tau theta_bar, the Hamiltonian equation becomes
R+4theta_bar=-4theta_bar, fixing theta_bar=-R/8.

Write sigma=tau S_bar. The collisionless radiation quadrupole equation is
(pi_nu/rho_nu)'=-(8/15)sigma, from the fourth angular moment of the
directional equation. The tracefree Einstein equation therefore gives

    3 S_bar + T = -(4f_nu/5) S_bar,
    S_bar = -5 T/(15+4f_nu).

This derives the neutrino denominator, not an assumed perfect-radiation
closure. Tight photon scattering suppresses photon shear at the leading
order in tau; its first finite mean-free-path correction is derived below.

For covariant conformal velocities b_i=gamma_ij dx^j/dtau, the regular
three-gradient coefficients are

    b_gamma,i = b_b,i = -tau^3 D_i R/144,
    b_nu,i = -tau^3(D_nu+8) D_i R/(144D_nu).                (68.2)

Radiation momentum is j_i=(4/3)a rho_r b_i at this order. The contracted
spatial Bianchi identity D_j T^j_i=D_i R/6 makes the momentum constraint

    D_i theta-D_j A^j_i
      = -tau(5+4f_nu) D_i R/(12D_nu) = a j_i,total.

Thus density, shear and velocity are parts of one constrained regular
solution, not separately chosen transfer-function seeds.

## 3. Native synchronous spatial gauge, including its nonlinear part

The saved native scalar metric has h0=-2eta0 I+6eta0 p_hat p_hat^T.
Define zeta1=-eta0. Start with a conformal seed exp(2zeta)I in coordinates X
and pull it back by a time-independent map

    X(y)=y+epsilon xi1(y)+epsilon^2 xi2(y),
    xi1=-3 grad laplacian^(-1) zeta1,
    xi2=-3 grad laplacian^(-1) zeta2.

The inverse Laplacian applies only to nonzero individual modes; constant
curvature/background rescaling is a separate channel. This choice gives

    h0(p) = [2I-6P_p] zeta1(p),  P_p=p p^T/p^2.

Let L=partial xi1. Expanding the actual pullback gives

    gamma2 = [2zeta2+2xi1.grad zeta1+2zeta1^2]I
             +2zeta1(L+L^T)+L^T L+(partial xi2+partial xi2^T).

For a symmetric ordered p,q convolution the quadratic kernel is

    gamma2Q(p,q) = [2-3(p.q)(1/p^2+1/q^2)]I
                  -6(P_p+P_q)+(9/2)(P_p P_q+P_q P_p).       (68.5)

The independent primordial coefficient is [2I-6P_K]zeta2(K), K=p+q,
when K is nonzero. It is stored separately and not assigned a value.

Conformal curvature and scalar pullback give an independent check:

    R1 = -4 laplacian zeta1,
    R2 = -4 laplacian zeta2 +8zeta1 laplacian zeta1
         -2(grad zeta1)^2 +xi1.grad R1,
    R2Q(p,q) = -4(p^2+q^2)+2p.q
               -6(p.q)(q^2/p^2+p^2/q^2).                 (68.6)

The mixed tensor Ric2 additionally includes Stage66's inverse-metric and
connection products. For G=G(Ric), the dynamic amplitude coefficients are

    h(tau)=h0+tau^2 G1,
    l(tau)=gamma2+tau^2[G2+Sym(h0_p G1_q)],
    A2=tau G2.

In particular A2-A2^T=-Sym([h0_p,A1_q]); discarding that mixed-index
antisymmetry breaks metric compatibility. The exported orthogonal pair has
R2Q=-0.08 Mpc^-2 for unit zeta inputs at p=.1 ex,q=.1 ey. Nonparallel
scalar products retain a TT component in this fixed chart. This is not an
observed or separately gauge-invariant gravitational-wave amplitude.
Opposite waves give a nonzero homogeneous curvature contribution; no K^-2
division deletes it. Cold density uses the primordial reference volume
sqrt(det gamma), not an assumed unit reference volume at second order.

## 4. Deriving the leading matter and mean-free-path corrections

To improve the boundary at a physically later starting time, use

    a=beta tau(1+mu tau/4), Hcal=1/tau+mu/4,
    mu=(grhob+grhoc)/(3 beta),
    B_b=grhob/(grhob+grhoc),
    kappa=kappa0/tau^2, lambda=1/kappa0,
    kappa0=(a^2 kappa)/beta^2,
    R_b=3rho_b/(4rho_gamma)=r_b mu tau,
    r_b=3 B_b/[4(1-f_nu)].

Here beta has units Mpc^-1, mu and lambda have units Mpc^-1 and kappa0
has units Mpc. Keep first powers of mu tau and lambda tau only; products
and squares are omitted. The cold/frozen-field background fractions used
to obtain mu are not replaced by their late-time matter abundance.

Define

    s0=-5/D_nu,
    sm=3(4f_nu-5)s0/[8(15+2f_nu)],
    sc=-8(1-f_nu)s0/(15+2f_nu).

Then

    theta=R[-tau/8+3mu tau^2/80],
    sigma=[s0 tau+(mu sm+lambda sc)tau^2]T,
    delta_c=delta_b=R tau^2(1-mu tau/5)/16,
    delta_gamma=delta_nu=4delta_c/3,
    pi_nu/rho_nu=[-4s0 tau^2/15-8(mu sm+lambda sc)tau^3/45]T,

The covariant velocities are b_species=g_species D_i R with

These follow from the baryon-loaded common-velocity Euler equation and the

## 5. Exact local screen transport on the inherited metric

The collision tensor from Stage67 acts in a local orthonormal frame. We
must transport that frame consistently when C changes along a ray.
Use the positive symmetric coframe E=sqrt(C), with inverse E^-1. Let n
be the local photon direction, u=dx/dtau=E^-1 n, and define

    S=(1/2)E^(-1) C' E^(-1),
    A=(1/2)C^(-1) C',
    D_ray E=partial_tau E+u^i partial_i E,
    Omega=(D_ray E)E^(-1)-E[A+Gamma(u)]E^(-1).               (68.9)

Metric compatibility makes Omega antisymmetric. If omega is the conformal
photon energy and e_pol a unit transverse polarization vector,

    x'=E^(-1)n,
    (ln omega)'=-n^T S n,
    n'=Omega n-S n+(n^T S n)n,
    e_pol'=Omega e_pol+n(n^T S e_pol).                       (68.10)

Derive this by parallel-transporting a four-vector f^mu along the null ray,
then imposing the screen gauge f^0=0 by adding a multiple of the null
momentum. The compensating multiple produces the last term in e_pol'.
Equations68.10 preserve n^2=1, n.e_pol=0 and e_pol^2=1. They include
direction changes and screen rotation, not just photon energy redshift.

For the a^4-rescaled bolometric screen matrix J, define
T_screen=Omega+n n^T S. Along the ray,

    J'=-4(n^T S n)J+T_screen J+J T_screen^T+C_conformal[J].  (68.11)

The trace of the two screen terms vanishes for J n=0; thus scalar brightness
obeys the Stage66 redshift equation. Hermiticity and positive polarization
are preserved by collision-free transport as a positive scalar times a
screen congruence. The physical photon energy is omega/a, adding the usual
-Hcal to its logarithmic redshift. Conformal invariance is used for the
retained minimal Maxwell geometric-optics sector; this does not erase any
parent higher-derivative remainder or describe wavelengths comparable to
the curvature scale.

### Second-order rotation that a fixed-screen shortcut misses

For C=I+epsilon h+epsilon^2 l,

    E=I+epsilon h/2+epsilon^2(l/2-h^2/8),
    S1=h'/2,
    S2=l'/2-(h h'+h' h)/4,
    Omega_time,2=[h,h']/8.                                 (68.12)

Noncommuting metric perturbations therefore generate time-dependent frame
rotation even in the symmetric-square-root coframe. The first spatial

The exact formula68.9 defines all second-order spatial terms and their

E(partial E)+(partial E)E=partial C.
