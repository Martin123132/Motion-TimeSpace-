# Testing the first nontrivial preservation coefficient

The saved balanced state is time symmetric. Write its actual coupled acceleration as a, including the source component. A second-order jet is

    z(t) = z0 + t^2 a/2,   v(t) = t a.

It is a derivative probe, NOT a numerical solution at finite time. At the initial point its acceleration agrees with H*a=F, so every sufficiently smooth trajectory with those initial data must have this second jet. It cannot be used as a trajectory beyond this order.

For this implementation the radial density depends on the jointly reversed velocities through the squared Eulerian temporal field, the squared source velocity and sqrt(N^2-v_source^2/F). The native load has no velocity. Thus the radial equations are even under v -> -v. On a locally unique smooth radial branch, g_dot(0)=0 and g_ddot(0)=G_z[a]+G_vv[a,a]. A position-only zero-rate radial recomputation would omit the second term.

Use s=t^2/2. Each probe recomputes the actual radial equations at z0+s*a and v=sqrt(2*s)*a. Let Q_i(s) be the native shape form including source-map and metric changes and the same pinned physical generator evaluated at its current radii. With the two field columns q0 and a_q, write its entries as Q00(s), Q01(s), Q11(s). Then

    B_i(s) = Q00(s) + 2*s*Q01(s) + s^2*Q11(s),
    B_i''(0 in t) = 2*Q01(0) + dQ00/ds(0).

The fixed-geometry field contribution is available directly, with no small-step subtraction. The geometry contribution is estimated from [Q00(s)-Q00(0)]/s and checked on successively smaller s. The quadratic-corrected full estimate is 2*Q01(s)+[Q00(s)-Q00(0)]/s; the removed s*Q11(s) is an exactly known finite-jet term, not a fitted cancellation. Raw and corrected estimates are both saved. No denominator A is divided by, clipped or zeroed in this calculation.

Initial fields, sources, native support and action coefficients remain fixed. The probes are evaluated at multiple held-out material labels, with all 33 generators, and on both field grids if available. A reproducible nonzero coefficient identifies a necessary preservation obstruction for this initial state and its computed acceleration, not a no-go theorem for all MTS initial data. A small coefficient at finitely many labels would instead require further uniform/error analysis; it would not certify propagation.
