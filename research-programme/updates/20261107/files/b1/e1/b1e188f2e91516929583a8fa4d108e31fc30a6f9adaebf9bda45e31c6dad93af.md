# Full two-mode shear response and action-owned quadrupolar interaction

3 October 2026. Private daughter calculation of the isolated condensate
candidate. The pre-condensate framework and recovery payload are unchanged.

## 1. What is new in this step

The preceding step derived the phase-only infrared shear continuum. This
step keeps BOTH mixed amplitude/phase branches, derives the cubic and
quartic vertices from the same potential, evaluates the finite-momentum
Gaussian shear spectrum, and derives a closed angular projection of the
OFF-SHELL matrix exchange kernel. It also obtains a nonzero on-shell
quadrupolar interaction and its low-momentum law.

These are distinct approximation levels: the Gaussian stress response is
not an interacting resummation; the action-derived tree kernel is not the
exact renormalized two-particle-irreducible kernel. Keeping those levels
separate prevents a constituent pole or a Born amplitude being mislabeled
a new collective gravitational mode.

## 2. Candidate, state, units and assumptions

Use the candidate already defined in ../01-first-condensate-derivation.md:

\[
 {\cal C}={\varrho\over\sqrt2}e^{i\theta},\qquad
 V(\varrho)={3g\over4}\varrho^{4/3}+{\lambda\over4}\varrho^4,
 \qquad g,\lambda>0 .
\]

The complex field, global U(1), positive quartic and coherent charged
background remain declared additions, not deductions from the old real
scalar. The original fractional term is retained. The background is
\(\varrho=\varrho_0>0,\theta=\mu t\), with \(\mu>0\).
The underlying metric is still the assumed flat metric of signature
(-,+,+,+). Work at zero temperature in the quasiparticle vacuum about
this state; finite-temperature occupation/scattering terms are not included.

Define
\[
 \mu^2=g\varrho_0^{-2/3}+\lambda\varrho_0^2,\quad
 y={\lambda\varrho_0^{8/3}\over g},\quad
 m^2\equiv M_a^2=\mu^2{2y-2/3\over1+y},\quad
 D=m^2+4\mu^2,\quad c_s^2={m^2\over D}.
\]
Here m² is radial static stiffness, not the squared gapped-mode frequency.
We work on y>1, which also passes the previous homogeneous
grand-potential comparison with the empty state. This comparison is not
a proof of global nonlinear stability or cosmological preparation.

In hbar=c=1, [varrho0]=[mu]=[k]=[omega]=1, [g]=8/3, [lambda]=0,
[m²]=[D]=2. Cubic vertices have dimension1, quartics dimension0.
The momentum-space stress spectral density below has dimension4.

Use rotating CARTESIAN fields for interactions:
\[
 {\cal C}=e^{i\mu t}{\varrho_0+\sigma+i\phi\over\sqrt2}.
\]
They agree with the preceding polar perturbations to first order, not
nonlinearly. The quadratic Lagrangian, up to a boundary term, is
\[
 {\cal L}_2={1\over2}\left[\dot\sigma^2+\dot\phi^2
       -(\nabla\sigma)^2-(\nabla\phi)^2-m^2\sigma^2\right]
       +\mu(\sigma\dot\phi-\phi\dot\sigma).
\]
There are no cubic/quartic kinetic vertices in these Cartesian coordinates.

## 3. Propagator, residues and canonical normalization

For exp(-i omega t+i k.x), write
\[
 K(z,k)=\begin{pmatrix}z^2-k^2-m^2&-2i\mu z\\
                     2i\mu z&z^2-k^2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 E_\pm^2=k^2+{D\over2}\pm\sqrt{D^2/4+4\mu^2k^2}.
\]
The retarded prescription is z=omega+i0. Then
\[
 K^{-1}={N(z,k)\over(z^2-E_-^2)(z^2-E_+^2)},\quad
 N=\begin{pmatrix}z^2-k^2&2i\mu z\\
                 -2i\mu z&z^2-k^2-m^2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its positive-frequency residue is
\[
 R_a(k)={N(E_a,k)\over2E_a(E_a^2-E_{\bar a}^2)},\qquad
 a\in\{-,+\}.
\]
For k>0 these are positive semidefinite rank-one Hermitian matrices.
Write \(R_a=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A_a&iB_a\\-iB_a&C_a\end{smallmatrix}\right)\).
The canonical spectral sum rules are
\[
 \sum_a(R_a-R_a^T)=0,\qquad
 \sum_a E_a(R_a+R_a^T)=I.
\]
Both have exact algebraic checks and numerical checks.

The crucial pair-creation contraction is
\[
 W_{ab}(k,p)=\operatorname{tr}[R_a(k)R_b(p)^T]
     =A_aA_b+C_aC_b-2B_aB_b\geq0.
\]
It is NOT tr(Ra Rb). For this rank-one spectrum it can be evaluated as
\[
 W_{ab}=\left[\sqrt{A_aA_b}
      -\operatorname{sgn}(B_aB_b)\sqrt{C_aC_b}\right]^2
\]
for nonzero mu. At mu=0 the decoupled residues supply its continuous limit.
This also avoids catastrophic subtraction between large nearly equal terms.

## 4. Full Gaussian finite-momentum shear spectrum

For q along z the Cartesian off-diagonal stress is exactly
\[
 T_{xy}=\partial_x\sigma\,\partial_y\sigma+
        \partial_x\phi\,\partial_y\phi .
\]
Its connected Gaussian commutator spectral density at omega>0 is
\[
 \rho_{xy,xy}(\omega,q)=
  2\sum_{ab}\int{d^3k\over(2\pi)^3}(k_xk_y)^2
  (2\pi) W_{ab}(k,|\mathbf q-\mathbf k|)
  \delta(\omega-E_a(k)-E_b(|\mathbf q-\mathbf k|)).
\]
The factor2 is the two Wick contractions; the ordered sum includes both
mixed channels. With u=cos angle(k,q), angular integration gives
\[
 \rho={1\over8\pi}\sum_{ab}\int dk\,k^6\int_{-1}^{1}du\,
       (1-u^2)^2 W_{ab}\,
       \delta(\omega-E_a(k)-E_b(p)).
\]
For q>0 eliminate the angular delta, taking
\(p=E_b^{-1}(\omega-E_a(k))\), \(v_b=dE_b/dp\):
\[
 \rho={1\over8\pi q}\sum_{ab}
       \int_{\text{support}}dk\,k^5{p\over v_b(p)}
          (1-u^2)^2W_{ab},\quad
 u={k^2+q^2-p^2\over2kq}.
\]
Support requires enough energy for each branch and -1<=u<=1. At q=0,
if \(E_a(k_*)+E_b(k_*)=\omega\),
\[
 \rho_{ab}(\omega,0)={2k_*^6 W_{ab}(k_*,k_*)\over
                     15\pi[v_a(k_*)+v_b(k_*)]}.
\]
Each branch threshold is the minimum pair energy at fixed total q.
Constituent gaps are not discrete tensor excitations: this expression
integrates a two-particle continuum. At q=0 the lower-pair continuum
begins at zero, so any positive-energy proposed collective feature
must also be analyzed for decay into that continuum.

Independent normalization checks:
- At mu=0, m²=1 and s=omega²-q²>0,
  \(\rho=s^2[1+\Theta(s-4)(1-4/s)^{5/2}]/(960\pi)\);
  it vanishes for s<=0. This is a massless plus unit-mass real scalar.
- In the infrared the result tends to the previously derived
  \((\omega^2-c_s^2q^2)^2\Theta(\omega-c_s|q|)/(960\pi c_s^3)\).
- At q=0 and high omega, it tends to two real massless scalars,
  \(\omega^4/(480\pi)\).

Local metric-source contact terms and subtractions affect the analytic
real response. They cannot be reconstructed from this positive-frequency
absorptive spectrum alone. No Einstein coefficient is extracted from an
unsubtracted dispersion integral.

## 5. Interaction tensors derived from the potential

Let alpha=g/varrho0^(8/3). Expand the Cartesian potential with
\(\eta_1=\sigma,\eta_2=\phi\):
\[
 {\cal L}_{int}=-{1\over3!}C_{abc}\eta_a\eta_b\eta_c
               -{1\over4!}D_{abcd}\eta_a\eta_b\eta_c\eta_d+\cdots .
\]
The nonzero tensors, including permutations, are
\[
 C_{111}=\varrho_0(6\lambda-2\alpha/9),\qquad
 C_{122}=\varrho_0(2\lambda-2\alpha/3),
\]
\[
 D_{1111}=6\lambda+10\alpha/27,\quad
 D_{1122}=2\lambda+10\alpha/9,\quad
 D_{2222}=6\lambda-2\alpha .
\]
All eight cubic and sixteen quartic entries were differentiated independently
in the symbolic runner. Higher vertices exist: the fractional potential
is not a polynomial quantum field theory. Loop calculations require a stated
local effective-theory domain and a regulator/renormalization prescription.

A useful chosen weak-coupling parameter at fixed y is
\[
 \epsilon={\mu^2\over\varrho_0^2},\qquad
 \alpha={\epsilon\over1+y},\quad\lambda={y\epsilon\over1+y}.
\]
Then C/mu is O(sqrt(epsilon)), D is O(epsilon). This is a local perturbative
scaling, not an all-momentum convergence theorem. Varying epsilon at fixed
mu,y changes physical couplings; it is not just a normalization convention.

## 6. Nonzero elastic quadrupole and its soft law

Consider lower-branch elastic COM scattering with equal external magnitude
k and states normalized with 2E. Its external weights are Z=2E_- R_-.
The contact and total-momentum s-channel amplitudes have no angular
dependence, so their l=2 projections vanish at tree level.

At zero transfer frequency, the phase exchange source cancels exactly:
\(w^\dagger C_\phi w=0\). The radial source is
\[
 J_\sigma(k)=C_{111}Z_{\sigma\sigma}(k)+C_{122}Z_{\phi\phi}(k).
\]
Thus the remaining t+u amplitude is
\[
 {\cal M}_{ex}(k,u)=J_\sigma^2
 \left[{1\over m^2+2k^2(1-u)}+{1\over m^2+2k^2(1+u)}\right].
\]
Define the angular projection, not an assumed medium unitarity normalization,
\[
 {\cal A}_l={1\over2}\int_{-1}^1du\,P_l(u){\cal M}(u).
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{{\cal A}_2(k)={J_\sigma(k)^2\over k^2}
      Q_2\!\left(1+{m^2\over2k^2}\right)},\qquad
 Q_2(x)={3x^2-1\over4}\log{x+1\over x-1}-{3x\over2}.
\]
This is nonzero. The candidate action does provide a quadrupolar interaction,
without an independently inserted tensor coupling.

Since Q2(x)~2/(15x³),
\[
 {\cal A}_2={16J_\sigma(0)^2\over15(m^2)^3}k^4+O(k^6),
 \qquad J_\sigma(0)=C_{122}{m^2\over D}.
\]
On y>1, m²/mu²>2/3. Radial exchange therefore stays short-range on
this branch; taking its mass to zero would leave the selected branch.
The tree interaction is soft-suppressed rather than a long-range pole.

A conservative all-k projection bound follows from
\(0<\operatorname{tr}Z_-=1-2\mu^2/\sqrt{D^2/4+4\mu^2k^2}\leq1\),
positive C122<=C111, |P2|<=1 and denominators>=m²:
\[
 |{\cal A}_2|\leq {2C_{111}^2\over m^2}
 ={2\epsilon(6y-2/9)^2\over(1+y)(2y-2/3)}<36\epsilon .
\]
The last inequality follows from the positive remainder numerator
(4320y-1952)/81. This is NOT a norm bound on a resummed integral
operator and must not be used as an exclusion of all collective modes.

## 7. Exact OFF-SHELL angular kernel: the next calculation is now specified

An elastic cancellation must not silently remove phase exchange from
internal virtual states. Here is the full matrix angular projection.

Let the exchanged frequency be nu (complex when analytically continued),
external relative magnitudes p,k>0, and define
\[
 a=\nu^2-p^2-k^2,\quad b=2pk,\quad t=a+bu,\quad
 r_\pm={m^2\pm\sqrt{(m^2)^2+16\mu^2\nu^2}\over2},
\]
\[
 N(t;\nu)=\begin{pmatrix}t&2i\mu\nu\\-2i\mu\nu&t-m^2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The exchange propagator stripped of its overall Feynman i is
\[
 \Delta(\nu,\mathbf p-\mathbf k)
 ={N(t;\nu)\over(t-r_+)(t-r_-)}.
\]
It has a matrix partial-fraction decomposition, so its l=2 angular
projection is explicitly
\[
 \boxed{\Delta_2(\nu;p,k)=
 -{N(r_+;\nu)Q_2((r_+-a)/b)
        -N(r_-;\nu)Q_2((r_--a)/b)
       \over b(r_+-r_-)}} .
\]
Use analytic continuation of Q2 with the propagator's chosen causal
prescription; do not substitute unrelated principal-log values across
cuts. Numerical checks here use imaginary nu, away from causal cuts.
When the two r roots coincide, replace the divided difference by
\[
 -{1\over b}\partial_r\left[N(r;\nu)Q_2((r-a)/b)\right]_{r=m^2/2}.
\]
This removable-degeneracy case has its own independent angular check.
If p or k is zero, an isotropic, nonsingular integrand has zero l=2
projection; take limits separately at simultaneous singular points.

At nu=0 and p!=k,
\[
 \Delta_2(0;p,k)=-{1\over2pk}
 \operatorname{diag}\left[
 Q_2\!\left({p^2+k^2+m^2\over2pk}\right),
 Q_2\!\left({p^2+k^2\over2pk}\right)\right].
\]
The phase entry has a logarithmic singularity as p approaches k.
The radial entry does not. The on-shell phase-current cancellation in
section6 does not authorize deleting this off-shell entry. Its
integrability, symmetry cancellations and effect after the internal
frequency integration are part of the upcoming calculation.

To fix frequency direction and index conventions, take the ordered four
incoming legs as
\[
 (P/2+p,a),\ (P/2-p,b),\ (-P/2-k,c),\ (-P/2+k,d),
 \qquad \mathbf P=0.
\]
Define Delta_mn(l) by the contraction of eta_m(l) with eta_n(-l).
At the first vertex the internal momentum is minus the sum of its
external momenta. The TREE invariant-amplitude quadrupole is therefore
\[
 {\cal V}^{(2)}_{ab;cd}(p_0,p;k_0,k)=
 -C_{acm}[\Delta_2(k_0-p_0;p,k)]_{mn}C_{nbd}
 -C_{adm}[\Delta_2(-p_0-k_0;p,k)]_{mn}C_{nbc}.
\]
Repeated field indices are summed. The u channel uses p+k spatial
transfer: its angle reversal supplies (-1)^l, which is +1 here.
Contact and s-channel terms project to zero for this bare, non-derivative
tree action at total spatial momentum zero. Directed exchange satisfies
Delta(nu)^T=Delta(-nu); transposing indices without reversing frequency
would be an error.

This formula specifies the bare tree exchange contribution rather than a
new adjustable separable ansatz. It does not include dressed vertices,
self energies, higher-order two-particle-irreducible diagrams or counterterms.
At nonzero total spatial P only axial symmetry remains; a lone COM l=2
sector is not automatically a closed finite-P helicity calculation.

## 8. The collective-mode equation and next bounded task

For a consistently chosen real-time or Euclidean convention, let B(P)
denote the FULL two-line propagator convolution on symmetric pair space,
including its integration measure, identical-particle factor and
continuation prescription. Let K2(P) be the corresponding properly
renormalized two-particle-irreducible l=2 kernel (not an arbitrary multiple
of V2). The homogeneous mode condition is
\[
 \Phi=K_2(P)B(P)\Phi.
\]
Equivalently an eigenvalue reaches one; a finite discretized determinant
is meaningful only after convergence and normalization checks. A
stress-visible pole also needs nonzero source overlap, the appropriate
residue sign and a controlled continuation/dispersion. A resonance in the
two-particle continuum is not automatically a stable massless mode.

The concrete next task is to derive the pair-space normalization and
frequency contour from the same action, then apply the explicit Delta2
above in a one-core, modest-resolution static/low-frequency operator
calculation. Resolve the logarithmic phase entry analytically or with a
demonstrated integrable subtraction; do not regulate it by an unexplained
small number. Check decoupling and angular normalization before interpreting
any eigenvalue. Specify the effective-theory cutoff and counterterms and
vary them. If a proposed mode exists only after an independently tuned
counterterm, record that dependency rather than calling it derived.

Weak-coupling bounds can be sought first; the on-shell bound alone is not
such a proof. A controlled negative result would constrain THIS minimal
candidate, not justify deleting the saved original framework.
