## 2. Controlled orbital regime

Use c=G_R=1 in the perturbation equations, restoring the companion's geometric
mass mu=G_R m_comp when required. The previously matched monopole action is

    S_comp = -m_comp integral d tau.

Varying it gives the geodesic equation and the distributional stress tensor

    T^{ab}(x) = m_comp integral u^a u^b delta4(x-z(tau))/sqrt(-g) d tau.

An integration by parts against a smooth test covector gives div T proportional
to u^a nabla_a u^b; therefore it vanishes on the background geodesic. This is
conservation at first order in epsilon=mu/M, not an externally forced orbit
whose missing support stress has been ignored.

For an equatorial circular orbit at areal radius D>max(R,6M),

    Omega² = M/D³,          u^t = (1-3M/D)^(-1/2),
    u^phi = Omega u^t,      u^r=u^theta=0,
    sigma = 2 Omega.

Take epsilon<<1 and a small enough perturbation for linear response. All
reported amplitudes are coefficients per unit geometric mu, and powers per
mu². Setting that normalization to one in the code does not assert a physical
companion heavier than this body. Companion size, spin and tides are omitted.

The source is O(epsilon), and its emitted power is O(epsilon²). Keeping the
geodesic fixed is consistent at this perturbative order; the corresponding
slow orbital evolution requires the next-order energy/backreaction calculation.
This stage does not substitute an equal-mass orbit into a test-mass source.

## 3. Stress projections and derived source jumps

Use scalar harmonics normalized to integral |Y_lm|² dOmega=1. For ell=m=2,
Y_22(pi/2,0)=sqrt(15/(32 pi)); its conjugate along the orbit supplies
exp(-2i Omega t). Define C_D=8 pi mu u^t Ybar_22(pi/2,0) and delta=delta(r-D).
The Martel-Poisson projections of 8 pi G_R T have coefficients

    Q^{tt} = C_D delta/r²,         Q^{tr}=Q^{rr}=0,
    Q^t = -(2i Omega/3) C_D delta, Q^r=0,
    Q_flat = C_D Omega² delta,
    Q_sharp = -C_D Omega² r² delta/6,
    Q_trace = -f C_D delta/r²,     f=1-2M/r.

Here Q^t is a vector-harmonic projection, not a coordinate component of the
original stress. The tensor factor follows from Y_phiphi=-Y at the equator
for this harmonic. Substitution into the two orbit-space and angular Bianchi
identities gives zero; the radial identity uses Omega²=M/D³.

Insert these projections into the covariant even-parity source, multiply its
two-dimensional wave equation by f, and collect radial distributions. Products
with delta derivatives must be collected before evaluating at the orbit:

    F(r) delta'(r-D) = F(D) delta'(r-D) - F'(D) delta(r-D).

The resulting source is G_c delta(r-D)+F_c delta'(r-D). In tortoise coordinate
x=r_star, its equivalent form is

    L_sigma Z = j1 delta(x-x_D) + j0 delta'(x-x_D),
    L_sigma = d²/dx² + sigma² - V_Z,
    j0 = F_c/f_D²,
    j1 = G_c/f_D + F_c f'_D/f_D².

For the conserved circular source, direct simplification gives

    j0 = C_D (D-2M)/[3(2D+3M)],

    j1 = -C_D [12D³-16D²M-24DM²-33M³]
          /[6 D² (2D+3M)²].

Consequently [Z]=j0 and [dZ/dx]=j1. The master field itself is discontinuous at
the particle; imposing continuity, as for the previous delta-only driver, would
be wrong. Both coefficients agree symbolically with the independent point-
particle expressions of Martel (2004), after canonicalizing its distributions.
The derivation and full canonical coefficients are in
`results/derivation/status.json`.

The inherited vacuum metric-to-master map is checked against the

Thus no untracked master-field factor is hidden in the flux normalization.

## 4. Physical reference and stable amplitude difference

Let U be the center-regular coupled condensate solution, H the Schwarzschild
solution ingoing at its horizon, and V the common exterior solution outgoing
at infinity. Set V~exp(i sigma r_star) at infinity. For W(A,B)=A B_*'-A_*' B,
source matching gives the asymptotic coefficient

    A[U] = [j1 U(D)-j0 U_*'(D)] / W(U,V),
    A_BH = A[H].

Both branches use identical M, D, Omega, source, exterior equation and outgoing
condition. Only the inner response changes. An exact determinant identity gives

    Delta A = -W(U,H) [j1 V(D)-j0 V_*'(D)]/[W(U,V) W(H,V)],

    Delta A/A_BH = -W(U,H)/W(U,V)
                   * [j1 V(D)-j0 V_*'(D)]/[j1 H(D)-j0 H_*'(D)].

This factorization avoids subtracting nearly equal total wave amplitudes when
the finite-size effect becomes tiny. Six extraction-radius tests and nine
reference-basis tests preserve the relative excess below 2.56e-15. The arbitrary
dynamic tidal coefficient from doc18 is not inserted into an orbital action.

The horizon solution is derived with H=exp(-i sigma r_star) h(x), where
x=(r-2M)/(2M), k=2M sigma. Its regular series satisfies

    A(x) h'' + B(x) h' + C(x) h = 0,
    A = x(1+x)²(7+4x)²,
    B = [(1+x)-2ik(1+x)³](7+4x)²,
    C = -[189+420x+336x²+96x³].

The coefficient of h_(n+1) in the recursion is
49(n+1)(n+1-2ik). Orders six and eight and two starting distances are compared.
At zero frequency the numerical horizon solution recovers the pure static
growing solution; its scaled decaying admixture is below 5.77e-14.

Outgoing amplitudes are integrated inward, as learned in doc18. Tiny imaginary
logarithmic derivatives are obtained from conserved wave flux, not subtraction
of nearly equal complex products. At the horizon this uses flux=-sigma for a
unit ingoing amplitude. Boundary normalization checks accompany every solve.

## 7. Derived distance law, including its first relativistic correction

In the far zone relative to the fixed body, the vacuum equation itself gives

    P = r³/6 + M r²/4 - sigma² r^5/84 + ...,
    Q = r^(-2) + 2M/(3r³) + sigma²/6 + ... .

Insert these into the conserved source overlap j1 Z-j0 Z_*', and use the
circular relation sigma²=4M/D³. The ratio becomes

    overlap(Q)/overlap(P) = D^(-5) [1 + (85/21) M/D + ...].

With the independently calculated static lambda and the physical black-hole
reference, this yields for the real leading tidal amplitude correction

    Re(Delta A/A_BH) = 3 lambda_static/D^5 [1+(85/21) M/D+...],
    Delta P_22/P_22,BH = 6 lambda_static/D^5 + ... .

The leading coefficient and 85/21 are derived, not regressions to the orbit
results. This is a fixed-body wide-orbit expansion. Finite-frequency body terms
enter through sigma² proportional to D^(-3), and can be important much closer
to the surface. Higher propagation terms and imaginary terms are not fixed by
this displayed truncation. In particular, do not use it as the full near-body
response: the full numerical calculation is used in the table above.

Using the static lambda computed with the same tighter body settings,
lambda_static=62823.92235, the first-correction prediction has relative errors
