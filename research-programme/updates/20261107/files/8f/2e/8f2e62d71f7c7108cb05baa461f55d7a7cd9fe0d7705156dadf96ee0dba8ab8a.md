# Stage115 — an interpolation error found, and a targeted repair tested

The useful result is not the first attractive overlap match. We found
why that agreement was misleading, then tested a numerical repair using
the same collision equations and saved histories.

## 1. We derived and computed the shared-band spectrum
On a fixed band spanning roughly x=0.0025--0.05, write

    Phi_overlap = Phi_outer + x e,
    e' = A e - rho_outer.

The outer collision defect rho_outer, gas, hard-photon driver, initial
state and exterior collars determine this prediction. No new amplitude,
rate or joining window is fitted. Future inner spectra are held out.
Both backgrounds, baseline and both correction columns are tested.

The direct and defect-driven backward-Euler formulations agree; a
positive residual envelope bounds their discrete correction. This
envelope belongs to these original BE solves, not the later BDF2 repair.

At the final epoch, that first fine-grid candidate leaves only about
0.29--0.32% of the old inner-minus-outer band norm. However, it fails the
important independent frequency-grid test: coarse/fine differences reach
2.784 TIMES the discrepancy being explained. Both calculations had used
the same low-order interpolation. Their close agreement was not sufficient.
The old candidate is not promoted as a physical overlap prediction.

See `DERIVATION.md` and `results/comparison/status.json`.

## 2. The physical Doppler check identifies a numerical cause
We evaluated the known outer spectrum directly at saved Doppler-shifted
frequencies, without depositing those shifts on a transport mesh.
The checks cover four frequencies, three epochs, both grids and all six
baseline/correction columns.

Compared with the old two-point background interpolation:
- Four-point interpolation leaves at most 0.000177777 of its source error.
- Six-point interpolation leaves at most 0.00000275409 of its source error.

These ratios describe the executed source checks, not an all-frequency
bound or an observational improvement. They show that preserving
equilibrium and chemical potential alone did not control spectral curvature.

See `INTERPOLATION-FINDING.md` and
`results/background-scattering/status.json`.

## 3. Evolve the small difference, not an inaccurately scattered background
We implemented

    e' = A_low e + S_high[Phi_outer,b,beta_H] - (Phi_outer/x)'.

The positive inherited operator acts on the SMALL unknown difference.
Higher-order interpolation evaluates scattering of the KNOWN outer
spectrum. No physical coefficient changes. A second-order time method
is tested separately. This is a numerical repair of the same equation.

All 18 new time/grid/forcing-order controls pass the unchanged 1% target,
measured against the original band discrepancy, not the total spectrum.

## 4. What the repaired spectrum says
The repaired common-band solution lies close to the saved OUTER history.
Its final difference from the outer history is about:
- 0.1214--0.1216% of the original band mismatch for baseline;
- 0.02826--0.02842% for the correction columns.

By contrast, essentially all of the old inner-minus-outer mismatch
remains when comparing the old inner spectrum with this repaired solution.
Together with the direct scattering check, this identifies inherited
inner transport interpolation as a major contributor to that shared-band
discrepancy. It is not evidence for a new physical signal.

The small numbers above are diagnostic ratios. Some numerical controls,
measured over the whole history, exceed the tiny final correction itself.
Do not quote its sign/amplitude as a precision physical prediction.
The new two-sided outer exterior is a shared-band test, not a replacement
for the physically derived infrared exterior in a full inner solve.

The full identity is retained:

    Phi_inner-Phi_outer
      = (Phi_inner-Phi_overlap)+(Phi_overlap-Phi_outer).

Both fixed windows obey the canonical orthogonal contraction test.
The second term is never discarded to manufacture a smaller answer.
Old joining terms, gas transfers and parent responses are unchanged.

## 5. Finite-frequency scope is explicit
The finite-Planck conductance multiplier is derived and bounded over
the shared band and all saved event ratios. Its largest bound is
0.00349406, about 0.3494%, in the corresponding Dirichlet estimate.
That is NOT a relative error bound on a small cancelling net force.
The tested weak energy correction is paired with the opposite gas value,
but the gas history is not re-evolved.

Recoil, finite-emission remainders and the unsampled Juttner tail are not
included in that bound. Those cannot be inferred from Doppler ratios alone.
See `PLANCK-BOUND-SCOPE.md` and
`results/planck-remainder/status.json`.
