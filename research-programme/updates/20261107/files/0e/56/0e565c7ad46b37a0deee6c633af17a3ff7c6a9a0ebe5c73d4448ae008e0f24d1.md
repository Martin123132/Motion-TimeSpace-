# Reuse the already derived density and boost, rather than assume them

Stage80's emitting-wave-response-20261008/DERIVATION.md owns these couplings.
We apply them to the actual unchanged Stage23096-function source and the new
EOS gas rows. This is not a claim to have newly derived Stage80's result.

At fixed photon spectrum, actual gas temperature and species fractions,
Compton and double Compton scale with n_e, BR with n_e n_i. Therefore

    C(f)=f(C_Compton+C_DC)+f^2 C_BR,
    dC/df|1=C_Compton+C_DC+2C_BR.

Perturbing heating per particle gives the gas density column

    -(delta Q-Q b)/(chi C) = -Q_BR b/(chi C)

for a pure common density perturbation. The ion-density term survives; using
the Compton-only cancellation would discard the entire nonzero density
heating coefficient. We compare the actual assembled matrix moment to an
independent high-precision derivative and retain that negative control.

The common-velocity transformation of the isotropic source is
delta_v C=-v mu(C+x C_x). On a finite frequency interval its dipole moment is

    CF_boost/v=Q-[x^4 C]_(xmin)^(xmax)/(3 I3).

The weak form uses V_AB=integral dmu(2psi_A+x psi_A')psi_B and the explicit
face vector [psi_A x^3 C]/I3. The boundary is measured, not discarded or
absorbed into the gas. This is the common boost of a known isotropic source;
it supplies neither relative-velocity drag nor the full angular collision
Jacobian. The inherited ambient and temperature mixed terms keep their own
source ownership and must be included in a complete wave calculation.

These are source-port tests on preselected saved epochs400,1600,3200,4800,6400
on both parents and both NR/EOS histories. All bank, basis and source weights
stay fixed. The1e-9 moment/identity and1e-10 independent derivative gates are
frozen before evaluation. Finite-window boundary size is a reported physical
domain diagnostic, not a zero enforced to obtain a pass.
