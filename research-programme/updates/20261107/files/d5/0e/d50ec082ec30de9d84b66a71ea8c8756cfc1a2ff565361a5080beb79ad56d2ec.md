## 1. What is connected now

The leading current-quadrupole propagation tail is evaluated from the retained
GR hereditary kernel at the actual axial forcing frequency. It improves both
the point-source baseline and the body-minus-BH infinity-power prediction.
We then derive how that sourced radiation changes the circular orbital rate,
separating the reference black hole's horizon absorption. A strict PN expansion
also gives the axial contribution to accumulated orbital phase.

The calculation is at leading order in q=mu/M, with a circular test particle
outside the same spherical condensate. It is an axial ell2,m=+/-1 contribution,
not a declaration that other radiative sectors vanish. The phase formulas are
linear in the magnetic body response and in the selected loss correction.
The full numerical wave power includes the squared induced-wave term.

Use inherited geometric units G_R=c=1. D is Schwarzschild areal radius,
x=M/D, Omega=x^(3/2)/M, and beta=sigma_bar/M^5. These remain conditional
dimensionless/geometric predictions until a physical body scale is fixed.
The retained Einstein action is an input to this branch, not newly derived
by obtaining its propagation tail.

## 2. Current-wave tail, including its phase

The primary retained-GR input is the radiative current quadrupole in
[Blanchet, Qusailah and Will (2005), Eq4c](https://www2.iap.fr/users/blanchet/images/0004-637X_635_1_508.pdf):

    V_ij(t)=J_ij''(t)
       +2M integral_0^infinity J_ij''''(t-tau)
                       [ln(tau/(2b))+7/6] d tau + higher orders.

The source PDF is `sources/blanchet-qusailah-will-2005.pdf`. PDFpage3,
printedpage510, was rendered and visually checked, including the distinction
between the current constant7/6 and mass-quadrupole constant11/12.
`sources/provenance.json` records the URL, hash and extraction. We evaluate
this known GR kernel; we do not claim its derivation from condensate microphysics.

For exp(-i omega t), omega>0, regulate the integral with exp(-epsilon*tau).
Differentiating Gamma(1+a)/s^(1+a) at a=0 gives

    I_epsilon=[-gamma_E-ln(epsilon-i omega)-ln(2b)+kappa]
               /(epsilon-i omega),
    I_0=-pi/(2omega)+i[kappa-gamma_E-ln(2b omega)]/omega.

Since J''''/J''=-omega^2, the relative wave-amplitude correction is

    delta T=-2M omega^2 I_0
           =pi M omega+2i M omega[gamma_E+ln(2b omega)-7/6].

The arbitrary scale b and constant term change the phase, not the leading
power. Therefore |T|^2=1+2pi M omega+... . For the present current quadrupole,
omega=Omega, so the power multiplier is1+2pi*x^(3/2). For the dominant mass
quadrupole at2Omega it is1+4pi*x^(3/2), as in stage21. The +/-m pair does not
double this fractional correction: each conjugate mode has the same multiplier.

Both orbital and induced current radiate at the same frequency through the
same monopole exterior. Thus the factor is shared:

    |T(A+delta A)|^2-|TA|^2
       =|T|^2 [|A+delta A|^2-|A|^2].

It multiplies the infinity-power difference but cancels from the amplitude
ratio (A+delta A)/A. Applying a tail only to the body, or using the electric
frequency for the current, fails the common-reference comparison.

`derive_tail_balance.py` checks the symbolic transform and its phase/power
parts. Four50-digit direct, convergent complex quadratures verify the regulated
integral separately from the transform formula; Abel-limit checks verify the
retarded logarithm branch. These are evaluations of the sourced kernel, not
independent experimental evidence for it.

## 4. Deriving the separate horizon contribution

Stage25 already kept the black hole's absorbed flux rather than silently
turning it off. Its low-frequency law can also be derived from the same source.
The unit-horizon static master is H=(r/(2M))^3. With the outgoing near-zone
solution O=-3/(omega^2 r^2),

    W(H,O)=15/[(2M)^3 omega^2],
    A_H=[K O(D)-J O_star(D)]/W(H,O)
        =-A_source*(2M)^3/(10D^2)+higher orders,
    P21_H/P0=x^5+... .

Using stage25's corrected decaying basis and the geodesic u^t gives

    P21_H/P0=x^5 [1+(16/3)x+O(x^2)].

The universal OUTGOING normalization multiplies both numerator and Wronskian
in A_H and cancels. It must not be added a second time as an infinity-tail
factor on absorbed power. At fixed near-zone radius the real source/basis
expansion is even in omega, while the first horizon-normalization correction
odd in omega is imaginary. Thus it gives no relative real x^(3/2) power term.
The outgoing regular admixture starts beyond this order for ell2. We use only
this low-frequency, nonspinning result, not a generic claim about absorption.

The computed horizon power recovers x^5 and16/3 independently; its remaining
normalized residual is consistent with relative x^2. At10R,
P21_H/P0=7.72923058099e-15, about1.7% of the small magnetic infinity increment.

The lossless regular axial body has zero net inward flux. Relative to this
same-mass BH reference, the selected channel's change in total loss is

    Delta P21_loss=Delta P21_infinity-P21_H.

This does not assert that other body sectors have no absorption or radiation.

## 5. Energy and angular momentum: an exact test-geodesic denominator

The exterior geodesic gives

    E_orb=mu[(1-2x)/sqrt(1-3x)-1],
    L_orb=mu*M/sqrt[x(1-3x)],
    D_E=-2 E_orb'/mu=(1-6x)/(1-3x)^(3/2).

Direct differentiation checks E_orb'=Omega*L_orb'. For x<1/6, D_E>0.
Each circular m-mode satisfies P_J=P_E/Omega, so energy and angular momentum
balance give the same orbital drift:

    dot x=2 P_loss/(mu D_E),
    dot x_N=(64/5)(mu/M^2)x^5,
    Delta dot x21/dot x_N=(Delta P21_loss/P0)/D_E.

At this leading test-mass order the induced conservative energy is O(mu^2),
whereas E_orb is O(mu). Its relative correction vanishes as q tends to zero
at fixed body and x. This is why the common geodesic denominator is legitimate
here; it is NOT a way to discard stage23's conservative terms at finite q.

For the infinity and removed-absorption pieces separately,

    Delta dot x21_infinity/dot x_N
      =-beta*x^6/3 [1+(299/84)x+2pi*x^(3/2)+O(x^2)],
    Delta dot x21_missing_H/dot x_N
      =-x^5 [1+(41/6)x+O(x^2)].

The173/84 flux coefficient becomes299/84 in the chirp because of D_E.
At fixed x the fractional Omega-dot increment has the same normalization.
This is a mode contribution to the orbital rate, not a model evolving on the
axial power alone. Mode powers add before computing a complete inspiral.

For the inherited body beta=-117765.12090566804, at10R the numerical pieces are

    infinity contribution / dot x_N =+4.549556212702590e-13,
    missing BH absorption / dot x_N =-7.746873005400991e-15,
    combined axial contribution     =+4.472087482648580e-13.

At wide separation the removed horizon term scales as x^5, while the magnetic
infinity term scales as x^6. Their leading crossing for this body is
x_cross=-3/beta, D_cross/R=589.9633. This is a conditional selected-channel
crossing, not an observational prediction or a global preference for either
object. The numerical calculation gives a positive total axial correction
at320R and a negative one at640R, robust to changed numerical settings.

## 6. A strictly ordered phase contribution

Define Phi=2 times orbital phase, the usual dominant-wave carrier phase,
not the axial m1 waveform phase itself. For a common reference flux P_ref,
linearizing at fixed x gives

    delta(dPhi/dx)=-2Omega*(-E_orb')*Delta P21_loss/P_ref^2.

For a1.5PN contribution use the known test-mass point reference from stage21,

    P_ref/P0=1-(1247/336)x+4pi*x^(3/2)+O(x^2),
    D_E/(P_ref/P0)^2=1+(995/168)x-8pi*x^(3/2)+O(x^2).

The point coefficient and mass-wave tail are retained GR baseline inputs,
already recorded in `../pn-balance-20261004/21-electric-tidal-PN-radiation-and-balance.md`.
They are not estimated from the current-mode calculation. Expanding at
consistent order, rather than resumming an incomplete denominator, yields

    q*delta Phi'_mag
      =(5beta/96)*x^(5/2) [1+(447/56)x-6pi*x^(3/2)+O(x^2)],
    q*delta Phi'_missing_H
      =(5/32)*x^(3/2) [1+(1891/168)x-8pi*x^(3/2)+O(x^2)].

The negative6pi in the magnetic PHASE correction is not a sign error: the
positive2pi current-flux tail combines with the negative8pi from the shared
point-flux-squared denominator. A radiation coefficient cannot simply be
copied into a phase formula. Primitives at this stated order are

    q*delta Phi_mag=(5beta/96)
        [(2/7)x^(7/2)+(149/84)x^(9/2)-(6pi/5)x^5],
    q*delta Phi_missing_H=(5/32)
        [(2/5)x^(5/2)+(1891/588)x^(7/2)-2pi*x^4].

Endpoint differences give the band contribution. The leading magnetic term
recovers stage23 as q tends to zero. Derivatives and60-digit quadratures check
both primitives. For20R->10R,

    q*delta Phi_mag=-2.121227622309898e-7 rad,
    q*delta Phi_missing_H=+4.560866012507734e-9 rad,
    q*delta Phi_total_axial=-2.075618962184821e-7 rad.

Reporting q times phase avoids inventing a finite mass ratio. The inverse-q
scaling belongs to the adiabatic test-mass approximation; it does not mean
that arbitrarily long real signals accumulate this idealized phase without
environmental or other-sector effects. Electric tides, other multipoles,
finite-q conservative terms, and their appropriate orders must be included
before treating this as a complete waveform. Products of the magnetic
response with other body responses are excluded from these linear formulas.
