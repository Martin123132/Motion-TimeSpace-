# Stage184: scattering-corner forcing, stiff gas response and sampling

All equations use the already retained scaled background variables and u=ln(a/a_start).
No new parent coefficient or physical temperature curve is introduced. The source
probe coordinate reverses u; symmetric finite-width curvature is unchanged by that
reversal. Write B(theta) for the bank, D(theta) for emission, r for the positive
rate, S=r(B-D), A=M^-1 S and q=p-Tg. Then

    p_u=Aq,  chi(g_u+g)=-1-e p_u.

## Exact corner statement at finite heat capacity

The saved bank is continuous, piecewise linear in theta; theta_u=-theta.
Let B_theta,warm and B_theta,cool be the two saved adjacent secants. Increasing u
crosses from warm to cool. The background rate and emission's cubic interpolator
have continuous first derivatives here. Thus, with jump=after-before,

    Delta S_u = r theta (B_theta,warm-B_theta,cool),
    Delta p_uu = M^-1 Delta S_u q,
    Delta g_uu = -e M^-1 Delta S_u q / chi.

For finite positive chi, p and g remain C1; their second derivatives can jump.
Do not call a gas first-derivative jump an exact result at finite chi. In particular,
e Delta p_uu+chi Delta g_uu=0: the corner is an exchange term, not lost energy.

## Fast-relaxation limit and a no-fit finite-width prediction

For a temporarily fixed photon state,

    C=chi-e A T > 0,
    G(p,u)=-(1+e A p)/C,
    delta=chi/C,
    delta g_u+g=G.

delta is the frozen-photon relaxation width in log expansion, not seconds or a
certified eigenvalue of the full coupled system. A small delta relative to probe
width supports a local slaving approximation but is not alone a global accuracy
proof. The coupled finite-capacity diagnostic below tests the amplitude directly.

On the slaved branch, p_u=A(p-TG) and

    G_u = -[e A_u(p-TG)+e A p_u]/C,
    J=Delta G_u = -e M^-1 Delta S_u (p-TG)/C.

This J is predicted from the anchor state and saved matrix secants, not fitted to
the two measured curvatures. Its leading one-sided contribution is J s_+, where
s=u-u_anchor. For C_d[g]=(g(-d)-2g(0)+g(d))/d^2,

    C_d[J s_+]=J/d,
    C_d[J s_+]-C_(d/2)[J s_+]=-J/d.

The widths are the unchanged d=.01 and d/2=.005. A quadratic one-sided term makes
the same contribution at both widths and cancels in their difference. Cubic and
higher terms, smooth evolution and interpolation remain separate remainders.

For the illustrative frozen-photon relaxation model, the rounded response is

    g_corner=J[s-delta(1-exp(-s/delta))] for s>=0, zero before.

Its width difference equals

    -J/d + J delta/d^2 [3+exp(-d/delta)-4exp(-d/(2delta))].

This gives a local heat-capacity scaling estimate, not an exact solution of every
coupled photon mode. The continuous jump and slaved prediction must be distinguished.

## Exact linear forced diagnostic, with the original bank preserved

Define S_ref=S before the knot. After it, continue the ORIGINAL warm secant; no
coefficient is fitted. Equivalently S_ref=S-Delta S where

    Delta S=r(theta-theta_anchor)(B_theta,cool-B_theta,warm).

This is a counterfactual for attribution, not a repaired physical bank. Starting
from the identical two saved states, let Y_ref use S_ref. For differences
delta_p=p-p_ref and delta_g=g-g_ref,

    M delta_p_u=S_ref(delta_p-T delta_g)+Delta S(p-Tg),
    chi(delta_g_u+delta_g)=-e delta_p_u,
    delta_W_u=chi delta_g.

The forcing uses the saved ORIGINAL history. All three equations are linear and
apply to the same variable-step BDF2 recurrence. Direct counterfactual evolution
and the forced-difference solution must reconstruct the original history. Test
both full-state identities at1e-10 and the small isolated curvature contribution
at1%; do not divide a native floating-point cancellation by a large denominator
and call the small contribution exact. Preserve both diagnostics.

## Sampling error: why an increment ratio near one half is not enough

At a fixed smooth-side probe u*, with cell width h and fractional position f,
linear interpolation has leading error

    epsilon_h(u*)=g_uu(u*) h^2 f(1-f)/2.

For f<1/4, two successive refinements have fractions2f and4f. Then

    epsilon_(h/2)-epsilon_h=-g_uu h^2 f/4,
    epsilon_(h/4)-epsilon_(h/2)=-g_uu h^2 f/8.

Their ratio is1/2 despite a locally second-order interpolation rule. Reflection
f->1-f gives the matching case near the other cell boundary. Apply the same
fixed curvature stencils to these probe errors, not a fitted temperature curve.

For an equation-based smooth-side prediction, differentiate the slaved relation:

    p1=Aq, q1=p1-T G1,
    G1=-e(A1 q+A p1)/C,
    p2=A1 q+A q1,
    G2=-e(A2 q+2 A1 q1+A p2)/C.

A1,A2 differentiate the existing matrix interpolators and physical rate within
one segment. For nonlocal-leading emission,

    D=K_DC theta^2 D_nonlocal+D_br(log theta),
    D_u=-2 K_DC theta^2 D_nonlocal-D_br',
    D_uu=4 K_DC theta^2 D_nonlocal+D_br''.

In each scattering segment B_u=-theta B_theta and B_uu=theta B_theta. The rate
r=opacity/H_conformal has ln(r)_u=-3-s, s=d ln(H_physical)/du. Its derivatives
follow from the retained radiation, dust and canonical scalar energy equations,
not numerical fits to g. Compare the analytic emission reduction to the original
constructor and retain any failed derivative/sampling predictions.
