# Time-dependent coupling: current and areal momentum derived together

## What this stage establishes

We derived and tested the horizontal current of the physical native-radius
kernel, including its moving geometry, local atom transport and temporal
endpoint. We also derived the momentum paired with A_t, the time rate of
the areal-radius field. This completes an essential time-response piece
that the preceding static angular-stress calculation could not supply.

The result rules out a misleading shortcut: on these saved states the
new Gram current reproduces the old current to arithmetic precision.
Its geometry-rate term was already represented by the old Jacobian
term. It must not be appended as an extra energy flux. The useful new
result is a consistent action-level relation between current, angular
response and mesh momentum, ready for a coupled Euler-equation test.

## 1. The current is an action derivative, not a mass-rate fit

Retain the specified source-anchored horizontal leaf T_R=c_A(T,R) and
T(s,B(s))=s. On each leaf the physical mesh R_e(s) is fixed during a
pure shift variation, while nodal histories and the coefficient density
are retimed. The operator is rebuilt on that leaf's geometry, NOT on
separately retimed positions R_e(T_e). The wave histories include the
moving-node factor T_s+c_A W_i. These choices define the extension.

At zero shift and A=r, let f=D_R q, l=S^T(f^2/2), e=beta*l and
beta=R^2 N sqrtF. Its full coefficient-loading rate is

    dot l=S^T[f(D_R qdot+dot D_R q)].

For a connection variation h, with I(R)=integral_b^R h, the weak Gram
current is

    <K_G,h>=-sum_i U_q,i qdot_i I(R_i)
             +sum_j [beta_j dot l_j+beta_r,j W_j l_j] I(X_j)
             +sum_j e_j W_j h(X_j).

The last term is the local moving-atom current. The source-anchored
interval current and this local term are checked independently. No
field/source Euler work is set to zero to obtain the formula.

The raw finite-action variation and the integrated current obey

    delta L_G=<K_G,h>+d_t B_G,   B_G=-sum_j e_j I(X_j).

Explicit Eulerian beta_t terms cancel in this identity; their cancellation
is derived before selecting frozen-metric jets for the numerical tests.
The endpoint is retained for the noncompact temporal test variations.
Ignoring it would confuse an instantaneous action derivative with the
time-integrated functional current.

## 2. The time-dependent areal coupling

The exact change from radial coordinate r to physical areal coordinate A
gives

    F_A=F A_r^2,   v_A=v_shift A_r-A_t,
    ds^2=-N^2 dt^2+(dA+v_A dt)^2/F_A+A^2 dOmega^2.

Both combinations also survive a time-dependent radial relabelling
r'=f(t,r): F'=F f_r^2, v'=f_r v-f_t,
A_(r')=A_r/f_r, and A_t|r'=A_t-A_r f_t/f_r. This supplies a consistent
radial-coordinate law for the stated extension when its mesh markers
are transformed too. It does not establish invariance under arbitrary
changes of the time foliation or determine a unique parent action.

At the base A=r, A_t=v_shift=0, an A_t variation changes the physical
mesh velocity and shift with opposite signs. Consequently

    Pi_A=P_mesh-Q_shift,    Q_shift=K_total/(N^2 F).

Q_shift is the functional shift covector after horizontal time integration
by parts, with its endpoint kept separately. Thus:

- The potential-only Gram sector has P_mesh,G=0 and
  Pi_A,G=-K_G/(N^2 F).
- For P2 waves, <P_mesh,w,kappa>=-integral alpha T q_R kappa_h dR,
  alpha=R^2/(N sqrtF), T=qdot-W q_R. All cells and the actual mesh
  velocities are included in the numerical contractions.
- For the source, mesh and shift momenta are equal. Indeed
  (V_A+v_A)^2/F_A=(V+v_shift)^2/F exactly, so A_t cancels from its clock.

The full areal Euler derivative therefore includes -partial_t Pi_A as
well as the spatial response. Appending only the preceding static angular
stress to unchanged time equations would omit this term.

## 3. What the combined balance now asks us to solve

The two explicit time pieces regroup as

    partial_t Q_shift+partial_t Pi_A=partial_t P_mesh.

This is an off-shell chain identity, not a demonstration that the total
balance is zero. For an unrestricted, transforming mesh, write the
mechanical Euler covector as E_e=dot P_e-L_(R_e). The radial coordinate
identity relates stress divergence to that Euler work. At A=r,

    E_A=-N_r E_N-F_r E_F-partial_r(2F E_F)
          +partial_t Q_shift-sum_e E_e delta_e.

Here E_N,E_F,E_A are functional metric covectors of the same reduced
action. With M=N r^2/sqrtF and the usual reduced stress definitions,

    E_N=-r^2 rho/sqrtF, E_F=-N r^2 p_r/(2F^(3/2)),
    E_A=2N r p_perp/sqrtF, Q_shift=M T^t_r,

this is precisely the weak radial stress-divergence relation, including
the temporal metric prefactor. Compact test variations or their explicit
boundary work are required. The final seal checks the algebraic signs.

The restricted source-fitted map used in the live solver does not supply
every independent mesh Euler equation automatically. Nor can a finite
P2/label Galerkin equation be assumed to set arbitrary localized field
work to zero. These are the equations to test next, not reasons to invent
a flux counterterm. The current and angular response now expose their
role within one declared action rather than leaving the time coupling
unspecified.
