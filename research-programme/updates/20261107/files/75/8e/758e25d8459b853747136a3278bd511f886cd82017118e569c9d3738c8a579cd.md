# Scope of the incident-history derivative

The existing Spectra loader uses linear interpolation in expansion time for
the saved native photon coefficients, gas departure and low-frequency slope.
Its spline is cubic in frequency, not in time. Therefore the reused incident
history is piecewise linear in u (and in xi=constant-u). Its time derivative
can jump at stored epochs.

The exact differentiable chain rules in DERIVATION.md and SECOND-DERIVATIVE.md
apply on smooth pieces, or to a smooth physical history obtained from the
equations. They do not prove a unique classical second derivative at a knot
of this saved interpolant. The moving-history stencil is a finite-step
variation of the supplied history. A stable finite-step result must not be
described as a proof of an infinitesimal derivative of that interpolant.
The fixed-incident branch avoids this particular history regularity issue.
No smoothing, new history fit or replacement of the saved input is performed.

For a source linear in h, there is also an exact finite-increment statement
that requires no differentiability:

    S(xi+delta,h_new)-S(xi,h_old)
      = [K(xi+delta)-K(xi)] h_old
        + K(xi+delta) (h_new-h_old).

The saved paired calculations keep both terms available. For either frozen
prediction order, define

    e_kernel = P_fixed - S_fixed,
    e_history = (P_moving-P_fixed) - (S_moving-S_fixed).

Then e_moving=e_kernel+e_history exactly. These vectors, their norms and their
cross term distinguish a microscopic-kernel miss from a history/cross-term
miss without modifying the prediction or using a new temperature fit.
Norms alone do not add: their cross term is retained.

The target is an independent microscopic computation, not an observed
astrophysical spectrum. Input histories and baseline approximations remain
the inherited ones. A successful selected-event test would justify a larger
quadrature test, not by itself certify the full source or its final spectrum.
