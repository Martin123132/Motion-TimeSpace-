# Stage181: gas curvature from the saved coupled energy equation

Let u be log expansion, p the 96-component photon profile, g the saved scaled
gas-temperature response, M the mass matrix, e the energy row, T the thermal
template, chi the constant scaled gas capacity, and S the rate-weighted
scattering-minus-emission matrix. These are the existing background solver's
variables, not new MTS coefficients. All derivative coordinates are dimensionless.

    M p_u = S (p - T g),  E = e p
    E + chi g + W = 0,  W_u = 1 + chi g
    chi (g_u + g) = -1 - E_u
    g_uu = -g_u - E_uu/chi

The temperature coordinate used for the source probes is t=-Delta u, so g_tt=g_uu.
The last identity is continuous; sampled histories do not directly prove it.
For the saved late-time step h, BDF2 is the FIRST derivative

    D f_i = (3 f_i - 4 f_(i-1) + f_(i-2))/(2h).
    D E_i + chi D g_i + 1 + chi g_i = 0.

At the discrete level write a=3/(2h), v=(2p_(i-1)-p_(i-2)/2)/h and
s=(2g_(i-1)-g_(i-2)/2)/h. With L=aM-S,

    f=L^-1 Mv,  r=L^-1 (-ST),  p_i=f+r g_i
    [chi(a+1)+a e r] g_i = chi s - 1 - e(a f-v).

This is an energy-balance prediction using previous states, not the current
stored g_i. It avoids dividing a small difference of order-one energy terms
by chi alone. Its denominator and separate numerator contributions are saved.
The original solver uses a positive-definite solve reading the upper triangle
of its binary64 L; a replay must retain that convention. The photon-equation
residual also checks the nominal full matrix, so triangle roundoff is visible.

For the already frozen widths d=0.01 and 0.005,

    C_d[g]=(g(u0-d)-2g(u0)+g(u0+d))/d^2
    Delta C[g]=C_0.01[g]-C_0.005[g]
    Delta U_gas=K_g Delta C[g], K_g=A_anchor[0,1].

Use the original linear interpolation and coordinates unchanged. Replaying the
bracketing native updates can test this amplitude without recalculating K_g or
the virtual integrals. Finite-width curvature is not a classical point derivative.
Identities using jointly evolved E,g,W are bookkeeping controls; bank-based RHS
and prior-state replay are stronger local consistency checks, still not an
independent time-step convergence test.
