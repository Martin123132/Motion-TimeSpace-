# Self-gravitating finite bodies — results, 3 October 2026

**Outcome:** the previously constructed condensate bodies admit converged
stationary solutions with weak gravitational back-reaction through the retained
parent Einstein sector. Their charge stays fixed while size and mass respond.

## What moved forward

- Solved three fixed-charge families through seven coupling values each,
  including their flat controls, at two numerical resolutions.
- Derived the curved compact boundary and used the proper-volume/redshift
  charge measure. No prescribed body density or extra surface force was added.
- Derived the leading binding-energy coefficient from the flat field profile:
  it contains rho+p_r, not rho alone. The coupled weak-gravity results match
  that coefficient to better than5e-10 relative after small-coupling
  extrapolation. This is numerical agreement, not observational precision.
- The pressure correction to the density-only Newtonian estimate decreases
  from11.0% to0.503% as the chosen flat body size grows from8 to80.
- Independently checked the interior mass integral, Komar mass, angular
  Einstein equation and mass/charge first law; no constant was fitted to pass.

## Concrete example

For the largest tested charge at nominal compactness3%, gravity reduces its
radius by **1.09%** and its mass measured from infinity by **0.962%**, keeping
charge unchanged. The local central phase frequency and the frequency at
infinity differ consistently with gravitational redshift. All tested metric
functions stay well away from a horizon.

## Evidence and precision

18 exact identity checks;42 coarse/fine stationary profiles;12 nearby-charge
first-law profiles;2 third-resolution profiles;4 pilot profiles.
Maximum coarse/fine observable change3.43e-9. Fine charge and mass consistency
errors are below1.7e-13. Differential equations have looser resolved errors:
scalar4.56e-6 and angular Einstein6.49e-5 relative; the worst angular case falls
to1.03e-6 at the third resolution. Do not quote the integral precision as the
pointwise equation accuracy. Maximum mass/charge first-law error2.32e-9.

These results concern the isolated complex-U(1) candidate, with unfitted
physical scales and the existing gravitational coupling. Original real-field
and bath documents remain unchanged. Stationary continuation does not by
itself establish coupled gravitational stability.
