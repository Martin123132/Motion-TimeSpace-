## 2. The translation zero fixes the shape-stiffness bound

For rotating Cartesian perturbations (u+i v)Y_lm, define

\[
 L_{+,l}=-\partial_r^2-\frac2r\partial_r+
          \frac{l(l+1)}{r^2}+W''(h)-w^2,\qquad
 L_{-,l}=-\partial_r^2-\frac2r\partial_r+
          \frac{l(l+1)}{r^2}+\frac{W'(h)}h-w^2.
\]

Differentiating the background equation gives L_{+,1}h'=0.
The background itself gives L_{-,0}h=0. These are translation and
constant-phase identities, not adjustable stabilizing terms.

With a=-h'>0 and inner product integral r^2 u v dr, integration by parts gives

\[
 \langle u,L_{+,1}u\rangle
 =\int_0^R r^2 a^2[\partial_r(u/a)]^2dr .
\]

The discarded boundary term is [r^2 a a'(u/a)^2]_0^R.
It vanishes for the regular solutions, and the inequality extends to
the closure of the corresponding finite-energy trial space.
The identical transform with a=h applies to L_{-,0}. Consequently,

\[
 L_{+,l}\ge\frac{(l-1)(l+2)}{R^2}\quad(l\ge2),\qquad
 L_{-,l}\ge\frac{l(l+1)}{R^2}\quad(l\ge1).
\]

In particular, the quadrupole amplitude and phase stiffnesses are at
least 4/R^2 and 6/R^2. This is an analytic conditional bound, stronger
than simply observing positive numerical Ritz values.

Regular solutions behave as r^l at the center. Since W''~2/d^2 and
W'/h~6/d^2 for d=R-r, homogeneous regular edge solutions have u~d^2,
v~d^3. The alternative exponents -1 and -2 are excluded by finite energy.
Finite elements need lie in the quadratic-form domain, not satisfy the
strong operator equation at every endpoint; the independent collocation
calculation below imposes the actual moving-edge expansion.

The assumptions matter: monotonicity, existence of a suitable compact
background and this energy-domain choice are required. The three numerical
profiles are evidence for those hypotheses, not interval-certified solutions.
This is not a nonlinear theorem allowing arbitrary changes of exterior support.

## 3. The actual oscillation gap is not a stiffness eigenvalue

The coupled equations are

\[
 \ddot u-2w\dot v+L_{+,l}u=0,\qquad
 \ddot v+2w\dot u+L_{-,l}v=0.
\]

Their conserved quadratic energy is positive for l>=2. For l=2 put
a=4/R^2, b=6/R^2 and S=a+b+4w^2. A conservative lower gap bound is

\[
 \Omega_{\min}^2\ge
 \frac{2ab}{S+\sqrt{S^2-4ab}}>0.
\]

For x=Omega^2 below min(a,b), the frequency pencil is strictly positive
if (a-x)(b-x)>4w^2x, by Cauchy-Schwarz on its off-diagonal coupling.
The smaller root supplies the displayed bound; it is intentionally loose.

In the discrete calculation both mass matrices are whitened. A and B
are the amplitude and phase stiffness matrices, and C is the projected
2w overlap. Discretizing the rotating-field Lagrangian *before* the
Legendre transform gives

\[
 H_{\rm even}=\begin{pmatrix}A+CC^T&-C\\-C^T&I\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 H_{\rm odd}=\operatorname{diag}(I,B).
\]

The eigenvalues of D^T H_even D, with D D^T=H_odd, are Omega^2.
Replacing CC^T by 4w^2 I changes the finite-space projection and is
not used. An independent first-order gyroscopic generator checks this
construction at the same resolution.

## 4. Derive the tide from the metric, not a guessed scalar force

Prescribe the local static vacuum tide

\[
 ds^2=-(1+2\Phi)dt^2+(1-2\Phi)d\mathbf{x}^2,\qquad
 \Phi=\epsilon r^2Y_{2m},\quad |\epsilon|R^2\ll1.
\]

This is a local applied field, not a growing asymptotically flat solution.
Through first order in Phi the fixed-w grand potential is

\[
 F=E-wQ=\int d^3x\left[
 \frac12|\nabla h|^2+W-\frac12w^2h^2
 +\Phi(2w^2h^2-2W)\right].
\]

The first-order spatial-gradient metric terms cancel. Keeping only the
lapse redshift would give the wrong drive. Writing delta h=epsilon uY
therefore yields

\[
 L_{+,2}u=J,\qquad J=2r^2[W'(h)-2w^2h].
\]

Because the quadrupole has zero angular average, delta Q=0 at first order.
The fixed-Q Legendre transform preserves this second-order relaxation
term; it does not require freezing the charge density at each point.
The static regular phase solution in this angular sector is zero.

Define the field-relaxation susceptibility in this metric/coordinate convention:

\[
 \chi_{\rm relax}=\int_0^Rr^2Ju\,dr
 =\langle J,L_{+,2}^{-1}J\rangle,\qquad
 0<\chi_{\rm relax}\le\frac{R^2}{4}\int_0^Rr^2J^2dr,
\]
\[
 \Delta E_{\rm relax}=-\frac12\epsilon^2\chi_{\rm relax}.
\]

All reported numbers are dimensionless. If Phi=epsilon_phys r_phys^2 Y,
then epsilon=epsilon_phys/mu_*^2 and the dimensional relaxation
coefficient is (v_*^2/mu_*^5)chi_relax, in natural units.
No physical scale has been fitted.

This is specifically the relaxation contribution from the linear imposed
metric. It excludes induced Einstein perturbations and explicit quadratic
metric terms. It is not a gauge-invariant relativistic Love number.
Obtaining that observable requires the coupled exterior matching.

## 7. The large body has a derived liquid-surface interpretation

\[

 =0.180622174271944.
\]

For an incompressible large body with displacement xi Y_lm, fixed-volume
surface energy and irrotational kinetic energy are

\[
 \Delta E_{\rm surface}=\frac{\widehat\sigma}{2}(l-1)(l+2)\xi^2,\qquad
 T=\frac{R_q^3}{2l}\dot\xi^2 .
\]

\[
 \xi/\epsilon\simeq-\frac{R_q^4}{4\widehat\sigma},\qquad
 \chi_{\rm relax}\simeq\frac{R_q^8}{4\widehat\sigma},\qquad

These are asymptotic approximations, not identities for finite bodies.
