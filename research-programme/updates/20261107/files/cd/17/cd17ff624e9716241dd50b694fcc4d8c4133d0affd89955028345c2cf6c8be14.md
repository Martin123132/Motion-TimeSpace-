## Result and model scope

The already calculated radiation now determines changes in both independent orbital actions, and hence in orbital size and eccentricity. This is an application of the geodesic radial-action first law to the actual body/BH wave spectra, not a new free damping prescription. The same inherited Einstein–Hilbert/G_R plus isolated global-U(1) scalar model, central mass, radius and conservative interior are retained.

We calculate the leading adiabatic **odd ell=2, m=+/-1 contribution** for a test companion of mass mu. Previous wave powers were coefficients per mu squared. The rates below are specific orbital-variable rates divided by mu. A physical application requires mu/M much smaller than one, slow evolution compared with an orbital period, and an exterior stable orbit. No physical mass/length scale is fitted here. ## 1. The radial action is derived from the orbit

Use geometric units G_R=c=1 and Schwarzschild coordinate time normalized at infinity. Write f=1-2M/r, specific energy E=-u_t and specific angular momentum h=u_phi. The radial mass shell gives

    (dr/dtau)^2 = U(r) = E^2 - f(1+h^2/r^2),
    p_r/mu = sqrt(U)/f

on the outward half orbit. For simple turning points r_p,r_a,

    j = J_r/mu = (1/pi) integral[r_p,r_a] sqrt(U)/f dr.

At fixed M, the endpoint terms in the first derivatives vanish because p_r vanishes there. The differentiated integrands have integrable square-root singularities. In particular,

    partial_E(sqrt(U)/f) = E/(f sqrt(U)) = dt/dr,
    partial_h(sqrt(U)/f) = -h/(r^2 sqrt(U)) = -dphi/dr.

Therefore

    j_E = T_r/(2pi) = 1/Omega_r,
    j_h = -Delta_phi/(2pi) = -Omega_phi/Omega_r,
    dE = Omega_r dj + Omega_phi dh.                          (1)

The circular case is taken as the continuous epicyclic limit of stable eccentric orbits, not as an integral over a zero-length interval before differentiating. Section 4 evaluates that limit explicitly.

This is established GR action-angle structure. Le Tiec's first-law papers provide independent context and the adiabatic interpretation; see SOURCES.md. The Schwarzschild specialization (1) is derived here directly, rather than inferred from a citation or a fitted orbit.

## 2. Energy and angular momentum jointly determine radial damping

Let Pbar_n be the emitted energy coefficient per mu squared for a paired m=+/-1 mode. Work with the m=1 labels and keep signed frequencies

    omega_n = Omega_phi + n Omega_r.

The reality partner has m=-1,n=-n,omega=-omega_n; it is already included in the pair normalization. Its m/omega and n/omega ratios equal those of the chosen representative. It must not be counted twice or thrown away when omega is negative.

For the nonabsorbing body, Pbar_n=Pbar_n^infinity. For the BH reference, Pbar_n=Pbar_n^infinity+Pbar_n^horizon. Averaged energy and angular-momentum balance, with periodic bounded near-zone storage, gives

    Edot/mu = -sum_n Pbar_n,
    hdot/mu = -sum_n Pbar_n/omega_n,
    jdot/mu = -(sum Pbar - Omega_phi sum Pbar/omega)/Omega_r
             = -sum_n n Pbar_n/omega_n.                      (2)

The final modal sum is numerically preferable: the n=0 carrier contributes identically zero to jdot, so circularity does not depend on subtracting almost equal total fluxes. Individual radial-action contributions need not have the same sign. No positive-definiteness argument for their sum is assumed.

An exactly zero-frequency mode has zero radiative flux and requires its static-field treatment; these wave calculations contain no such frequency. Formula (2) is not permission to divide a numerical zero by zero.

The inherited smooth-difference retarded kernel also yields an independent route. Stage31 derived

    Delta Pbar_n = 2 omega_n Im Fret_n,
    Delta Lbar_n = 2 Im Fret_n.

Here Delta means body minus (BH infinity plus BH horizon). Substituting into (1), not adding a new coupling, gives

    Delta jdot/mu = -2 sum_n n Im Fret_n.                    (3)

The wave-flux and source-work calculations agree. The pericentre Fourier phase and dual-source convention remain those of the inherited kernel; an arbitrary phase rotation does not license squaring a rotated complex overlap without its dual.

### Perturbative order

E_orb=mu E_0+O(mu^2), L_orb=mu h_0+O(mu^2), and J_r=mu j_0+O(mu^2). Leading flux is O(mu^2), giving specific-variable drifts O(mu). Conservative O(mu^2) orbital corrections differentiated along those drifts enter at O(mu^3), under the adiabatic nonresonant ordering. Thus geodesic derivatives are appropriate for the leading dissipative coefficient computed here; previous conservative response terms are not silently spliced into a mismatched order.

The BH absorbs at O(mu^2); its resulting mass/spin changes affect this instantaneous leading-order orbital coefficient at higher order. Secularly accumulated background changes, resonant storage, transients or post-adiabatic phasing would require the enlarged system. No instantaneous self-force or complete inspiral trajectory is reconstructed by (2).

## 3. A regular coordinate system for the orbit

Set z=e^2 and use dimensionless semilatus p, with r_p=Mp/(1+e), r_a=Mp/(1-e). Stable bound Schwarzschild orbits satisfy 0<=z<1 and p>6+2 sqrt(z). Define

    D=p-3-z, N=(p-2)^2-4z,
    E=sqrt(N/(pD)), h=Mp/sqrt(D).

Their exact derivatives are

    E_p=[2(p-2)pD-N(D+p)]/[2E(pD)^2],
    E_z=(p-4)^2/(2EpD^2),
    h_p=M(p-6-2z)/(2D^(3/2)), h_z=Mp/(2D^(3/2)).

In the denominator of E_z, `2Ep` means `2 * E * p`, not an energy derivative. Their Jacobian is

    det d(E,h)/d(p,z)
       = -M[(p-6)^2-4z]/[4EpD^(5/2)].                       (4)

It is nonzero throughout the stable domain, including z=0,p>6. The p,e Jacobian has an additional factor 2e and is singular at circularity only because e is a square-root coordinate. Equation (4) still vanishes at the separatrix; replacing e by z does not cure the physical loss of adiabatic stability there.

With det denoting (4),

    pdot = (Edot h_z-E_z hdot)/det,
    zdot = (E_p hdot-Edot h_p)/det.                          (5)

An equivalent and more stable implementation near circularity uses

    j_p=(E_p-Omega_phi h_p)/Omega_r,
    j_z=(E_z-Omega_phi h_z)/Omega_r,
    det_action=h_p j_z-h_z j_p=-det/Omega_r,
    pdot=(hdot j_z-h_z jdot)/det_action,
    zdot=(h_p jdot-hdot j_p)/det_action.                     (6)

Both inversions are implemented and compared. Only for e>0 is edot=zdot/(2e) used. The displayed edot=0 at e=0 denotes the regular adiabatic limit proved below, not an evaluation of 0/0.

## 4. Circularity and the small-e law

Transforming the radial action to Darwin anomaly chi gives the cancellation-free integral

    j = M p^(3/2) z/[pi sqrt(D)]
        * integral[0,pi] sin^2(chi) sqrt(p-6-2e cos chi)
          /[(p-2-2e cos chi)(1+e cos chi)^2] dchi.           (7)

It follows that, at fixed p>6,

    j=C(p)z+O(z^2),
    C(p)=M p^(3/2) sqrt(p-6)/[2(p-2)sqrt(p-3)],
    C'/C=3/(2p)+1/[2(p-6)]-1/(p-2)-1/[2(p-3)].            (8)

At circularity, Omega_phi=1/(Mp^(3/2)), Omega_r=Omega_phi sqrt(1-6/p), j_p=0 and j_z=C. Only n=0 remains in the source, so (2) gives jdot=0. Equations (6) then yield

    zdot=0, pdot=hdot/h_p,
    E_p=Omega_phi h_p.                                    (9)

For a smooth source and smooth response away from a pole, the first sidebands have amplitudes O(e), hence powers O(z), and higher sidebands enter at higher powers. Writing

    jdot/mu = R0(p) z + O(z^2),
    pdot/mu = P0(p) + O(z),

equations (6)–(8) imply

    zdot/(mu z) -> [R0-C' P0]/C.                          (10)

Thus edot is O(mu e), not O(mu/e). We test this law with new actual wave calculations at e=.01,.02,.04 and the SAME p=5R/M; these are not the fixed-periapsis family used for the larger eccentricities. Their periapses still lie outside the body. Small-z extrapolation estimates a limit and its sensitivity, not a newly fitted physical parameter or a certified error bound.
