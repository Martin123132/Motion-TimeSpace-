# Collective motion and the leading interaction of the condensate bodies

4 October 2026, Europe/London. Private daughter of the fixed-theory sequence.

## 1. The action and the question

Continue the isolated complex-scalar candidate of the previous stages:

\[
 S=\frac1{16\pi G_R}\int\sqrt{-g}\,R+S_{\rm boundary}
 +\int\sqrt{-g}\left[-\frac12(\nabla f)^2
 -\frac12f^2(\nabla\theta)^2-V(f)\right],
\quad V=\frac{3g}{4}f^{4/3}+\frac\lambda4 f^4.
\]

The sign convention is(-,+,+,+), and c=hbar=1. The fractional potential is
understood as the radial potential of the complex field, not a fractional
power of a negative Cartesian component. The asymptotic vacuum is f=0.
The gravitational boundary term is chosen consistently with the ADM
Hamiltonian and fixed asymptotic lapse/shift. No free surface wall is added.

Einstein gravity and its previously retained G_R=1/(8pi M_R squared) are
inputs here, in the earlier convention M_R squared G_munu=T_munu. The new
derivation concerns the mechanics of our extended field configurations:
which energy supplies their inertia and gravitational coupling, and whether
their leading long-distance force follows from the same action.

The original real-field/bath theory, additional parent sectors, cosmological
background, higher-curvature terms, and the pre-condensate bookmark are not
replaced or silently included. The isolated limit requires those omitted
terms to be small on the scales being matched. This stage adds no coupling
fit and does not identify the global U(1) charge with electric charge.

## 2. A flat-space body can be boosted exactly

Write the stationary rest-frame field as
\(C_0=f(r)e^{i\omega t}/\sqrt2\), with compact support r<=R. Its stress
is stationary even though its phase rotates. Let E be its rest energy and
Q its charge, computed from the full amplitude/phase field.

For a body moving at constant speed beta along x,

\[
 t'=\gamma(t-\beta x),\quad x'=\gamma(x-\beta t),\quad
 r'^2=x'^2+y^2+z^2,\qquad C_\beta=C_0(t',r'),
\quad\gamma=(1-\beta^2)^{-1/2}.
\]

This is the Lorentz-transformed solution, not the ansatz f(|x-z(t)|) with
an unchanged laboratory phase. On a fixed laboratory time slice,
\(d^3x=d^3x'/\gamma\), while \(j^0_\beta=\gamma j^0_0\). Therefore
Q is unchanged. The support is contracted; the complex field and its
vanishing first/second edge derivatives transform together.

For the rest-frame stress, set \(S_{xx}=\int T^{xx}\,d^3x\). Directly
transforming the full field derivatives gives

\[
 E_\beta=\gamma(E+\beta^2S_{xx}),\qquad
 P^x_\beta=\gamma\beta(E+S_{xx}).
\]

The rest solution obeys \(\partial_jT^{ji}=0\). Integrating
\(\partial_j(x^kT^{ji})=T^{ki}\), with no stress at the compact edge,
gives \(\int T^{ki}d^3x=0\). In spherical notation this is also explicit:

\[
 p_r'=-2f'^2/r,\quad p_t=p_r-f'^2,\quad
 (r^3p_r)'=r^2(p_r+2p_t),\quad
 S_{xx}=\frac{4\pi}3\int r^2(p_r+2p_t)dr=0.
\]

Consequently \(E_\beta=\gamma E\), \(P^x_\beta=\gamma\beta E\), and
\(E_\beta^2-\boldsymbol P_\beta^2=E^2\). Internal pressure has not been
neglected; its integral cancels by the field equation and boundary conditions.
This flat-space Laue argument must not be applied to gravitating matter
alone, whose stress is covariantly rather than ordinarily conserved.

## 3. The phase is the important fixed-charge bookkeeping step

The rest-frame field Lagrangian on the stationary solution is

\[
 L_{\rm rest}=\omega Q-E.
\]

It is not -E. Under the exact boost, the scalar Lagrangian density and the
slice Jacobian give \(L_\beta=(\omega Q-E)/\gamma\). The phase at the
body centre rotates at \(\dot\vartheta=\omega/\gamma\).

The physical problem holds Q fixed. Eliminate the cyclic phase by the
fixed-charge Routh transform, not by deleting its contribution:

\[
 \boxed{L_Q=L_\beta-Q\dot\vartheta=-E\sqrt{1-\beta^2}.}
\]

It follows that
\(P_i=\partial L_Q/\partial v_i=\gamma E v_i\) and
\(H_Q=\boldsymbol v\cdot\boldsymbol P-L_Q=\gamma E\).
Using the unreduced action would instead mistake E-omega Q, largely a
surface/grand-potential contribution, for the inertial mass. Our numerical
profiles quantify this incorrect alternative; it is not used as a prediction.

## 4. Include gravitational binding through the ADM boundary

The constrained Einstein–matter canonical action has bulk terms
\(\pi^{ij}\dot\gamma_{ij}+p_f\dot f+\pi_\theta\dot\theta
-N\mathcal H-N^i\mathcal H_i\) and the asymptotic energy boundary term.
For the static spatial geometry and stationary phase, the constraints
vanish, the amplitude momentum and extrinsic curvature vanish, and the
remaining rest-frame Lagrangian is

\[
 L_{\rm on\ shell}=\omega Q-M_{\rm ADM},\qquad
 L_Q=-M_{\rm ADM}.
\]

Thus gravitational binding belongs in the mass. Neither bare charge times
frequency nor proper-volume matter energy is a substitute for ADM energy.
The preceding self-gravity stage independently matched ADM and Komar mass.

For this specific spherical body the exterior also allows an explicit
boosted ADM check. In rest-frame isotropic Cartesian coordinates its exact
vacuum metric is

\[
 ds^2=-\left(\frac{1-a/r'}{1+a/r'}\right)^2dt'^2
 +(1+a/r')^4d\boldsymbol x'^2,\qquad a=G_RM_{\rm ADM}/2.
\]

At leading1/r after a boost, put U=G_R M/r', with
\(r'^2=\gamma^2(x-\beta t)^2+y^2+z^2\). Then

\[
 h_{00}=h_{xx}=2U\gamma^2(1+\beta^2),\quad
 h_{0x}=-4U\gamma^2\beta,\quad h_{yy}=h_{zz}=2U.
\]

On the t=0 laboratory sphere,
\(\partial_jh_{ij}-\partial_i h_{jj}=4G_RM\gamma^2x_i/r'^3\).
With \(K_{ij}=(D_i\beta_j+D_j\beta_i-\dot\gamma_{ij})/(2N)\),
the axial momentum flux has the same angular kernel times beta/2.
The ADM surface integrals reduce to

\[
 E_{\rm ADM}=\frac{M\gamma^2}{4\pi}
 \int\frac{d\Omega}{[1+(\gamma^2-1)\mu^2]^{3/2}}=\gamma M,
 \qquad P_x=\gamma\beta M,
\]

because \(\int_{-1}^1[1+(\gamma^2-1)\mu^2]^{-3/2}d\mu=2/\gamma\).
The transverse momenta vanish by reflection. O(1/r2) metric terms drop out
in the ADM limit, not at a finite integration radius.

`test_adm_boost.py` additionally differentiates the exact boosted exterior
metric, constructs lapse, shift, spatial connection and extrinsic curvature,
and integrates their ADM expressions on successively larger coordinate
spheres. It does not merely insert gamma M into the answer. A finite-radius
estimator is not called an exact quasilocal mass.

## 5. Match the passive coupling, rather than add a force

Use an external metric that varies slowly across the body. In the buffer
region R<<b<<external-variation-scale, match to its isolated solution.
At zeroth derivative order the local external lapse N_ext rescales proper
time. At fixed Q, the on-shell phase and Hamiltonian terms both acquire
that lapse; their Routhian is -N_ext M_ADM.

A moving local solution is fixed by the boost result. Together these match
the coefficient of the leading reparameterization-invariant body action:

\[
 \boxed{S_{\rm body}^{(0)}=-M_{\rm ADM}(Q)\int d\tau_{\rm ext}.}
\]

This is a leading-derivative matching result, not an assumed microscopic
point source. The exterior1/r coefficient fixes the same active mass, the
boost fixes the same inertial mass, and lapse variation fixes the same
passive mass. Charge is constant in the separated-body sector; charge
transfer/contact would require keeping its dynamics instead.

There is no other zero-derivative scalar or long-range field in this
specified isolated vacuum to supply an independent scalar charge coupling.
Curvature, internal response and acceleration-dependent operators are at
higher derivative order; this does not yet bound their coefficients.

Varying the leading action gives \(u^\nu\nabla_\nu u^\mu=0\).
For \(g_{00}=-(1+2\Phi)+O(\Phi^2)\) and slow motion,

\[
 L_Q=-M+\tfrac12M\boldsymbol v^2-M\Phi
 +O(Mv^4,M\Phi v^2,M\Phi^2),\quad
 \boldsymbol a=-\boldsymbol\nabla\Phi.
\]

Self energy is already in M; Phi here is the companion/external field, not
the body's own singular point-field extrapolation.

## 6. Obtain the interaction by eliminating the Einstein field

The Newton potential equation need not be inserted. For a static weak
metric, write N=exp(Phi), gamma_ij=exp(-2Psi)delta_ij. The spatial-curvature
part of the Einstein action is

\[
 N\sqrt\gamma\,{}^{(3)}R
 =e^{\Phi-\Psi}[4\nabla^2\Psi-2(\nabla\Psi)^2].
\]

Expanding to quadratic order and integrating by parts, consistently with
the asymptotic boundary, gives

\[
 L_G^{(2)}=\frac1{8\pi G_R}\int
 [(\nabla\Psi)^2-2\nabla\Phi\cdot\nabla\Psi]d^3x.
\]

Matched slowly moving monopoles contribute -int rho Phi, with
\(\rho=\sum_aM_a\delta^3(\boldsymbol x-\boldsymbol z_a)\).
Variation gives \(\nabla^2\Psi=4\pi G_R\rho\) and
\(\nabla^2(\Phi-\Psi)=0\). Decaying boundary conditions yield Phi=Psi,
and the reduced field action is

\[
 L=\sum_a\tfrac12M_av_a^2-\int\rho\Phi\,d^3x
 -\frac1{8\pi G_R}\int(\nabla\Phi)^2d^3x.
\]

Use \(\nabla^2\Phi=4\pi G_R\rho\) in this action, retaining its field
term. The on-shell potential part is -one-half int rho Phi. Remove the
self terms already matched into each M, not the interaction terms. For two
bodies at separation D,

\[
 \boxed{L_{12}=\frac{G_RM_1M_2}{D},\quad
 U_{12}=-\frac{G_RM_1M_2}{D},\quad
 \boldsymbol a_1=-G_RM_2\frac{\boldsymbol z_1-\boldsymbol z_2}{D^3}.}
\]

Counting each body's external potential without the field action would
double the interaction. No density-fit acceleration law or free force
coefficient is introduced.

In the inherited dimensionless normalization, \(\kappa=G_Rv_*^2
=\alpha/(4\pi)\) and \(u_{12}=-\kappa e_1e_2/D\). The physical
energy scale remains v_star squared/mu_star, and length1/mu_star.

## 7. An extended-source check, including pressure

At first order in G_R, before self-binding corrections, the companion's
vacuum isotropic field is h00=2GM1/d, hij=2GM1 delta_ij/d. Its action coupling
to the unperturbed second field-body is

\[
 L_{12}=G_R M_1\int\frac{\rho_2+p_{r2}+2p_{t2}}
 {|\boldsymbol D+\boldsymbol r|}d^3r.
\]

For D>R1+R2 the angular average of the kernel is1/D. Spherical symmetry and
the Laue identity make the integral exactly E2/D at this order. Direct
quadrature of the extended profiles and the differentiated force kernel
checks this without dropping the local pressure. There is no static
R2/D2 monopole correction from an undeformed spherical source at this order.
This observation does not eliminate induced tides, radiation, or higher-PN
effects, and it does not license applying flat matter stress to a bound
gravitating body's inertia.

The finite-charge profiles are compact, not exponentially decaying. At G=0,
two exactly disjoint supports have an additive local scalar action, independent
of their relative phase or separation until contact. There is therefore no
classical static scalar-tail force to add in this vacuum sector. It would be
incorrect to extrapolate this statement to an ambient nonzero condensate,
overlapping interfaces, arbitrary perturbations of the non-C2 vacuum, or a
different retained parent coupling.

## 8. What remains to control in the body approximation

Require R/D<<1, v<<1 and G_R M/D<<1. Internal modes that couple to the
external forcing must not be excited resonantly. A positive radial gap is
useful but is not the tidal gap: the leading vacuum tide is trace-free and
couples to the quadrupole, ell=2, not directly to a radial ell=0 mode.

The next concrete calculation is the static quadrupole response and its
coupled amplitude/phase/metric equations with the compact boundary. It will
fix the tidal susceptibility from the field model and establish whether
an anomalously soft shape mode spoils the apparent scale separation.
The displacement/translation ell=1 zero must remain a symmetry, not a
spurious instability. We do not fit an arbitrary tidal coefficient now.

The first resolved radial instability from the previous stage is a warning
against using its near-zero-mode body as an adiabatic reference. The scale
example here instead uses the earlier weak R_flat20,Cnom.03 body twice,
