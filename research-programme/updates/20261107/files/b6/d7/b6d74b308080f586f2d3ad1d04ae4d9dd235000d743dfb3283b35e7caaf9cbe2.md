## 1. Variables and the physical target

N=ln(a), H=a'/a is the CONFORMAL expansion rate, omega_j=a*m_j, and prime means conformal-time differentiation. H, omega and m are in Mpc^-1; normalized conformal density R is in Mpc^-2; conformal time is in Mpc. These are not observational H(z) in km/s/Mpc. Scalar fields have the retained gravitational normalization.

For each canonical scalar, v_j=phi_j' and

    phi_j,N = v_j/H
    v_j,N = -2 v_j - omega_j^2 phi_j/H
    rho_j = (v_j^2 + omega_j^2 phi_j^2)/2

## 3. Exact heavy polar equations

Write omega=omega_h, u=H/omega, theta=atan2(-v_h,omega*phi_h), f=rho_h/(3H^2), eta=H_N/H. Then

    rho_h,N = rho_h(-1+3 cos(2theta))
    theta_N = 1/u - 1.5 sin(2theta)
    u_N/u = eta-1
    eta = eta0 + 1.5 f cos(2theta)
    eta0 = (R_external,N + rho_s,N - rho_h)/(6H^2).

Let j=ln[a*rho_h/(a0*rho_h0)]. Its derivative is3 cos(2theta). Define

    A = 1.5(1-eta0), B = (9/8)(2-f)
    g1 = 1.5 u sin(2theta)
    g2 = -(A/2)u^2 cos(2theta) -(B/4)u^2 cos(4theta)
    I2 = j-g1-g2.

Direct differentiation, checked independently by symbolic cancellation, gives EXACTLY

    I2_N = u^2 * [
      (A_N/2 + A(eta-1)) cos(2theta)
      +(B_N/4 + B(eta-1)/2) cos(4theta)
      +1.5 sin(2theta)(A sin(2theta)+B sin(4theta)) ].

There is no imposed dust equation here. The rapidly varying terms were removed by explicit near-identity corrections to the exact scalar energy equation.

## 4. A global drift bound and a closed bootstrap

Positive canonical quadratic energies and the thermal identities imply eta,eta0 in[-2,1], f in[0,1], and u_N<=0. For mu_s=omega_s/H,

    rho_s,NN = 8v_s^2 + 2omega_s^2 phi_s^2
               + 6omega_s^2 phi_s v_s/H
    |rho_s,NN| <= (16+6mu_s)rho_s
    |eta0_N| <=16+3mu_s, |f_N|<=8.

Consequently A<=4.5, B<=2.25, |A_N|<=24+4.5mu_s, |B_N|<=9, and

    |I2_N| <= (41.25+2.25mu_s) u^2
    |g1|<=1.5u, |g2|<=2.8125u^2.

The positive initial CDM+baryon density R_cb0 supplies H^2>=R_cb0*a0/(3a). Therefore a finite target a1 supplies

    mu_s,max = sqrt(3)*m_s*a1^(3/2)/sqrt(a0*R_cb0).

Take a continuity bootstrap |I2-I2(0)|<=delta*=1e-4. This number is an auxiliary mathematical ceiling, NOT a changed numerical acceptance tolerance. With t=N-N0,

    L = |g1(0)+g2(0)| + 1.5u0 + 2.8125u0^2 + delta*
    U_m = [R_external0+rho_h0 exp(L)]/[3(a0*m_h)^2]
    U_s = rho_s0/[3(a0*m_h)^2]
    u^2 <= U_m exp(-3t)+U_s
    delta(t) = (41.25+2.25mu_s,max)*[U_m(1-exp(-3t))/3+U_s*t].

If delta(t1)<delta*, continuity closes the bootstrap. The constants use initial data and the previously frozen target only. There is no fit to the late background. Set

    u_upper=min[u0,sqrt(U_m exp(-3t)+U_s)]
    I20=-g1(0)-g2(0)
    rho_bar=rho_h0 exp(-t+I20)
    b(t)=1.5u_upper+2.8125u_upper^2+delta(t).

Then rho_h lies between rho_bar*exp(-b) and rho_bar*exp(b). The phase-dependent normalization I20 is calculated from the initial scalar state. It is not an empirical dust-density adjustment. At the initial endpoint rho_bar need not equal rho_h0; the explicit tube includes that difference.

## 5. Propagate the uncertainty through the slow field and clock

Use slow energy coordinates Z=(omega_s*phi_s,v_s). With D=diag(1,-2) and J=[[0,1],[-1,0]],

    Z_N = D Z + (omega_s/H) J Z.

Evolve Z_bar with the same equation but

    H_bar^2 = [R_external+rho_bar+|Z_bar|^2/2]/3.

Let e bound |Z-Z_bar|, with e(0)=0, E=|Z_bar|,

    d_h=rho_bar*(exp(b)-1)
    H_lo^2=[R_external+rho_bar exp(-b)+max(E-e,0)^2/2]/3
    C=omega_s/[3 H_lo H_bar (H_lo+H_bar)].

An upper Dini-derivative comparison is

    e_N <= e + C[E*d_h + E^2*e/2 + E*e^2/2].

Derivation: subtract the exact and mean equations. The fast rotation of the error is skew and contributes zero to its squared norm. D has largest eigenvalue1. The source density difference is bounded by d_h+Z_bar dot(error)+e^2/2. In the mixed contribution, orthogonality of Z_bar and J Z_bar gives |(Z_bar dot error)(J Z_bar dot error)|<=E^2 e^2/2. This yields the stated half coefficient rather than an unnecessary full norm-product factor. The case e=0 follows by the upper Dini derivative. Positivity of R_external keeps H_lo positive, and the bound extends by continuation while the comparison solution is finite.

Put d_total=d_h+E*e+e^2/2. Then

    |H-H_bar| <= d_total/[3(H_lo+H_bar)]
    |tau_N-tau_bar,N| <= d_total/[3 H_lo H_bar(H_lo+H_bar)].

Integrate the last expression from zero to bound the clock difference. Cold control has rho_h=d_h=e=0; its slow/clock solve is the same source equation without a heavy averaging correction. Neither model is granted a different tolerance.
