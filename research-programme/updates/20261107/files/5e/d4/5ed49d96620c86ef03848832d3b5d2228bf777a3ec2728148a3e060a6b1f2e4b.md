# Stage85 finite thermal event source

The Stage84 thermal amplitude is now combined with real emission/absorption
and the dressed-state phase space. The result is a conditional forward-event
source for a resting electron and an incoming photon in an isotropic Planck
photon bath. It supplies a calibrated component of the existing MTS matter
source; the full moving-electron gain/loss operator is a separate next step.
The parent action, constants, fitted histories and galaxy work are unchanged.

## 1. Keep the observable and normalization fixed

Use m_e=c=hbar=1. The initial electron is at rest, the incoming photon energy
is E, its direction is z, and the harder outgoing photon is observed at fixed
cosine c. At Born order h0=E/[1+E(1-c)]. Normalize all rows to the elastic Born
differential rate including its final hard Bose factor H0=1+N(h0).
This factor is part of the physical reference rate, not a fitted amplitude.

The nine-component event weight is

    F=(1, Delta k_gamma^0, Delta k_gamma^x, Delta k_gamma^y,
         Delta k_gamma^z, Delta p_e^0, Delta p_e^x, Delta p_e^y, Delta p_e^z).

Its first entry is EVENT COUNT, not photon-number change. For signed second
photon energy q, use the exact Stage83 recoil solution h(q) and

    Delta k_gamma=k_h(q)+q*n_soft-k_in,
    Delta p_e=(sqrt(1+|p_final|^2)-1, p_final).

The electron energy is evaluated independently from its mass shell. Emission
has q>0; absorption has q<0, so the bath photon removed in absorption contributes
negative four-momentum to the photon sector. Both obey Delta k_gamma+Delta p_e=0.

Let R(q) be the signed real-emission differential density, previously checked
against the full spin/polarization sum. The physical absorption density is
-R(-q)>0. Set H(q)=[1+N(h(q))]/H0. Order the two final photons by energy for
emission, with upper q=U, avoiding an extra identical-particle factor. The
incoming seed in absorption is specified; the bath photon is summed, not
averaged or assigned another factor1/2.

## 2. Dressed phase space is not the squared amplitude

The isotropic one-thermal-photon pole shift derived in Stage84 is

    d=delta m^2=2*pi*alpha*T^2/3,
    E_star(p)=sqrt(1+|p|^2+d)+O(alpha^2).

Stage84 normalized external spinors to 2*E_bare at fixed spatial momentum and
included their canonical frequency-pole residues in the amplitude. In that
same convention the outgoing phase-space factor, apart from constants fixed
in the ratio, is

    Phi(d)=h(d)/[E_bare_final(d)*(1-v_star_final(d).n_h)],
    h(d)=E/[1+E(1-c)/sqrt(1+d)].

It follows by eliminating the energy delta function in the canonical spectral
state measure. It must not be combined with a second external Z factor.
With v=(E*c-h0)/Ef and Ef=1+E-h0,

    delta h=d/2*(1-1/Ef)/(1-v),
    delta E_bare_final=-v*delta h,
    delta v=-delta h/Ef-v*delta E_bare_final/Ef-v*d/(2*Ef^2),
    delta ln Phi=delta h/h0-delta E_bare_final/Ef+delta v/(1-v),
    delta ln H0=-N(h0)*delta h/T.

The event weight itself changes by
delta Delta k_gamma=delta h*(1,n_h). The independently differentiated
electron pole energy changes by the opposite amount. These weight derivatives
belong in the energy/momentum source even when a scalar event-rate correction
looks small.

An independent normalization control replaces the photon self-energy by a
constant scalar mass insertion. The canonical spinor, propagator and measure
variations then reproduce the mass derivative of the Klein-Nishina formula
for nine combinations of energy and angle. This check is not a fit or an
assumption that the directional thermal self-energy is a scalar mass.

## 3. Matched finite real and virtual terms

For each bath direction, R(q)=a/q+b+O(q). The finite Stage84 virtual component
removes its full thermal soft term over the whole positive q axis. For an
analytic infrared-safe event weight, the additional real thermal contribution
in precisely that convention is

    I_F = integral dOmega {
      integral_0^U dq R(q)[H(q)-1]F(q)
      + integral_0^infinity dq N(q) [
          1_{q<U} R(q)H(q)F(q)
          - R(-q)H(-q)F(-q) - 2*a*F(0)/q ] }.

The first line is essential: it is the spontaneous real term's changed hard
final-state enhancement relative to the vacuum baseline, not another soft
thermal photon. The second line contains stimulated emission, absorption and
the common subtraction. Omitting any of these changes the result.

If V_T is the finite virtual interference/Born from Stage84, the conditional
thermal correction is

    delta Q_F=[V_T+delta ln Phi+delta ln H0]*F(0)
                 +delta F(0)+I_F.

Adding the unchanged Stage83 vacuum moment and Born term yields the photon-cut
candidate source in this benchmark. The virtual phase comes from the stated
Feynman boundary values; medium-electron and collective corrections are not
implicitly included in this addition. Before using the result as a complete
kinetic coefficient, the thermal cut/gain-loss projection must be completed.

## 4. Why the infrared-safe moments are finite

Write G(q)=R(q)H(q)F(q)=a*F0/q+B+C*q+O(q^2). For 0<q<U,

    G(q)-G(-q)-2*a*F0/q=2*C*q+O(q^3).

Since N(q)=T/q+O(1), the paired integrand has a finite endpoint. Direct samples
show its linear approach; omitting absorption restores an inverse-square
divergence. The omitted integral below a small positive lower limit therefore
vanishes linearly, which is also checked using complete numerical integrals.

Photon number is different: its event increment changes from+1 to-1 under
crossing, rather than approaching a common analytic F0. The corresponding
conditional number integrand is N(q)[R(q)H(q)+R(-q)H(-q)], which can retain
a 1/q term. This source has not been set to zero or absorbed into the virtual
elastic correction. Its full kinetic gain/loss and physical infrared treatment
remain part of photon production, not part of this hard-event cancellation.

## 5. The matching profile cancels

Replace the whole-axis soft subtraction by chi_L(q)=exp[-(q/L)^2]. Because
chi_L=1+O(q^2), its difference from unity gives a finite thermal conversion:

    delta V_L=-2*A integral_0^infinity dq N(q)[1-chi_L(q)]/q,
    A=integral dOmega a.

The real moment changes by -delta V_L*F0, leaving the total unchanged.
Reintegrating with L/T=0.2,1,3 confirms this cancellation. At L/T=0.2 the
conversion is-8.555966726047178e-9, a significant fraction of the final small
answer; omitting it is detected. A profile with a linear departure at zero
would not provide a finite separate conversion in a Bose bath and is not used.

## 7. The collision weak form and reverse reactions

For the real reaction e(p)+gamma0 <-> e(p')+gamma1+gamma2, use the same matrix
element and invariant phase space in both directions. Its statistical weights
for dilute electrons are

    Wf=f(p)*n0*(1+n1)*(1+n2),
    Wr=f(p')*n1*n2*(1+n0).

Their difference, rather than either conditional forward contribution alone,
enters the kinetic source. The inverse process is the same reaction, not an
additional independently normalized copy. Integrating an unordered final
photon pair carries1/2!; restricting to ordered pairs removes that factor.
At common temperature and zero photon chemical potential Wf=Wr eventwise.

For perturbations phi_e=delta f/f and phi_gamma=delta n/[n(1+n)], define

    Delta phi=phi_e(p')+phi_gamma(k1)+phi_gamma(k2)
                 -phi_e(p)-phi_gamma(k0).

Linearizing gives Wf-Wr=-W_eq*Delta phi. Consequently, the weak real collision
form for a test function A is -integral dmu*W_eq*Delta A*Delta phi. Its
entropy quadratic is nonpositive; common four-momentum and electron number
are null directions. Photon number is not a null direction. Direct nonlinear
weight differentiation verifies the linearization, including moving-electron
events with velocities comparable to the saved thermal gas dispersion.

This weak form is the route into the MTS kinetic equations. One must retain
the photon polarization density matrices and add the consistently matched
elastic virtual part before calling it the full projected radiation operator.
The conditional source benchmark does not supply that projection by itself.

## 8. A derived route to the moving-electron bath

In the electron rest frame a Planck bath moving with velocity-v has

    N(k)=1/{exp[gamma*omega*(1+v.n)/T]-1},
    T_direction(n)=T/[gamma*(1+v.n)].

Thus the exact Stage84 radial functions can be evaluated at a direction-
dependent temperature rather than replaced by an effective fitted scalar.
The angular identity <T_direction^2>=T^2 explains why the leading photon-cut
mass-squared shift stays scalar, even though the vertex and spinor dressing
retain directional dependence. Both occupation covariance and this moment
identity are checked explicitly. The actual angular loop average for moving
electrons is the next calculation, together with its boost and relative flux.

The next physical step is to assemble the matched weak kernel with these
moving-electron distributions, preserve its energy/momentum moments, and
project it into the retained finite-wavenumber radiation/gas/metric evolution.
The existing MTS histories and paired baselines remain the destination.
