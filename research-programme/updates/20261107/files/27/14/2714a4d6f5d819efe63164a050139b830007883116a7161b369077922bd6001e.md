## 2 Explicit radiation and matter bookkeeping

Densities below are normalized to today's critical density. We keep
\(H_{0,\mathrm{ref}}=70\ {\rm km\,s^{-1}Mpc^{-1}}\),
the inherited \(\Omega_m=0.30317310859139274\), and the same late zero-Lambda scalar shape at that reference point. Radiation now comes from explicit \(T_{\rm CMB}=2.7255\) K, \(N_{\rm eff}=3.044\), and one massive-neutrino sector with summed mass0.06eV, instead of a single imposed \(\Omega_r=9\times10^{-5}\). The baryon input is \(\omega_b=0.02239\).

For heavy share \(F\), ordinary cold matter is reduced by exactly \(F\Omega_m\):
\[
 \omega_c=(1-F)\Omega_m h_{100}^2-\omega_b-\omega_\nu,\qquad
 \rho_h(a)\longrightarrow F\Omega_m a^{-3}.
\]
Thus baryons and massive neutrinos are not counted twice. We test \(F=0\) and \(F=1/2\), with the same total late matter budget.

CAMB supplies the expansion-independent species densities. Its density interface returns \(8\pi G a^4\rho_i\) in Mpc units; division by \(3(H_0/c)^2a^4\) gives the normalization used here. For the massive-neutrino table \(g_\nu(a)=a^4\rho_\nu(a)\),
\[
 w_\nu(a)=\frac{1-\partial_{\ln a}\ln g_\nu(a)}3.
\]
This reproduces the radiation and nonrelativistic limits and supplies pressure as well as density. The implementation uses the installed and source-hashed version1.6.6, not an unpinned package update. [CAMB density interface](https://camb.readthedocs.io/en/latest/results.html).

## 3 Coupled expansion and the slow canonical mode

Let \(N=\ln a\), \(\chi=\phi_{\rm slow}/(\sqrt6 M_{\rm Pl})\),
\(x=d\chi/dN\), \(E=H/H_0\), and \(\mu=m_{\rm slow}/H_0\).
With standard species summed into \(\rho_{\rm std},p_{\rm std}\),
\[
 E^2=\frac{\rho_{\rm std}+\rho_h+\mu^2\chi^2}{1-x^2},
\]
\[
 \chi_N=x,\qquad
 x_N=-(3+s)x-\frac{\mu^2}{E^2}\chi,\qquad
 s=\frac{-3(\rho_{\rm std}+p_{\rm std})+\rho_{h,N}}{2E^2}-3x^2.
\]
The slow stress is
\[
 \rho_s=E^2x^2+\mu^2\chi^2,\qquad
 p_s=E^2x^2-\mu^2\chi^2.
\]
We shoot the regular radiation-era state \(x_i=-\mu^2\chi_i/(5E_i^2)\) to \(E(1)=1\). This is a flatness constraint, not another fit to distance data.

For the heavy field \(u=\phi_h/(\sqrt6 M_{\rm Pl})\),
\(v=\dot u/M\), \(\nu=M/H_0\),
\[
 u_N=(\nu/E)v,\qquad v_N=-3v-(\nu/E)u,\qquad
 \rho_h=\nu^2(u^2+v^2).
\]
The regular seed is \(v_i=-\nu u_i/(5E_i)\). Its amplitude is shot to the prescribed late action density \(F\Omega_m\), using the same corrected oscillator invariant as Stage51:
\[
 J=\nu^2 a^3\left[u^2+\left(v+\frac{3E}{2\nu}u\right)^2\right].
\]
The heavy integration runs from \(a=10^{-9}\) to \(4\times10^{-4}\). The slow density is frozen only during this early heavy solve; the independently solved slow history changes by less than \(8\times10^{-12}\) over that interval at the reference point. This bounds the early freezing approximation, not the entire nonlinear theory.

## 4 Controlled averaging at the background level

The rapidly oscillating heavy density is smoothly replaced by its late dust density over \(a\in[a_{\rm sw}/2,a_{\rm sw}]\). In \(N\), use the compact quintic
\[
 s(t)=t^3(10-15t+6t^2),\qquad
 \ln\rho_h^{\rm tab}=(1-s)\ln\rho_h^{\rm exact}
                  +s\ln(F\Omega_m a^{-3}).
\]
Here \(s\) denotes the interpolation weight only, not the expansion slope in section3. Its first two derivatives vanish at both endpoints. The tabulated pressure is defined consistently by
\[
 p_h^{\rm tab}=-\rho_h^{\rm tab}
                    -\frac13\partial_N\rho_h^{\rm tab}.
\]
Consequently the numerical background table conserves energy exactly by construction and has no density jump. This interpolation is an asymptotic numerical averaging device, **not a newly derived parent interaction, a claim of pointwise exact scalar pressure, or a perturbation matching prescription**. Its observable sensitivity is measured at \(a_{\rm sw}=0.00015,0.0002,0.00025\). Stage51's field-to-fluid perturbation stress matching remains the owner of that separate operation.

This early matching/late effective-fluid separation is consistent with the motivation of [Passaglia and Hu, 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.10238); the specific quintic background interpolation here is not attributed to their method.

## 5 Background bridge and its prohibition for perturbations

The combined dark table is
\[
 \rho_D=\rho_h^{\rm tab}+\rho_s,\qquad
 w_D=\frac{p_h^{\rm tab}+p_s}{\rho_D}.
\]
Its use in the native background solver supplies \(H(a)\), recombination, baryon drag, and distances. The direct expansion and native expansion are compared throughout the history.

The sum cannot generally be evolved as the existing single fixed-sound-speed fluid. At a moment with zero individual rest-frame momenta, choose
\(\delta\rho_h=A\), \(\delta\rho_s=-A\).
After heavy averaging,
\[
 \delta\rho_D=0,\qquad
 \delta p_D=(c_h^2-1)A,
\]
which is nonzero for generic heavy sound speed. A relative entropy degree of freedom is missing from a one-fluid closure. Even adiabatic initial conditions do not remove the need to evolve the individual stresses as the two components respond differently.

Therefore the \(F=1/2\) combined table is used **only** for background and thermal quantities. No heavy matter power spectrum is generated from CAMB's PPF surrogate. The \(F=0\) native transfer run can use the earlier5194 canonical slow-field fluid with \(c_s^2=1\). Its early numerical floor and an exact-background PPF comparator are tested separately. [CAMB model interface](https://camb.readthedocs.io/en/latest/dark_energy.html), [multi-species perturbation equations](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9506072).

CAMB's standard-component equality label does not count a dust-like component stored in its dark-background slot. That raw label is not interpreted as total physical equality for \(F>0\). The validation output computes the total matter/radiation equality separately, including the heavy contribution and neutrino transition.

The native cold controls share the same empirical primordial amplitude and tilt, A_s=2.1e-9 and n_s=0.9653. There is no separate sigma8 renormalization. Their saved transfer arrays are controls for the forthcoming heavy integration, not heavy predictions.

## 6 Physical ruler and measured distance likelihood

The thermal calculation determines the drag epoch; an independent integral checks
\[
 r_d=\frac{c}{H_0}\int_0^{a_d}
 \frac{da}{a^2 E(a)\sqrt{3[1+3\rho_b/(4\rho_\gamma)]}}.
\]
We then calculate \(D_M/r_d,\ D_H/r_d,\ D_V/r_d\), with
\(D_H=c/H(z)\) and \(D_V^3=zD_HD_M^2\).
The full13-by13 covariance is used in
\[
 \chi^2=(d_{\rm model}-d_{\rm data})^T C^{-1}
                      (d_{\rm model}-d_{\rm data}).
\]
The inherited vector and covariance were verified numerically against the pinned primary repository, commit bb0c1c9009dc76d1391300e169e8df38fd1096db. [DESI DR2 vector](https://github.com/CobayaSampler/bao_data/blob/bb0c1c9009dc76d1391300e169e8df38fd1096db/desi_bao_dr2/desi_gaussian_bao_ALL_GCcomb_mean.txt), [covariance](https://github.com/CobayaSampler/bao_data/blob/bb0c1c9009dc76d1391300e169e8df38fd1096db/desi_bao_dr2/desi_gaussian_bao_ALL_GCcomb_cov.txt).

For diagnosis only, the free inverse-ruler profile is solved analytically:
\[
 \beta_*=\frac{d_{\rm raw}^TC^{-1}d_{\rm data}}
                  {d_{\rm raw}^TC^{-1}d_{\rm raw}},\qquad r_{d,*}=1/\beta_*.
\]
This separates distance-shape compatibility from absolute acoustic calibration. It does not authorize replacing the calculated sound horizon by the fitted value.

All branches also receive the same actual \(H_0\) profile over55–85km/s/Mpc. \(\Omega_m,\omega_b,N_{\rm eff}\), neutrino masses and each branch's physical scalar masses are held fixed. As \(H_0\) changes,
\[
 \mu(H_0)=\mu(70)\frac{70}{H_0},\qquad
 \nu(H_0)=\nu(70)\frac{70}{H_0}.
\]
The flatness and heavy-abundance shots are rerun at each point. Holding the dimensionless mass ratios instead would silently change the action masses and is not done.

These are conditional Gaussian BAO distance tests, not an independent held-out sample or a joint SN/CMB/full-shape analysis. The inherited late parameters already used these distance data. No AIC/BIC or global model preference is inferred from a one-parameter profile.
