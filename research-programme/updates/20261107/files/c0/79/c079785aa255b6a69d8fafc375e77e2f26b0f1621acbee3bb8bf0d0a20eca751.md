# Why the extended coupled history has no new gas pole

This is a conditional matrix lemma for the retained finite-dimensional history
equations, not an assumption about the uncomputed microscopic source.

Let M be symmetric positive definite, chi>0, e=M T, and let
K=rate*(B-D) be symmetric negative semidefinite. The response R to gas in a
positive-alpha implicit history step is

    R=(alpha M-K)^(-1)(-K T),
    denominator=chi*(alpha+1)+alpha*e^T R.

Write S=M^(-1/2)(-K)M^(-1/2), v=M^(1/2)T. Then

    e^T R = v^T (alpha I+S)^(-1) S v >= 0.

Thus the denominator is at least chi*(alpha+1)>0: the gas response cannot
acquire a new pole while these conditions hold. The increment form used by
the saved solver replaces alpha by coefficient/step and multiplies the
denominator by step; the same positivity statement applies. No gas response
coefficient is independently fitted.

For two solutions of the same history equation let dp,dg be their difference,
and d=dp-T*dg. The common affine forcing -1 cancels. Their quadratic energy is

    Q=(dp^T M dp+chi*dg^2)/2,
    dQ/du=d^T K d-chi*dg^2 <= 0.

Q is a stability functional, not the linear physical photon/gas/work energy
ledger. This gives homogeneous stability of the continuation. If candidate and original
operators differ, the equation instead contains an explicit forcing:

    dQ/du=d^T K_candidate d-chi*dg^2
          +d^T (K_candidate-K_original)(p_original-T*g_original).

Therefore the actual repair need not decay monotonically or vanish after the
old interval. Its forcing must be computed, which is why this stage evaluates
the continued microscopic source rather than simply carrying a free boundary
constant to the final epoch.

The lemma is exact under its stated hypotheses. Its application here has
floating-point evidence: Stage199's three Bernstein collision controls establish
numerical negative-semidefiniteness of the quadratic candidate over the enlarged
interval; the saved emission and mass/energy identities are checked at the new
target. Neither sampled emission eigenvalues nor floating-point spectral checks
constitute an interval-rounded continuum certificate. The infrared tail and
original source-history interpolation remain their previously declared inputs.
