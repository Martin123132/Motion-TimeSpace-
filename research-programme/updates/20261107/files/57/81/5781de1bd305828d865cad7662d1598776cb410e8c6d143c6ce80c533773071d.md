# Same-field refinement, motion test and an explicit curvature repair

## What moved forward

The previously balanced nonzero initial field survives doubling its field resolution from 4376 to 8752 unknowns while retaining exactly the same physical P2 profile and all 1094 native interaction points. The motion test then identifies a separate, concrete compatibility condition that the two-parabola interpolation did not satisfy. A higher-order initial field has now been explicitly constructed to repair that condition on the inherited geometry, without changing the native values or first slopes.

All work is local/private. No GitHub actions, subagents, changed fundamental coefficients or observational fits were used. At most two single-core BelowNormal/BLAS1 workers ran at once; all are finished at handoff.

## What the motion test found

The generic off-null second shape derivative was evaluated using the action-derived acceleration and fresh radial solutions along z=z0+s*a, v=sqrt(2*s)*a. This includes the velocity-squared gravitational response. These are derivative probes, not finite trajectories.

The largest sampled corrected second derivatives are -1.3142714788e-4 and -2.2033693178e-4 on the third and fourth grids, at material label -0.4840801198, generator23 (zero-based). Each is stable under the last step reduction to about 1e-9 relative; the metric/map contribution is only of order1e-14. However, the grid difference is about40.4% of the larger coefficient. It would be wrong to call this a converged continuum value or to reject all MTS states from it.

The direct local explanation is a lower-order condition. A common moving field trace must have the same second time derivative on its two sides. The bulk equation therefore requires

    c^2*[q_RR]+(d+a_X)*[q_R]=0.

The old candidate has a maximum residual 0.005339830668 on BOTH grids. Its previously tested initial force/jump conditions do not imply this additional curvature condition. This explains why a bounded Galerkin acceleration can coexist with a poorly behaved slope-acceleration test. It excludes the assumed classical second-time traces for this specific initial interpolation, not weaker evolution or other initial fields.

## Repair actually constructed

Keeping the native values, first slopes and mean curvatures, prescribe the required curvature jumps from that equation. Each native interval gets the unique quintic matching its six endpoint data. The interval containing the distinguished source receives a degree-six endpoint-jet-null lift to enforce its separate q(b)=0 condition.

The saved polynomial coefficients are actual new profiles, tested at11 material labels on both grids:

- Native curvature-corner residual: at most6.15e-65 in64-digit arithmetic. This is an algebraic reconstruction accuracy, not a physical bound.
- Native values and first/second endpoint jets independently replayed at75-digit arithmetic.
- Source value retained below1e-45; inherited-metric source-corner residual below2.68e-11.
- Wave energy increases by at most3.140e-9 fraction, approximately3.14 parts per billion. Independent12/20 quadratures agree.
- Native potential and initial transmission values are preserved on the inherited metric because the native samples and first slopes are retained; the source interval is smooth.

The repair is currently on the inherited metric and source acceleration. Its own gravity and full coupled acceleration have NOT been recomputed. It is not yet an evolved solution or a solution of the second shape-preservation equation.
