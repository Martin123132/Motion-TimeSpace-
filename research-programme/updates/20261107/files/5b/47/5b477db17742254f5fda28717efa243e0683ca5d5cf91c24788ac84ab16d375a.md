# Stage230: one shared expansion rate for matter, photons and the parent fields

Retain Stage229's electron EOS/NR-ion approximation, original bank and the
existing canonical parent fields. This stage couples that source to the
existing Friedmann constraint at each step; it does not derive a new action.
The calculation uses s=ln(a/a_start) and conformal H=a H_physical.

## 1. The scalar root and its derivative

For fixed step histories, alpha>0, beta=alpha+2, energy coordinates
x_j=m_j phi_j, y_j=pi_j/a and Omega_j=a m_j/H give

    [alpha,-Omega; Omega,beta] (x,y)^T = (m history_phi,history_pi/a)^T.

Its symmetric part is diag(alpha,beta)>0. The explicit solution is

    x=(beta b_x+Omega b_y)/(alpha beta+Omega^2),
    y=(alpha b_y-Omega b_x)/(alpha beta+Omega^2).

Differentiate at fixed histories:

    (x,y)_lnH = inverse_matrix * Omega*(-y,x).

The scalar density derivative is sum(x x_lnH+y y_lnH). With the Stage229
thermal solve at r=opacity/H, define

    F(H)=H^2-a^2[rho_reference+rho_fields(H)+chi rho_star(E(H)+U(H))]/3.

The same H enters both field and thermal equations. Initial field variations
are zero only for the named fixed-initial-fields experiment; initial H already
includes gas heat and its electron correction.

The thermal Schur derivative is obtained by solving the same coupled linear
block with differentiated photon forcing. Because the gas first law uses the
same energy row,

    alpha E_lnH+chi[C0(alpha-1)+2]g_lnH=0,
    [chi(E+U)]_lnH = chi^2(C0-2)g_lnH/alpha.

Thus the order-chi energy-exchange response cancels; the remaining direct
thermal density sensitivity is a gas-work term. Neither a zero coupling nor
a fitted suppression factor is inserted. The shared Jacobian is

    J=H F'(H)/(2H^2)
     =1-a^2[rho_fields,lnH+rho_star chi^2(C0-2)g_lnH/alpha]/(6H^2).

It is checked against independent differentiation. Positive J identifies the
regular expanding branch in the tested brackets; do not assert unrestricted
global uniqueness from a few numeric samples.

## 2. Why a regular branch is expected, with an explicit sufficient condition

For the physical symmetric weak collision form, K is nonpositive and the
energy row is t^T M. The thermal block after eliminating the photon energy
derivative has the form G+r S, with S nonnegative and

    G=diag(alpha M,chi gamma), gamma=C0(alpha-1)+2>0.

The right side b=(M vector,history_U-(alpha-1)A0-2) is independent of H.
In G coordinates, (I+r Sbar)^-1 has norm<=1 and its lnH derivative has
norm<=1/4, since lambda/(1+lambda)^2<=1/4 for lambda>=0. Consequently

    |g_lnH| <= ||b||_(G^-1)/(4 sqrt(chi gamma)).

Also ||(x,y)||<=||b_field||/alpha, so a scalar-density derivative is bounded
by ||b_field||^2 Omega/alpha^3. These give a computable sufficient J>0 bound,
not a plateau or an attraction-gap axiom.

For a physical energy-row defect epsilon=||(Erow-t^T M)M^-1/2||, a rank-one
resolvent perturbation has eta=sqrt(alpha/(chi gamma))*epsilon. If eta<1,
the temperature derivative bound becomes
||b||_(G^-1)/[4 sqrt(chi gamma)(1-eta)^2]. This is a theorem under the stated
passive weak-form assumptions, not a certificate for arbitrary corrupted banks.

Stored floating matrices retain their inherited upper-triangle solve convention.
The numeric code differentiates that actual represented solve, including its
thermal column and raw mass RHS. No matrices are edited/clipped to manufacture
the theorem. Analytic physical-operator bounds and binary numerical residuals
remain separate claims; a full interval-arithmetic certificate is not supplied.

## 3. Resolve small metric responses without subtracting two universes

Three branches share one discretization: zero added heat geometry, NR gas,
and the electron-EOS gas. The last two have their own dynamically solved H.
Track NR-minus-zero fields and EOS-minus-NR fields directly.

If H_new=H_base(1+d), then

    Delta Omega=-Omega_base*d/(1+d),
    A_new Delta w=Delta b-Delta Omega J_rotation w_base,
    Delta rho_field=w_base dot Delta w+|Delta w|^2/2.

This is an exact algebraic difference of the two implicit field solves.
Similarly the photon difference has RHS

    M vector_difference+(r_new-r_base)(K_effective B_base-K_raw t*g_base),
    r_new-r_base=-r_base*d/(1+d).

Here K_effective denotes exactly the symmetric upper-triangle representation
used by the unchanged positive-definite solver. K_raw t and M_raw vector
remain the original thermal column/RHS. This avoids an inconsistent derivative
of the implemented matrix while retaining raw physical-equation diagnostics.

The difference Friedmann equation is evaluated as

    H_base^2*d*(2+d)=a^2 Delta rho/3,

not H_new^2-H_base^2. Solve d=scale*x, with known scale chi for NR and
chi[A0(0)-1] for EOS. x is only a numerically rescaled unknown, not a parameter.
The scalar residual is normalized by the same physical scale. Inherited
initial fields, temperatures and photon states are unchanged.
