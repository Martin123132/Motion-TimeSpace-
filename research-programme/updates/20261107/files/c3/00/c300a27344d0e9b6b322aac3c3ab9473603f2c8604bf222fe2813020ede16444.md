# Microscopic log-temperature derivative

Let xi=ln(theta), theta=k_B T/(m_e c^2), at fixed comoving incoming frequency x.
On the native saved background u=ln(a/a_start), dxi/du=-1. For each component
c of the matched collision source, use the unchanged finite quadrature

    S_c(xi) = - sum_n W_n(xi) F_c(P_n(xi), h(xi)).

P contains energy, electron velocity, boost/bath, recoil, Bose factors and the
temperature-dependent limits of the soft integration. h contains the incident
photon functions and gas departure. The event functional is linear in h.
The derivative is therefore

    D S_c = -sum_n W_n [(D ln W_n) F_c
                       + (D_P F_c) D P_n + F_c(P_n, D h)],
    D = d/dxi.

This is a derivative of the microscopic source construction, not of a fitted
temperature polynomial. Sum the finite real-minus-control, virtual and state
terms before assessing the small correction; their cancellations matter.

## Electron and photon temperature factors

For a fixed generalized-Laguerre radial node y, the saved relativistic gas rule
has gamma=1+theta*y, p^2=theta*y*(2+theta*y), beta=p/gamma and normalized weights
w_j proportional to q_j gamma_j sqrt(2+theta*y_j). Direct differentiation gives

    D ln beta_j = 1/[gamma_j(gamma_j+1)],
    a_j = theta*y_j/gamma_j + theta*y_j/[2(2+theta*y_j)],
    D ln w_j = a_j - sum_k w_k a_k.

Thus sum_j D w_j=0; the derivative must include the normalization, not merely
differentiate an unnormalized Boltzmann factor. Angles and comoving seed nodes
are fixed, lab photon energy obeys D epsilon=epsilon, and all recoil/boost
quantities follow through the original context equations.

For occupation n(z)=1/(exp(z)-1), D n=-n(1+n)D z. In a boosted soft mode
z=omega*(bath dot ray)/theta, so

    D ln z = D ln omega + D ln(bath dot ray) - 1.

The hard stimulated factor and the Born differential weight must be
differentiated as well. A bare theta^2 law for the thermal mass shift does not
determine the complete radiative source: vacuum/recoil functions, motion,
occupations, test-function shifts and h all remain.

## Matched subtraction and boundaries

The implemented spontaneous piece for one direction is

    I = 1/2 integral_l^b [rho(omega) P(omega)-a P0/omega] d omega
        + 1/2 a P0 ln(2b).

Differentiate the FINITE combined expression. With R the bracketed integrand,

    D I = 1/2 integral_l^b D R d omega
          + 1/2 R(b) D b - 1/2 R(l) D l
          + 1/2 D(a P0) ln(2b) + 1/2 a P0 D b/b.

The thermal piece also differentiates its Bose factor and every moving panel
boundary. Adjacent artificial panel terms cancel when the same integrand is
used on both sides; emission/absorption thresholds retain their physical
terms. Re-evaluating the original matched quadrature on each nearby temperature
includes these effects; freezing its cutoffs or differentiating only the
unsubtracted real term would not. Differentiability and discretization are
checked numerically, not assumed from the formal expression.

## Evaluating, freezing and testing the derivative

For the matched source evaluated from the equations, not interpolated source
values, use h=.01 and h/2=.005 in xi:

    S'_h = [S(xi+h)-S(xi-h)]/(2h),
    S''_h = [S(xi+h)-2S(xi)+S(xi-h)]/h^2,
    P1(delta) = S(xi) + delta S'_(h/2),
    P2(delta) = P1(delta) + delta^2 S''_(h/2)/2.

The first derivatives and curvature are NUMERIC evaluations of the functional
derivative, not claimed exact analytic numbers. Compare the two differentiation
steps before trusting a prediction. No Richardson convergence order or error
tail is presumed. The independent target is not in either stencil. Freeze
both P1/P2, then evaluate S at the target directly from the microscopic
equations. Source prediction accuracy, differentiation stability and original
moment identities are separate gates.
