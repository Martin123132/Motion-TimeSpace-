## Additional primary source

Sazonov & Sunyaev, Astrophysical Journal543 (2000)28–55,
DOI https://doi.org/10.1086/317078 . Version3:
https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9910280v3 and
https://arxiv.org/html/astro-ph/9910280v3 .

Read sections1.1–1.2, detailed balance in2.1, and section3 equations52–59.
They supply reference kinematics, scattering law and relative-flux convention,
and distinguish a finite redistribution kernel from a smooth-spectrum
expansion. Our angular/frequency quadrature and the reciprocal-pair proof
are implemented independently; no numerical table or fitted coefficient is
borrowed. Archive: `sources/sazonov-sunyaev-9910280v3.html`, hash and acquisition
metadata in `results/source-acquisition/status.json`. Experimental HTML
was read; it was not visually checked against that paper's PDF.

The MNRAS URL https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/490/3/3705/5581514
could not be opened through the web tool. It supplies no equation or result
in this stage. The earlier abstract-only leads astro-ph/0103248 and
astro-ph/9912312 were not used.

## New calculations and reproducibility

`derive_step.py`: symbolic conjugation and coupled/discrete entropy identities.
`spectral_stability.py`: retained accepted/excluded frequency grids.
`evolve_defect.py`: preferred conservative equilibrium-subtracted stepping.
`compare_response.py` and `compare_synthetic.py`: response-level refinements.
`finite_shift.py`: Gaussian characteristic, reciprocal polarized pair identity,
and a deliberately retained failed complete-kernel candidate.
`moving_kernel.py`: initial36 scalar probes; console-only formatting failure
after result was saved. `moving_kernel_checked.py`: successful independent
rerun and100 kinematic/equilibrium-statistics checks; use this entrypoint.

The scalar kernel uses normalized nonrelativistic Maxwell velocities only
to check the leading thermal coefficient. Exact finite-temperature thermal
reciprocity and polarization discretization are the next implementation,
not presumed from positivity or scalar agreement. Established scattering
physics is not a new MTS discovery; it is part of the coupling calculation.
