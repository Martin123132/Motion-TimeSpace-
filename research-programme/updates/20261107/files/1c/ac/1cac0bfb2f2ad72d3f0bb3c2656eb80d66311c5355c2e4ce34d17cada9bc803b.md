## Exact finite-window derivation

Differentiating W with the two evolution equations gives

```text
W' = -2 H W + 2 P_s - k^2 phi d - k z phi v + E.
```

Since phi d=(D_s-P_s)/(2 omega^2), rearrangement yields

```text
c = k^2 / (4 omega^2 + k^2)
b = 2 omega^2 / (4 omega^2 + k^2) = (1-c)/2

P_s = c D_s + b [W' + 2 H W + k z phi v - E]
b' = 4 H omega^2 k^2 / (4 omega^2 + k^2)^2.
```

c is a derived algebraic factor. Calling it an effective sound speed would additionally require controlling every remaining term; that step is not smuggled into the identity.

Let alpha=sigma/k be the saved gauge generator. The previously retained Newtonian-gauge convention is

```text
D_N = D_s + G_D,       G_D = -3 H v^2 alpha
P_N = P_s + G_P,       G_P = alpha [v v' - H v^2 - omega^2 phi phi'].
```

The exact dense-background density transformation would also have alpha(v e_v+omega^2 phi e_phi). We retain the existing canonical density convention and measure that residual separately; its windowed amplitude is below 5e-13 of the density norm. The pressure conversion uses actual saved background derivatives, as in the preceding stage.

For a fixed finite interval [tau_-,tau_+], with Delta tau=tau_+-tau_- and angle brackets denoting its uniform d tau average, integration by parts gives

```text

      + [b W]_-^+ / Delta tau
```

These six terms are respectively gradient-density, endpoint, expansion, metric forcing, dense-background defect, and gauge correction. Products are averaged as products; neither c nor H is silently pulled outside the window.
