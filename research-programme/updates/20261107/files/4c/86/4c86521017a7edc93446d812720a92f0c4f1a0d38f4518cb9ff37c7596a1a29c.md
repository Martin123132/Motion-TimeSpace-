# Temperature dependence from the collision and emission equations

## Exact inherited finite-basis equation

Use u=ln(a/a_start), theta=k_B T/(m_e c^2), and fixed comoving frequency
x=h nu/(k_B T). Along this saved background d ln(theta)/du=-1.
M is the photon mass matrix, t the temperature vector, p the photon
coefficients, g the scaled gas temperature, and chi the fixed heat ratio.

    M p_u = r [L(theta)(p-t g) + J_p(u)]
    chi(g_u+g) = -r t^T L(theta)(p-t g) + r J_g(u) - b
    L = K_Compton - D_DC - D_BR

b=1 for the baseline and baseline+MTS; b=0 for the MTS increment. The
current numerical-source contract has t^T J_p=J_E and J_g=-J_E.
The independent raw source failures do not become physical conservation proofs
when this existing numerical representation is used.

Consequently the temperature law is exactly

    d p/d ln(theta) = -r M^-1 [L(theta)(p-t g)+J_p]
    chi d g/d ln(theta) = chi g + r t^T L(theta)(p-t g)-r J_g+b.

This changes the independent variable, not any interaction or parameter.
It is driven, nonautonomous and retains gas/radiation memory.

For a fixed spectral readout V and fixed temperature/mu/y projector Q, the
remaining shape R=Q V p obeys

    dR/d ln(theta) = -r Q V M^-1 [L(theta)(p-t g)+J_p].

Even knowing R at one temperature does not determine its derivative: adding a
resolved temperature, mu or y component, or changing g, can leave R unchanged
while changing its future drive. Thus a scalar power R proportional to
theta^n cannot be inferred merely from the low-frequency relaxation rate.

## What temperature scaling really is derived

The inherited nonlocal-leading DC matrix scales exactly as theta^2 times its
fixed matrix. In the soft limit Gamma_DC=C_DC theta^2/x^2.
The inherited free-free formula has n_H proportional to theta^3 and its
prefactor proportional to theta^(-7/2). At fixed comoving x,

    Gamma_BR(theta,x) = C_BR theta^(-1/2) G(theta,x)
                       (1-exp(-x))/x^3,

where G is the H/He Gaunt combination evaluated at gamma_Z^2 proportional
to Z^2/theta. Therefore

    d ln Gamma_BR/d ln theta = -1/2 + d ln G/d ln theta.

The Gaunt dependence is retained, not replaced by a fitted exponent.
The soft nonrelativistic Compton limit is proportional to theta, but the
prediction uses the full saved finite-frequency operator instead.
All these rates also acquire the unchanged common r(theta) in u-time.

For f=Phi/x the earlier derived soft equation is

    f_u = r [M_soft f + (lambda1-Gamma)f
             + Gamma_DC beta + Gamma_BR g - lambda1 g + J_soft],

with hard-photon slope beta, Gamma=Gamma_DC+Gamma_BR and the applicable
source representation J_soft. Replacing d/du by -d/dln(theta) supplies its
temperature law. DC and BR have different temperature powers and different
drivers. Freezing the rates, discarding J_soft or dropping the hard/gas
history is an extra approximation, not a consequence of low frequency.
The forecast below uses the finite-basis equation rather than extending this
soft approximation across the whole x<1 band.

## Stable coupled realization

Let y contain the independent radiation energy E and the 95 nonpivot photon
coefficients, so p=T y. Let H=E+chi g, hence g=(H-E)/chi. Define
C=T_test L T and d=T_test L t, with the same inherited mass coordinates.

    M_y y_u = r [C y - d(H-E)/chi + J_y]
    H_u = E-H + r(J_E+J_g) - b.

This follows directly by adding the two original energy equations.
In the closed numerical reference H_u=E-H-b. Integrate expansion work
W_u=chi g=H-E, so H+W+b(u-u0) stays constant. This explicit balance is
checked independently of the integration scheme.

The independent numerical scheme is two-stage, third-order, L-stable Radau IIA:

    c=(1/3,1),  A=[[5/12,-1/12],[3/4,1/4]],  weights=(3/4,1/4).

Each stage evaluates the existing nonautonomous coefficients and fixed source
curve at its own temperature. The two stage equations are solved together;
their last stage is the new state. No coefficients are fitted to target data.

input manifest are written. The source curve is NOT withheld: this is a

In particular, transport cannot by itself derive the microscopic temperature
dependence of J_p. Symbolically J=DeltaK(theta)h(theta) has

    dJ/dln(theta) = (dDeltaK/dln(theta))h
                   + DeltaK dh/dln(theta).

The first term needs the finite real/virtual/state collision kernel, with its
matching subtraction. DC/BR damping powers alone do not determine it.
The current stage derives and tests the evolution part without substituting a
temperature polynomial for that missing kernel derivative.
