The first k0 construction fails its unchanged inverse-weighted
material48/64 gate on reference fine: 1.859918954e-4 versus 2e-11.
The forcing scale is about 3.10837 in that inverse norm. The two
original-momentum derivative routes agree to about 1.24e-15.

The decomposition diagnoses this, rather than merely raising order:
the metric-coefficient force contributes 1.859896918e-4 of the
inverse discrepancy. Removing P does not remove it. The P-only
discrepancy is about 9.42e-15. Thus the dominant sensitivity is the
ADVECTED BACKGROUND coefficient alpha_R U / beta_R U, not the new
position metric solution. Increasing the global label rule to 96
and 128 does not settle it; increasing spatial order 12 to 20 changes
the metric term only at about 1.14e-14 in the inverse norm.

The background derivatives vary rapidly across the thin source region.
A fixed global label quadrature does not resolve the resulting moving
cell intersections adequately. The corrective test interchanges the
integrations, rather than changing the action or the acceptance gate:

    integral dlabel w integral dxi J f
      = integral dR integral_{active labels} dlabel w f.

At every physical radius, split the label integration at the inherited
inverse images of EVERY material cell edge. Use Gauss-interior radii
on the owned radial cells. Apply the already derived material
derivative before changing variables. In particular, for the old field
force the varying J and inverse physical test-gradient Jacobian cancel;
for the momentum rate retain (delta J/J)*p_t.

The test pairs are radial24/label28 and radial32/label36 for FIELD
covectors. Source covectors and the native contribution retain their
material48/64 comparison. No source/field baseline is subtracted.

and rational cell widths, avoiding cancellation-prone nodal gradient
evaluation in very small cells. Both momentum derivative routes and
the whole corrected Euler residual remain required.

physical repair, a changed model, or permission to relax 2e-11. The
first failed attempt and its full 48/64 arrays remain immutable;
