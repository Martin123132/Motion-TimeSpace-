## 1. Current and shear responses

Use the already qualified spatial branch, with N=ln(a), conformal Hubble H,
and the original normalized physical output ell e(T):

    M = 3 H Q_g/(2 k^3),   S = Pi_g/k^2,
    L = 1 + 3 W_q/(2k^2),  g = 3 H W/(2k^2),
    Y = -3(H+g)/(k L),     J = -M,
    d = 3 H b_z/(k L),     C = lambda^T d,
    G = lambda^T(b_alpha + Y b_z).

The coupled adjoint satisfies lambda_N=-A_n^T lambda. Therefore

    C_N = lambda^T(d_N - A_n d),
    ell e(T) = -[C M] + integral((G+C_N) M + G S) dN.

This retains the initial endpoint and the full coupled adjoint, with no
assumption that the phase, adjoint or source is slowly varying.

Define K_M=G+C_N and K_S=G. Holder and triangle inequalities give

    |ell e(T)| <= E_M ||M||_infinity + ||K_M||_1 ||M||_infinity
                 + ||K_S||_1 ||S||_infinity,
    E_M = |C(N_initial)| + |C(T)|.

This is a sufficient finite-interval criterion for each physical readout.
A family has a controlled limit if the displayed products tend to zero
with controlled initial preparation/readout. Small H/k alone does not prove
that: the source Q/k^2 and the response gains must also be controlled.
This turn evaluates all three terms separately, without an unknown
candidate-minus-GR density history as an input.

## 2. Exact cancellation in the heavy current-response direction

Let B=a(sqrt(omega) delta_phi+i delta_phi_prime/sqrt(omega)) exp(i theta)
be the existing scalar amplitude, omega=a m, mu=theta_N, and
gamma=k^2/(2 omega H). The retained driver gives exactly

    B_N = i(mu-omega/H-gamma) B
          + (3/2-i gamma) exp(2i theta) B*
          + b_B z,
    b_B = -i a k v exp(i theta)/(H sqrt(omega)).

Here v is the real background conformal field velocity; z is the existing
linear metric-expansion functional of the full perturbation state.

In unnormalized physical amplitude coordinates the primitive direction is

    c_d = -3i a exp(i theta)/(L sqrt(omega)),
    d_B = c_d v,
    (d_B)_N = c_d [v_N + (1/2+i mu-L_N/L) v].

Because v,L,omega,a are real, exp(2i theta) d_B*=-d_B. The internal
oscillator action on this direction consequently reduces to

    A_internal d_B = [i(mu-omega/H)-3/2] d_B.

Thus the heavy component of the current-response vector
h=b_alpha+Y b_z+d_N-A_n d is

    h_B = c_d [v_N + (2+i omega/H-L_N/L) v]
          + b_B [Y-z_n[d]].

Both the arbitrary carrier derivative mu and the EXPLICIT gamma cancel.
Physical omega/H, v_N and the full state-dependent metric functional z_n[d]
remain. Gradient dependence still enters the physical frequency and metric
couplings; it has not been erased from the theory.

Symbolic identities and97 native off-node evaluations at each of the three
retained k values check this expression. This is not inferred by fitting
the terminal errors or by averaging over the scalar phase.

## 3. A frequency-uniform bound for the homogeneous scalar block

The cancellation above alone does not control the adjoint response. There
is, however, a positive energy norm derived directly from this same scalar
equation. Assume the constant positive scalar mass of the retained branch,
fixed comoving k, and an expanding background.

Remove the arbitrary carrier: xi=exp(-i theta) B. Its real two-vector obeys,
before the metric source,

    (Re xi, Im xi)_N =
        [[3/2, omega/H],[-omega/H-2gamma,-3/2]] (Re xi, Im xi).

Set

    Omega = sqrt(omega^2+k^2),
    q = k/omega,
    r = sqrt(Omega/omega) = (1+q^2)^(1/4),
    T = diag(r,1/r),
    r_N/r = -q^2/[2(1+q^2)].

This is a determinant-one change of amplitude coordinates. With
u=T(Re xi,Im xi), direct transformation, INCLUDING T_N T^-1, gives

    u_N = [[h,Omega/H],[-Omega/H,-h]] u + f,
    h = 1 + omega^2/(2 Omega^2),       1 <= h <= 3/2.

Equivalently W=a(sqrt(Omega) delta_phi+i delta_phi_prime/sqrt(Omega))
exp(i theta). Its positive norm has

    |W|^2 = a^2[Omega delta_phi^2 + delta_phi_prime^2/Omega].

The frequency matrix is skew. Restoring the arbitrary carrier adds only a
skew rotation, so neither mu nor Omega/H enters the symmetric growth rate.
For the upper right derivative of the norm, including at W=0,

    D+|W| <= h |W| + F |z|,
    F = a k |v|/(H sqrt(Omega)).

Consequently

    |W(N)| <= exp(integral h)|W(N_initial)|
              + integral exp(integral_s^N h) F(s)|z(s)| ds.

In particular the homogeneous gain is bounded by exp(3 Delta_N/2). This
is an analytic bound, not an estimate fitted to our three k values.
It retains the physical gradient through Omega and the forcing through F.

The transformation's condition number is Omega/omega. It is not uniformly
bounded for arbitrarily large k at fixed omega. It IS controlled on any
specified family with bounded k/omega. Physical observables must be mapped
back; a better norm is not permission to change their normalization.

## 4. What is now controlled, and the coupled step

The scalar homogeneous block no longer needs a pessimistic growth factor
proportional to its rapid oscillation frequency. That removes one genuine
obstacle in bounding the response.

But z is generated by the matter/gravity state, not a prescribed independent
signal. To close the full estimate, use the retained constraint to write
z=z_W[u]+z_rest[r_rest], include the feedback coefficient multiplying u, and
bound the remaining matter block in a compatible physical norm. A block
comparison inequality must keep these off-diagonal couplings.

The next derivation is that coupled feedback estimate, using the existing

closure, free cutoff, exponent fit, or broad wavenumber sweep is needed to
start it. The scalar energy result above is not a statement that the whole
