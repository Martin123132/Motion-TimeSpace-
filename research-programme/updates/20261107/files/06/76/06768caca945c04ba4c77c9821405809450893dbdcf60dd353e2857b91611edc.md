# Exact canonical rotating-amplitude derivation

This stage retains the existing MTS canonical two-field/Einstein system. Let H=a'/a, omega=a*m, phi'=v and d'=u, with d=delta phi. The actual scalar perturbation equation is u'=-2H u-(omega^2+k^2)d-k z v. Dense-background departures are recorded as e_phi=phi'-v and e_v=v'+2H v+omega^2 phi.

Define an arbitrary differentiable real carrier phase theta and complex variables

```text
A = a (sqrt(omega) phi + i v/sqrt(omega)) exp(i theta)
B = a (sqrt(omega) d   + i u/sqrt(omega)) exp(i theta).
```

These are invertible coordinate changes, not extra fields. For example d=Re[B exp(-i theta)]/(a sqrt(omega)) and u=sqrt(omega)Im[B exp(-i theta)]/a. No H' or second derivative of the dense background is needed.

Direct differentiation, using omega'=H omega, gives

```text
A' = (3H/2) exp(2i theta) A*
   + a exp(i theta) [sqrt(omega)e_phi+i e_v/sqrt(omega)]
   + i(theta'-omega) A

B' = (3H/2) exp(2i theta) B*
   - i k^2/(2omega) [B+exp(2i theta)B*]
   - i a k z v exp(i theta)/sqrt(omega)
   + i(theta'-omega) B.
```

The final phase-defect term is retained even when theta is chosen by integrating a background interpolation of omega/H over N=ln a. Thus interpolation of the carrier does not silently change the physical equation.

Using A-A*exp(2i theta)=2i a v exp(i theta)/sqrt(omega), the perturbation equation is equivalently

```text
g = k^2/(2omega)

B' = -i g B - (k z/2) A
   + exp(2i theta) [(3H/2-i g)B* + (k z/2)A*]
   + i(theta'-omega)B.
```

This explicitly identifies the slow gradient/metric terms and the counter-rotating terms. The present computation keeps them ALL. Setting the oscillatory bracket to zero is a further approximation requiring an error bound, especially because z itself is generated by the coupled matter/radiation/metric system.

The native canonical stress and the prior endpoint quantity follow exactly:

```text
D_s = omega/a^2 Re(A B*)
P_s = -omega/a^2 Re(A B exp(-2i theta))
Q_s = k/(2a^2) [Im(A B exp(-2i theta))+Im(A B*)]
W   = 1/a^2 Im(A B exp(-2i theta)).
```

Consequently a FORWARD B evolution determines the finite-window endpoint W and pressure without importing future perturbation endpoints. The background A is supplied by the already validated background solution, as usual for linear perturbations; it is not a future perturbation forcing function.
