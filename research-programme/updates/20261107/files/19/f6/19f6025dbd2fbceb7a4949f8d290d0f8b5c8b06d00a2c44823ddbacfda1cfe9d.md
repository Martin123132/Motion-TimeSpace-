# Stage87: joint reaction source and derived thermal population correction

This continues the same MTS radiation/gas parent. Stage86 supplied a
conditional moving polarized response; this stage derives a weak kinetic
gain-minus-loss form, integrates real redistribution over the saved thermal
population and spectrum, and replaces the previously imported rounded
soft-population correction with a calculated coefficient. It does not

## 1. Joint polarized weak form

For a real reaction e(p)+gamma0 <-> e(p')+gamma1+gamma2, the spin-resolved
amplitude T maps the incident polarization space to the two-photon product
space. For a test observable A define

    A_in = A0 + a_e(p) identity,
    A_out = A1 tensor identity + identity tensor A2 + a_e(p') identity,
    D_A = A_out T - T A_in.

Photon equilibrium occupation is n_i identity; write its linear departure
as n_i(1+n_i) Phi_i. For the dilute electron population write delta f=f phi_e.
At common temperature, W=f_p n0(1+n1)(1+n2)=f_p' (1+n0)n1n2.

The nonlinear matrix occupation weak expression contains

    (f_p/4) Re sum Tr[D_A N0 T_dagger ((1+N1) tensor (1+N2))]
      -(f_p'/4) Re sum Tr[D_A (1+N0) T_dagger (N1 tensor N2)].

The factor1/4 is the spin/incident-polarization convention used by the
retained unpolarized amplitude. Linearizing the matrix factors, even when
the perturbations do not commute with the test observables, gives

    delta C[A] = -(W/4) Re sum Tr[D_A D_Phi_dagger].

The inherited approximation here is dilute, weak-coupling kinetic evolution
with factorized asymptotic occupations and unpolarized electrons; electron
Pauli blocking is neglected in that same dilute limit. The products above
are not a claim that an arbitrary interacting quantum state factorizes.

Consequently common four-momentum is a null by event kinematics, the entropy
quadratic form is nonpositive and scalar observables recover
-W |T|^2_average Delta A Delta phi. A photon-number observable gives
Delta A=1, not0. Both emitted photons and the removed seed are included.
The direct nonlinear matrix-occupation derivative is checked in
`verify_joint_weak_form.py`; conservation is not enforced by a posteriori
projection. This derivation uses the real tree reaction and its adjoint;
it does not replace a finite-loop kinetic cut analysis by conjugating an
absorptive one-loop amplitude.

## 3. Resolved finite-frequency real redistribution

The on-shell real amplitude is batched without changing its six diagrams.
Use incoming rest energy E, outgoing energies h and q, and
D=1+E a01-q a12. Its spin/polarization-averaged intensity X has rest-frame
differential cross section

    d sigma_DC / sigma_Thomson
      = [3 alpha/(128 pi^3)] X h q/(E D) dq dOmega_h dOmega_q.

Lab relative flux is 1-v.n_in. The cross section follows from the retained
amplitude normalization and the invariant phase measure; it is independently
checked by its Lightman-Thorne soft limit. The extra photon's LAB energy is
ordered below the outgoing harder photon, removing the identical-final-
photon factor without double counting. All three photons stay inside the
resolved band x_min <= x <=60 for the real block computed here.

For each event form the photon test functions 1, x, x^2/(1+x),
x^3/(1+x)^2 and the gas energy test. Their changes d_i produce

    K_ij = - integral dP_e [x0^2 dx0/I3] n0 (1+n1)(1+n2)
                  (1-v.n_in) (d sigma_DC/sigma_Thomson) d_i d_j,

    source_i/chi = - integral same_weight d_i
       [B(x1)+B(x2)-B(x0)+b*(gamma'-gamma)/theta].

I3=pi^4/15, and time is Thomson optical time d_tau=n_e sigma_T c dt.
The moment normalization is reference photon energy density; the number
row is correspondingly T times number change divided by that energy density.
The weak matrix is symmetric negative semidefinite. Its common-temperature
null vector is (0,1,0,0,1); this does not make photon number conserved.
The gas heat readout is the opposite photon energy readout. The small matrix
is a diagnostic projection of the exact real reaction, not a substitute for
all96 modes or a new full time evolution.

## 4. Symmetric reaction representation and virtual matching

The same real Dirichlet block can be written as one half the sum of ordered
emission and ordered absorption. Absorption must order its two INPUT photons:
the tagged seed has lab energy at least that of the other incoming photon.
Otherwise integrating a tagged absorption map over all seeds counts reactions
twice. This distinction is unnecessary for a conditional tagged experiment
but mandatory for a population collision integral.

At fixed seed and directions, emission ends where h_lab=q_lab; absorption
ends at q_lab=E_seed_lab. The finite reference is subtracted on the same
whole radial axis as the virtual term, not at whichever arbitrary event
threshold is most convenient. For infrared-regular potentials Phi(q)=O(q),
the paired weak soft density factors into

    (1/2) W_elastic [(1+2N(q))*a_Omega/q] D_A_elastic D_Phi_elastic_dagger.

This is the coefficient to cancel against the corresponding virtual soft
term. For a photon chemical potential Phi(q)->constant, an additional real
number-production term survives. It is not canceled by an elastic
counterterm, and must not be projected out. `verify_weak_soft_structure.py`
checks these different limits on the actual amplitude.

The finite resolved real heat/redistribution block can depend strongly on
x_min because it still contains its elastic-like soft component. It must not
be added wholesale on top of the existing Compton source before the virtual
weak correction and matching conversion are assembled. The real block and
the independently derived soft-production coefficient have different roles.

## 5. Derivation of the old14.16 coefficient

Let n(x)=1/(exp(x)-1), H=1+n, w=nH, and I4=integral x^4 w dx=4pi^4/15.
Integrating the exact soft current over the second-photon direction gives

    a_integrated = (2 alpha/pi)[atanh(beta_recoil)/beta_recoil-1].

This multiplies the ordinary rest-frame Compton cross section. In the
chemical-mode leading pole the lab cutoff and directional soft temperature
cancel: integral N(q,T_direction) dq/q has leading1/x_min. Thus the
coefficient is a gas/seed/hard-angle average of the Compton cross section,
hard Bose factor and this integrated current. `soft_population.py` calculates
that integral, including exact recoil and thermal velocities, rather than
using a rational approximation for it.

To derive its first temperature correction, put a=1-c. Thomson angular
weight is (1+c^2)a, and the ratio of its next a-moment to its normalization is
(56/15)/(8/3)=7/5. At rest, cross-section recoil times soft current gives
1-3 theta*x*a; the final-hard Bose factor adds -theta*x^2*a*H'(x).

For the moving gas, the product of incident relative flux and squared rest
energy is gamma^2(1-v_z)^3. Its mean is1+6theta to this order. The outgoing
lab frequency obeys, after azimuth and isotropic velocity averaging,

    mean(delta y/x at second order)=3theta*a,
    mean((delta y/x at first order)^2)=2theta*a,
    mean(-3v_z*delta y/x at first order)=3theta*a.

Combining motion and stimulation with the recoil contribution gives

    g(theta)=1+theta*[6+(7/5)/I4 * integral x^4 n
                     ((6x-x^2)H' + x^2 H'' -3xH) dx]+O(theta^2).

Since H'=-w and H''=w(1+2n), the integral is

    -3 integral x^5 w(1+2n) dx +2 integral x^6 w^2 dx.

Integration by parts gives integral x^5 w(1+2n)=5I4. The identity
w''=w+6w^2 gives integral x^6 w^2=5I4-I6/6, where
I6/I4=20pi^2/7. Therefore

    g(theta)=1-[1+4pi^2/3]*theta+O(theta^2),
    1+4pi^2/3=14.1594725347858...

The existing14.16 is this coefficient rounded to two decimal places. It is
not an MTS fit parameter. The old inverse1/(1+14.16theta) reproduces the first
order but is not derived to all orders by this argument. The population
integral provides its replacement across the retained temperature history.
Alpha and the electron mass remain inherited physical inputs.

The separate pieces can also be integrated: recoil plus hard stimulation
for resting electrons contributes -2pi^2*theta; thermal motion including
hard stimulation contributes +(2pi^2/3-1)*theta. Their sum is the expression
above. Thus neither simply keeping electrons at rest nor multiplying by
the unstimulated1+6theta enhancement recovers the required coefficient.

## 6. Fixed-density quasiparticle population derivative

Stage86 derived the fixed-p kernel chain rule. Averaging that kernel at NLO
also requires the population to move when the thermal mass shell changes.
For a fixed electron number density,

    f_m(p) proportional exp[-sqrt(p^2+m^2)/T],
    Z(m)=4pi m^2 T K2(m/T),
    d ln f/d(m^2) at m=1 = [K1(1/T)/K2(1/T)-1/gamma]/(2T).

The subtraction is fixed by normalization, not fitted. Multiplying this score
by d=2pi alpha T^2/3 supplies the population contribution to a Born-source
average, in addition to the kernel variation. Its mean is zero. Direct
differentiation of the normalized massive population checks the expression.
For example, omitting it gives the wrong derivative of mean squared speed.
For the real DC tree block this extra correction is of higher radiative order;
it belongs in the finite virtual/elastic NLO population assembly.
