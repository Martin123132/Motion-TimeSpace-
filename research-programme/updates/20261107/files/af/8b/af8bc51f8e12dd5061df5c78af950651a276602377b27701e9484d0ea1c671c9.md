# Stage109 — standard mu/y shapes with an explicit remainder

## Physical definitions, derived before reading amplitudes

Retain x=h nu/(k_B T_star), n_P=1/(exp(x)-1), w=n_P(1+n_P),
delta n=chi w Phi. The numerical vector represents Phi, not occupation.
Temperature potential is g=x. The same inner product as Stage108 is

    <f,h>_D = integral_D x^2 w f h dx/I3, I3=pi^4/15.

The saved epoch is a=1e-5, not today.

A Bose–Einstein occupation at small chemical potential has
delta n=w(x epsilon-mu). Fixing its photon number to first order gives
epsilon=mu/beta, beta=3 zeta(3)/zeta(2), because
integral x^2 w dx=2 zeta(2) and integral x^3 w dx=6 zeta(3).
Thus the standard number-preserving mu potential is

    m0(x)=x/beta-1.

For a small Compton-y distortion of a Planck spectrum,
Y_n=x^-2 d_x[x^4 d_x n_P]. Since n_P'=-w and
w'=-w coth(x/2), its potential is

    q0(x)=Y_n/w=x[x coth(x/2)-4].

This derives TEMPLATE NORMALIZATIONS, not a new microscopic source,
a kinetic-equilibrium assertion, or the approximation used by the parent
finite-frequency collision operator.

## Energy matching and two-template projection

Define C=<g,g> and h_j=<g,s0_j>/C for s0=(m0,q0). Use

    s_j=s0_j-h_j g, <g,s_j>=0.
    eta=E/C, d=Phi-eta g.
    G_ij=<s_i,s_j>, v_i=<s_i,d>.
    a=G^-1 v, R=d-a_mu s_mu-a_y s_y.

Use normalized weighted columns and QR (checked against SVD), rather
than a poorly conditioned normal-equation solve. Then

    <s_i,R>=0
    ||d||^2=||a_mu s_mu+a_y s_y||^2+||R||^2.

The two templates are NOT mutually orthogonal. Their separate squared
norms cannot be added as percentages. Report joint captured squared norm
and residual norm fraction, plus signed amplitudes and correlation.

All fields are retained through (eta,a_mu,a_y,R). The residual is a
sampled function plus the original source vector, so no assumption that
analytic q0 lies exactly in the96-mode space is needed.

For the primary full band use the inherited700-node inner product.
The independent saved energy may differ from its spectral dot product
at floating-point cancellation level. Carry it rather than overwriting
it; test the resulting energy residual against explicit input-size
roundoff allowances. Restricted-band diagnostics recompute energy
on their own band; they do not silently reuse the full-band E.

In the entire Planck domain <g,g>=4, <1,g>=6 zeta(3)/I3,
and <g,q0>=4. Thus energy-matched shapes reduce to

    s_mu = -1 + x [6 zeta(3)/(4 I3)]
    s_y  = x[x coth(x/2)-5].

Actual readouts use the finite-domain moments, not these limits blindly.

## Physical amplitudes and temperature conventions

    mu_physical=chi a_mu
    y_physical=chi a_y
    epsilon_energy=chi eta
    epsilon_standard=chi(eta-h_mu a_mu-h_y a_y).

The last temperature accompanies the STANDARD number-preserving mu/y
templates. It need not equal the energy-matched temperature. In particular
energy in standard mu/y shapes can cancel their accompanying temperature
piece even when the total energy-matched temperature is extremely small.
Changing convention does not change the spectrum. Signed increments
do not by themselves imply a thermodynamic equilibrium or a new particle.

At each sampled frequency,

    Phi=g(eta-h.a)+a_mu m0+a_y q0+R
    delta I_nu=(2 h nu^3/c^2) chi w Phi.

All96-mode original vectors remain saved. The2-template fit is a fixed
linear shape decomposition, not an empirical fit of the MTS action or
an instrument-noise-weighted likelihood.

## Symmetric comparison and uncertainty

Apply IDENTICAL templates, metric, bounds, quadrature and projection to
the window correction and the existing saved parent baseline. The
baseline is the same earlier photon/gas calculation, not a separately
fitted LCDM spectrum, observed data or a reference-free perfect blackbody.

The map is linear. For each independently saved43/54 source component,
project its amplitude and residual before adding absolute amplitudes or
residual norms. Preserve their coherent sum only as a separate diagnostic.
Do not manufacture a residual by subtracting two large nearly equal
baseline predictions; project the saved correction directly.

Both inputs get the same1400/2100-node readout refinement and
lower-frequency cutoff comparisons. These reuse the96-mode vectors.
They change representation/metric sensitivity, not source quadrature
or the physical history. Window timestep/terminal/restart variants are
also mapped without rerunning any evolution.

A leftover orthogonal to mu/y in this metric is not an instrument
principal component, evidence of new physics, or proof of observable
detectability. Unresolved infrared continuation, outside-window source
and later-time transfer remain separate physical scopes.
