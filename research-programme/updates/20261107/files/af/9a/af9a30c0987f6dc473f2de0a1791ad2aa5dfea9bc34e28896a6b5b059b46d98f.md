# Stage84 — unexpanded thermal photon coupling

This extends Stage83, not a new MTS parent theory. The retained matter sector
contains an electron and its electromagnetic coupling. We derive its thermal
photon insertion consistently before putting it into the existing photon/gas/
metric evolution. The electron rest mass and alpha remain inherited inputs.
The new calculation is a QED source-component derivation, not new empirical
evidence distinguishing MTS from another cosmological parent.

## 1. Conventions and the common thermal line

Use metric (+---), m_e=c=hbar=1, e^2=4*pi*alpha, and ubar*u=2.
Let S(P)=(slash(P)+1)/(P^2-1), D(P)=slash(P)-1 and
C[A]=-g_mu_nu gamma^mu A gamma^nu. The positive bath momentum is
k=omega*n, n=(1,n_hat), n^2=0. For an unpolarized occupation N(k), the
stripped photon-cut insertion is integrated with

    e^2 d^3k N(k) / [2 omega (2*pi)^3].

Both signs of the on-shell thermal line are included in

    Sigma_hat(P,k) = sum_{s=+1,-1} C[S(P-s*k)],
    V_hat(eps;Pout,Pin,k)
        = sum_s C[S(Pout-s*k) slash(eps) S(Pin-s*k)].

No expansion in omega/E or E/T has been made. These are the photon-cut
sector in the stated covariant contraction, not a claim that the finite
density electron sector vanishes or that every thermal gauge prescription
is equivalent without its other terms.

Since S(A) slash(A-B) S(B)=S(B)-S(A), with q=Pout-Pin,

    V_hat(q;Pout,Pin,k) = Sigma_hat(Pin,k)-Sigma_hat(Pout,k).

This is the first-order Ward identity for inverse propagator D-Sigma.
It is a matrix identity before angular averaging or external spin sums.

## 2. Eight internal Compton insertions, not a fitted counterterm

Write p'=p+k0-k1, r=p+k0, t=p-k1, A=slash(eps0), B=slash(eps1).
The tree matrix is M0=B S(r) A + A S(t) B.
For the first hard ordering the four insertions are

    B S(r) Sigma_hat(r) S(r) A,
    B S(r) V_hat(eps0;r,p),
    V_hat(eps1;p',r) S(r) A,
    sum_s C[S(p'-s*k) B S(r-s*k) A S(p-s*k)].

Add the crossed hard ordering. The last term is the spanning box; removing
it fails the off-shell identity. Varma1992 Figure3 provides the independent
diagram inventory. We reconstruct its underlying Dirac chains rather than
importing the paper's subsequent hard-photon expansion.

Contraction with k0 reduces the order-e^2 correction to the first-order part of

    D_full(p') S_full(t) Gamma_B(t,p)
        - Gamma_B(p',r) S_full(r) D_full(p).

Here D_full=D-e^2*Sigma, S_full=S+e^2*S*Sigma*S and
Gamma_B=B+e^2*V_B, with the common bath integration understood.
The four chain terms telescope using the one-photon identity. Bare external
spinors alone therefore leave a self-energy defect; internal insertions
alone must not be advertised as an on-shell gauge-invariant amplitude.

## 3. External poles, spinors and residues from that same self-energy

At P^2=1 the positive/negative thermal-energy pair gives exactly

    Sigma_hat(P,k) = 2 slash(k)/(P.k).

For fixed spatial momentum, delta_E=ubar*Sigma_hat*u/(2E)=2/E.
For an isotropic Planck bath this implies

    delta_m^2 = e^2*T^2/6 = 2*pi*alpha*T^2/3,
    delta_m = pi*alpha*T^2/3,

to first order about m_e=1. This reproduces the established mass benchmark.
It does not derive the vacuum electron mass. More generally the photon-cut
mass-squared shift for isotropic N is (e^2/pi^2) integral omega*N(omega)domega.

Let H0=E-gamma0*D(P), Pminus=(1-H0/E)/2. A spinor correction with unchanged
Euclidean norm at fixed spatial momentum is

    delta_u = Pminus gamma0 Sigma_hat(P,k) u/(2E).

It solves D(P)delta_u+(delta_E*gamma0-Sigma_hat)u=0. Merely changing a scalar
mass without changing the spinor would miss the directional bath term.
For a resting incoming electron and a fixed outgoing photon direction h,

    delta_Ehard = (delta_Einitial-delta_Efinal)/(1-vfinal.h).

The final spatial electron momentum changes by -delta_Ehard*h. Its ordinary
free-spinor derivative is also included. Direct perturbative checks of both
hard-photon Ward legs fall quadratically with a formal test coefficient:
reducing that coefficient by100 reduces the residual by10000. That test
coefficient is not a fitted physical parameter.

The frequency-pole residue at fixed spatial momentum follows independently
by differentiation, with d=P.n:

    ubar*(partial Sigma_hat/partial P0)*u/(2E)
        = 2/(omega^2*d^2) - 2/(E*d).

We use this canonical frequency-pole convention, together with the stated
spinor normalization. Conversion to a kinetic rate must carry its consistent
state measure and flux; this residue is not silently identified with an
invariant wave-function-renormalization convention in a different calculation.

## 4. The thermal infrared pole now cancels from actual diagrams

At each fixed bath direction, projecting the internal soft coefficient
between on-shell external spinors gives

    M_internal,-2 = -2 (p.p')/[(p.n)(p'.n)] * M0.

The two half-residues add [1/(p.n)^2+1/(p'.n)^2]*M0. Their sum is j^2*M0,
where j=p'/(p'.n)-p/(p.n), and n.j=0. Hence the virtual interference density is

    + alpha/(2*pi^2) j^2 * N(omega)/omega d omega d Omega.

Stimulated emission and absorption each give the corresponding positive
real soft density, totaling

    - alpha/(2*pi^2) j^2 * N(omega)/omega d omega d Omega.

The coefficients cancel for the same N direction by direction, without
requiring N to be exactly Planckian. At the sampled physical energy and
chosen directions they are -9.578443608031888e-11 and its positive opposite.
Removing the external residues fails this cancellation.

This removes the unresolved hard-process pole, not physical photon creation.
The finite photon source and its energy moment remain to be matched. A common
bath and a controlled infrared completion are still necessary for that finite
operator; a cancellation of coefficients is not an integral over an arbitrary
unknown occupation below the saved spectral floor.

## 5. Exact radial reduction through thermally occupied poles

For bare on-shell external electrons each shifted external propagator is
constant plus 1/omega. Each shifted internal propagator has one linear pole.
Summing the two thermal-energy signs makes the radial matrix even. It follows
algebraically that the eight-insertion matrix has the finite decomposition

    M_hat(omega)=C0+Csoft/omega^2
                  +Cr/(omega^2-ar^2)+Ct/(omega^2-at^2),
    aR=abs((R^2-1)/(2 R.n)), R in {r,t}.

C0 comes from the large-omega limit, Csoft from the two external denominators
in the spanning box, and CR=2*aR*Res_{omega=aR} M_hat. The coefficients are
Dirac matrices derived from residues, not interpolation fits. Matrix checks
against the original chains cover both sides of the poles and span omega/E
from0.03 to20. Degenerate coincident poles require the continuous sum of terms;
the present benchmark excludes the exactly forward hard-photon limit.

After subtracting the soft term, only the Planck second moment and

    J(z) = PV integral_0^infinity x/[(exp(x)-1)(x^2-z^2)] dx
         = [ln(z/(2*pi))-Re psi(i*z/(2*pi))]/2

are needed. The second equality follows by taking the boundary value of
Binet's digamma identity, NIST DLMF5.9.15. Direct singularity subtraction and
independent symmetric-excision quadrature agree with this expression.

For original internal denominator D_R(omega)=R^2-1-2*s*omega*R.n+i0,
the term multiplying CR is

    J(aR/T) - i*pi*sign(D_R')*N(aR)/2.

The sign is retained from the original denominator; partial fractions alone
must not erase it. Analytic continuation of the Binet expression independently
checks this distributional limit. No finite width is retained or fitted. The
imaginary part is a Feynman cut component, not by itself a collision or decay
rate in the medium.

For one benchmark direction ar/T=0.999485313116349 and at/T=1.000182175778383,
with occupations0.58245 and0.58181. These are populated poles, not remote
ultraviolet corrections. At z=1 the exact J=-0.6452302634163338; the leading
large-z approximation gives -1.6449340668482264. It cannot be substituted here.

## 6. Finite dressed amplitude and its subtraction convention

Add to the internal radial finite matrix the spinor shifts, the derivative
of M0 under the energy-conserving pole shifts, and the finite external
half-residue coefficient

    -1/[Einitial*(p.n)] -1/[Efinal*(p'.n)].

These external terms are omega-independent before weighting and therefore
multiply pi^2*T^2/6. The resulting finite spin/polarization amplitude satisfies
both hard Ward identities, including its complex pole boundary values.
The analytic external derivative is checked against direct symmetric
differentiation of the dressed tree amplitude.

SUBTRACTION CONVENTION: remove the full coefficient j^2*M0/omega^2 over the
whole positive radial axis before the Bose-weighted integral. A different
soft subtraction profile changes this finite component and must change the
matching real remainder by the opposite amount. This intermediate finite
amplitude is therefore not a stand-alone measured correction.

At a resting-electron benchmark E=T=0.00045962015785263325 and cos(theta)=0.1,
angular orders4,6,8 give the same complex tensor to better than the1e-9
relative gate. The finite interference 2Re(M0*deltaM)/|M0|^2 is
-5.3333294959920135e-9 in this scheme. No rate correction, new MTS signal,
full bath average or uncertainty on a cosmological observable is inferred.

## 7. Genuine absorption, not a guessed negative-emission rate

Cross the second final photon through the six-diagram Dirac amplitude. For
p=(1,0), absorbed energy s>0 and geometric aij=1-ni.nj,

    Ehard = [E+s+E*s*a02]/[1+E*a01+s*a12].

The invariant expression agrees with the explicit spin/polarization sum;
all three photon Ward legs pass. The phase-space density is -R(-s), where
R is the previously derived signed emission density. This sign is from
the positive incoming phase space, not a negative physical probability.
The incoming hard photon and electron are averaged by1/4; the bath photon
polarizations are summed against occupation per state, not averaged again.

For equilibrium Maxwell-Boltzmann electrons the weights obey

    n(E)n(s)[1+n(Ehard)]
      = exp[-(Eelectron_final-Eelectron_initial)/T]
        n(Ehard)[1+n(E)][1+n(s)].

Tests include absorbed photons with s/E=1.4, not just the soft limit.
Photon and electron four-momentum balance is checked at each crossed event.
Finite thermal source assembly must retain the hard final-state occupations,
reverse reactions, gain/loss structure and identical-photon counting.

## 8. Connection to the actual saved MTS histories

We reuse the heavy and cold Stage79 histories read-only, with their actual
electron density and temperature, rather than replacing them with a new model.
At a=1e-6, ne=2.20624888398e17 m^-3 and T/m=0.00045962015785263325.
The electron gas cannot be dismissed using exp(-m/T): it has a nonzero
chemical potential fixed by its density.

An explicit equilibrium occupation bound can nevertheless be derived.
Write f=z*y/(1+z*y), y=exp[-(E-m)/T], and I1=2 integral d^3p*y/(2*pi)^3.
Then z*I1/(1+z) <= ne <= z*I1. With z0=ne/I1<1 this implies
fmax=z/(1+z)<=z0, where in natural units

    z0=ne*pi^2/[theta*exp(1/theta)*K2(1/theta)].

This bound is1.01443e-14 initially, falling to3.21039e-16 at a=1e-5.
It controls Pauli occupation, not every resonance-enhanced electron-loop
integral. A uniform bound on the omitted medium sector is not claimed.

The leading cold-plasma response follows from m*dv/dt=-e*E and j=-ne*e*v:
omega^2=|k|^2+omega_p^2, omega_p^2=4*pi*alpha*ne in electron-mass units.
Its energy/T is7.42618e-8 initially, below the saved x_min=1e-5. This is a
physical scale for a future collective infrared treatment, not permission to
insert an arbitrary photon mass into the current calculation. Relativistic
and thermal collective corrections to this leading estimate are separate.

The resolved nonthermal mass moment is also read from n=nP+chi*w*B:

    delta_m2_resolved / delta_m2_Planck
        = chi * integral_{xmin}^{xmax} x*w(x)*B(x) dx / (pi^2/6).

At the final heavy-history time this is+3.27966986176121e-9 (cold:
+3.28019638686425e-9), with independent quadrature refinement. It weights
the spectrum differently from total radiation energy, so it need not have
the sign of the integrated baryon-cooling energy transfer. Nothing is assigned
to the unknown nonthermal spectrum outside the saved frequency interval.
