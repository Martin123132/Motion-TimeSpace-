# C0 reaction sensitivity without a new fitted coefficient

Scope: the represented annular action and the inherited linear position
family. Hold its baseline, position displacement, and first time tangent
fixed. Compare two computed old-Euler reactions k0 and k0+delta k, with
mixed metric response H and H+delta H. This measures numerical reaction
sensitivity, not a new physical parameter or an uncertainty distribution.

Let g be the field gradient, v its material rate, W the material transport
speed, T=v-Wg, and alpha=R^2/(N sqrt(F)). The only changes in the regular
position-column density are

    delta alpha_t = -alpha*(delta H_nu-delta H_mu/(R*F)),
    delta T_t = delta k_field-D*delta k_source*g,
    delta A = delta alpha_t*T+alpha*delta T_t.

Here delta refers to changing the reaction inside the position derivative,
not changing the baseline action. All metric and geometric weights remain
at that baseline. For the fixed raw material test (z,eta),

    delta C0_force_density = delta A*(z-eta*g).

For h=(R-6)^p, p=0,1,2, with I_h=integral_source^R h and the P2 interpolant
V_h of the nodal v_i I_h(R_i), the three current rows simplify to

    delta C0_current_density = delta A*(T*I_h-V_h).

Proof: the flux has no acceleration or mixed-time dependence. In the
current, delta E_dot+(delta alpha_t/alpha)*E equals T*delta A, because
delta beta_dot=-beta*delta alpha_t/alpha cancels the gradient-energy
part. This leaves the two displayed terms. The force similarly depends
on these changes only through A. This is exact linearity in the represented
reaction arguments, not a small-change Taylor approximation.

Evaluate T*I_h-V_h without subtracting nearly equal primitive values:
sum the P2 nodal weights times v_i*(I_h(R)-I_h(R_i)), then subtract W*g*I_h.
Use the polynomial divided-difference identity for each primitive
difference, retaining the actual physical cell width.

The gravitational current adds the integral of
N/(kappa*sqrt(F))*delta H_mu*h. The inherited source-clock and native
position-row functions contain position and rate tangents, but neither
k0 nor H. Thus their sensitivity is zero for THIS comparison. Likewise
all position-boundary atoms use baseline densities and the fixed position
displacement; their reaction sensitivity is zero. This does not assert
that their original position-column contributions vanish.

Validation: compare the simplified four densities with an independent
complex perturbation of the complete density formula; test pure field,
pure source, pure metric and combined changes. Actual integration must
sample nonempty active support, compare two rules, and retain an explicit
sensitivity size instead of fitting it away. A sampled absolute-density
integral is a diagnostic, not a rigorous continuum error bound.
