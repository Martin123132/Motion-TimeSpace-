## What advances

The axial response can now be driven by a physical, noncircular test companion,
not by switching the circular source on and off. We derive its stress projection,
both moving distributions, the master-field jumps, the contact cancellation in
the metric, and an acceleration-free Fourier source integral. This integral
can be inserted into the existing body/BH wave matching without fitting a new
coefficient. Numerical tests here validate the source, not a new eccentric flux.

The model is still the inherited Einstein-Hilbert/G_R plus complex-scalar body.
Its background, surface response and parameters are not re-solved or refitted.
The exterior is the same Schwarzschild geometry for body and same-mass BH;
the different inner response is retained. Work is linear in companion mass mu
in the field; any future radiation power is quadratic in mu. Using mu=1 in
the source files means a coefficient PER UNIT MASS, not an actual companion
heavier than the reference central body. Physically mu/M must be small here.

## 2. Start with a conserved worldline, not a window

Let s be proper time, E=f u^t, h=r_p^2 u^phi, and w=dr_p/ds. For an equatorial
timelike Schwarzschild geodesic:

    u^t=E/f, u^r=w, u^phi=h/r_p^2,
    w^2=E^2-f*(1+h^2/r_p^2),
    dw/ds=-M/r_p^2+h^2/r_p^3-3M*h^2/r_p^4,
    v=f*w/E, u=w/E, Omega=f*h/(E*r_p^2),
    1-u^2=f*(1+h^2/r_p^2)/E^2 > 0.

The normalized invariant point stress is

    T^{alpha beta}=mu*u^alpha*u^beta/(u^t*r^2)
                    *delta(r-r_p(t))*delta(cos(theta))*delta(phi-phi_p(t)).

For a complete geodesic worldline, integrating its divergence against a smooth
compact test covector and integrating by parts in s yields the acceleration
term mu*integral a^beta test_beta ds, which vanishes. No particle creation or
destruction endpoints are introduced. The time, radial and angular geodesic
equations, and normalization, are separately checked in derive_eccentric.py.

Only verifying the axial Bianchi identity would be insufficient: it primarily
constrains angular momentum and allows arbitrary radial driving. The explicit
radial geodesic check prevents that loophole. A radial period used below is a
fundamental integration interval of an existing orbit, not a temporal cutoff.

At the equator, conjugate X_phi=sqrt(15/(8pi))*exp(-i phi_p), and conjugate
X_phiphi=-i*conjugate X_phi in the inherited convention. Define

    A(t)=(8pi/3)*mu*h/r_p^2*sqrt(15/(8pi))*exp(-i phi_p),
    A_dot/A=-2v/r_p-i Omega.

Direct projection gives

    P^t=A*delta(r-r_p),
    P^r=v*A*delta(r-r_p),
    P=-i*Omega*r_p^2*A/kappa*delta(r-r_p).

The m=0,+/-2 odd quadrupole projections still vanish for this equatorial orbit.
The m=-1 sector follows the usual reality relation to m=+1. In particular,
nonzero radial velocity does NOT permit dropping P^r.

The coefficients of both delta and delta-prime vanish in

    partial_t P^t+partial_r P^r+2P^r/r-kappa*P/r^2=0.

Multiplying all projected sources by an arbitrary window W(t) instead leaves
W_dot*A*delta(r-r_p). An actual inspiral needs consistent perturbative forces
and accompanying field changes; a window is not such a derivation.

## 3. Canonical source and exact moving jumps

From the general CPM source:

    S_x=(2r/kappa)*[partial_t P^r+f*partial_r(f*P^t)]
       =G_r(t)*delta(r-r_p)+F_r(t)*delta_prime(r-r_p),
    F_r=2r_p*A*(f_p^2-v^2)/kappa,
    G_r=(2A/kappa)*(r_p*a-v^2-i*r_p*v*Omega-f_p).

Multiplication by a radial prefactor must include its derivative on delta-prime.
To change measure to x, use

    delta(r-r_p)=delta(x-x_p)/f_p,
    delta_prime(r-r_p)=delta_prime(x-x_p)/f_p^2
                       +f'_p*delta(x-x_p)/f_p^2.

Thus S_x=G*delta(x-x_p)+D*delta_prime(x-x_p), where

    J=2r_p*A/kappa,
    J_dot=J*(-v/r_p-i Omega),
    D=(1-u^2)*J,
    G=u_dot*J-f_p*J*(1+u^2)/r_p-i*Omega*u*J
     =d(uJ)/dt-f_p*J/r_p.

Brackets mean the outer trace minus inner trace on the moving worldline.
Set J=[psi], K=[partial_x psi], H=[partial_t psi]. Distributional differentiation
and the chain rule give

    D=(1-u^2)*J,
    G=(1-u^2)*K+2u*J_dot+u_dot*J,
    H=J_dot-u*K.

Solving, without a small-radial-speed expansion:

    [psi]=2r_p*A/kappa,
    [partial_x psi]=-f_p*J/r_p+i*u*Omega*J/(1-u^2),
    [partial_t psi]=-i*Omega*J/(1-u^2).

The denominator is strictly positive for the exterior timelike orbit above.
At v=0 these recover the old circular J=2r_p*A/kappa and K=-2A*f_p/kappa.
Although the value jump is unchanged in form, reusing the circular derivative
jump on an eccentric orbit fails the source equation.

Dimensions in geometric units: A and K are dimensionless; psi,J,D have length;
G is dimensionless; P^t,P^r have inverse length and P is dimensionless. Both
terms in S_x have inverse length, as does the master operator acting on psi.

## 4. Reconstruct the metric and remove source contacts correctly

For general P^t AND P^r, not merely a circular source:

    h_t=f*partial_r(r*psi)/2-r^2*f*P^t/kappa,
    h_r=r*partial_t psi/(2f)+r^2*P^r/(kappa*f).

With the general master equation imposed, these satisfy the CPM definition
and all three projected Einstein equations, provided the projected Bianchi
identity holds. derive_eccentric.py verifies the stronger off-shell identities:
each discrepancy in P^t/P^r is proportional to the master residual and its
derivatives, while the tensor equation is precisely the source Bianchi identity.

The distribution in partial_r psi cancels the explicit P^t contact in h_t.
The distribution -v*J*delta(r-r_p) in partial_t psi cancels the explicit
P^r contact in h_r. Neither cancellation may be omitted.

The regular metric traces need not be individually continuous:

    [h_t]=(f_p*J+r_p*K)/2,
    [h_r]=r_p*H/(2f_p).

But they obey the stronger physically useful relation

    [h_t]+v*[h_r]=0.

The combination sampled by the worldline's odd metric coupling is continuous.
For two solutions with the SAME orbit, mass M, source normalization and smooth
initial data in their common exterior, all the master and metric source jumps
cancel in Delta=body-BH. The difference solves the homogeneous exterior equation
and has a smooth trace through the worldline. Different inner boundary responses
still give a nonzero difference. This is not a claim that the individual
point-particle wave energy or full self-force is already regularized.

## 5. A usable source integral without acceleration terms

For any smooth test field eta(t,x), including the smooth difference field,
the distributional source pairing over a time interval is

    integral dt dx S_x*eta
      =[uJ*eta(t,x_p)]_endpoints
       -integral dt J*[u*partial_t eta+partial_x eta+f_p*eta/r_p]_{x_p}.

The bulk term is -2J/r_p times the source-free metric combination h_t+v*h_r
constructed from eta. This identity connects the master source to the same
metric coupling and keeps its endpoint term explicit. Pairing delta-prime
with a discontinuous individual psi instead is NOT justified by this identity.

For a bound eccentric orbit define the radial period T_r, accumulated azimuth
Delta_phi, Omega_r=2pi/T_r, Omega_phi=Delta_phi/T_r and

    omega_n=Omega_phi+n*Omega_r, n integer, for m=+1.

At successive radial periods the projected source changes by exp(-i Delta_phi).
Using eta=exp(i omega_n t)*y(x), the endpoint term cancels over a full period;
starting at pericentre also makes u zero at both endpoints. The radial source
functional ready for retarded matching is

    I_y(n)=(1/T_r)*integral_0^T_r dt exp(i omega_n t)*(G*y-D*y_x)
          =-(1/T_r)*integral_0^T_r dt J*exp(i omega_n t)
                         *[(f_p/r_p+i omega_n*u)*y+y_x].

This retains radial motion and orbital phase while eliminating acceleration
and derivative distributions from numerical quadrature. For circular motion,
n=0 recovers K*y-J*y_x; n!=0 gives zero. Finite intervals that are not complete
periods retain the endpoint term: the tests explicitly reject its omission.

Let B,H,O be the old body-regular, horizon-ingoing and infinity-outgoing radial
solutions at omega_n, W their tortoise Wronskian, and O0 the normalization that
restores unit outgoing amplitude. Linearity gives

    A_infinity(B)=I_B/[W(B,O)*O0],
    A_infinity(H)=I_H/[W(H,O)*O0],
    Delta A_infinity=-W(B,H)*I_O/[W(B,O)*W(H,O)*O0].

The last identity avoids subtracting nearly equal emitted amplitudes and
generalizes the saved circular matching formula to a distributed orbital
radial range. Use the absolute difference near a zero of I_H; a relative
excess there would be ill-conditioned. Zero/negative frequencies require their
proper static/conjugate branches in the NEXT wave calculation, not insertion
into a positive-frequency-only integration routine.
