# Integrated native-force precision experiment

Use the exact forward derivative checked independently in
`DERIVATION-20260929-native-force-directional-arithmetic.md`. Reuse the
four settled reactions and their inverse. Change one precision layer at
a time; do not refit coefficients or alter the physical native kernel.

For fixed binary64 quadrature nodes and weights, integrate the pointwise
native covector both by ordinary summation and by Decimal70 summation.
Compare legacy, pre-rounded geometry/interpolation, and complete extended
precision modes. Save every node force so another evaluation can test the
projection without repeating the expensive kernel. Compare integrated
forces using the inherited inverse and the unchanged2e-11 tolerance.

If needed, also evaluate Gauss-Legendre roots and weights in Decimal70.
Initialize each root with the standard binary64 rule, then use Newton's
method on P_n and its analytic derivative, without renormalizing weights:

    w_i = 2 / ((1-x_i^2) P_n'(x_i)^2),
    label_i=x_i/2,
    material_weight_i=(w_i/2)*6*(label_i+1/2)*(1/2-label_i).

Symmetric root pairs are represented symmetrically. Check low-order moments
against their closed form: odd moments vanish; even moment p equals
3/(2^p*(p+1)*(p+3)). No quadrature weights are tuned to force agreement.

Report the difference between two integrated Decimal vectors before their
final rounding separately from the difference between rounded outputs.
That separates quadrature sensitivity from the final storage floor.
Saved numerical convergence is a diagnostic, not a rigorous error bound.

An accepted candidate must still be propagated through the live response
and independently checked with its correspondingly evaluated equations.
The ordinary wave force remains a separate integration target. A native
pass alone does not certify the whole reaction or current column C0.
