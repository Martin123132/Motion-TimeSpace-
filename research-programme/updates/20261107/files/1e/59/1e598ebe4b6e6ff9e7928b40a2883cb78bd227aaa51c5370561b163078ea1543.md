## Constructive follow-up: eliminate existing velocities, do not guess inertia

If the actual directional equation remains nonzero, use the saved positive fixed-metric field/source Hessian M to test whether this is merely a poor choice of field transport. With base combined direction (z,eta), d=z-eta*g and m0=integral alpha*d^2, assemble v_i=integral alpha*V_i*d, where V_i are the OLD field/source velocity designs. Solve M w=v using its existing banded inverse. The optimal compensated direction is (z,eta)-w in the old degrees of freedom, with

    m_eff=m0-v^T M^(-1)v
         =integral alpha*(d-Vw)^2 + source_inertia*(w_source)^2 >=0.

The NEW independent mesh direction is zero at the source. The existing source variable is still included in M and may adjust in w; its inertia and its contribution to mesh velocity are retained. The positivity statement is for the represented finite space with fixed metric, not an assertion of a new fundamental kinetic term.

Check the second expression independently with direct quadrature including the source proper-clock inertia. The force is F_eff=F_direction-w^T E_old, with E_old taken from the saved full solved Euler residual, not set to zero by assertion. The frozen-metric linear acceleration response would be -F_eff/m_eff if m_eff>0. This is a diagnostic of conditioning, NOT a physical evolution law: independent-support changes to the metric constraint and its response are not included. Test both reference and MTS, both grids, same quadrature and fixed source treatment. A growing response as the finite-space mass shrinks motivates a constraint-compatible support/operator derivation instead of promoting discretization mass to physical inertia.
