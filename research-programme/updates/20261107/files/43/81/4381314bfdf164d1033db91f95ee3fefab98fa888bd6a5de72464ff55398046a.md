# Stage184: the saved bank corner explains the local gas-curvature discrepancy

## Outcome

The one-sided scattering-bank slopes predict the dominant gas-curvature width
difference without fitting a coefficient. A finite-capacity forced calculation
isolates the contribution: it accounts for 99.4583% (heavy) and 99.4476% (cold)
of the saved quarter-grid gas width difference. The leading analytic amplitude
agrees with that isolated contribution to 0.01906% and 0.01950%, respectively.

The observed near-halving of successive sampling increments is also explained:
linear interpolation depends on where the fixed probes fall inside each grid
cell. Its equation-based prediction matches all twelve saved sampling changes
within 0.082%. This does not require a first-order time integrator.

These are local numerical attribution results, not new astronomical evidence.
No scattering matrix, emission matrix, source curve, temperature history, fitted
parameter, probe, or tolerance was changed. The original full-source decision
is preserved separately; this stage identifies a concrete numerical mechanism.

## Derivation and units

Use the retained scaled variables, u=ln(a/a_start), photon coefficients p, scaled
gas response g, constant heat coefficient chi, mass matrix M, energy row e,
temperature template T, and S=r(B-D). The inherited equations are

    M p_u = S(p-Tg),
    chi(g_u+g) = -1-e p_u.

At theta=6.129133311507764e-5, the bank is continuous but its two theta secants
differ (relative matrix norm jump 0.033269343279553935). With increasing u,
theta falls, so the jump from warm to cool is

    Delta S_u = r theta (B_theta,warm-B_theta,cool),
    Delta p_uu = M^-1 Delta S_u (p-Tg),
    Delta g_uu = -e Delta p_uu / chi.

At finite chi the state and its FIRST derivative are continuous; do not confuse
the exact second-derivative jump with a first-derivative jump. The differentiated
energy exchange is exactly balanced. All derivative units here are per unit u;
the numbers below are scaled gas curvatures, not kelvin or an observed distortion.

For the fast, frozen-photon relaxation reduction,

    C = chi-e M^-1 S T,
    G = -(1+e M^-1 S p)/C,
    J = -e M^-1 Delta S_u (p-TG)/C.

The leading slaved response is J(u-u_anchor)_+. For the unchanged widths
d=0.01 and d/2=0.005, its curvature difference is -J/d. This is a local
approximation, tested below against coupled finite-capacity evolution. The
frozen-photon relaxation widths are about 8.53e-11 and 8.51e-11 in u, not seconds
or a proven relaxation bound for every coupled mode. The corresponding simple
boundary-layer scaling correction is about 2.6e-8 of the leading amplitude.

See `DERIVATION.md` for the exact jump, forced recurrence and sampling algebra.
