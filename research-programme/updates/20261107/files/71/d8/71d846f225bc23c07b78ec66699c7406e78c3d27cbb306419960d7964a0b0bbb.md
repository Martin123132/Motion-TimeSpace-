## Source and metric equations

For adiabatic standard species at this gradient order,

    delta rho_fluid = -(rho_fluid+p_fluid) s Rhat
    delta p_fluid   = -(p_fluid,N/3) s Rhat,   N=ln a.

Define F=sum_fluid(rho+p), G=-sum_fluid(p_N)/3, and the energy and pressure coefficients

    E = -F s + sum_I(phi_dot_I f_dot_I + m_I^2 phi_I f_I)
    P = -G s + sum_I(phi_dot_I f_dot_I - m_I^2 phi_I f_I).

Hamiltonian and canonical Klein-Gordon equations give

    4 H sdot = 2 E - a^-2
    fddot_I + 3 H fdot_I + m_I^2 f_I = -sdot phi_dot_I.

The source on the right is fixed by volume expansion in the covariant field action. No fitted conversion from curvature to field density enters. The background is re-evolved with Friedmann and both canonical KG equations, starting from the inherited fields, rather than differentiating a noisy oscillatory density table.

The resulting identities are

    Edot + 3H(E+P) + [F+sum(phi_dot_I^2)] sdot = 0
    sddot + 2H sdot = -(E+3P)/2.

The second follows by differentiating the Hamiltonian constraint and using Hdot=-(F+sum(phi_dot^2))/2. This gives a genuine propagation condition rather than imposing a second independent evolution law for s.

## Collisionless quadrupole, including massive neutrinos

For isotropic collisionless comoving occupation f0(q), eps=sqrt(q^2+a^2 m_nu^2), write I4=integral(4pi q^4 f0/eps dq) and I6=integral(4pi q^6 f0/eps^3 dq). Then p=I4/(3a^4). The anisotropic response induced by B is

    pi^i_j = -Cnu b RhatTF^i_j
    Cnu = (5 I4-I6)/(15 a^4) = -p_nu,N/5.

This follows by integration by parts of integral(q^5 f0'/eps dq), with a distribution decaying at both endpoints. It includes the inverse-metric contribution to the stress, not just a changed distribution. The regular homogeneous quadrupole integration constant is zero, as in the parent seed. It reproduces Cnu=4rho/15 in the relativistic limit and Cnu=p in the nonrelativistic limit. The tracefree Einstein equation is

    bddot + 3H bdot + 2 sum(Cnu) b = -2/a^2.

Phase-space and hierarchy conventions: Ma and Bertschinger (1995), https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9506072, with archived primary text `../adiabatic-curvature-seed-20261007/sources/ma-bertschinger-initial-data.html`. The general Cnu=-p_N/5 reduction here is derived within the stated gradient approximation; it is not asserted to replace the full finite-k massive hierarchy.

## Momentum is an independent check

Write the covariant momentum density J_i=J partial_i Rhat. Tight-coupled photon/baryon momentum and neutrino momenta obey

    Jpg_dot + 3H Jpg = (4rho_gamma/9) s
    Jnu_dot + 3H Jnu = -(p_nu,N/3) s + Cnu b/6
    Jphi = -phi_dot f;  Jphi_dot + 3H Jphi = -Pphi.

The combined photon/baryon current retains baryon inertia; this is not a claim that baryon velocity is zero. Cold synchronous-frame CDM has Jc=0. The momentum constraint and its propagation are

    Cmom = (2/3) sdot - bdot/12 - Jtotal
    Cmom_dot + 3H Cmom = 0.

This is checked without feeding Cmom back into the integration. Photon anisotropic stress/slip are absent in the tight-coupling approximation; their size is tested against the native hierarchy. Velocity divergence and higher multipole streaming affect the next gradient order.

## Radiation start and stable numerical variables

The initial seed uses s=-tau^2/16, b=-tau^2/(3Dnu), Dnu=1+4Rnu/15, f_I=-phi_I0 m_I^2 beta^2 tau^6/1680, as already derived. Start a=2e-9; repeat at1e-9. The radiation seed at finite a has a small matter correction, so exact zero initial residual is not claimed. Its decaying remnant is measured.

For stability, integrate Ys=s(aH)^2, Yf=f(aH)^2, Yg=f_N(aH)^2, Yb=b(aH)^2, Yw=b_N(aH)^2, YJ=a^2 H J. With h=H_N/H, V=phi_N, omega=m/H,

    Etilde=a^2 E=-(F/H^2) Ys + sum(V Yg + omega^2 phi Yf)
    Srate=(aH)^2 s_N=(2 Etilde-1)/4
    Ys_N=Srate+2(1+h)Ys
    Yf_N=Yg+2(1+h)Yf
    Yg_N=(h-1)Yg-omega^2 Yf-Srate V
    Yb_N=Yw+2(1+h)Yb
    Yw_N=(h-1)Yw-2(Cnu/H^2)Yb-2.

The implementation divides field responses by the inherited initial field amplitude for conditioning only. It does not rescale any physical coupling. Absolute tolerances for the tiny slow-field response are specified separately.

## What survives heavy-phase averaging?

Before averaging, the Hilbert heavy stress gives Eh=phi_dot fdot+M^2 phi f and Ph=phi_dot fdot-M^2 phi f. The response of its local comoving energy is

    Nh=a^3(Eh+rho_h s)
    Nh_dot=-a^3[3H(Ph+p_h s)+p_h sdot].

For cold dust Nh is identically zero for this volume-following adiabatic data. For the heavy field, a response can be generated at onset and subsequently retained. Whether it becomes constant is calculated, not postulated.

A separate WKB diagnostic uses chi=a^(3/2)phi. Define U=phi_dot+3Hphi/2,

    I0=a^3(U^2+M^2 phi^2)/2
    Ihat=a^3[s(U^2+M^2 phi^2)/2
              +U(fdot+3Hf/2+sdot phi/2)+M^2 phi f].

This is the derivative of the local-volume oscillator action with Hlocal=H+sdot Rhat/3. It is NOT the Hilbert energy density and cannot silently replace Eh in Einstein's equations. The unperturbed action drift is Idot=(3Hdot/2+9H^2/4)chi chi_dot; finite-H/M corrections must remain in the matching error.

At retained gradient order, Rhat multiplies these same time coefficients including its quadratic-in-zeta terms. Products of two curvature-induced field responses start at four gradients. This explains precisely the sense in which the derived nonlinear seed can be transported and phase-projected here. It does not license a full finite-k quadratic transfer by copying the linear result.

Native scalar comparison is delta_c=-4k^2 zeta s, delta_phi=4k^2 zeta f, eta=-zeta+k^2 zeta(b-8s)/6. Normalization comes from early eta alone, not a density fit. Higher-amplitude export to the native chart must retain the Stage62 spatial pullback and tensor Jacobian.
