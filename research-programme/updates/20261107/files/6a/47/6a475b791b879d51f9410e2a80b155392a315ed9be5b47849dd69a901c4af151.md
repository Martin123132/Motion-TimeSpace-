# Completed metric jet and the remaining old-coordinate reaction

Let e(t) be the completed field/source mode, eta the reference-fixed
independent support mode, and let Y denote the radial constraint solution.
For a trial history q_epsilon=q+epsilon*s*e, R_epsilon=R+epsilon*s*eta,
the field/source velocity changes by epsilon*(sdot*e+s*edot). Therefore

    delta Y = s*(X+A_old(edot)) + sdot*V,
    delta Y_t |(s,sdot,sddot)=(0,1,0) = X+A_old(edot)+Vdot,
    delta qddot = 2*edot.

Here X is the new position partial at fixed velocities, V is the saved
completed velocity response, and Vdot is the already constructed total
time derivative of V along the original coupled flow. They are different
objects. In particular, Vdot does not include X automatically. The new
validator evaluates all three terms, saves the resulting metric functions
and checks the differentiated outer lapse boundary. A nonlinear polynomial
metric functional independently checks the chain-rule coefficients.

## Why another old-coordinate solve may be required

Kinetic orthogonality of the mode means delta p_old=0 for a pure velocity
variation, including metric feedback. It does not by itself say that the
complete velocity perturbation obeys every old Euler equation. At the
zero-position jet the remaining old Euler column is schematically

    r1 = P_position[e,eta] + P_velocity[edot]
         + P_metric[X+A_old(edot)] - delta_velocity(L_q),

where the force derivative includes the metric variation V. The derivative
of the identically compensated velocity momentum is already zero; it
must not be added a second time. This is a constructive formula, not a
claim that r1 is zero. It is also obtainable directly by differentiating
the full old Euler covectors with delta qdot=e, delta qddot=2*edot,
delta Y=V and delta Y_t=X+A_old(edot)+Vdot.

If r1 is nonzero, solve the existing live operator

    L*a1 = -r1, L=M+P_metric*A_old,

and add a1 to the old acceleration column and A_old(a1) to the metric-time
column. This is the old fields' forced response, not a fitted support
inertia or a new freely chosen coefficient. Use the same source/field
force functional, including the native physical-kernel source derivative;
on the saved fixtures its agreement with the source-affine action was
checked earlier, not assumed universally.

Only then assemble the corrected C1 force/three-current column alongside
the owned C2 acceleration column and test rank/compatibility on all four
matched controls. Retain material-order differences and propagate them
through any inferred amplitudes. A large coefficient multiplying a tiny
column cannot establish a finite-amplitude solution through linearization
alone. No baseline subtraction, guessed reaction or proof-by-ledger.

## Useful exact simplification for the next solve

Put the metric constraint into the definitions of the old momentum and
force, p_hat and F_hat, without assuming that this creates a new reduced
variational action. Let Ppos be the position derivative of p_hat along
(e,eta), and Fvel the velocity derivative of F_hat along that same mode.
Then kinetic orthogonality is p_hat,v[e,eta]=0 along the original curve.
The complete first variation is

    delta p_hat = s*(Ppos+L*edot),
    r1 = Ppos+L*edot-Fvel.

Thus only one live inverse is required. Writing b1=L^(-1)(Ppos-Fvel),

    a1 = -edot-b1,
    delta old acceleration after reaction = edot-b1,
    delta Y_t after reaction = X+Vdot-A_old(b1).

This cancels the unnecessary intermediate A_old(edot) algebraically rather
than by subtracting two nearly equal computed responses. It remains
essential to calculate Ppos and Fvel from the actual material/native
functional; b1 is not free data and is not set to zero here.

A scalar counterexample verifies the need for this solve. For
Lagrangian=(qdot-f(q)*sdot)^2/2+epsilon*sdot^2/2-U(q), the old momentum
is p=qdot-f(q)*sdot. The mode (delta qdot,delta sdot)=(f(q),1) has delta
p=0. Nevertheless, at base sdot=sddot=0, its natural transported jet
delta qddot=2*f'(q)*qdot produces delta E_q=2*f'(q)*qdot. Orthogonality
alone demonstrably does not close the old equation. The validator checks
this nonlinear example symbolically, including the corrective reaction.
