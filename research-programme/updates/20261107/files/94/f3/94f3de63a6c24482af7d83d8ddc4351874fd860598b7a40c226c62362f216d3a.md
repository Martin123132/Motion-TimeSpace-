## Derived Newton-force error, not an assumed Newtonian closure

With the existing scaled Newtonian-gauge density D, current Q and anisotropic stress Pi, the retained linear Einstein constraints give

```text
2 k^2 Phi + D + 3 H Q/k = 0
Phi - Psi = Pi/k^2
Psi_P = -D/(2 k^2)
Psi - Psi_P = -3 H Q/(2 k^3) - Pi/k^2.
```

The force-split identity is checked independently. Relative to the relativistic force sup norm, the Poisson-only force differs by0.190675% for heavy MTS and0.275771% for the cold control. This is the size of an approximation, NOT a claim that MTS fits observations better. The force-to-velocity integral replay errors are3.10e-8/2.97e-8; the calculated source-interpolant velocity bounds contain the measured differences.

For nonzero density sup norm, the triangle inequality gives the source-conditioned bound

```text
||Psi-Psi_P|| / (||D||/(2 k^2))
 <= 3 sup(H/k) ||Q||/||D|| + 2 ||Pi||/||D||.
```

The measured left side/bound are0.00190617/0.00192631 heavy and0.00275783/0.00277529 cold. Therefore H/k becoming small alone is NOT sufficient: current must remain controlled relative to density, and anisotropic stress relative to density must vanish. No missing stress condition is replaced by an axiom.

For fixed relativistic sources, the CDM equation yields (a theta)'=a k^2 Psi. Its integrating factor gives the corresponding integral error bound. This is not an independently evolved self-consistent Poisson cosmology; backreaction after replacing the force requires a further calculation.
