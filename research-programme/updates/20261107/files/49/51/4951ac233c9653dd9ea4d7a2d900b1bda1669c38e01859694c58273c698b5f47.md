# Stage103: source curvature and its physical interpretation

This extends, rather than replaces, the Stage102 temperature/history law.
Let R_theta[z] mean the theta derivative of the retained collision source
at fixed departure z. On a smooth history segment, provided the combined
regularized collision functional can be differentiated twice, linearity
in z gives

R''(u) = theta^2 R_theta_theta[z] + theta R_theta[z]
         - 2 theta R_theta[z'] + R[z''].

Here theta'=-theta, theta''=theta. The four terms describe temperature
curvature, the exponential-temperature acceleration, the mixed
temperature/profile change, and curvature of the photon/gas/tail history.
None is a newly fitted exponent or independent free coupling.
Differentiating separated singular pieces would require their cancellation
and domination to be justified first; this conditional differential law
does not assert that such an interchange has already been established.

The finite withheld-source calculation does not need that interchange.
For interval width H and midpoint defect Delta from DERIVATION.md,

    weighted_average_R_second = -8 Delta/H^2.

When R is C2, this equals an average of R'' with normalized nonnegative
triangular weight w(v)=4 min(v-u0,u2-v)/H^2. Its integral is one.
This is an exact identity componentwise, including the independent
moments; it is not a pointwise estimate, an upper error bound, or a
unique decomposition among the four temperature/history terms above.
A large average signals curvature at the tested finite quadrature; it
does not establish continuum accuracy of that quadrature.

Because the response equation is linear at this order and fixed geometry,
the triangular-hat forcing transports the observed curvature defect into
photon, gas, energy, number and accumulated expansion-work changes.
