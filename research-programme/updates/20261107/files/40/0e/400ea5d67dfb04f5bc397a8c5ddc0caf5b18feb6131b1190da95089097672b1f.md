# Why the source cannot be transported by operator temperature alone

Stage208 derived the retained semidiscrete history repair equation in the
saved expansion coordinate u:

    dq/du = A_c(u) q + h(u)
    h = (A_c-A_b)y_b - r_b
    r_b = dy_b/du - A_b y_b - f.

The saved reference interpolant's residual r_b must remain unless its
vanishing is proved. The corrected and reference histories are not assumed
to solve the same operator equation exactly.

Here t=ln(theta/theta0)=0.875 ln(10)-u, hence du/dt=-1. With A=A_c(u(t))
and h=h(u(t)),

    dq/dt = -Aq-h
    Delta S(t) = L(t)q(t)
    d Delta S/dt = (L_t-LA)q-Lh.

Differentiating again without commuting any operators gives

    d² Delta S/dt²
      = [L_tt-2L_t A+L(A²-A_t)]q + (LA-2L_t)h-L h_t.

Thus source curvature contains physical history forcing and mixed
operator/history terms even if L itself varies smoothly. A source-value
polynomial can miss those terms. Stage209 does not estimate them from source
fit residuals: it evolves the existing history equation to obtain q_* and
applies the anchor maps directly to that state.

The remaining frozen prediction discrepancy has the exact form

    P_*-L_*q_* = (sum_i w_i L_i-L_*)q_*.

This removes only the interpolation of different history states. If the
computed history differs from a continuum history by delta q, a corresponding
additional source error is L_* delta q (and the physical/discretization
operator may have its own error). The half/quarter and80/100-digit checks
measure particular numerical differences; they are not substituted for a
proved operator-norm bound or a new fundamental closure.
