## Source slopes also have their own variational test

For the inherited first-spatial-derivative bulk wave action plus the proper-clock source action, if the one-sided source slopes are freely varied while q(b)=0, native field values and b are held fixed, the source variation contains -T*delta_J. Bulk integration by parts leaves only a trace-value boundary term, which vanishes under the Dirichlet trace. In a classical solution without an additional slope-dependent boundary action, stationarity therefore requires T=0. Imposing J as an essential boundary restriction would instead be an additional assumption, not this derivation. This statement is conditional on the stated inherited action and allowed variations, not a claim about an unspecified parent action.

Since

    T = C_h*J - z^T*W*r,        C_h=z^T*W*z,

C_h is positive whenever W is positive and z is nonzero. On a time-symmetric slice with J=0, preservation requires

    C_h*J_ddot = (z^T*W*r)_ddot.

At fixed coupled right-hand sides a residual T_ddot is corrected by delta_J_ddot=-T_ddot/C_h. If the initial source slopes and curvatures match, the regular bulk equation gives J_ddot=c_b^2*[ell]_b. Therefore the source third derivatives need not match: their jump can own this extra condition. Both the metric response and the native trace accelerations must be updated after changing the profile.
