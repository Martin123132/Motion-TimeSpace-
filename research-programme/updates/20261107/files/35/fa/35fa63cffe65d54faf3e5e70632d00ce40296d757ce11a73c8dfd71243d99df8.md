## 4. A useful exact separation

The physical third-divided-difference kernel has exactly the sampled quadratics as its nullspace. The inherited Gram template is injective because its diagonal-square margins are positive; their exact minimum here is185/3528. Consequently a source-flat field with J=0 and at least three zero native samples cannot have zero native energy and also remain nonzero on the native support.

This statement holds independently of the positive metric weights. It does not exclude finite inter-knot bubbles, and zero energy is sufficient but not necessary for all shape constraints to vanish. The distinction prevents recycling the old null-energy shortcut as if it solved this source-flat construction.

## 5. Construct the next family rather than repeat this one

Use a physical source core, y=R-b,

    q=p*y+(k/2)*y^2,
    k=-(d+a_b)*p/c^2,
    c=N*sqrt(F), d=2*c^2/b+c*partial_R c.

Both source traces now have the same slope and curvature. A sufficiently wide quadratic core annihilates the native source-neighbourhood stencils without forcing the whole annulus to be quadratic. An explicit C3 taper joins it to the interior and zero endpoint buffers. This avoids the former endpoint-imposed opposite source slopes.

The curvature law is implicit: a_b must come from the full coupled equations at the same state, and c,d from its own metric. It is not a fitted source acceleration. The next numerical task is to build these core columns and attempt the joint source-corner/shape solve, retaining withheld tests; the joint solve is not executed in this checkpoint.

For a general non-null constrained state at zero initial rates, the first preservation test is also derived:

    B_i,ddot = 2*q_ddot^T Q_i q + q^T Q_i,ddot q.

Do not freeze Q_i in this equation. The metric's second derivative can include the velocity-Hessian contribution from newly developing kinetic energy, even though all initial velocities vanish. The previous quartic radical formula is a special case, not the generic test for this new family.
