# Stage69: an evolved, coupled finite-wave response

7 October 2026. This extends the retained parent branch, not a replacement

Stage65 supplies the canonical field vertex, Stage66 the Einstein/kinetic

## 1. State and normalization

Use conformal synchronous coordinates, c=8 pi G=1 in the inherited field
normalization, with comoving lengths and conformal time in Mpc:

    ds^2 = a^2[-d tau^2 + C_ij dx^i dx^j],
    A = (1/2) C^-1 C', theta = tr A, H = a'/a, Eframe = sqrt(C).

The background density variables contain the inherited 8 pi G normalization
and have units Mpc^-2; the canonical fields are in the corresponding Planck
normalization. Newton's constant is calibrated, not derived anew here.

All perturbative coefficients use the no-factorial convention. A quadratic
ordered pair means Sym(X_p Y_q)=(X_p Y_q+X_q Y_p)/2. The two first-order
modes are p=.1 e_x and q=.1 e_y, with K=p+q, in Mpc^-1. There are four
independently stored response blocks: linear p, linear q, linear K per unit
primordial zeta2, and the quadratic p,q particular response at zeta2=0.
The particular response includes its required nonlinear regular initial
geometry; it does NOT mean setting every second-order initial variable to
zero. No primordial spectrum, covariance or numerical zeta2 is assigned.

For baryons evolve the conserved particle number D_b=sqrt(det C) gamma n_b/
n_b0, where n_b0 is the homogeneous physical density at the same a,
and W_i=P_i/a. The physical velocity to second order is
v1=W1, v2=W2-(1/2)Sym(h1 W1). Canonical W is not coordinate velocity.
For photons evolve the local transverse polarization matrix J(n); for
ultrarelativistic neutrinos evolve the scalar bolometric brightness F(n).
Angular averages use normalized sphere measure. Their energy, flux and
stress are <I>, <n I>, <nn I>, with I=tr J or F. Both canonical fields and
their derivatives are kept; the heavy field is never averaged into dust in
this interval. Cold-control parameters are inherited, not re-fitted.

## 2. Gravity and field coupling

The retained exact mixed spatial Einstein equation is

    A' = -2 H A - R[C] - H theta I - theta A
         + a^2 {delta S + (delta E - tr delta S) I/2}.

The background part has been subtracted using its own Friedmann equation.
Together with C'=2 C A this generates every quadratic metric product used
by `coupled_system.py`. R[C] is the mixed three-dimensional Ricci tensor;
raising its first index matters at quadratic order. The constraint pair is

    Hc = R + 4 H theta + theta^2 - tr(A^2) - 2 a^2 delta E,
    Mc_i = partial_i theta - D_j A^j_i - a j_i.

The covariant physical momentum density is j_i, not the brightness flux
alone. The local radiation stress is converted as
j=a Eframe F and S=Eframe^-1 S_local Eframe. Consequently j2 contains
(a/2)Sym(h F1), and S2 contains (1/2)Sym([S1,h]). Removing those terms
would spoil the momentum constraint and the matter conservation check.

For each canonical field, with background phi and perturbations u1,u2,

    u1''+2 H u1'+(k^2+a^2 m^2)u1 = -theta1 phi',
    u2''+2 H u2'+(K^2+a^2 m^2)u2 = -theta2 phi'
      -Sym(theta1 u1')
      +Sym([K.h_p.q -(p.q) tr(h_p)/2] u_q).

Its quadratic energy, pressure, gradient stress and momentum are included
in the metric source. Thus this is not a field equation driven by an
externally prescribed native metric: fields, geometry and ordinary species
change each other during the integration.

## 3. Radiation and baryon exchange

The orthonormal screen is transported, rather than frozen under nonparallel
mixing. At first order, writing S=A1,

    Omega_ab = n^c (partial_a h_bc - partial_b h_ac)/2,
    n' = Omega n - S n + (n.S.n)n,
    T_screen = Omega + n n^T S,
    u_ray1 = -h n/2.

For a first-order photon matrix, the quadratic gravitational source is the
symmetric p,q product of coordinate streaming, sphere-direction advection,
bolometric redshift and screen transport:

    -i(q.u_ray1p) Jq - n'_p.grad_n Jq
    -4(n.A_p.n)Jq + T_p Jq + Jq T_p^T.

The isotropic background gives the separate linear-in-A2 source
-2(n.A2.n)P, P=I-nn. For scalar neutrino brightness the two matrix screen
terms are absent and its background redshift is -4(n.A2.n). Background
screen advection and screen transport cancel; neither may simply be
discarded when acting on the anisotropic first-order matrix.

Use the Stage67 polarized elastic Thomson operator with kappa=a n_e sigma_T.
The baryon rest-density perturbation multiplies the photon collision
operator. The opposite baryon force is derived from its moments, including
conversion to canonical W. Density cancels from acceleration because both
electron number and baryon inertia share the same local density at fixed
ionization. At quadratic order the additional force is

    kappa (rho_gamma/rho_b) [ (1/2)Sym(h_p F_q)
                            -Sym((S_gamma,p+E_gamma,p I)W_q) ].

The cold baryon number equation supplies
D_b2'=-iK.W2-iK.Sym(D_b1 W1-h1 W1), and the canonical convective force is
-i Sym((q.W_p) W_q). Baryon kinetic energy and stress are retained even
though thermal pressure is omitted in this bounded early model.

An independently evaluated boosted collision law checks the implemented
Taylor coefficient. Its quadratic angular moments obey

    <tr C2> = -v1.F1 + (4/3) v1.v1,
    <n tr C2> = delta_b(-F1+4v1/3) + (E1 I+S1)v1,

before adding the linear action on J2 and v2. The first line is kinetic
work; it is not lost energy or a new energy sink.

## 4. Conservation and constrained initial data

For the total stress, conservation in this chart reads

    E' + 3 H E + H tr S + theta E + tr(A S)
       + (1/a) D_i(C^-1 j)^i = 0,
    j_i' + (3 H+theta)j_i + a D_j S^j_i = 0.

These equations provide checks independent of evolving Einstein's
constraints. The implementation differentiates the constructed total
stress along the full coupled right-hand side, including collisions, and
compares the result to these identities at several stored times. Multiple
finite-difference steps test cancellation error. The test is numerical
evidence for the implementation, not a replacement for its stated scope.

The seed includes the derived matter-loading, photon viscosity and
neutrino odd-multipole corrections. Gradient and finite-mass remainders at
a finite starting time are not assumed to vanish. A longitudinal/trace
adjustment of A solves the actual initial Hamiltonian and momentum
constraints, without changing its transverse-traceless part. Before this
projection, A2's antisymmetric part is fixed by C A being symmetric.
The adjustment sizes are recorded. Constraints are NOT projected after
initialization, so later violations cannot be hidden by repeated resets.
Moving the starting scale from 1e-6 to 5e-7 tests finite-start sensitivity.

## 5. Stiff evolution and accuracy protocol

Background fields and Friedmann expansion are integrated self-consistently.
The polarized collision operator's exact frozen projectors evolve the stiff
photon/baryon relaxation modes. A second-order exponential predictor and
corrector uses phi1 and phi2. The frozen operator is evaluated at the step
midpoint; [L(t)-L_mid]y is included in the remainder at both endpoints.
This includes varying baryon loading and opacity rather than silently
treating the operator as constant over the history.

Every full-step refinement divides ALL steps, including the initial
fractional-time steps, by two and then four. Separate cap-only preliminary
refinements are retained but not substituted for this check. The angular
calculation differentiates Cartesian components on the sphere through
spherical harmonics; lmax=6 and8 use over-resolved product quadratures.
Endpoint density, flux, canonical-field and metric-trace responses are
compared separately, not hidden inside a norm dominated by a constant
initial metric. `compare_refinements.py` records all 48 component-group
comparisons per refinement. Zero heavy fields in the cold control stay zero.

The first full time refinements failed the frozen0.001 flow-response gate
in BOTH models. They are preserved in `results/refinements/status.json`,
not reclassified as passes because the gravity residuals were small.
Further eight-way and sixteen-way subdivisions target the SAME gate;
their decision is recorded in `results/accepted-refinements/status.json`.
The final time-refined principal packet uses lmax6 and start a=1e-6.
Angular and start-time probes remain the independent quarter-step tests;
the result does not assert a rigorous joint-finest-grid error certificate.

The saved constraint maximum is over the 41 stored samples, not a rigorous
supremum over continuous time. Its Hamiltonian normalizer is
k^2 max(1,||metric||), and the momentum normalizer adds max(1,tau).
Report this convention rather than presenting these numbers as direct
observational fractional errors.

## 6. Physical approximation bounds

Let y=a m_nu/T_nu0 in the inherited thermal-neutrino normalization. For a
Fermi-Dirac distribution, sqrt(q^2+y^2)-q <= y^2/(2q) gives

    0 <= [rho_nu(y)-rho_nu(0)]/rho_nu(0) <= 5 y^2/(7 pi^2).

This is a background-energy bound, not a uniform bound on all nonlinear
perturbation moments. Direct integration checks it. Native thermal history
also supplies baryon sound speed, electron temperature, ionization and
opacity comparisons. Fully ionized opacity is checked against that model,
not claimed as a new derivation of recombination.

Electron thermal/recoil corrections are more important than numerical
roundoff: `bound_early_scope.py` evaluates the inherited cold-electron
Klein-Nishina diagnostic and thermal expansion parameter. A cross-section
change is NOT directly a bound on cosmological transfer error. Full thermal
Compton energy exchange, massive-neutrino phase-space evolution and later
recombination need their proper equations before extending the interval
into a precision CMB prediction. This is an explicit early controlled
subsystem, not a silent extrapolation to the whole observable history.
