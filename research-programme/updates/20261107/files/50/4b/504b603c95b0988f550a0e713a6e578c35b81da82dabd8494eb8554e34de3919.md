## 2. Scattering strength, rather than fitted friction

For an incident nonrelativistic, low-energy electromagnetic wave, the
Lorentz equation gives the electron acceleration amplitude e E/m_e. The
far-zone radiated electric field is proportional to
e n_out cross (n_out cross acceleration)/(4 pi epsilon_0 c^2 r).
Dividing its Poynting flux by incident flux gives, for specified incoming
and outgoing transverse polarizations,

    d sigma/d Omega = r_e^2 |epsilon_out* dot epsilon_in|^2,
    r_e = e^2/(4 pi epsilon_0 m_e c^2).

Summing outgoing and averaging incoming polarizations gives

    d sigma/d Omega = (r_e^2/2)(1+cos^2 chi),
    sigma_T = (8 pi/3) r_e^2.

The saved NIST CODATA2022 table gives sigma_T=6.6524587051e-29 m^2.
The rounded radius reproduces this to relative2.07e-11. The inherited
native value is6.6524587321e-29 m^2, a relative4.06e-9 difference; it is
recorded, not silently replaced. See `results/calibration/status.json`
and `sources/nist-codata-2022.txt`.

This is the usual calibrated EM interaction, derived here in the conventions
needed by the coupled solver. It is not a novel Thomson-scattering claim.

## 3. Polarization-resolved local collision map

Use a local normal orthonormal frame, signature(-,+,+,+), c=1. Let n be a
photon direction, P(n)=I-n n^T, and J(n) a Hermitian screen intensity matrix:
P J P=J. It is bolometric; tr J is the intensity. Normalize angular averages
as <f>=(1/4 pi) integral f d Omega. Common physical units reside in J.

    E_gamma = <tr J>,
    F_gamma_i = <n_i tr J>,
    S_gamma_ij = <n_i n_j tr J>.

The Maxwell polarization amplitude gives the electron-rest-frame gain

    G[J](n) = (3/2) P(n) <J> P(n),
    C_rest[J] = n_e sigma_T (G[J]-J).

The coefficient before an unnormalized angular integral is3/(8 pi).
For J=I_gamma P/2, G[J]=J. No angular fluid closure is imposed.

### Moving electrons: exact within the cold, elastic Thomson limit

For electron velocity v, define gamma=(1-v^2)^(-1/2),

    D = gamma(1-v dot n),
    n_electron = [n + (gamma^2/(gamma+1)(v dot n)-gamma)v]/D,
    B = [I + gamma^2/(gamma+1) v v^T
           + gamma n_electron v^T] P(n).

The screen map obeys B^T B=P(n) and B B^T=P(n_electron).
Lorentz invariance of the occupation tensor gives

    J_electron(n_electron) = D^4 B J(n) B^T,
    d Omega_electron = D^(-2) d Omega,
    M_electron = <D^2 B J B^T>.

The electron-rest proper density n_e sets the rate. Transform the gain back
to the normal screen and include the encounter-rate Doppler factor D:

    C_normal[J]/(n_e sigma_T)
       = (3/2) D^(-3) B^T M_electron B - D J.                 (67.1)

Equation67.1 is exact in bulk velocity for the elastic Thomson model; its
physical use still requires small photon energy in the electron rest frame
and negligible electron thermal recoil. A conformal-time equation multiplies
the proper normal-frame rate by the lapse a used in Stage66.

## 4. The force required by the Einstein conservation equations

Let u^a=gamma(1,v), E_u=T_gamma^{ab}u_a u_b and

    F_u^a = -T_gamma^{ab}u_b - u^a E_u,
    f_b^a = n_e sigma_T F_u^a,
    f_gamma^a = -f_b^a.                                      (67.2)

The angular zeroth/first moments of67.1 equal f_gamma^a.
Also u_a F_u^a=0: scattering transfers no energy in a cold electron's
instantaneous rest frame, but generally does transfer normal-frame energy.
Dropping the normal-frame energy term at second order would violate the
metric-side Ward identity derived in Stage66.

Expand without factorials: v=epsilon v1+epsilon^2 v2,
T=T0+epsilon T1+epsilon^2 T2 and
n_e=n_e0(1+epsilon delta_e1+epsilon^2 delta_e2).
For an isotropic background with w_gamma0=4 rho_gamma0/3,

    f_b^{0,(2)}/(n_e0 sigma_T)
      = v1 dot F_gamma1 - w_gamma0 v1^2,

    f_b^{i,(2)}/(n_e0 sigma_T)
      = F_gamma2_i - w_gamma0 v2_i
        -(delta E_gamma1+delta P_gamma1)v1_i
        -pi_gamma1_ij v1_j
        +delta_e1(F_gamma1_i-w_gamma0 v1_i).                 (67.3)

At first order only the vector F_gamma1-w_gamma0 v1 remains. The scalar
in67.3 equals baryon kinetic work at this order when baryon number is
conserved. Both energy and momentum transfers cancel between matter and
radiation. Background expansion and transport are separate Liouville terms.

Electron density is not automatically baryon density: delta_e1 includes
ionization perturbations. Near recombination a perturbation of x_e must be
computed or bounded, not suppressed by choosing a notation. The mean opacity
in the calibration test is taken from the unchanged native thermal history.

## 5. Explicit quadratic polarized collision operator

Take J=J0+epsilon J1+epsilon^2 J2 and v=epsilon w+epsilon^2 z.
Write s=n dot w, t=n dot z. The required expansions are

    D = 1-epsilon s+epsilon^2(w^2/2-t),
    B = P+epsilon B1+epsilon^2 B2,
    B1 = n w^T P,
    B2 = (s n w^T-w w^T/2+n z^T)P,
    D^2 = 1-2epsilon s+epsilon^2(s^2+w^2-2t),
    D^(-3) = 1+3epsilon s+epsilon^2(6s^2-3w^2/2+3t).

Define

    U1 = J1+B1 J0+J0 B1^T,
    U2 = J2+B1 J1+J1 B1^T+B2 J0+J0 B2^T+B1 J0 B1^T,
    M0 = <J0>,
    M1 = <U1-2s J0>,
    M2 = <U2-2s U1+(s^2+w^2-2t)J0>,
    H0 = (3/2) P M0 P,
    H1 = (3/2)(P M1 P+B1^T M0 P+P M0 B1),
    H2 = (3/2)(P M2 P+B1^T M1 P+P M1 B1
                +B2^T M0 P+P M0 B2+B1^T M0 B1),
    C0 = H0-J0,
    C1 = H1+3s H0-J1+s J0,
    C2 = H2+3s H1+(6s^2-3w^2/2+3t)H0
         -J2+s J1-(w^2/2-t)J0.

The complete second coefficient is

    C^{(2)} = n_e0 sigma_T [C2+delta_e1 C1+delta_e2 C0].       (67.4)

For the isotropic background C0=0. J2 and z remain unknown response fields
in67.4; they are not discarded. Setting them to zero only isolates the
quadratic source when assembling a retarded evolution operator.

`check_collision_operator.py` evaluates the analytic coefficients separately
from the unexpanded Lorentz map. Symmetric amplitude refinement decreases
the second-coefficient error from1.14028e-3 to4.44924e-6. Four-velocity and
polarization moment tests exercise both energy and all three momentum
components, not just a scalar drag coefficient.

## 6. Exact relaxation modes remove the stiff collision timescale

For M=<J>, let M_iso=I tr M/3, M_tf=(M+M^T)/2-M_iso,
and M_anti=(M-M^T)/2. The spherical fourth-moment identity gives

    <G[J]> = [7 M_sym+5 M_anti+I tr M]/10.

Consequently the isotropic energy mode has collision eigenvalue0,
the symmetric trace-free polarization mode -3 kappa/10,
the antisymmetric/circular mode -kappa/2, and all remaining angular modes
-kappa, with kappa=a n_e sigma_T in conformal time. The projectors are

    J_iso = (3/2) P M_iso P,
    J_tf = (15/7) P M_tf P,
    J_anti = 3 P M_anti P,
    J_fast = J-J_iso-J_tf-J_anti.

The rest-frame frozen-coefficient semigroup multiplies these four components
by1, exp(-3 depth/10), exp(-depth/2), exp(-depth), respectively. Its
positive-gain/loss construction preserves positive physical polarization
matrices; direct tests check this and energy conservation up to depth1000.

For a coupled linear photon/baryon state, remove the photon dipole from the
fast component and diagonalize it jointly with baryon velocity. Define

    R=3 rho_b/(4 rho_gamma), v_gamma=3 F_gamma/(4 rho_gamma),
    V=(v_gamma+R v_b)/(1+R), Delta=v_gamma-v_b.

At fixed background, collisions give

    V'=0, Delta'=-kappa(1+1/R)Delta.

Thus the stiff relative velocity relaxes exactly, with total momentum
unchanged. Streaming, gravity and slowly changing backgrounds can be evolved
around this exact block. The solver uses exponential/phi1/phi2 functions in
a second-order exponential time step with frozen midpoint kappa,R. It does
not mistake a frozen exact propagator for exact time-dependent evolution;
time and angular refinement are measured separately.

### Deriving the native32/45 quadrupole

For an axial scalar perturbation with axis d, mu=n dot d and
S=shear_scalar+k v_gamma_scalar, the traceless metric/streaming forcing is
-2S(mu^2-1/3)P. Applying the inverse anisotropic collision block gives

    J_quad = (S/kappa)[-2(mu^2-1/3)P
                     +(4/3)P(d d^T-I/3)P].

With the native scalar convention F2=-<P2(mu) tr J>,

    F2 = (32/45) S/kappa.

The coefficient follows from the polarized inverse operator, not a fit.
Native v_photon is the flux contrast and equals4v_gamma_scalar/3 here.
The independent integrator uses this expression only for the starting
quadrupole, then evolves the full angular state without a tight-coupling
closure. Native density/velocity initial data are retained.

## 7. Attachment to the Stage66 canonical metric variables

With spatial metric a^2 C, canonical momentum Q n and
e(n)=sqrt(n^T C^(-1)n), define the physical direction
r=C^(-1/2)n/e. At a point,

    d Omega_r = det(C)^(-1/2) e^(-3) d Omega_n,
    I_physical = a^(-4) e^4 I_canonical.

These factors reproduce the Stage66 kinetic stress measure: the photon
energy integrand is a^(-4)det(C)^(-1/2)e I_canonical. A collision source is
converted back with the inverse intensity factor and conformal lapse a.
Polarization additionally needs the screen/tetrad map; using the scalar
intensity relation on fixed matrix components without that map is invalid.

For Stage66 normal energy E and covariant momentum j_i, the added
conformal sources are a f^{hat0} and a^2(C^(1/2))_{i a} f^{hat a}.
Matter and radiation carry opposite signs. The metric dependence of this
local conversion and of the Liouville transport must each be included once,
not omitted or counted twice.

density on the saved heavy and cold metrics at k=0.1 Mpc^-1. It uses
a=1e-6 through0.000265,241 comparison times, native mean opacity and native

These are equation-to-equation calibration checks, not new observations.
The numerical agreement concerns the elastic Thomson model. The saved
