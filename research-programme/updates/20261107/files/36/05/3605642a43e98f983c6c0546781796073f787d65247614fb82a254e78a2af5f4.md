## 1. Conventions and domain

Use the dimensionless coexistence units of
`../self-gravity-20261003/10-self-gravity-and-weak-binding-law.md`.
Write the radial coordinate as r rather than s, and

    ds² = -N(t,r)² dt² + dr²/F(t,r) + r² dΩ²,
    F = 1 - 2α m/r,       N(infinity)=1,
    C = (a+i b)/sqrt(2),
    W(h) = 3 h^(4/3)/8 + h^4/8,       h=|a+i b|,
    α = 4π G_R v_*².

This is polar-areal, zero-shift gauge, on a horizonless spherical patch
F>0. It is not a coordinate system for continuing through horizon formation.
The action is the retained minimal Einstein-complex-scalar action. Extra
parent operators and other matter have not been inserted or integrated out
anew here. The global charge below is not identified as electric charge.

Factor 4π out of the reduced action. Canonical radial momenta are

    p_a = r² a_t/(N sqrt(F)),       p_b = r² b_t/(N sqrt(F)).

In this gauge K_theta^theta=K_phi^phi=0; only radial extrinsic curvature
can remain. Thus K_extrinsic²-K_ij K^ij=0 in the Hamiltonian constraint,
even for a time-dependent scalar. The momentum constraint fixes the radial
extrinsic curvature. In spherical symmetry there is no additional radiative
gravitational degree of freedom. Gauge fixing R=r and its polar-slicing
conjugate condition removes the gravitational contribution to the reduced
symplectic form, leaving the canonical matter pairs above.

Regular centre, finite mass, asymptotically flat normalization, vanishing
boundary flux, and the compact-profile form domain are assumptions throughout.

## 2. Exact elimination of the spherical constraints

Define

    K = a_r²+b_r²+(p_a²+p_b²)/r^4,
    rho = F K/2+W,                p_radial = F K/2-W.

The constraint m'=r² rho is a LINEAR equation for m for specified canonical
matter data:

    m' + α r K m = r²(K/2+W),       m(0)=0.                 (1)

The polar lapse condition gives

    (log(N/sqrt(F)))' = α r K.

Consequently

    Xi(r) = exp[-integral_r^infinity α t K(t) dt] = N/sqrt(F),
    m(r) = Xi(r)^(-1) integral_0^r Xi(t)t²[K(t)/2+W(t)]dt,
    H = m(infinity),              e_ADM = 4π H.            (2)

Xi in the integral for m includes the same normalization as its prefactor;
the common exterior constant cancels. The reduced Hamiltonian is the ADM
boundary mass, with the time coordinate normalized at infinity. The conserved
charge, in the same 4π-factored normalization, is

    Qbar = q/(4π) = integral(a p_b-b p_a)dr.                (3)

Linearizing (1), with F held only inside the displayed background coefficients,

    δm' + α r K δm = r²(F δK/2+δW),
    δm = Xi^(-1) integral_0^r Xi t²(F δK/2+δW)dt,
    δH = integral Xi r²(F δK/2+δW)dr.                     (4)

The factor F in (4) is essential: differentiating an unredshifted matter
integral while ignoring the mass constraint does not give the ADM variation.

Put A=Xi F=N sqrt(F). Hamilton's equations are

    a_t = A p_a/r²,                 b_t = A p_b/r²,
    p_a,t = (A r² a')' -Xi r² W_a,
    p_b,t = (A r² b')' -Xi r² W_b.                        (5)

They reproduce the minimally coupled Klein-Gordon equations. Two useful
independent consistency identities follow directly:

    m_t = Xi F²(p_a a'+p_b b'),
    (a p_b-b p_a)_t = [A r²(a b'-b a')]'.                (6)

For the first, substitute (5) into K_t and W_t. The candidate mass flux
g=Xi F²(p_a a'+p_b b') satisfies

    g'+αrK g = r²(F K_t/2+W_t).

This is the time derivative of (1), with the same zero central value, proving
constraint propagation locally. The second identity integrates to conserved
Qbar when the endpoint flux vanishes. The algebraic mass-flux test is executed
in `derive_radial_hamiltonian.py`, not inferred from a good stationary fit.

## 3. Rotating background and first-order geometric response

In the frame rotating at w=omega_infinity/mu_*, the Hamiltonian is H-w Qbar.
The stationary state at a chosen phase has

    a=h(r), b=0, p_a=0, p_b=P=r² w h/A.

Perturb (a,b,p_a,p_b) by (u,v,pi_u,pi_v). Then

    δK=2h'u'+2P pi_v/r^4,       δW=W' u,
    δQbar=integral(Pu+h pi_v)dr=0,                        (7)
    δF=-2α δm/r,
    δXi/Xi=-integral_r^infinity α t δK(t)dt,
    δN/N=δXi/Xi+δF/(2F),        δA=Xi δF+F δXi.          (8)

The charge condition is GLOBAL. Setting a local density perturbation to zero
pointwise would remove physical modes and impose a different problem.
At fixed charge, background stationarity also gives δH=w δQbar=0: the
first-order exterior mass perturbation is zero. This does not remove interior
δm or the interior lapse response.

The complete rotating-frame linear equations are

    u_t = A pi_u/r²+w v,
    v_t = A pi_v/r²+δA P/r²-w u,
    pi_u,t = [A r²u'+δA r²h']' -Xi r² W''u-δXi r²W'+w pi_v,
    pi_v,t = (A r²v')' -Xi r²(W'/h)v-w pi_u.             (9)

Equations (4), (7)--(9) are the coupled radial matter--geometry system. No
extra lapse, shell stiffness, density-dependent force, or independent wall
parameter has been fitted.

## 4. Canonical energy Hessian, including gravitational feedback

Differentiating (1) twice gives

    δ²m'+αrK δ²m = r²[F δ²K/2+δ²W]-2αr δK δm,
    δ²K=2[u'²+v'²+(pi_u²+pi_v²)/r^4],
    δ²W=W''u²+(W'/h)v².

Hence the second derivative (not the coefficient of epsilon²) is

    B = δ²(H-w Qbar)
      = integral Xi {r²F(u'²+v'²)+F(pi_u²+pi_v²)/r²
                     +r²[W''u²+(W'/h)v²]-2αr δK δm}dr
        -2w integral(u pi_v-v pi_u)dr.                  (10)

The physical dimensionless energy Hessian is 4π B. We report eigenvalues
of the 4π-factored form with the specified radial L² normalization, NOT
oscillation frequencies or dimensional masses. The nonlocal term in (10)
is the result of solving the Einstein constraint, not a guessed Newton
binding term. It is not discarded in the spectrum calculation.

## 5. Phase sector and exact flat-space check

The odd pair (v,pi_u) has δK=δm=δXi=0 at first order. Its form is

    B_odd = integral {A r² v'²
                +[Xi r² W'/h-w²r²/A]v²
                +A/r²(pi_u+w r²v/A)²}dr.

The background scalar equation and integration by parts turn this into

    B_odd = integral A r² h²[(v/h)']²dr
            +integral A/r²(pi_u+w r²v/A)²dr >=0.         (11)

This statement is conditional on the stated domain and vanishing boundary
term [A r² h h'(v/h)²]. The phase zero is v=h, pi_u=-P. It is a U(1)
symmetry, not an instability. The numerical phase zero must converge to zero.

At α=0, Xi=F=1. Minimizing (10) over the momenta at fixed (u,v) and δQ=0
gives pi_u=-w r²v and

    pi_v = w r²u - (2w/I_r)r²h integral r²hu dr,
    I_r = integral r²h²dr.

The even form becomes precisely

    integral r²[u'²+(W''-w²)u²]dr
      +(4w²/I_r)(integral r²hu dr)².                    (12)

This is the previously accepted fixed-charge, rank-one flat operator.
In flat space (9) likewise gives

    u_tt = Laplacian(u)-(W''-w²)u+2w v_t,
    v_tt = Laplacian(v)-(W'/h-w²)v-2w u_t.

Recovering (12) is a stronger control than merely obtaining positive new
numbers. The finite Galerkin version uses the projection of h into its
momentum basis; its inertia converges to I_r, so comparison to the continuum
rank-one formula need not be machine-exact at a finite mesh.

## 6. Compact endpoint and admissible variations

For d=R-r and F_s=F(R), h~(12F_s)^(-3/2)d³. Therefore

    W''~2F_s/d²,           W'/h~6F_s/d².

After division by the positive gradient coefficient, the regular reduced
amplitude operator solution behaves as d²; the regular phase solution as d³.
A first-order shift of the support edge produces u proportional to h'~d²:
the analysis does not artificially exclude movement of the surface. The
singular inverse-square coefficients are retained, not replaced by a mass
around vacuum. The potential is C¹ but not C² at zero.

Piecewise-linear Galerkin functions are used in the energy form domain for
the reduced variables r u and pi_v/r, with zero endpoint values. They need
not individually satisfy the operator-domain d² asymptotic, just as form
trial functions need not solve the eigen-equation. The weighted singular
potential integral is finite. This is an interior/free-edge tangent test;
it is not a proof controlling arbitrary nonlinear exterior vacuum creation.

## 7. Charge-constrained numerical reduction

Assemble the even form for coefficients x (r u) and y (pi_v/r):

    B_even=x^T Haa x+2x^T Hab y+y^T Hbb y,
    c_a^T x+c_b^T y=0.

When Hbb is positive, define

    D=Hbb,
    Hfree=Haa-Hab D^(-1)Hab^T,
    c_eff=c_a-Hab D^(-1)c_b,
    Hfixed=Hfree+c_eff c_eff^T/(c_b^T D^(-1)c_b).          (13)

Check Hbb's least generalized eigenvalue, not just whether a solver returned
a result. Diagonalize Hfixed against the radial L² mass matrix. Reconstruct
the minimizing y and directly verify both δQ=0 and the original form (10).
As a diagnostic only, compute the frozen-metric form omitting the nonlocal
gravity term; compare it to the coupled result rather than using it as
the physical stability conclusion. Also report the unconstrained negative
direction, demonstrating the role of charge conservation.

Gauss quadrature on each linear element integrates the Volterra constraint;
the within-cell primitive is the integral of the Gauss-node interpolation
polynomial, not a midpoint step approximation. Refine mesh and quadrature
separately. Reconstruct Xi and m independently from the background matter.

Positive Ritz/Galerkin values are numerical evidence, not certified continuum

The conserved quadratic form supplies the standard energy route to radial
