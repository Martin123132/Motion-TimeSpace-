## 4. What breaks, and the derived replacement test

The simple family is not dynamically invariant. In the flat radial-wave control its acceleration is a*(6-2*(b+sigma)/R), sigma=L or U, not a quadratic. Its third radial derivative is12*a*(b+sigma)/R^4. The fixed outer boundary also fails second-order classical corner compatibility. This rejects that smooth invariant-family proposal, not the existence of weak wave evolution or all states satisfying the larger shape constraint.

On the actual fresh curved metric, the interior scalar acceleration has a nonquadratic component. A degree2 projection leaves maximum residuals about9.84e-7/left and7.74e-7/right, relative L2 residuals3.04e-5/2.25e-5. This is an interior diagnostic; it is not a full weak field/source-acceleration solve.

The stronger useful result is that the null section n(x) lies in the RADICAL of the native shape forms: Q_i n=0, because both Dn and the complete transported derivative V_i n vanish. Thus

    B_i(q,x,g) = (q-n(x))^T Q_i(x,g) (q-n(x)).

At zero initial configuration rates, define

    A_normal = q_ddot - Dn_section[x_ddot].

For a sufficiently smooth admissible curve,

    B_i(t)=t^4*A_normal^T Q_i A_normal/4 + o(t^4),
    B_i^(4)(0)=6*A_normal^T Q_i A_normal.

Checking only initial B_i or its first time derivative would therefore be misleading. Source and mesh accelerations belong in A_normal; neither may be replaced by an arbitrary fitted acceleration.

The actual native kernel passes six two-grid/material-label oracle cases. Radical entries are below1.52e-73. Independent scalar-adjoint evaluation of q=n+t^2*w/2 agrees with the quartic law, including factor16 on halving t.
