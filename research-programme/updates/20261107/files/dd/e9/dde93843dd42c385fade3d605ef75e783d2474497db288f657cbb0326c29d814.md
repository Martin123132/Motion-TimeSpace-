# Corrected zero-position support force and current columns

The old-field reaction must complete successfully before this calculation
runs. All inputs are rehashed. We use the same two grids and both matched
reference/MTS cases, without changing their trajectories or subtracting a
reference baseline.

At s=0 the corrected velocity column uses

    delta qdot=e, delta W=eta+D e_source,
    delta qddot=edot-b1, delta Y=V,
    delta Y_t=X+Vdot-A_old(b1).

The independent acceleration column keeps positions and velocities fixed,
uses delta qddot=e and delta Wdot=eta+D e_source, and delta Y_t=V.
It is rebuilt and compared to the previously owned C2 column.

Differentiate the existing support Euler contraction, including all its
cell-face terms, and the inherited weak horizontal-action current for
h=1,(R-6),(R-6)^2. Field/source compensation has already satisfied the old
Euler equations to recorded tolerances. It is not a new force law.

The native current is linear in beta/beta_R and in field/map velocities.
Compute its derivative by the product rule and compare to a signed
70-digit simultaneous perturbation. Include its metric-coefficient change;
freezing beta would omit a term even though the native potential has no
explicit velocity dependence. The source proper-clock current is also
differentiated. The gravitational current column is

    integral N/(kappa sqrt(F)) *
      [delta mu_t + mu_t*(delta nu + delta mu/(R F))] * h dR.

The second term belongs in C1 and vanishes in C2. All weak moments are
evaluated at material orders48/64 and radial orders24/32. Baseline and C2
replays, cell-face identities, and spatial8/12/complex-step comparisons
check that the new column is inserted into the actual inherited test.

Finally form the four-row, two-column system

    residual_base + C1*sdot + C2*sddot = 0.

Column-normalized least squares and singular values are descriptive
diagnostics, not a fitted theory. Propagate material/radial quadrature
differences through the proposed amplitudes; record whether rank and
compatibility are numerically resolved. The resulting sampled uncertainty
also includes the explicitly solved old-reaction material48/64 change,
inserted into both acceleration and metric-time parts of C1. It
is not a rigorous error bound and excludes the inherited unresolved
pointwise mixed-time/position-density accuracy. Large amplitudes cannot
establish a finite-amplitude solution from this linearized calculation.

To avoid a conclusion depending on least-squares row weights, also
eliminate acceleration using the force row (when C2_force is nonzero):

    J_reduced = J_base - C2_J F_base/C2_force
                + (C1_J - C2_J C1_force/C2_force)*sdot.

Each of the three current conditions now specifies the same remaining
velocity amplitude if they are compatible. Solve force plus each current
in turn and test all remaining rows, with the same propagated sampled
uncertainty. This tests compatibility; it does not choose a new force law.
