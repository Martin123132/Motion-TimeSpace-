# Source acceleration after the transmission forces balance

This is a conditional reduction of the inherited annular action on its zero-rate slice, not an assumption that the full MTS parent has already reduced to GR. Use the existing diagonal metric ds^2=-N^2 dt^2+dR^2/F+R^2 dOmega^2, c^2=N^2 F, positive source mass M and the already retained proper-clock term

    L_source=-M*sqrt(N(b)^2-b_dot^2/F(b)).

At b_dot=0 its velocity Hessian is M/(F*N), and its position derivative is -M*N_R. If the other sectors exert zero net source force, its Euler equation is therefore

    a_source=-F*N*N_R=-c^2*(log N)_R.

This is also -Gamma^R_tt for the stated metric. The action fixes the acceleration; it is not an adjustable input.

## When does the field force actually vanish?

Write the two wave-action integrals on either side of the source. At fixed common trace q(b)=0, their potential variation under a source displacement is

    delta V_wave|source = [beta*q_R^2]*delta b/2.

One way to check the sign is to retain BOTH the interval-endpoint term and delta q=-q_R*delta b on each moving endpoint. The source wave force is the negative of this variation. It vanishes for continuous beta and equal one-sided source slopes. Do not drop it merely because the source field value is zero.

Ordinary native points contribute their complete shape covectors B_j=A_j*g_mean,j+C_j, paired with the source-support displacement. They are silent only when the transmission force balance is satisfied. The compensated source-hinge covector must also vanish; the quadratic source buffer supplies that condition in the exact compact-support construction. Boundary tractions and any additional parent source coupling must be retained if present. These are explicit premises, not a replacement for solving the coupled system. A finite P2/material projection can retain residuals and is checked numerically rather than declared exactly geodesic.

## Simplified source-curvature law

For a nonzero smooth source slope p and curvature k, compatibility with q(b(t),t)=0 at zero initial rates gives

    c^2*k+(d+a_source)*p=0,
    d=c^2*(2/b+(log N)_R+F_R/(2*F)).

Once the force-silence premises hold, substitution of the action-derived acceleration cancels the lapse derivative:

    k/p = -2/b-F_R/(2*F).

Thus the source-corner law need not remain an opaque fitted curvature rule. Its simple geometric form follows conditionally from the wave corner, the proper-clock action and balanced native/source tractions. For the current numerical pilot, compare the actual coupled acceleration to -F*N*N_R and the constructed curvature ratio to this expression; do not impose either as an extra equation or use them to hide a residual. Finite-time propagation remains separate.
