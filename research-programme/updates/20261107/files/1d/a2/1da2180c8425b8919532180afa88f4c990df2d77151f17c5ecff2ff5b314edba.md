The previous step established a static gravitational tidal response. This step
derives its time-dependent Einstein-scalar completion, including the internal

gravitational radiation. An actual outgoing, weakly damped quadrupole resonance

The earlier real-field/bath construction is not replaced by these calculations.

## 2. Definitions and conventions

Use the parent's dimensionless radius/time, with physical frequency equal to
mu_star times the reported frequency in c=hbar=1 units. The background is

    ds² = -N² dt² + dr²/F + r² dOmega²,
    C = h(r) exp(i w t)/sqrt(2),
    W(h) = 3 h^(4/3)/8 + h^4/8,
    alpha = 4 pi G_R v_star².

The normalization of C here uses the dimensionless amplitude h; physical amplitude
is v_star h. Write n=nu'=N'/N, b=w²/N², and

    rho = (F h'² + b h²)/2 + W,
    p   = (F h'² + b h²)/2 - W,
    F' = (1-F)/r - 2 alpha r rho,
    n  = [(1-F)/2 + alpha r² p]/(r F),
    A  = 2/r + n + F'/(2F) = (1+F-2 alpha r² W)/(r F),
    D  = W' - 2 b h - 2 F n h'.

Primes on W denote h derivatives; other primes denote radial derivatives.
For an l=2 harmonic Y, suppress exp(-i sigma t) and set

    delta g_tt = N² H Y,       delta g_rr = H Y/F,
    delta g_tr = -i sigma J Y,
    delta g_ab = r² K Y Omega_ab,
    delta h = u Y,             delta theta = i sigma s Y.

The equality of the independent tt/rr amplitudes follows from the traceless
angular Einstein equation. The variables J,s are scaled to have a regular static
limit; sigma=0 denotes that limit, not an arbitrary static phase perturbation.
For nonzero frequency the ansatz retains both real scalar degrees of freedom.
Complex frequency describes a pole of the retarded response, not a normalizable
standing-wave eigenfunction over the whole asymptotically flat exterior.

## 3. Derived interior equations

Direct variation of Ricci and of

    R_munu = 2 alpha [h_,mu h_,nu + h² theta_,mu theta_,nu + g_munu W]

and of the amplitude/current equations gives

    K' = (n-1/r) K + H/r + (3/r²-2 alpha b h²) J
         + 2 alpha w h² s' - 2 alpha h' u,

    J' = (H+K-4 alpha w h² s)/F - (A-2/r) J,

    H' = K' - 2 n H + 4 alpha h' u - sigma² J/N²,

    u'' = -A u'
          + [6/r² + W'' - b + 4 alpha F h'² - sigma²/N²] u/F
          + D H/F - 2 w h sigma² s/(F N²),

    s'' = -(A+2h'/h) s'
          + [6/r² - (sigma²+4 alpha w² h²)/N²] s/F
          - 2 w u/(F N² h) + (2w/N²)(1/r-n+h'/h) J.

There is one algebraic constraint, so these are not seven independent physical
radial integration constants. A readable form uses the previous static expression

    K_static = [(3-F)/2 + r² F n² + alpha r²(F h'²-b h²)/2] H
               + r² F n H'/2 - alpha r² F h' u'
               + [alpha r²(W'-b h)-2 alpha r F(1+r n)h'] u.

The exact dynamical constraint is

    0 = K - K_static
        + sigma² r/(2 N²) [2 alpha w r h² s + F(1-r n) J - r K].

Substitute the H' equation and then K' to solve algebraically for H. The coefficient
divided by is

    D_H = [7-3F + alpha r²(3F h'²-b h²-2W)]/4.

It stays above approximately 1 on the sampled weak bodies. Where D_H could vanish,
this particular elimination would require another formulation; no global theorem
about all backgrounds is inferred from the weak samples.

The numerical state is (K,J,u,u',s,s'). All coefficient functions of its algebraic
H reconstruction are saved in `results/reduction/status.json`. The unreduced
Einstein and field equations are in `results/derivation-v3/status.json`.
The constraint's preservation by this six-equation flow is checked symbolically,
not just imposed at one radius. `results/compact-constraint/status.json` verifies
that the compact constraint has precisely the old static part.

At alpha=0, with no imposed metric perturbation, v=i sigma h s converts these
equations into the previously derived rotating-field system

    u_tt - 2w v_t + L_plus,2 u = 0,
    v_tt + 2w u_t + L_minus,2 v = 0.

At sigma=0 the H/u subsystem and K constraint reproduce the full static gravity
problem. The divided phase/current equations determine a finite limiting s,J;
their actual phase and shift perturbations vanish with sigma. The first-order
global charge variation vanishes by the angular integral of Y, not by freezing
charge locally.

## 4. Moving compact surface: flow supplies the boundary law

Let d=R-r, F_s=1-2M/R, c_e=(12 F_s)^(-3/2), and h~c_e d³.
The finite-energy amplitude branch is u=a_e d²+..., rather than its d^-1 partner.
The regular phase variable has s=s0+s1 d+..., rather than its d^-5 partner;
the Cartesian phase perturbation h delta theta therefore starts at order d³.

The displacement and phase derivative are not independent:

    zeta = a_e/(3 c_e),
    s'_R = w a_e/(3 c_e F_s²) + w J_R/F_s.

This follows twice: from the leading 1/d coefficient of the current equation and
from charge transport. The limiting perturbed radial current velocity is

    delta(j^r/j^t) = -i sigma (F_s²/w)(s'_R-w J_R/F_s) Y.

It equals the velocity -i sigma zeta Y of the moving support. Thus the boundary
law comes from the field/current equations rather than a separately prescribed
surface rigidity or a frozen phase. The cubic background also makes the first
stress variation vanish at the edge; there is no added thin-shell stress.

The solver matches u through d^5, s through d^3, and K,J through d² to the analytic
edge. Its u cubic coefficient is still

    c3 = (8-C) a_e/[10 R(1-C)] + 3 c_e H_R/2,
    C = 2M/R.

Time dependence first changes subsequent coefficients. The recurrence is in
`dynamic_system.py`; nine static replays reproduce the earlier edge coefficients.
Direct dynamical Taylor substitutions show the expected quadratic current
remainder under edge-distance halving. This non-C2 vacuum is not assigned the
smooth-potential exponential scalar tail used in ordinary massive boson-star models.

At the center u,s,K~r² and J~r³; the leading relation is

    J = r(2K-4 alpha w h0² s)/3 + O(r^5).

## 5. Vacuum gravity and outgoing matching

Outside the compact support, all scalar sources vanish. With F=1-2M/r, introduce
the Zerilli variable Z and dr_star/dr=1/F. The l=2 equation is

    d²Z/dr_star² + [sigma² - V_Z] Z = 0,
    V_Z = F (24r³+24Mr²+36M²r+18M³)/[r³(2r+3M)²].

The reconstruction in our shift convention is

    K = (6r²+6Mr+6M²) Z/[r²(2r+3M)] + F Z',
    J = (2r²-6Mr-3M²) Z/[(r-2M)(2r+3M)] + r Z',
    H = [2r(r-2M)+M(r-3M)-sigma²r^4] K/[(r-2M)(2r+3M)]
        - (3M-sigma²r³) J/[r(2r+3M)].

Substitution checks all three vacuum metric equations exactly. This agrees with
the standard polar construction after J=-H1 in Macedo et al.'s convention; their
smooth-potential scalar tail and their numerical mode values are not imported.
Primary sources, checked 4 October 2026:
[arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.4812),
[paper, equations 61-63 and following reconstruction](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1307.4812),
[published DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.88.064046).

The outgoing condition is Z~exp(+i sigma r_star). Write x=sigma r,
mu=M sigma, Z=exp(i sigma r_star) sum a_n x^-n. The series recurrence follows
by substituting into

    [4x^5+4mu x^4-15mu²x³-18mu³x²] W_xx
    + [8i x^5+24i mu x^4+(18i mu²+8mu)x³+24mu²x²+18mu³x] W_x
    - [24x³+24mu x²+36mu²x+18mu³] W = 0.

For M=0 it terminates at W=1+3i/x-3/x², supplying an independent exact exterior
control. The curved runs integrate this outgoing solution inward, not a reflecting
boundary outward. The pole sets the difference between interior and outgoing
(dZ/dr_star)/Z at R to zero. Far phase, series order, and interior edge join are
varied. This direct exterior method targets the weakly damped pole here, not
arbitrary deeply damped resonances.

V_Z tends to zero at infinity: gravitational waves remain gapless. The computed
pole is a body-response timescale, not a positive gap in the total Einstein-field
spectrum. Its negative imaginary part describes outgoing damping with exp(-i sigma t).

## 7. What this means for the long-distance mechanics

sqrt(2M/D³); the l=m=2 forcing frequency is twice that. At D=20R it is
.0001964722, or .00960434 times this body's calculated quadrupole frequency.
At D=10R and 40R the respective ratios are .0271652 and .00339565.

This replaces the inappropriate radial-gap comparison by a relevant computed
quadrupole timescale. A small ratio is useful scale separation; it does not by
itself bound the entire driven response. Pole residues, the analytic background,
the exterior continuum, and the driving convention must enter that bound.
