# Stage168 — held-out temperature discrepancy in the final spectrum

## Result

The saved cubic held-out discrepancy has a resolved effect under the existing
time profile. Its final residual-shape norm is **0.137725–0.137759% of the current
MTS residual**, or **119.10–119.30 times Stage167's tracked partial numerical
allowance**. About **98.749–98.752% of its squared residual norm is below output
frequency x=1**.

This identifies temperature-history sensitivity that the tracked numerical
refinements do not explain away. It does not turn the partial allowance into a
total error bound, or the assumed time profile into a derived physical history.
No temperature curve, source bank, physical parameter or selected central
spectrum has been changed.

The actual nonzero baseline, current MTS and current baseline-plus-MTS spectra
are retained. The frozen-predictor counterfactual uses the same baseline and
readout. Adding that baseline gives the same held-out increment; it is not a
separate prediction or a refit of the baseline.

## What was propagated

For normalized terminal-interval nodes A, W, C, H, B at
s=0, 1/4, 1/2, 3/4, 1, respectively:

    P_cubic(3/4) = A/4 - W + 3C/2 + B/4
    E = H - P_cubic(3/4)
    D = L[b(s) E]
    frozen-predictor MTS = current saved MTS - D

Here L is the unchanged linear response and b is the pre-existing cold
sensitivity profile. The current saved history already contains H's correction:
adding D again would double-count it.

The repaired endpoints reduce the source miss from the previously quoted
1.811715–1.814435% to **1.674470–1.677507%**. The interpolation weights and source
accuracy threshold are unchanged. The discrepancy still exceeds the existing
1% source criterion; it has not been made to pass by fitting H.

For the cubic route the exact saved profile is

    b(s) = s(s-1/4)(s-1/2)(s-1) / (-3/128).

Its fourth divided-difference coefficient is -128 E/3. A quartic surrogate
would have fourth derivative -1024 E with respect to s. This is an
interpolation identity, **not** a fourth-temperature-derivative law. The stage
checks the identity against the saved interpolation at 129 phases per route.
One held-out temperature does not determine a unique discrepancy history.

## Independent source-moment audit

Forty raw source-moment checks in the saved interior-source combinations fail
the existing 1e-10 checks. They remain explicitly recorded. Numerical
propagation is not permission to relabel those physical-source checks as passes.

The historical response contract uses the independently stored energy moment,
its corresponding spectral pivot and opposite gas forcing. An additional audit
instead transports the raw spectral and gas moments, tracking their supplied
imbalance explicitly:

    d(E_radiation + chi*g + work)/dt = rate*(J_E_effective + J_g).

Subtracting the integrated imbalance closes the numerical ledger. The
unchanged homogeneous matrix and the historical closed case are checked
separately; this construction does not derive conservation for the raw source.

For the cubic cases, the raw-minus-reference residual sensitivity is at most
1.097e-9 of the Stage167 residual allowance. Its mu and y sensitivities are at
most 1.122e-7 and 2.190e-7 of their corresponding allowances. Thus this measured
moment-representation effect is too small to explain the held-out effect
within the tested response. Raw physical-source qualification remains open.

Two original smoke diagnostics failed in the new, near-pure-gas audit
direction. Those failures are retained, not erased. The old normalization
also reaches 4.315e-7 in production against its 1e-7 threshold. For this new
direction only, the additional full-original-equation backward-error test
includes the gas-driven collision term; its production maximum is 1.139e-9
against the same 1e-7 threshold. Ordinary channels, including the complete raw
defect, retain the original normalization. No evolution values changed in the
smoke comparison. See AUDIT-NORMALIZATION.md for the explicit distinction.
This is not a forward-error certificate and is not a raw-source repair.
