# Stage72 — conservative spectral exchange and the finite-shift route

## Scope and inherited variables

This extends Stage71, not a replacement MTS action. The calibrated heavy
and cold parents are unchanged. This is a local linearized collision
substep; expansion, free streaming and metric evolution are held fixed
during it. Optical depth is the time variable. The production observational
authority remains Stage64, not these collision tests.

Let x = photon energy/(k_B T_*), theta = k_B T_*/(m_e c^2),
n = (exp(x)-1)^(-1), w = n(1+n), and delta f = w X, with f the screen
polarization occupation tensor. Let r = delta T_e/T_* and v = delta u/c
along the scalar mode. The evolved positive heat capacity chi is NOT the
earlier MTS field with a similar symbol. All displayed operators below are
dimensionless per unit Thomson optical depth. Energy/momentum are in the
same reference-photon-density units used by the inherited parent.

Stage71 operators R,S,U act on photon angle/polarization; see
`../angular-thermal-source-20261007/71-angular-polarized-Compton-source-and-moment-coupling.md`.
Writing D=x d/dx, weight conjugation gives

    L X = (x^2 w)^(-1) d/dx[x^4 w dX/dx]
        = D^2 X + [3-x(1+2n)] D X,
    g = 2x(1+2n)-2x^2 w,
    A X = (R-1)X + theta{S L X + (U+2S)X + g(1-S)X}.

`derive_step.py` checks the conjugation symbolically. The common-temperature
and velocity vectors are q_T=x P and q_v=x(nhat.axis)P in tensor notation.
Their scalar I,Q representation is I=2x and I=2x mu respectively, Q=0.
The sourced temperature term is -theta Lx times P; it equals -A q_T.
Bulk motion gives -A q_v. Thus

    C = A[X-q_T r-q_v v].

The monopole constant P is the chemical-equilibrium null vector.

## Finite heat capacity, exchange and exact Schur solve

The conservative finite-volume frequency mass is M_i=integral_cell x^2w dx;
x_i is its energy-weighted centroid. Angular I,Q entropy weights are
diag(w_mu,w_mu)/2. With the reference normalization 2 I3,

    W = diag(M_i) tensor angular_metric / (2 I3),
    E = [M_i x_i tensor angular_number] / (2 I3),
    F = [M_i x_i tensor angular_flux] / (2 I3),
    W q_T = E^T, W q_v = F^T.

The frozen-background coupled equations are

    X' = C,  chi r' = -E C,  B v' = -F C,
    B = rho_b/rho_ref + 5 chi/3 > 0.

Number N X, energy E X+chi r and momentum F X+Bv are conserved.
Finite chi remains in the equations and solve: temperature is not replaced
by an instantaneous equilibrium prescription.

For an implicit stage alpha X-A X=h+c_T r+c_v v, solve the photon block
for p, t, b. Set X=p+t r+b v and solve

    [chi+E t, E b; F t, B+F b] [r;v]
      = [(chi h_r+E h)/alpha-E p; (B h_v+F h)/alpha-F p].

This is exact block elimination, not a closure or chi->0 approximation.
Midpoint uses alpha=2/dtau and the corresponding old-state histories.
The improved runner subtracts ONE equilibrium obtained from initial
invariants. It evolves the defect, without reprojecting or refitting it.

For complex Fourier amplitudes define the quadratic entropy defect

    H = (X^dagger W X + chi |r|^2 + B |v|^2)/2.

Putting Y=X-q_T r-q_v v gives H'=Re(Y^dagger W A Y). Midpoint obeys exactly
H_new-H_old=dtau Re(Y_mid^dagger W A Y_mid). Dissipation therefore requires
W A to be negative semidefinite on the resolved space. This is a linear
collision entropy identity, not a nonlinear cosmological entropy theorem.

## A real obstruction: the truncated operator is not uniformly stable

On the intensity dipole R=0, S=-2/5 and U=2/5, so

    A_1 = -1 + theta[-(2/5)L -2/5 +(7/5)g].

At large logarithmic-frequency wavenumber omega its real principal symbol
is -1+(2/5)theta omega^2. For every theta>0 it becomes positive eventually.
This is failure of an anisotropic small-shift expansion outside its smooth
spectral domain. Changing the time integrator cannot make that continuum
truncation uniformly dissipative. We do not label this an MTS instability.
The numerical N=384 early-epoch positive eigenmode is retained and excluded
from time evolution; coarser successful runs do not erase it.

## Deriving the finite-shift principal part

From delta ln(E)=beta.(nout-nin) and isotropic Maxwell covariance
<beta_i beta_j>=theta delta_ij, the shift variance is 2theta(1-mu).
The exact characteristic function of THIS leading Gaussian shift is
exp[-theta(1-mu)omega^2]. With normalized angular probability
K(mu)=3(1+mu^2)/4, its multipole generator is

    lambda_l(omega) = integral_-1^1 (dmu/2) K(mu) P_l(mu)
                     exp[-theta(1-mu)omega^2] -1 <= 0.

Positivity/normalization of K and |P_l|<=1 prove the inequality.
The dipole Taylor term is exactly -1+(2/5)theta omega^2; keeping the finite
shift rather than truncating it removes the offending high-frequency term.
Since 0<=exp(-z)-1+z<=z^2/2 for z>=0, the absolute Taylor error is bounded
by (7/10)theta^2 omega^4. This result repairs the principal part only:
it does not supply all recoil, drift or polarization coefficients.

## Constructive polarized reciprocal exchange

For screen Hermitian potentials Phi_i and positive node measures W_i,
take a reciprocal channel J_ji=J_ij^dagger and conductance a_ij=a_ji>=0.
The pair generator is

    C_i += a/W_i [J Phi_j J^dagger - {J J^dagger,Phi_i}/2],
    C_j += a/W_j [J^dagger Phi_i J - {J^dagger J,Phi_j}/2].

It conserves sum W_i tr(Phi_i). Direct expansion gives the pair identity

    sum_i W_i Re tr(Phi_i C_i)
       = -a ||Phi_i J-J Phi_j||_F^2 <= 0.

Sum this over pairs and polarization channels. Replace Phi_i by
Phi_i-x_i(r+nhat_i.v)1 and evolve plasma energy/momentum with the opposite
trace moments. The same square proves combined dissipation and preserves
common chemical, temperature and velocity equilibrium. This is a useful
derived discretization structure, but physical transition rates must still
match the scattering amplitude: arbitrary positive rates are not enough.

## Rejecting a tempting incomplete rate, then recovering the coefficient

Integrating nonrelativistic Maxwell electron momentum at fixed photon
transfer gives a Gaussian with Delta=x_j-x_i and
q^2=x_i^2+x_j^2-2x_i x_j mu:

    S_ij = exp[-(Delta+theta q^2/2)^2/(2theta q^2)]/sqrt(2pi theta q^2).

It satisfies S_ij/S_ji=exp(-Delta). Multiplying by
x_i x_j dx_i dx_j n_i(1+n_j) gives symmetric positive pair conductance.
This passes the reciprocal-square proof, but a static Thomson screen
overlap with this kinematic factor alone MISSES the parent dipole
coefficient. At x=1,3,8 and Phi=x, mismatch is about -3.4x. These numbers
are observations of the test, not coefficients we inserted as a repair.
`results/finite-shift/status.json` retains that rejected candidate.

We instead integrate the physical scalar moving-electron law: boost the
incoming photon to the electron rest frame, scatter with recoil and the
Klein-Nishina angular rate, boost back, include relative incident flux and
the equilibrium Bose weight. Inputs are established Compton physics,
not fitted MTS parameters; source details are in `SOURCES.md`.

The resulting numerical finite-shift integral recovers the inherited
intensity response for l=0..3, x=1,3,8 and potentials x^p, p=0,1,2: 36 probes.
Two separate small-temperature ladders and quadrature refinements agree.
This checks the coefficient of theta, not exact finite-temperature
equivalence. Normalized nonrelativistic Maxwell velocities suffice for
this first-order comparison; a finite-temperature reciprocal production
kernel needs the relativistic equilibrium measure and polarization maps.

For each exact recoil event the final electron is on shell. The Juttner
electron factor and Bose photon factors balance by total energy
conservation. The event checks do not by themselves prove that a chosen
finite-grid quadrature preserves that balance: that is the next task.
