## Action and assumptions

Use signature(-,+,+,+), natural units, fixed coupling constants and compactly supported variations or the appropriate boundary conditions:
\[
 S_{\rm eff}=\int d^4x\sqrt{-g}\,[P(X)-A(X)Y],\qquad
 X=-\tfrac12\nabla_\mu\theta\nabla^\mu\theta,\quad

\]
The previous stage derived, by eliminating the amplitude,
\[
 V'(v(X))=2Xv(X),\quad
 M(X)=V''(v(X))-2X>0,\quad
 P=v^2X-V(v),\quad A=\frac{2v^2}{M^2}=\tfrac12 v_X^2.
\]
Here M denotes radial mass squared, so M^2 means the square of that stiffness. The approximation retains two derivatives of the slowly varying X in addition to P(X). Further derivative operators are omitted.

The following variations are exact for this displayed truncated functional. Their interpretation as an approximation to the two-field theory is through its retained derivative order. ## Phase variation and current

Integrating the derivative of delta X by parts gives
\[
 \delta_X S=\int\sqrt{-g}\,Q\,\delta X+\text{boundary},\qquad
 \boxed{Q=P_X+A_XY+2A\Box X.}
\]
In particular, the intermediate coefficient is
\[
 P_X-A_XY+2\nabla_\mu(A\nabla^\mu X)
 =P_X+A_XY+2A\Box X.
\]
Both the A_X term and the differentiated A term matter.

Since delta X=-nabla^mu theta nabla_mu delta theta for a fixed metric,
\[
 E_\theta:=\frac1{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta S}{\delta\theta}
 =\nabla_\mu(Q\nabla^\mu\theta).
\]
Define J^mu=Q nabla^mu theta so E_theta=div J. The opposite generator convention gives the future-directed physical charge current
\[
 j^\mu=-J^\mu=-Q\nabla^\mu\theta.
\]
For theta=mu t,mu>0 on a flat background, j^0=Qmu>0 when Q>0. Both sign conventions give the same conservation equation; do not confuse the sign of J^0 with negative physical charge density.

## Hilbert stress from the same variation

Metric variation includes the dependence of X on the inverse metric even inside nabla X:
\[
 \delta X=-\tfrac12\theta_\mu\theta_\nu\delta g^{\mu\nu},
 \qquad \theta_\mu=\nabla_\mu\theta,\quad X_\mu=\nabla_\mu X.
\]
After the delta X integration by parts and the volume variation,
\[
 \boxed{
 T_{\mu\nu}=-\frac2{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta S}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}
 =Q\theta_\mu\theta_\nu+2A X_\mu X_\nu
 +g_{\mu\nu}(P-AY).}
\]
Holding the eliminated amplitude or Q artificially constant while varying the metric would not produce this source.

Its four-dimensional trace is
\[
 T=-2XQ+4P-2AY.
\]
All terms have stress/energy-density dimension4: X has dimension2, Q dimension2, A dimension-2 and Y dimension6.

## Exact off-shell conservation identity

Use the symmetry of second covariant derivatives of a scalar:
\[
 \theta^\mu\nabla_\mu\theta_\nu=-X_\nu,\qquad
 2X^\mu\nabla_\mu X_\nu=\nabla_\nu Y.
\]
Direct differentiation then gives
\[
 \nabla_\mu T^\mu{}_\nu
 =E_\theta\theta_\nu
 +[-Q+P_X-A_XY+2\nabla_\mu(A\nabla^\mu X)]X_\nu,
\]
and the bracket vanishes by the definition of Q:
\[
 \boxed{\nabla_\mu T^\mu{}_\nu=E_\theta\nabla_\nu\theta.}
\]
Thus the candidate's stress is conserved on solutions of its phase equation. This is an off-shell identity of its action, not an independent conservation closure, and it holds on a general background metric.

For the effective expansion, evaluate the equation and stress consistently on a perturbative solution through the same order; their residual is then of omitted order. Replacing higher time derivatives in only the equation while leaving an unrelated stress tensor is not a consistent reduction.

## Matching the original amplitude rather than assuming the source

The closed two-field candidate on the same metric is
\[
 S_C=\int\sqrt{-g}\,[-\tfrac12(\nabla\varrho)^2+\varrho^2X-V(\varrho)].
\]
Its amplitude equation is
\[
 \Box\varrho+2X\varrho-V'(\varrho)=0.
\]
Let the amplitude follow the slowly varying stationary branch. The first correction is
\[
 \varrho=v+\delta v,\qquad
 \delta v=\frac{\Box v}{M}
 =\frac{v_X\Box X+v_{XX}Y}{M}
\]
up to higher derivative order. Since v_X=2v/M and A_X=v_Xv_XX,
\[
 \boxed{\varrho^2=v^2+2v\delta v
       =P_X+2A\Box X+A_XY=Q}
\]
through the retained order.

The other pieces match independently:
\[
 \nabla_\mu\varrho\nabla_\nu\varrho
 =v_X^2 X_\mu X_\nu=2A X_\mu X_\nu+\text{higher order},
\]
\[
 -\tfrac12(\nabla\varrho)^2+\varrho^2X-V(\varrho)
 =P-AY+\text{higher order}.
\]
The linear algebraic correction in delta v cancels because V'(v)=2Xv. Thus the effective current and Hilbert stress are exactly the low-derivative reduction of the two-field current and stress, not newly postulated constitutive expressions.

This also fixes
\[
 A_X=\frac{16v^2}{M^3}-\frac{8v^3 C_{111}}{M^4},
\]
where C111=V'''(v). No extra stress-source coefficient is introduced.

## Perturbative order reduction and initial data

Around the constant background the preceding stage found
\[
 {\cal L}_2=\tfrac12\dot\chi^2-\tfrac12c_s^2(\nabla\chi)^2
 +\beta[(\ddot\chi)^2-(\nabla\dot\chi)^2],\qquad
 \beta=\frac{2\mu^2}{M(M+4\mu^2)}.
\]
Put c=c_s^2 and E0(chi)=-ddot chi+c nabla^2 chi. Up to integration by parts, the higher-derivative term is
\[
 \beta\{c(c-1)(\nabla^2\chi)^2
 +(1-2c)E0(\chi)\nabla^2\chi+E0(\chi)^2\}.
\]
The perturbative field redefinition
\[
 \chi=\chi_r-\beta[(1-2c)\nabla^2\chi_r+E0(\chi_r)]
\]
removes the E0-proportional pieces to this order. The reduced quadratic action is
\[
 {\cal L}_{2,r}
 =\tfrac12\dot\chi_r^2-\tfrac12c(\nabla\chi_r)^2
 -\beta c(1-c)(\nabla^2\chi_r)^2.
\]
Its equation is second order in time:
\[
 \ddot\chi_r-c\nabla^2\chi_r+2\beta c(1-c)\nabla^4\chi_r=0.
\]
For beta>0 and0<c<1 its quadratic Hamiltonian is positive, including the positive coefficient beta c(1-c) of the squared spatial Laplacian. The phase mode requires its value and first time derivative as initial data. The heavy amplitude mode is outside this reduced description; it has not turned into a spurious additional low-energy root.

This explicit reduction is about the homogeneous quadratic background. It is not a complete nonlinear curved-background order-reduction theorem.
