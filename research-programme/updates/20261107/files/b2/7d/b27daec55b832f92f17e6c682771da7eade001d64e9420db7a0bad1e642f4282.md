# Stage100: the thermal-speed measure and its exact conditional moments

## Existing physical assumption, not a new fitted law

The original gas rule in `../kinetic-gain-loss-20261008/kinetic_common.py`
uses an isotropic dilute equilibrium population with weight
exp[-(gamma-1)/theta] d^3p. Here p=|physical momentum|/(m_e c),
gamma=sqrt(1+p^2), theta=k_B T/(m_e c^2), and velocity is in units of c.
This stage derives its integration measure/moments; it does not derive
equilibrium or the electron mass from the parent action, or change the action.
The number-weighted d^3p distribution is the implemented one; an invariant
d^3p/gamma collision factor cannot silently be substituted for it.

## Exact radial change of variable

Set u=(gamma-1)/theta, so gamma=1+theta u,
p=sqrt(theta u(2+theta u)), and dp/du=theta gamma/p.
Consequently

    p^2 dp exp[-(gamma-1)/theta]
      = theta^(3/2) u^(1/2) exp(-u) F_theta(u) du,
    F_theta(u) = (1+theta u) sqrt(2+theta u).

The common theta^(3/2) cancels from normalized moments. This is exactly the
factor multiplying generalized Laguerre weights in the saved gas_rule.
For any integrable observable H(p), its radial mean is

    <H> = integral w(u) F_theta(u) H(p(u)) du / N(theta),
    w(u)=u^(1/2) exp(-u), N(theta)=integral w(u) F_theta(u) du.

Normalization must therefore be computed on the same radial rule as its
numerator. Unit sum of normalized discrete weights is not proof that the
underlying continuous normalization or other moments have converged.

## Exact normalization and moment identities

Put z=1/theta and p=sinh(t),gamma=cosh(t).
Using K_n(z)=integral_0^infinity exp[-z cosh(t)] cosh(n t) dt,

    integral p^2 exp[-z(gamma-1)] dp
       = exp(z) [K_3(z)-K_1(z)]/4
       = theta exp(z) K_2(z).
    N(theta) = exp(z) K_2(z)/sqrt(theta).

The recurrence K_3-K_1=4 K_2/z follows by integrating the derivative of
exp[-z cosh(t)] sinh(2t); its endpoint terms vanish for theta>0.
Stable evaluation uses the scaled Bessel function, not exp(z) in binary64.

The partition-function derivative and the same recurrence give

    <gamma>   = K_1(z)/K_2(z) + 3theta,
    <gamma^2> = 1 + 3theta K_1(z)/K_2(z) + 12theta^2.

Also, integrating d[p^3 exp[-(gamma-1)/theta]] from0 to infinity gives

    <p^2/(3gamma)> = theta.

All endpoint terms vanish for finite positive theta. The last identity is the
ideal dilute-gas pressure moment of THIS number-weighted population.
Angular isotropy then yields <p_i p_j/gamma>=theta delta_ij. This is not
a newly derived stress tensor for all MTS sectors.

## What the two and three radial nodes guarantee

Generalized Gauss-Laguerre order n with alpha=1/2 integrates polynomials in u
through degree2n-1 against w(u),not arbitrary F_theta(u)H(p(u)).
The normalized base moments are Gamma(k+3/2)/Gamma(3/2).

The monic degree2 polynomial is u^2-5u+15/4. Its squared norm divided by
Gamma(3/2) is15/2; hence the two-node base rule underestimates <u^4> by15/2.
The monic degree3 polynomial is u^3-(21/2)u^2+(105/4)u-105/8.
Its corresponding squared norm is315/4; the first missed moment is u^6.
These identities derive from orthogonality,not data fitting.

For orientation only, as theta tends to0,

    F_theta(u)/sqrt(2)
       = 1 + (5/4)theta u + (7/32)theta^2 u^2
         - (3/128)theta^3 u^3 + (11/2048)theta^4 u^4 + ...
    (N_radial2-N)/N = -(165/4096)theta^4 + O(theta^5),
    <gamma>_radial2-<gamma> = (45/256)theta^4 + O(theta^5),
    <p^2/(3gamma)>_radial2/theta-1
       = (5/128)theta^3 + O(theta^4).

These are small-theta asymptotics,not uniform Taylor series on0<u<infinity.
Their finite-order interpretation follows by a dominated remainder argument
for theta>=0 with the exponentially decaying base weight. They do NOT bound
the full nonlinear scattering/virtual source or its cancellation remainder.
The script checks these moment statements at high precision against the exact
continuous identities and independently verifies the actual binary64 gas nodes.
