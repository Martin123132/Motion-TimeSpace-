# Photon production: a spectrum-owned response, not a fitted radiation temperature

This supplement prepares the next physical extension. It does not change the
no-emission background or any of the Stage78 wave comparisons.

## Primary input and approximation

Acharya and Chluba, arXiv:2112.06699v1, equations17–18, give the isotropic
nonrelativistic emission/absorption approximation with a double-Compton
coefficient depending on the actual photon occupation. Their reference
temperature scales as a^-1; it is not the perturbed electron temperature.
Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2112.06699v1
Journal version: https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stac2137

Ravenni and Chluba, arXiv:2005.06941v1, equations44–46, distinguish emission/
absorption from the accompanying Compton-like redistribution. A full double-
Compton energy calculation includes both. The approximate paired gas-energy
equation below must not be relabelled an exact treatment of that full kernel.
Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2005.06941v1
Journal version: https://doi.org/10.1088/1475-7516/2020/10/025

These are established QED inputs. Neither alpha nor the electron mass is
derived by this step. BR, finite-frequency Gaunt corrections and angular/
polarization-dependent emission have not been supplied by these formulas.

## Derivation in our fixed frequency frame

Use x=E/(k_B T_star), xi=T_e/T_star and optical depth d tau=n_e sigma_T c dt.
At a fixed background time T_star is a reference, not a fluctuating field.
Define dimensionless quantities

    I[n] = integral_0^infinity x^4 n(x)[1+n(x)] dx,
    kappa = (4 alpha/3 pi) theta_star^2,
    H(n,xi;x) = exp(-x/xi)[1+n(x)]-n(x),
    C(n,xi;x) = kappa I[n] H(n,xi;x)/x^3.

C is the occupation change per Thomson optical depth. In expansion time
multiply by opacity/Hubble. A change of this prefactor contributes its own
fractional perturbation times the background C; it is not part of delta I.

The first two functional derivatives follow by expanding the quadratic I:

    delta I[h] = integral x^4 [1+2 nbar(x)] h(x) dx,
    delta^2 I[h,k] = 2 integral x^4 h(x) k(x) dx.

For delta xi=t, the full first variation of this approximate source is

    delta C = kappa/x^3 { Hbar delta I
                + Ibar[-(1-exp(-x/xibar)) delta n
                  + exp(-x/xibar)(1+nbar) x t/xibar^2] }.

Thus the radiation Jacobian is a diagonal absorption operator plus one outer
product: U(x) J[h], where U=kappa Hbar/x^3 and J=delta I. In a spectral basis
delta n=w sum_j psi_j X_j, J_j=integral x^4(1+2nbar)w psi_j dx.
It is possible to retain the entire spectrum-dependent response as a rank-one
update without inventing a fitted photon temperature. This rank statement is
for the leading isotropic coefficient; frequency-dependent Gaunt kernels
need not have rank one.

At nbar=1/expm1(x/xibar), Hbar=0. Differentiating this equilibrium family gives
delta n=x nbar(1+nbar)t/xibar^2 and makes delta C exactly zero. A chemical
potential perturbation alone does not have this null property. This is the
required change from the number-conserving Compton sector.

## Which small terms must be retained

For nbar=n_P+chi w B and xi=1+chi b,

    Ibar=I_P+chi integral x^4(1+2n_P)w B dx
             +chi^2 integral x^4 w^2 B^2 dx,
    Hbar/chi -> (1-exp(-x))w(x b-B).

The leading background source therefore uses I_P; the correction to Ibar
first enters that source at chi^2. But a cosmological temperature perturbation
has delta I of order its own amplitude epsilon. Consequently Hbar delta I
is order chi epsilon: the very mixed order retained in our current wave
calculation. Dropping it because the background distortion is small would
discard a term of the same order as the signal we are calculating.

As an independent frame check, a Planck spectrum with temperature ratio xi
has I=xi^5 I_P when integrated over all positive x. This is not an extra
xi^5 factor to insert after changing frequency variables. Keeping the same
fixed x and theta_star throughout avoids confusing reference-temperature
rescaling with an electron-temperature derivative.

## Stress coupling and implementation boundary

For the stated approximate isotropic model, let E be the same discretized
photon-energy moment used in the radiation stress. The gas collision source
must be -E C, and its perturbation -E delta C (with background-density and
heat-capacity perturbations restored when converting to gas temperature).
This fixes exchange within that approximation without fitting its sign or
normalization. It does not supply the omitted full-kernel redistribution.

The following stage should evolve this matched background first, then derive
its angular, density, lapse and velocity perturbations in the same rest-frame
convention as the existing wave system. Do not apply this source to the saved
no-emission background while continuing to call its old derivative on shell.
The present low-frequency rate is not a licensed extrapolation over every
observational band. Source checks are in
`results/emission-jacobian/status.json`; no new sky prediction is made here.
