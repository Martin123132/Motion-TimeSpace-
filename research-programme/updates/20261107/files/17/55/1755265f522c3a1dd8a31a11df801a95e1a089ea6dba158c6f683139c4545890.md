## What can and cannot be inferred

Let E be the saved source-prediction error and H the saved cold source.
If the unknown error in E has norm at most B and the error in H at most C,
then the triangle inequalities give

    true_relative_error >= max(||E||-B,0)/(||H||+C).

The inequality is derived; the proposed B and C are NOT certified bounds.
The following scenarios exclude unquantified endpoint/other-axis errors and
require their stated assumptions. They are neither statistical confidence
intervals nor a selection of whichever estimate looks most favourable.

| Conditional scenario | Numerator allowance B, % coldH4 | Conditional residual floor, % |
|---|---:|---:|
| Latest matched-panel steps used as radii | 0.22112–0.22131 | 1.58471–1.58761 |
| Latest independent site/component/panel steps used as radii | 1.48834–1.48965 | 0.32163–0.32304 |
| Selected-panel geometric tails, other panels' last steps | 1.26319–1.26422 | 0.54636–0.54804 |
| Two latest independent steps in selected panels; last elsewhere | 4.22599–4.23046 | 0 |

For the geometric scenario, 1–3 uses the larger of the observed 7-minus-6 /
6-minus-5 and 8-minus-7 / 7-minus-6 component ratios. The 3–8 panel uses its
observed 7-minus-6 / 6-minus-5 ratio. All selected caps are below one:
**0.209986–0.595686**. Applying them to every future increment remains an
additional assumption. The post-rule tail is last_step*r/(1-r), excluding
the last computed step because it is already present in the current result.
The other panels receive no invented contraction factor.

The independent last-step budget needs only a factor **1.2168–1.2175**
increase to cover the entire discrepancy. That limited margin is why a
finite-rule comparison should not be presented as a proof of separation.

Endpoint A/B incoming accuracy, external-frequency tails, electron/hard-angle
rules, real-emission and virtual-loop integration, frequency-basis/source-history
accuracy, and physical approximation errors have no new certified radius here.
They remain explicit unresolved axes, not zeros in a global error estimate.

## Shape of the remaining discrepancy

At the predeclared SVD cutoffs 1e-8, 1e-10 and 1e-12, the nine observed
matched-panel step directions have rank nine. **93.432–93.460% of the cubic
residual norm lies outside their span.** This supports the observation that
the residual is not simply the latest numerical step in disguise.

This is a norm fraction, not a variance percentage. The projection allows
arbitrary coefficients; it is not an admissible source correction. An unseen
integration bias could have another direction, so the projection does not
turn a conditional remainder estimate into a proof.

7.82433–7.82671%; it is not substituted for the cubic result.

## Decision and next test

**Keep the temperature curve fixed.** The numerical evidence is encouraging
for a persistent residual, but the more conservative allowance can still
cover it. Do not fit away the discrepancy or claim it is wholly physical.

Next targeted calculation: raise **8–20 from order6 to7 at H/W/C**, retaining
1–3 at8 and3–8 at7, and reuse the same baseline/MTS histories. Then update
this existing budget rather than starting another full sweep.

Endpoint accuracy must also be addressed before attributing a residual to
the temperature law. Under the independent-last-step hypothesis, the remaining
unbudgeted numerator allowance before the residual can vanish is only
**0.32264–0.32405% of coldH4**. The endpoint pair enters through
(||delta A||+||delta B||)/4; this gives an explicit accuracy requirement,
not an achieved bound. Other unmeasured axes must share that allowance.
Once that budget is controlled, work on deriving the temperature dependence
rather than selecting a better-looking interpolation.
