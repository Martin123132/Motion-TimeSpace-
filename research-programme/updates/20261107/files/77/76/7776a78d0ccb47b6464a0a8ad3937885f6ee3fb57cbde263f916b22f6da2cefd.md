# Coupled radial gravity: a positive stability test

3 October 2026. Private continuation of the condensate-origin daughter.

## What moved forward

The preceding stage constructed stationary gravitating bodies. This stage
derives how their field AND geometry respond to radial perturbations, solves
the spherical Einstein constraints, and tests the resulting fixed-charge
energy Hessian. Geometry is not held fixed to get the positive result.

The nine tested configurations have positive lowest constrained radial
energy eigenvalues on successively refined meshes. The phase symmetry zero
converges towards zero as expected. Removing the charge constraint exposes
a negative direction in every finest-mesh case: the conservation law is
doing real work, rather than every version of the calculation returning a
positive answer.

## Derivation and checks

- Exact reduced ADM Hamiltonian from the radial constraint, including lapse
  reconstruction and the nonlocal gravitational term in its second variation.
- Coupled rotating-frame field/metric perturbation equations; mass-constraint
  propagation and a local conserved-charge flux identity.
- Conditional analytic nonnegativity of the phase sector by a ground-state
  transform. Exact continuum recovery of the previous flat fixed-charge
  rank-one operator when gravity is switched off.
- 18 executed symbolic identities; 45 spectral calculations including pilots,
  mesh refinements, and independent quadrature orders. Nine physical cases,
  not 45 independent predictions.
- Largest last-mesh change in the lowest eigenvalue: 0.313%. Mesh changes
  decrease by approximately a factor of four. Independent 8- versus 12-point
  quadrature changes the compared eigenvalues by at most 9.1e-12 relatively.
- 18 smooth finite-variation directions, each at four step sizes. At the
  smallest step the nonlinear Routhian second difference agrees with the
  derived Hessian within 1.83e-7 relatively; the exactly charge-corrected ADM
  difference agrees within 1.90e-6. These are separate tests from diagonalizing
  the Hessian itself.
- Six independent adaptive integrations of the nonlinear mass constraint
  agree with its integrating-factor evaluation within 4.06e-13 relatively.
  That integration agreement is not the spectral discretization accuracy.

All calculation inputs are the saved stationary profiles; no new physical
coefficient or data fit was introduced. One below-normal-priority, one-core
worker at a time; no subagents, no publication.

## Numerical correction retained in the record

The first validator saved six directions then stopped: a gate required the
last finite-difference error to improve tenfold relative to the first even
after cancellation started to dominate. In the offending case the last
relative error was about 1.06e-8, after an intermediate 1.44e-9. That is not
a physical instability. The original script, partial result, and error log
remain untouched. The daughter validator retains the 2e-5 final-accuracy gate
and tests initial step halving with an explicitly recorded cancellation
allowance. It passes all 18 directions. This allowance is a numerical estimate,
not interval arithmetic or a rigorous error bound. No field equation, Hessian,
coupling, or spectrum was changed to obtain the pass.

## Meaning and next step

This is converged numerical evidence for a positive fixed-charge radial
canonical energy of the tested weakly gravitating condensate bodies. Positive
Galerkin minima are not certified continuum lower bounds. The compact vacuum
edge requires its own domain care; nonlinear exterior disturbances and
nonspherical gravitational modes are outside this test. The Einstein sector
and candidate complex-U(1) assumptions remain the inputs of this daughter.

Next: construct the actual radial pulsation spectrum from the derived coupled
system, remove the charge/phase symmetry correctly, and follow the gravitating
branch towards its first instability. Use the stable part to derive the
long-wavelength effective body motion and its Newtonian interaction. This
connects the stability work to motion and gravity rather than treating a
positive Hessian as the end of the programme.
