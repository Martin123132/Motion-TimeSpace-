## 1. Why the elastic half factor is necessary

For e(p)+gamma(k) <-> e(p')+gamma(k'), let

    d_A = A_gamma(k') + A_e(p') - A_gamma(k) - A_e(p).
    W = f_p n_k (1+n_k') = f_p' n_k' (1+n_k).

Here f is dilute, normalized Maxwell-Juttner at fixed electron number
density and n is the common-temperature Planck reference. The linear
departure conventions remain delta n=n(1+n) Phi and delta f=f phi_e.
The elastic weak bilinear, when the initial-final integration covers both
orientations, is

    delta C_el[A] = -1/2 integral dP W |M|^2 d_A d_Phi.

The factor is not an identical-photon factor. Relabeling all initial and
final variables gives the same unordered reaction twice. A direct nonlinear
gain-minus-loss derivative in `verify_elastic_counting.py` fixes its value.
Stage87's ordered DC reaction has one net gain/loss bracket; its equivalent
emission-plus-inverse representation has its own one-half factor. Keeping
both factors consistent makes the weak soft residues cancel.

A first variation of a distribution-dependent scalar rate multiplies the
vanishing equilibrium gain/loss bracket. Thus the linear scalar source
uses the rate at the reference bath, rather than differentiating the loop
occupation a second time. Changes of mass shell, statistical weight and
the observables themselves remain necessary; see section5.

## 2. Scalar cut: what happens to the absorptive loop

The inherited one-loop photon-bath amplitude contains principal-value
radial integrals and interior-pole residues with their Feynman signs.
Separate them BEFORE contraction with the Born amplitude.

In the unpolarized spin and photon sum, the interference of the Born
amplitude with an absorptive residue is i times a real Dirac trace. The
trace uses real four-vectors, real physical transverse projectors, real
scalar residue coefficients and no gamma5 insertion. Even gamma traces
reduce to real metric contractions. Its real part therefore vanishes,
direction by direction, without discarding the absorptive amplitude.

This establishes the scalar O(alpha) rate projection in the stated
photon-bath/dilute-electron approximation. It is not an assertion that
the whole complex loop is real or that all polarized kinetic cuts vanish.
Pauli/Stokes insertions can retain interference phases. `split_virtual.py`
reconstructs the full directional amplitude independently, verifies the

map. Coherent polarization transport still needs its own kinetic treatment.

Thermal equilibrium with no external time-reversal-breaking field supplies
the reciprocal scalar scattering rate under reversal of the entire event.
Both crossed real reactions use the actual tensor and its correct phase
space, not an independently invented inverse rate.

## 3. A matched weak kernel with no hidden soft separator

Fix a lab seed, initial electron and observed harder-photon lab direction.
Normalize to the Born differential cross section including relative flux,
angular Jacobian and final hard Bose factor. Let B=d d^T for the elastic
test-function jump vector and J_+(q), J_-(q) its real emission/absorption
counterparts. Let D_+(q), D_-(q) be positive normalized real densities,
including the ratio of the actual to Born hard Bose enhancement.

For each second-photon rest-frame direction,

    D_+(q), D_-(q) ~ a_Omega/q,
    a_Omega = alpha/(4pi^2) |p_recoil cross n|^2 /
                                  (E_recoil-p_recoil.n)^2.
    N(q)=1/(exp(q/T_direction)-1).

Emission ends at equal outgoing LAB energies U_+. Absorption ends when
the softer INPUT reaches the tagged input energy U_-. These are different
boundaries. The real positive bilinear integrand is

    1/2 [ D_+(q) (1+N) J_+(q) 1_(q<U_+)
                        + D_-(q) N J_-(q) 1_(q<U_-) ].

The collision operator is MINUS this positive-bilinear convention. The
saved matrices are corrections to that bilinear, not rates with the loss
sign already applied.

For test functions A_gamma(q)=a1 q+O(q^2), both J tend to B. The soft
reference is a_Omega(1+2N) B/(2q). Split it into vacuum and thermal pieces:

    R_v = 1/2 integral_0^U+ [D_+ J_+
                         - a_Omega B 1_(q<Lambda)/q] dq,
    R_T = 1/2 integral_0^infinity N [
                 D_+ J_+ 1_(q<U_+) + D_- J_- 1_(q<U_-)
                         - 2 a_Omega B chi(q)/q ] dq.

Here 0<Lambda<=U_+ and chi(0)=1, 1-chi=O(q^2). Real events retain their
physical bounds while the THERMAL reference is integrated on the whole
axis. The matched finite additions are

    1/2 [V_vac + C_massive] B
      + 1/2 integral dOmega a_Omega log(2 Lambda) B
