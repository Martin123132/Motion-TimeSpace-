## Result

The actual circular companion source and the smooth body-minus-black-hole
metric response determine the magnetic interaction, including its coefficient:

\[
 F\equiv\Delta L_{\rm circ}
 =-\frac{3}{8\pi}\Re\{\Delta C\,\mathcal O[V]\}
 \longrightarrow
 \frac{6\bar\sigma\mu^2\Omega^2}{D^4}
 =\frac{\bar\sigma}{3}B_{ij}B_{ij}.
\]

This recovers the leading magnetic action used in stage23 without inserting
that action as a normalization condition. The frequency-dependent response
also determines the conservative circular charges,

\[
 \Delta J=\frac{dF}{d\Omega},\qquad
 \Delta E=\Omega\frac{dF}{d\Omega}-F.
\]

An independently derived source-work identity connects the imaginary response
to the difference in total axial loss:

\[
 2\Omega\Im\mathcal F_{\rm ret}
 =\Delta P_{\infty,21}-P_{H,21}^{\rm BH}.
\]

The reference horizon term is essential. This is a *difference* of responses,
not a positive absorption susceptibility of the body.

## 2. Source reciprocity, not a guessed coupling

Set \(f=1-2M/r\), \(d/dr_*=f\,d/dr\), and
\(H=\sqrt{15/(8\pi)}\). The stage25 distributional source gives

\[
 A=\frac{8\pi}{3}\mu\Omega u^t H,\qquad
 J=[\psi]=\frac{AD}{2},\qquad
 K=[\psi_*]= -\frac{Af_D}{2}.
\]

For a homogeneous response V, define
\(\mathcal O[V]=K V(D)-J V_*(D)\).
The reconstruction is

\[
 h_t=\tfrac12 f(r\psi)'-\tfrac14r^2f P^t.
\]

The explicit source contact is identical for the two objects and cancels
*before* evaluation on the worldline. There is no evaluation of a naked
delta function on that worldline. For the remaining smooth difference,

\[
 h_{kk}^{(1)}[V]=\Omega H(f_D V+D V_*)
             =-\frac{3}{4\pi\mu u^t}\mathcal O[V].
\]

The physical pair is \(h_{kk}=2\Re h_{kk}^{(1)}\). Expanding the particle
action gives \(\mu u^t h_{kk}/2\). Eliminating the quadratic field supplies
another factor of one half: for \(L=\phi A\phi/2+j\phi\),
the stationary value is \(j\phi/2\). Consequently

\[
 F=\frac{\mu u^t}{4}\Delta h_{kk}^{\rm cons},\qquad
 \Delta z=-\frac{u^t}{2}\Delta h_{kk}^{\rm cons},\qquad
 \partial_\mu F=-\Delta z.
\]

The mass derivative holds at fixed central body and Omega. The latter identity
uses F proportional to mu^2. Allowed comparison gauges preserve the helical
symmetry, asymptotic time normalization and the same orbit/frequency labels.

## 3. Boundary-to-source kernel

Let U, H_BH and V be respectively the regular body, ingoing BH and outgoing
exterior solutions. Normalize their values to one at R; let
\(W(A,B)=A B_*-A_* B\). The smooth difference is

\[
 \Delta\psi=\Delta C V,\qquad
 \Delta C=-\frac{W(U,H_{\rm BH})\mathcal O[V]}
                  {W(U,V)W(H_{\rm BH},V)}.
\]

Thus
\(\mathcal F_{\rm ret}=-3\Delta C\mathcal O[V]/(8\pi)\).
Its real part is the time-symmetric conservative interaction on this
periodic family. The complex quantity is response-generating notation:
an ordinary single-history real action does not generate dissipative
equations by varying this retarded quadratic expression. No such claim is
used here. Outgoing normalization and phase cancel between numerator and
Wronskians. Setting U=H_BH makes the entire difference vanish.

## 4. Recovering the low-frequency magnetic operator

In the leading near zone take \(g=r^3\), \(d=r^{-2}\),
\(W(g,d)=-5\). The previously derived body's asymptotic coefficient is
\(C_{\rm master}=24\bar\sigma\), while
\(\mathcal O[d]=A/(2D^2)\) to leading order. Substitution gives

\[
 -\frac{3}{8\pi}\frac{24\bar\sigma}{-5}
       \left(\frac{A}{2D^2}\right)^2
 =\frac{6\bar\sigma\mu^2\Omega^2}{D^4}.
\]

For the circular tidal tensor, \(B_{ij}B_{ij}=18\mu^2\Omega^2/D^4\).
Equivalently a matched auxiliary current may be written
\(L_S=2S^{ij}B_{ij}/3-S^{ij}S_{ij}/(3\bar\sigma)\);
stationarity gives \(S_{ij}=\bar\sigma B_{ij}\) and the same eliminated
operator. This is static matching, not a new microscopic oscillator model
or a stability conclusion from the sign of its auxiliary quadratic term.

*Gravitomagnetic tidal effects in gravitational waves from neutron star
binaries*, Phys. Rev. D101,064003(2020), doi:10.1103/PhysRevD.101.064003,

## 6. Stored conservative energy and angular momentum

Let \(F_0=6\bar\sigma\mu^2\Omega^2/D^4\). Along the background circular
family it scales as Omega^(14/3). Hence

\[
 \Delta E=\frac{11}{3}F_0
      \left[1+\frac{208}{33}x+\cdots\right],\qquad
 \Delta J=\frac{14F_0}{3\Omega}
      \left[1+\frac{128}{21}x+\cdots\right].
\]

These obey \(d\Delta E/d\Omega=\Omega\,d\Delta J/d\Omega\).
Taking a *total* frequency derivative incorporates the leading orbital

This is the first-order stationary-orbit identity. For a nonlocal
time-symmetric kernel the derivatives refer to period-averaged circular
charges, with central solution and its conserved parameters held fixed.

## 7. Source-work optical identity

For real J,K, the wave current \(j=\Im(\psi^*\psi_*)\) satisfies

\[
 [j]=-\Im(K\psi-J\psi_*).
\]

For this pair the luminosity is \(3\Omega[j]/(4\pi)\).
The body is lossless at the imposed real interior boundary, whereas the BH
reference has an ingoing horizon. Taking their difference gives

\[
 \Delta P_\infty-P_H^{\rm BH}=2\Omega\Im\mathcal F_{\rm ret}.
\]

Omitting the horizon contribution is a wrong control, not a negligible
term assumed away. The imaginary part may have either sign after subtraction.
This identity is a consistency relation, not a separate measurement of
the theory. The source and conserved-current algebra make these checks
correlated by construction.

## 8. Numerical defect found and repaired

The first candidate used the returned normalized complex surface slopes
directly. Binary64 complex division z/z need not yield an *exact* real one.
Multiplication by the real slope then contaminates the very small imaginary
slope, despite a separately enforced normalized value of one.
At D/R=80 in the first coarse run it even reversed the outgoing current.
The optical error reached0.96277 of the sum of its two flux components.

Fix, without tuning any physics coefficient: set the normalized surface
slopes' imaginary parts from their conserved asymptotic currents,
\(\Im y_V=+\Omega/|V_R^{\rm raw}|^2\) and
\(\Im y_H=-\Omega/|H_R^{\rm raw}|^2\).
The finite-start asymptotic flux errors are monitored separately.
Propagate the tiny phase with a real fundamental solution Q satisfying
Q(R)=0,Q_*(R)=1:

\[
 V(D)=\Re V(D)+i\Im(y_V)Q(D),\quad
 H(D)=\Re V(D)+(y_H-\Re y_V)Q(D).
\]

The common basis has W(ReV,Q)=1 in exact arithmetic. Its numerical error
and the directly integrated horizon solution are independent controls.
Both the outgoing and ingoing slope normalization receive the same fix.
