# Efficient evaluation of native coefficient-cut atoms

The naive event loop rebuilds a 1094-point native kernel at every radial
coefficient crossing. This repeats the same smooth geometry calculation
and is unsuitable for the bounded work session. The saved wave and
matter rules are unchanged. The interrupted partial event accumulator
was not saved and is explicitly discarded, not treated as evidence.

For fixed material ordering the native image and its geometry/field
derivatives are smooth functions of layer label. They do not depend on
the radial beta/beta_R coefficient jumps. Factor each event contribution
into a smooth covector and an independently evaluated jump:

    atom_j = A_beta,j(label_c) * [beta]_c
             + A_betaR,j(label_c) * [beta_R]_c,
    A = weight(label) * U_j/R_j' * native coefficient covector.

The existing coefficient covectors were derived and checked against the
full native force/current calculation. Represent their label dependence
with deterministic Chebyshev interpolants of degree16,24,32. This is
numerical interpolation of the same known functions, not a fitted
physical coefficient or an alternative force law. Positive material
widths and the previously checked monotone source map keep the real
interval away from geometry poles. Topological ordering is fixed.

Enumerate every crossing and compute its root and70-digit coefficient
jump. Sum all atoms at each interpolation order. Require successive total
differences <=2e-20, stricter than the2e-17 complete-column gate. Independently
rebuild the native covectors at uniformly selected events and the eight
largest predicted event contributions. Require their contribution errors
<=2e-20. These are sampled convergence checks, not rigorous uniform
interpolation-error bounds; retain that limitation in the report.

Source-clock cut events remain directly evaluated. No completed wave,
regular native/source or gravitational calculation is repeated.
