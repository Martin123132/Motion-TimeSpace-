# Transport identity: distinguish an algebraic omission from integration error

For each fixed material label, let L be any one of the four represented
force/current densities and U the continuous piecewise-linear support
displacement. Hold the label fixed. On every open piece between radial
polynomial cuts and material faces, the chain rule gives

    D_material L + U_R L = D_Eulerian L + d_R(U L).

The metric value polynomial and the stored radial-coefficient polynomial
are differentiated as the functions actually represented. They need not
be silently identified: the spatial derivative of the former is not
replaced by the latter. Metric-time polynomials follow the same rule.

Integrating the final total derivative piecewise yields

    integral d_R(U L) = [U L]_outer - sum_material U[L] - sum_radial U[L].

Right-minus-left defines every jump. Thus material regular volume plus
radial atoms equals Eulerian regular volume plus moving-material faces
and outer terms. At coincident cuts the combined one-sided jump is
counted once; it is not two independent contributions. No new dynamics,
fit parameter, or physical zero assumption enters this identity.

The current diagnostic evaluates THREE densities independently on the
same actual quadrature nodes: material derivative, Eulerian derivative,
and a spatial derivative of the baseline density times U with U_R L.
The local defect is their difference, evaluated before summation. It also
reuses the already saved boundary integrals rather than recomputing them
with another discretization. Consequently

    observed_material_minus_Eulerian
      = quadrature_FTC_defect + integrated_local_identity_defect

up to the measured arithmetic/replay residual. The FTC defect is
Q[d_R(U L)]+radial_atoms-material_faces-outer. This is a localization
diagnostic, NOT a correction to be fitted or silently subtracted.

Binary64 local tests can suffer cancellation even when the calculus is
correct. The original pointwise diagnostic failed its1e-10 relative test
for MTS and remains a failed record. Separate96-digit calculations on
the same binary64 input values test the identity with central steps1e-24
and1e-28. No underlying data gain physical precision. Worst binary64
points and distributed control points are included, with unchanged
binary failure flags. A small sampled defect is not a continuum proof;
the chain-rule derivation supplies the identity under the stated
piecewise differentiability and consistent geometry assumptions.
