## 5. Plasma heat capacity and a derived fast-temperature limit

Define epsilon_th=(3/2)n_tot k_B T_*/rho_ref, where rho_ref is Planck photon

This is called chi in the code; it is NOT any pre-existing MTS chi field.
Its value is fixed by the inherited baryon/helium/opacity calibration,
not fitted. One common plasma temperature uses the fast electron-ion
equilibration assumption; a sourced Coulomb-rate estimate tests its scale
separation, rather than claiming a new microscopic Coulomb derivation.

Let s=ln(a/a_in), w=n_P(1+n_P), n=n_P+epsilon_th w v(x,s), and
r=1+epsilon_th t(s). The spectral weight obeys w'=-(1+2n_P)w. At leading

    v_s = k(s)[A v+b t], k=kappa theta_*/H,
    A v=(x^2 w)^-1 partial_x(x^4 w partial_x v),
    b=-(x^2 w)^-1 partial_x(x^4 w),
    epsilon_th t_s=-1-epsilon_th t-E[v_s],
    E[v]=integral x^3 w v dx/I3_Planck.

5/3 adiabatic index, not an assigned equality T_e=T_gamma. It yields

    E[v(s)] + epsilon_th t(s) + integral_0^s[1+epsilon_th t] ds =0.

energy-projected frequency operator

This is a dynamical singular limit of a sourced heat capacity, not a fitted

The rate coefficient controls the spectral shape and electron temperature,

## 6. Put the derived stress back into gravity

    rho_heat = rho_ref (1-s),
    P_heat = rho_ref (2-s)/3.

Including only photon cooling would fail this equation. The gas's initial
