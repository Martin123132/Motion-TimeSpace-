# Stage82 — the soft cancellation has a derived coefficient

## Place in the MTS calculation

This calibrates the existing radiation/source sector against its local-inertial
QED limit. It does not introduce another MTS action, replace either heavy/cold
parent, or derive the inherited charge, electron mass or Newton constant.
The purpose is to supply the collision/source terms needed by the already
developed radiation/gas/metric equations without inserting a damping closure.

## 1. Keep the photon polarizations until the end

Set m_e=1 and use metric(+---). For three signed photon momenta
q=(k0,-k1,-k2), the stripped amplitude is

    M/e^3 = ubar(p') sum_permutations
      slash(eps_3) S(p+q_1+q_2) slash(eps_2) S(p+q_1) slash(eps_1) u(p),
    S(r)=(slash(r)+1)/(r^2-1), p'=p+q_1+q_2+q_3.

The ordering labels in each term follow its permutation. All six terms are
retained; there is no width inserted into the electron propagator. Outgoing
polarizations are conjugated in a general complex basis; the saved amplitude
uses real transverse bases and can be contracted with arbitrary input density
matrices afterwards. Spinors obey ubar*u=2m.

The Ward identity follows by inserting
slash(k)=S^-1(r+k)-S^-1(r). Adjacent insertion positions telescope, leaving
external inverse propagators which annihilate the on-shell spinors. A single
diagram need not vanish. Numerically deleting one diagram gives the required
negative control. Summing over final spins/polarizations and averaging the two
initial spin and photon states reproduces the independent sourced X with no
fitted scale. Boosted-electron checks test covariance as well as rest-frame
agreement. This is the polarized tree amplitude, not a thermal loop amplitude.

Topology references: the Gamma=0 limit of equations4 and21 in
https://arxiv.org/html/2203.05252v1; independent X in AppendixA of
https://arxiv.org/html/2005.06941v1. We do NOT adopt the former paper's proposed
finite-width regularization.

## 2. Factor the actual soft current

In the limit k_s->0 the six terms give

    M_DC = e*[p'.eps_s/(p'.k_s)-p.eps_s/(p.k_s)] M_C + O(k_s^0).

This holds before hard-photon polarization or electron-spin averaging.
The current j=p'/(p'.k_s)-p/(p.k_s) satisfies k_s.j=0.
Its angle-integrated coefficient is

    A(p.p') = (2alpha/pi)[atanh(v_rel)/v_rel-1],
    v_rel^2 = 1-1/(p.p')^2.

We evaluate the small-speed series rather than subtract two nearly equal
numbers. The direct angular integral of -j^2 independently verifies A.
For a cold electron and a low-energy hard photon of energy omega=theta*t,

    p.p'-1 = omega^2(1-c)+O(omega^3),
    A = [4alpha/(3pi)] theta^2 t^2(1-c)+O(omega^3).

The resulting hard angular weight is the Thomson kernel times(1-c), with
normalized intensity moments lambda_l(1) already derived in Stage81.

## 3. Why the virtual coefficient is fixed

For one soft oscillator, coupling the conserved classical eikonal current
produces a displacement U=D(eta). In a thermal oscillator state,

    Tr[rho_T Udag exp(i H z) U exp(-i H z)]
      = exp{|eta|^2 [(1+n)(exp(i omega z)-1)
                         +n(exp(-i omega z)-1)]}.

Derivation uses the displacement product identity and its Gaussian thermal
trace. At z=0 unitarity gives1. Thus the negative term is -(1+2n), not an
adjustable cancellation coefficient. The terms represent emission, absorption
and the virtual/no-resolved-transfer contribution of the same soft current.
An independent truncated-oscillator matrix calculation checks this identity.
The sourced QED derivation is Weldon, https://arxiv.org/html/hep-ph/9308249,
equations3–8 and16–20. Its finite hard amplitudes remain distinct from the
universal soft factor. We do not replace them by zero.

## 4. The finite thermal transfer functional

In units x=omega/Tstar, soft temperature T/Tstar=tau, and for an energy
conjugate z, the exponent up to a hard/soft separator Delta is

    R(z)=A integral_0^Delta dx/x H(x,z),
    H=i*sin(x*z)-2*coth[x/(2tau)]*sin(x*z/2)^2.

The soft factor applies where Delta*Tstar is small relative to the relevant
hard scattering scales. Arbitrary oscillator displacements used in the
unitarity test are numerical probes, not MTS parameters or fitted source terms.

This evaluates the sum stably; it does not subtract enormous floating-point
rates. Exactly H(0,z)=0 and H(x,z)/x -> i*z-tau*z^2.
For real z and an infrared cutoff delta, the omitted tail obeys

    |R_0-R_delta| <= A*delta*(|z|+tau*|z|^2+delta*|z|^2/2).

Use |sin y|<=|y|, 1-cos y<=y^2/2 and n(x)<=tau/x. The bound tends to zero,
unlike the separate rates which grow inversely with delta. Thermal detailed
balance is R(z)=R(-z+i/tau). Normalization R(0)=0 is automatic.

The first two energy cumulants are

    mean=A*Delta,
    variance=A integral_0^Delta dx x*coth[x/(2tau)].

Delta remains an explicit matching scale. Neither these finite cumulants nor
the infrared tail bound make the COMPLETE observable separator-independent.
For a general soft occupation N(x), the same Gaussian-current construction
needs integral_0^Delta x*N(x)dx finite. Our finite-window emitting histories do
not by themselves prove this condition below their numerical xmin.

For four-transfer replace x*z by k_mu*z^mu. In the leading rest frame the
soft directional distribution is proportional to1-(n.u)^2, where u is the
unit hard recoil direction. Its mean spatial direction is0 and its second
moment is(2delta_ij-u_i*u_j)/5. Each photon transfer is paired with the opposite
hard-system transfer. This is a conditional kinematic/source check, not yet an
integrated cosmological heating calculation. Covariant context is supplied by
https://arxiv.org/html/hep-ph/9507341; its Q>>T hard-process approximation is
not silently applied to all thermal photons in our background.

## 5. Match the Stage81 singularity without fitting

Emission and absorption supply1+2n~2/x. For the unresolved HARD collision the
leading real coefficient is therefore2A/xmin times its Born scattering map;
the virtual coefficient is its negative. Let A_l denote the Thomson angular
collision matrix weighted by(1-c), including I,E,B,V as applicable, and

    M_x2 = integral dx x^2 * [x^2 nP(1+nP)/I3] Phi Phi^T.

Then the cold, leading-energy hard-process pole matrix is

    C_real,pole=[4alpha/(3pi)]theta^2/xmin * 2 A_l tensor M_x2,
    C_virtual,pole=-C_real,pole.

The angular matrices are assembled with the existing transverse tensors, not
guessed from intensity eigenvalues. Two angular quadratures agree. Contracting
with the temperature template x gives

    beta_l=[40pi^2/7]*(1-lambda_l(1))/xmin,

since integral x^6 nP(1+nP)dx=720*zeta(6) and the temperature-template norm is4.
For l=1 this is precisely8pi^2/xmin, derived independently from the Gould
three-photon kernel in Stage81.

For the ACTUAL finite-shift Compton events, evaluate p.p'=1+omega_rest^2(1-c)r,
r=1/[1+omega_rest(1-c)], and multiply each original event weight by2A(p.p').
This keeps the Juttner electron distribution, recoil, polarization and Bose
weights already used by the parent transport calculation. The virtual pole
uses the identical event map, including its gas energy/momentum partner.
The new bank samples three temperatures in the original interval. It is not
a converged time-interpolation bank for a new spatial evolution.

The Stage81 scalar1/(1+14.16theta) is an approximate emissivity correction,
not an exact coefficient for this general pole. It cannot be combined with an
unmodified exact virtual term. The new calculation exposes that mismatch
quantitatively rather than hiding it inside a numerical subtraction.

## 6. Do not erase real photon production

An infrared-safe hard observable assigns vanishing weight to a zero-energy
extra photon. For photon number, its weight is1: D(1)=1-1-1=-1 although the
hard-event difference1-1 vanishes. The soft pole matrices are not a blanket
cancellation of all DC emission/absorption. Read POLE-READ-CONTRACT.md before
using them on a spectral coefficient vector.

There is a useful sharper statement in the retained Gould/Planck setting.
For smooth f(x)=O(x), write h=lambda_l(1). The soft-share derivative of the
hard angular moment vanishes at w=0. Expanding the quadratic reaction measure
gives the would-be logarithmic coefficient

    -(1-h)/I3 * d/dt [t^4 nP(t)(1+nP(t)) f(t)^2].

Its integral is a frequency-boundary term, zero when both endpoints vanish.
This is not a proof for arbitrary chemical-potential profiles, finite endpoints,
nonequilibrium media, or a general relativistic collision operator.
