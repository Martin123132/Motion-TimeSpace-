# Fast gas memory and tail response without microscopic time stepping

This follows from GAS-INTERPOLATION-AND-RELAXATION.md, not from a new cutoff.
The local diagnosed gas rate is roughly1.2e10 per logarithmic scale factor.
The first saved half-step spans about2.16million local relaxation times.
A straight line between its endpoints therefore spreads an initial rapid
response across an interval far longer than its physical response time.

For a frozen local gas rate Gamma_g>0 and amplitude a_g, the transient
gas departure is a_g exp(-Gamma_g tau). Let A be the frozen retained tail
generator and b_g its unit gas forcing vector. With zero initial tail
difference, variation of constants gives exactly for this frozen system

    s_fast(tau) = a_g integral[0,tau] exp(A(tau-v)) b_g exp(-Gamma_g v) dv
                = a_g (A+Gamma_g I)^-1
                    [exp(A tau)-exp(-Gamma_g tau) I] b_g.

Use the integral or a phi-function evaluation at resonance; the inverse
formula is only valid when A+Gamma_g I is invertible. No blanket invertibility
assumption is needed for the integral representation.

If A is Metzler with A*1 <= -lambda*1 and lambda>0, its infinity-norm
contraction yields

    ||s_fast(tau)||inf <= |a_g| ||b_g||inf
           [exp(-lambda tau)-exp(-Gamma_g tau)]/(Gamma_g-lambda),

with tau exp(-lambda tau) in the equal-rate case. A simpler uniform bound is

    ||s_fast||inf <= |a_g| ||b_g||inf / Gamma_g.

The final bound follows directly by dropping tail attenuation in the
integral and integrating the positive gas exponential. It does not require
Gamma_g>lambda. With time-dependent coefficients, replace the exponentials
by the appropriate propagators and include variation of b_g and the
slow-equilibrium/lag terms; frozen values alone do not certify that case.

For the coupled problem, p is not held fixed by assertion: its equation
feeds r_star(u,p), whose derivative supplies the exact slow forcing of
r-r_star. A practical next calculation should retain the gas memory and
slow lag analytically, test photon/gas energy residuals, and compare its
off-node predictions to saved fine states before changing any history.

This calculation explains why shrinking every global time step to the fast
gas scale would be wasteful. It supplies an integration route, not permission
to discard the initial layer or to silently promote the original failed gates.
