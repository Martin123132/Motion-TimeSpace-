# 28. Causal axial memory and stored energy

5 October2026. New private daughter; stage27 and all earlier sealed work
remain unchanged. This step constructs and tests a time-domain interior
response, rather than fitting a delay to a periodic result.

## Result in brief

The condensate's already-derived radial wave operator supplies its own
response modes and their couplings. Eliminating those modes with initial
conditions gives a causal memory kernel. The same construction gives the
energy stored in that response and an exact surface-work identity.

Three actual-background spatial resolutions recover the earlier static,
frequency-squared and frequency-fourth coefficients. Pulse tests then
verify the work/storage relation, including energy retained after forcing
switches off. A separately integrated initial-value problem checks the
analytic convolution. This is an interior boundary experiment, not a
computed inspiral trajectory.

## 1. Normalization and inherited operator

Let x=r/R, zeta=rstar/R, tau=t/R. Write psi for the ell2 CPM master,
and q(tau)=psi at the compact surface. In this document q is a boundary
amplitude, NOT the binary mass ratio of some earlier documents.
The two real quadratures represent the m=+/-1 pair. Their radial action is

    S = kappa/2 int d tau d zeta
        [ |psi_dot|^2 - |psi_zeta|^2 - Vhat |psi|^2 ],
    kappa = 3/(4*pi), Vhat=R^2 V.

The normalization gives outgoing pair power
3 Omega^2 |psi_infinity|^2/(4pi), as in stages25-27.
Dots below mean tau derivatives, not physical-time derivatives.

The inherited operator A=-d_zeta^2+Vhat is positive. Its regular-center,
zero-surface realization A_D has the proved lower bound

    lambda_n >= lambda_gap = 3*N0^2 = 2.82664473495455.

Let u0 solve A u0=0, u0(surface)=1, and define

    Y0 = u0_zeta(surface),
    I = <u0,u0> = I0/R.

The inner product integrates d zeta. This is the actual compact-body
operator from
`../dynamical-axial-20261004/24-dynamical-axial-equation-and-controlled-response.md`
and `../dynamical-axial-20261004/axial_wave.py`.
The central background profile, Einstein-Hilbert term and G_R are retained.

## 2. Derive the modes and their source, rather than postulating them

Lift the boundary data:

    psi(tau,zeta) = q(tau)*u0(zeta) + eta(tau,zeta),
    eta(surface)=0.

The mixed spatial-energy integral vanishes because A u0=0 and eta has
zero boundary value. Expand eta=sum eta_n e_n with A_D e_n=lambda_n e_n,
<e_n,e_m>=delta_nm, a_n=<e_n,u0>. For one real quadrature,

    L/kappa = 1/2 [
        I*q_dot^2 + 2*q_dot*sum(a_n*eta_n_dot)
        + sum(eta_n_dot^2-lambda_n*eta_n^2) - Y0*q^2 ].

Varying eta_n and the surface q gives

    eta_n_ddot + lambda_n*eta_n = -a_n*q_ddot,
    p = psi_zeta(surface)
      = Y0*q + I*q_ddot + sum(a_n*eta_n_ddot),
    delta S_interior / delta q = -kappa*p.

These are derived radial modes, not independently selected oscillator
frequencies or fitted relaxation parameters.

For an initially unexcited interior the solution is explicitly causal:

    eta_n(tau) = -a_n int_0^tau
        sin[sqrt(lambda_n)*(tau-s)]/sqrt(lambda_n) * q_ddot(s) ds.

Nonzero initial mode amplitudes add known homogeneous solutions. They are
physical initial data, not covered by a claim that all memory begins at zero.

## 3. Frequency-domain recovery and retarded variation

For adiabatically switched harmonic forcing, k=Omega R, s=k^2,

    eta_n = a_n*s*q/(lambda_n-s),
    Y_ret(k)=Y0-I*s-s^2*sum[a_n^2/(lambda_n-(k+i0)^2)].

In the last expression s denotes k^2 on the real axis; the analytic
continuation uses (k+i0)^2 throughout. This exactly recovers the stage24
Dirichlet-to-Neumann resolvent, not a new definition of sigma_bar(k).

The continuum kernel is

    R_D(tau)=theta(tau)*sum[a_n^2*sin(sqrt(lambda_n)*tau)/sqrt(lambda_n)],
    p = Y0*q + I*q_ddot - (R_D * q_fourth),

when the initial derivatives required by integration by parts vanish.
Otherwise retain the explicit initial-data terms or the eta_n equations.

After elimination, a causal interface functional can be written with
q_plus=(q1+q2)/2 and q_minus=q1-q2:

    S_IF = -kappa int q_minus(tau) [Y_ret * q_plus](tau) d tau.

Vary with respect to q_minus before taking q_minus=0. This gives -kappa p.
A single-history quadratic expression instead symmetrizes the kernel and
cannot by itself generate the retarded equation. Our two-time matrix
control checks that distinction explicitly.
This bookkeeping follows the established initial-value framework of
[Galley2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.2745) and
[Galley, Tsang and Stein2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.3082);
the specific kernel here comes from our inherited radial operator.
Metadata and attribution scope are recorded in `references.json`.

Interior elimination is reversible storage in modes, not intrinsic friction.
Outgoing exterior conditions and the comparison BH horizon supply their
own flux terms. The boundary coupling to the exterior follows by variation:
the exterior's lower boundary contributes +kappa*p_ext*delta q, so the
combined stationary interface imposes p_ext=p_int, with psi continuous.
No adjustable junction force is introduced.

## 4. Positive stored energy and work

Remove the physical prefactor kappa/R and call the remaining energy Ehat:

    Ehat = 1/2 [
      I*q_dot^2 + 2*q_dot*sum(a_n*eta_n_dot)
      + sum(eta_n_dot^2+lambda_n*eta_n^2) + Y0*q^2 ].

This is just the positive original field energy. Parseval gives
I=sum a_n^2 for the complete continuum Dirichlet basis. For a finite
projection with I_remaining=I-sum_retained a_n^2 >=0, its positivity is
also transparent:

    2 Ehat = I_remaining*q_dot^2
       + sum[(eta_n_dot+a_n*q_dot)^2+lambda_n*eta_n^2] + Y0*q^2.

Using the mode equations, with no assumption about a sinusoidal source,

    d Ehat/d tau = q_dot*p.

For both quadratures sum the two identities. In physical units,

    E_int=(kappa/R)*Ehat,
    dE_int/dt=(kappa/R^2)*Re(q_dot^* p).

The exterior's interface flux has the opposite sign. Its outer outgoing
flux then completes the energy budget when the exterior is included.
A negative instantaneous surface power means energy is returning, not a
negative stored energy. The BH-subtracted kernel need not have a positive
energy or positive imaginary part by itself.

For a harmonic complex pair of surface amplitude q, the independent
Wronskian identity gives -dY/ds=||u_s||^2. Therefore

    E_int = kappa |q|^2/(2R) [Y(s)-2s Y'(s)],
    L_int,circ = -kappa |q|^2 Y(s)/(2R).

Thus E_int=Omega*dL_int,circ/dOmega-L_int,circ at fixed q. Eliminating the
exterior and source by stationarity preserves the corresponding on-shell
frequency derivative used for the full source action in stage27.
The local mode energy, the resolvent derivative and the periodic action
are therefore three representations of the same storage term.

## 5. Derived slow-forcing expansion, and where it is allowed

Define the positive spectral moment

    J1=<u0,A_D^(-1)u0>=sum a_n^2/lambda_n.

The leading derivative action and its boundary traction are

    L_local/kappa = (I*q_dot^2-Y0*q^2+J1*q_ddot^2)/2,
    p_local = Y0*q+I*q_ddot-J1*q_fourth.

Its stored-energy expansion is NOT obtained just by changing the sign
of a potential:

    Ehat_local = [I*q_dot^2+Y0*q^2
                 +J1*(q_ddot^2-2*q_dot*q_third)]/2,
    dEhat_local/dtau = q_dot*p_local.

The fourth-order expression is a low-frequency expansion of the causal
positive system, not a fundamental higher-derivative equation to integrate
with additional arbitrary initial conditions. Such extra solutions are
outside the expansion; the full modal system supplies the initial data.

For band-limited forcing with k_max^2<lambda_gap, the operator remainder
after the quartic term satisfies

    |Y-Y0+I*s+J1*s^2| <= s^3*J1/(lambda_gap-s).

The corresponding uniform multiplier bound gives an L2 time-domain bound.
Our compact-time pulses are NOT band-limited, so that inequality is not
misapplied to them. Instead, for initially vanishing derivatives,

    |p-p_local|(tau)
      <= [sum a_n^2/lambda_n^(3/2)] int_0^tau |q_sixth(s)| ds
      <= (J1/sqrt(lambda_gap)) int_0^tau |q_sixth(s)| ds.

The first version is used for the retained modal projection; continuum
conclusions also require control of the unresolved spatial spectrum.
For omitting modes n>N in a given Galerkin model,

    |delta p_tail| <= [sum_tail a_n^2/sqrt(lambda_next)]
                     int_0^tau |q_fourth(s)| ds.

These are causal history bounds, not fits to the pulses.

## 6. Computing the actual spectral ingredients

`causal_system.py` uses quadratic finite elements for the regular envelope
psi=x^3*w. For f=N*sqrt(F) and C=8 alpha R^2 W/F, the generalized forms are

    K(w,v)=int_0^1 [f*x^6*w'*v'+f*x^6*C*w*v] dx,
    M(w,v)=int_0^1 x^6*w*v/f dx.

The full spatial energy also includes the surface contact
3*f(1)*w(1)^2. Its derivation is checked symbolically. It must not be dropped
when computing Y0, the energy, or the Schur complement.

The reference background is not solved again. Spatial meshes64,128,256
cells are compared, with independent radial ODE values from stage24's
equation and an exact flat-space Bessel control.

The256-cell results are

    I = 0.14698388060248258,
    J1 = 0.0024746268887044613,
    lambda_1(DIRICHLET, dimensionless) = 31.25249023645274.

The last eigenvalue is a clamped-surface interior frequency squared,
NOT a radiating full-body quasinormal frequency. The analytic continuum
lower bound remains2.82664473495455; this numerical value is not substituted
for that bound without continuum error certification.

The positive inertia and quartic moment agree with the previous ODE
coefficients. A flat vacuum also has frequency dependence (I=1/7,
J1=1/441), so raw surface memory is not automatically a material Love number.
Body-minus-reference exterior matching must be retained for observables.

## 7. Pulse experiments and independent checks

Drive the surface with

    q(tau)=sin(pi*tau/T)^6*cos(0.25*tau), 0<tau<T,
    q=0 otherwise.

It has the endpoint smoothness needed above. Exact finite cosine
decomposition evaluates the retarded mode convolution without a stiff
time integrator. Runs compare64/128 retained modes, two spatial meshes
and time quadratures4097/8193/16385 points. No pulse parameter is fitted.

|T|Quadratic traction error / peak|Quartic traction error / peak|
|---:|---:|---:|
|4|0.1054431|0.0734207|
|16|3.1579118e-4|1.1666877e-5|
|64|9.7423475e-6|4.5644589e-8|

The fast pulse deliberately stresses the derivative expansion: even its
quartic approximation has a7.34% error. For slower envelopes the computed
quartic term improves the response substantially, rather than being
declared valid for every forcing history.

The64-to128-mode fast-pulse difference was4.1223e-7 of peak traction,
above the2e-7 resolution gate. It was not accepted as converged.
A256-mode refinement at16385 time points reduces the successive-mode
difference to5.6599e-8 and passes the unchanged gate. Its quartic error
is0.0734207095. Both coarse evidence and refinement remain saved;
the slow-pulse mode comparisons pass separately.

For T=4 the post-pulse stored Ehat is0.003866158959934466. It remains
constant once q=0. This is residual ringing of a clamped, lossless interior;
it is not energy remaining forever in a freely radiating body.
The very small T=64 remainder is below the final-work quadrature resolution;
an80-digit algebra replay checks the modal cancellation, not the continuum
accuracy of such a tiny physical prediction.

An independent8-mode DOP853 integration starts at tau=-1, before forcing.
It produces zero pre-forcing excitation and agrees with the analytic
convolution's traction within7.14e-14 of the peak. Its separately integrated
source work agrees with stored energy within2.85e-15 of the peak.
This is a check of two methods applied to the same finite projection.
