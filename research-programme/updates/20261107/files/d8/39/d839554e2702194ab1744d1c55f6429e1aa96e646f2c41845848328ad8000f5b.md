    V_j=e_j-(N_j/N_0)e_0,    j=1,...,p-1,    N V=0.

    X_l=q_T theta_l+chi V y_l,
    t_gas=theta_0+chi eta.

still use the parent's local heat_h(s); h(s)=heat_h(s)/chi is retained, even
for its tiny floating-point variation. We do not silently change the parent
coefficients to force this ratio to unity.

The map u=P z is invertible for chi nonzero and D nonzero. Higher Stokes
moments use the same template convention; they are not new conserved photon

For M_u u'=L_u u, a constant P gives M_u P z'=L_u P z. We apply an invertible
row normalization to obtain photon rows theta_l', y_l'. The gas row retains

    theta_0'+chi eta'

on its left side. Baryon velocity retains the parent's inertia mass. Metric,

## 3. Assemble the cancellation before taking floating-point differences

The Planck-linearized monopole collision is

    C0=A0(X0-q_T t_gas)
      =chi A0(V y0-q_T eta).

The order-one photon and gas temperatures cancel algebraically. They are
never separately assembled as two large source columns and then divided by
chi. The actual polarized mixed derivative is added from the saved source
bank, with its occupation, temperature and matter-rest-velocity terms.

Write each physical collision in expansion time as C_l=C_large+chi C_small.
With n=N/D and P_T=1-q_T n, its scaled rows are

    theta_l'=n C_large+chi n C_small,

For the intensity monopole, C_large is exactly zero after the common
temperature cancellation (the retained kernel has no I/V mixing). Higher
angular channels retain their actual recoil-dependent temperature response;
they are not artificially set to zero. Streaming maps theta to theta and y
to y with the original angular coefficients, so no spurious shape is formed
by subtracting two rounded copies of the temperature template.

Metric-redshift forcing uses q_T+heat_h R1, with the parent's weak finite-band
dilation R1. Its thermal and shape parts are inserted separately. The observer
readout later retains the strong derivative and the finite boundary term,

## 4. The gas exchange follows from the same photon moments

The parent nonequilibrium background supplies a photon density source
heat_h B_s d_b. In the gas-temperature equation, the corresponding background
heating-per-particle term cancels this explicit density contribution. The
temperature equation must not include one without the other.

After retaining their common collision core C_small, the gas equation is

    theta_0'+chi eta'
      =-(chi/heat_h) E C_small
       -(2 r/3)(2 sigma_T+sigma_L+i k v_b)/H
       -theta_0-chi eta,

where r is the inherited background temperature ratio. This is the parent's
gas equation, not an imposed instantaneous equilibrium. The finite slip eta
is evolved, including its fast relaxation.

The baryon momentum row uses minus one-third of the same photon dipole-energy
moment, with the existing inertia derivative and pressure. The new mixed
operator therefore preserves the original paired energy and momentum exchange.
The density-dependent background energy belongs also to r d_b in the matter
stress; a collision-only test must not mistake this background dependence
for a separate conserved gas-temperature energy.

All stress rows enter gravity as J_u P z. The metric is evolved without
projecting its constraints after each step. The phase-space representation
does not add a gravity closure or erase an existing source.

## 5. Keep the small physical response separate during integration

We retain the stable paired-response idea from Stage76. Let z_b solve the
baseline and d=z_full-z_b. For the analytically assembled mixed operator
Delta L in these coordinates,

    M z_b'=L_b z_b,
    M d'=(L_b+Delta L)d+Delta L z_b.

We evolve d directly from zero; it is not computed by subtracting large
solutions. Unlike the older stored variable, this coordinate difference is
not globally divided by chi. Shape components were already scaled by the

Reconstructing that analytic initial state differs from the rounded parent
encoding only at its recorded machine scale. The original initial metric
constraint solution is retained; no later constraint projection is introduced.
