## What actually caused the native discrepancy

The old implementation formed interpolated field and moving-edge
directions in binary64, THEN converted them to Decimal for the native
kernel. Spatial slope/curvature differences consequently operated on
already-rounded directions. Later high precision could not recover those
lost cancellations. Increasing label quadrature order sampled a different
sequence of these perturbations rather than removing them.

Six off-grid probes across both MTS grids separate field-direction,
edge-direction, beta-direction, beta-radial-direction, and background-
packet changes. Field and edge contributions are around1e-9 to1e-8 in
the inherited inverse norm; beta-direction contributions are around1e-18.
The signed decomposition closes below8e-63. These norms cannot be summed
as scalars. A forward derivative of the existing native gradients replays
the old finite-difference derivative to inverse differences below5e-57.
The independent metric coefficient chain-rule tests also pass.

## The minimal correction

Form both directional interpolants in Decimal before the native spatial
differences. Keep the SAME background packet, binary64 cardinal values,
quadrature nodes and weights, action coefficients, and precision gate.
Accumulate the completed covector in Decimal before final binary64 storage.
The derivative is propagated directly through the original kernel rather
than obtained by subtracting perturbed gradients.

Ordinary versus Decimal summation alone changes the result by only about
1e-15 in the inverse norm and does NOT fix the old4.4e-10 disagreement.
The wider arithmetic experiment also confirms that extending all metric
and cardinal evaluation precision is unnecessary to obtain the first
integration improvement: geometry-mode48-to64 gives4.12e-15 and full-
precision mode gives3.91e-15 on MTS-fine.

already-interpolated edge displacements. A smaller repair than changing
integration form is available: differentiate edge vertices on the fixed
reference cell before interpolating in the label. The two operations
