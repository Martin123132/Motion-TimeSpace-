## 2. Derive the static nonspherical equations

For a real normalized l=2 harmonic, use static even-parity Regge-Wheeler
gauge, with the perturbation amplitude epsilon suppressed below:

\[
 g_{tt}=-N^2(1-HY),\quad g_{rr}=F^{-1}(1+HY),\quad
 g_{ab}=r^2(1+KY)\Omega_{ab},\qquad h\longmapsto h+uY.
\]

An initially independent radial metric amplitude equals H by the angular
traceless equation. This sign convention means H=-2r^2 for the flat
external potential Phi=epsilon r^2Y used in the previous stage.

The derivation starts from the trace-reversed Einstein equation

\[
 R_{\mu\nu}=2\alpha\left[
 \partial_\mu h\partial_\nu h+h^2\partial_\mu\theta\partial_\nu\theta
 +g_{\mu\nu}W\right],\qquad \theta=wt .
\]

Direct linearization of the connection and Ricci tensor, followed by the
r-angular constraint, gives

\[
 K'=H'+2\nu'H-4\alpha h'u.
\]

Define

\[
 A=\frac{1+F-2\alpha r^2W}{rF},\qquad
 D=W'-2bh-2F\nu'h',
\]
\[
 B_H=\frac{-4\alpha r^2W-6-(1-F+2\alpha r^2p)^2/F
                    +8\alpha r^2bh^2}{r^2F},
\]
\[
 B_u=\frac{6/r^2+W''-b+4\alpha Fh'^2}{F}.
\]

The coupled radial equations are

\[
 H''+AH'+B_HH-\frac{4\alpha D}{F}u=0,\qquad
 u''+Au'-B_u u-\frac{D}{F}H=0 .
\]

The remaining angular metric amplitude is reconstructed algebraically:

\[
 K=\left[\frac{3-F}{2}+r^2F\nu'^2+
       \frac{\alpha r^2}{2}(Fh'^2-bh^2)\right]H
   +\frac{r^2F\nu'}2H'-\alpha r^2Fh'u'
\]
\[
 \hspace{1cm}
   +\left[\alpha r^2(W'-bh)-2\alpha rF(1+r\nu')h'\right]u.
\]

The radial and angular Einstein equations and the differentiated K
constraint are checked against this reduced system, not assumed away.
The compact expression for K is independently checked against the original
expanded symbolic expression.

A regular static phase perturbation has the positive integral form

\[
 \int_0^R\left[N\sqrt F\,r^2h^2(\delta\theta')^2
       +6\frac N{\sqrt F}h^2(\delta\theta)^2\right]dr=0,
\]

so it vanishes under the regular/no-flux boundary conditions. This does
not justify deleting phase perturbations in a time-dependent calculation.

The first-order charge perturbation is proportional to the angular average
of [2hu+(H+K)h^2]Y. It vanishes since integral Y dOmega=0.
No local charge-freezing condition or first-order frequency adjustment
is imposed.

The general static Einstein-complex-scalar tidal method is established,
not a new formalism claimed here. Sennett et al. (2017), discussed below,
provide a method comparator. Our calculation rederives the equations in
these conventions and treats this fractional potential's compact free edge,
which differs from their exponentially decaying scalar backgrounds.

## 4. Read the gravitational response from the exterior

For M=alpha m(R), the exact growing vacuum solution is

\[
 P(r)=r(r-2M).
\]

A decaying solution normalized to r^-3 at infinity is

\[
 Q(r)=\frac{1-z}{r^3}\,{}_2F_1(3,5;6;z),\qquad z=2M/r.
\]

It follows by reduction of order:
Q=5P integral_r^infinity [s(s-2M)]^-3 ds.
Its Wronskian satisfies P Q'-P' Q=-5/P, checked numerically without
subtracting large logarithmic expressions at small compactness.

Write H=aP+dQ and fix a=-2, the same applied-tide normalization as before.
In the local asymptotic rest-frame convention

\[
 g_{tt}=-1+\frac{2M}{r}+\frac{3Q_{ij}^{\rm geom}n^in^j}{r^3}
         -{\cal E}_{ij}x^ix^j+\cdots,\qquad
 Q_{ij}^{\rm geom}=-\lambda_{\rm geom}{\cal E}_{ij},
\]

matching gives

\[
 \lambda_{\rm geom}=\frac d{3a}=-\frac d6,\qquad
 k_2=\frac{3\lambda_{\rm geom}}{2R^5},\qquad
 \Lambda=\frac{\lambda_{\rm geom}}{M^5}.
\]

The exterior is a local tidal matching region, not a globally asymptotically
flat solution with an r^2 growing perturbation. The normalization separates
that imposed field from the induced response.

Here lambda_geom is in mu_*^-5 units. Physical mass-quadrupole response is
lambda_geom_physical/G_R. Lambda and k2 are dimensionless. As alpha->0,
geometric lambda tends to zero, while lambda/alpha remains finite;
Lambda diverges because M itself tends to zero. This is a normalization
limit, not evidence for an infinite physical deformation.

## 5. A derived weak-limit check, including the metric term

The earlier relaxation coefficient alone is not the full gravitational
answer. Expanding the new equations at small alpha with H_0=-2r^2 gives

\[
 \boxed{\lim_{\alpha\to0}\frac{\lambda_{\rm geom}}{\alpha}
 =\frac1{30}\left[2\chi_{\rm relax}
 +\int_0^R r^6(18w^2h^2+2h'^2-12W)\,dr\right].}
\]

This is derived, not fitted to the small-alpha solutions. To see the
coefficient, the first-order H equation has source

\[
 {\cal L}_2 H_1=-16m/r+16r^2(w^2h^2-W)+4D_0u,\qquad
 {\cal L}_2=\partial_r^2+2r^{-1}\partial_r-6r^{-2}.
\]

Subtract 4rm(r), which extends the exterior growing Schwarzschild
correction into the interior. The remaining source is compact.
The decaying Green coefficient is -1/5 times its r^4 moment.
Integration by parts using m'=r^2 rho, rho(R)=0 and
chi_relax=2 integral r^4 D_0u dr gives the boxed result.
The extra moment contains explicit metric/background contributions; it
does not change the earlier definition or solution for chi_relax.

## 7. Match the zero-frequency finite-size mechanics

Let Q_ij be the physical mass quadrupole, with lambda_mass=lambda_geom/G_R.
The conservative static response can be represented by

\[
 U[Q]=\frac{Q_{ij}Q^{ij}}{4\lambda_{\rm mass}}
       +\frac12Q_{ij}{\cal E}^{ij}.
\]

Stationarity gives Q_ij=-lambda_mass E_ij and, after eliminating Q,

\[
 U_{\rm tide}=-\frac{\lambda_{\rm geom}}{4G_R}
                    {\cal E}_{ij}{\cal E}^{ij},\qquad
 S_{\rm tide}=+\frac{\lambda_{\rm geom}}{4G_R}
                   \int{\cal E}_{\mu\nu}{\cal E}^{\mu\nu}d\tau .
\]

The electric Weyl tensor is taken in the body's rest frame and evaluated
on the applied vacuum field. This matches a computed zero-frequency
coefficient; it is not a new fitted oscillator or a time-dependent response
calculation. Gravitational radiation and derivative operators are additional.

For two identical widely separated bodies, the leading instantaneous
static correction obeys

\[
 \frac{|U_{\rm tide,pair}|}{|U_N|}
       =\frac{3\lambda_{\rm geom}}{D^5},\qquad
 \frac{|F_{\rm tide,pair}|}{|F_N|}
       =\frac{18\lambda_{\rm geom}}{D^5}.
\]

The prior reference at D=20R gives an energy ratio 1.96915e-8,
force ratio 1.18149e-7 and maximum fractional surface displacement
1.57751e-5. At D=10R the energy ratio is 6.30127e-7.
No physical size/mass was assigned, and these are conditional static
estimates rather than evolved binaries. They show how the derived
