## 2. Preserve the very small radiative phase

Let O be surface-normalized outgoing and H surface-normalized horizon-ingoing
vacuum solutions, with O(R)=H(R)=1. Let Q solve Q(R)=0,Q_x(R)=1, and let
O_R denote the real part of O. Define surface slopes

    O_x(R)=alpha_O+i beta_O, H_x(R)=alpha_H+i beta_H,
    beta_O=omega/abs(O0)^2, beta_H=-omega/abs(H0)^2,
    O=O_R+i beta_O Q,
    H=O_R+(alpha_H-alpha_O+i beta_H)Q.

O0,H0 restore unit asymptotic wave amplitudes. We integrate asymptotic outgoing
and horizon series, not finite Sommerfeld surfaces. Both current normalizations
are checked. Negative-frequency waves use the conjugated positive-frequency
boundary solutions, BUT their orbital source integral is recomputed at the
signed frequency; it is not copied from a different positive-frequency n.

This real-basis/current representation retains the small dissipative phase that
naive propagation/subtraction lost in the older circular prototype. The identity
W(O_R,Q)=1 is checked across the entire orbital radial interval. An independent
outward body solution provides another amplitude comparison.

In the canonical convention pericentre has t=phi=0. Under reflection through
a full radial period, r is unchanged, u changes sign and
omega_n t-phi becomes2pi n-(omega_n t-phi). Therefore the source integrals
against REAL radial functions are real. Writing J=j exp(-i phi), the real
half-period quadrature is

    I_y=-(2/T_r) integral_0^(T_r/2) dt j*
          [(f*y/r_p+y_x) cos(omega_n t-phi)
           -omega_n*u*y sin(omega_n t-phi)].

It is tested against both full-period distributional and integrated-source
formulas. Let I_R=I_(O_R), I_Q=I_Q, and b=Y_body/R. Then

    I_O=I_R+i beta_O I_Q,
    I_H=I_R+(alpha_H-alpha_O+i beta_H)I_Q,
    I_B=I_R+(b-alpha_O)I_Q.

The body response Y_body is solved from its inherited interior wave equation
at every frequency, with no phenomenological damping or adjustable delay.

## 3. Stable amplitudes, signed fluxes, and source work

Using W(V,O)=V O_x-V_x O,

    C_H=I_H/W(H,O),
    Delta C=-W(B,H)*I_O/[W(B,O)W(H,O)],
    A_infinity,BH=C_H/O0,
    Delta A_infinity=Delta C/O0,
    A_horizon,BH=I_O/[W(H,O)H0].

The actual body amplitude is the BH amplitude plus Delta A. The difference
is calculated by the stable expression rather than subtracting nearly equal
large outputs. A direct I_B/W(B,O) calculation is retained as a control.
Replacing the body boundary by the FULL complex BH boundary returns zero
difference, including the absorptive part of that boundary.

For the odd21 reality pair, c21=3/(4pi),

    P_n=c21*omega_n^2*abs(A_n)^2,
    Ldot_n=c21*omega_n*abs(A_n)^2=P_n/omega_n,
    Delta P_infinity=c21*omega_n^2*
        [2 Re(conjugate(A_BH)*Delta A)+abs(Delta A)^2].

Energy is nonnegative for both frequency signs. Angular momentum has the signed
m/omega relation. Exactly zero frequency has zero radiation; it would need a
separate static field solution for its field, which is not set to zero by that
statement. None of the benchmark modes is exactly zero. The smallest tested
abs(omega R) is1.571269e-5, the e=.6 precession sideband n=-1.

In the canonical real-source radial convention above,

    F_ret=-(c21/2)*Delta C*I_O,
    2 omega_n Im(F_ret)=Delta P_infinity-P_horizon,BH,
    2 Im(F_ret)=Delta Ldot_infinity-Ldot_horizon,BH.

The last two identities express dissipative source work and torque in the
smooth body-minus-BH response. They are proved algebraically for arbitrary real
I_R,I_Q, real body slope and signed boundary currents, then checked numerically.
For an arbitrary shifted source phase the bilinear uses the dual conjugate
source pairing; blindly squaring a phase-rotated overlap would be incorrect.
No square of a point-source delta-prime or difference of divergent stored
energies is used. The algebra identity is a consistency test, not independent
experimental confirmation of the retained action.

Different n are orthogonal in the radial-period average. Thus modal energy and
angular-momentum fluxes add. Infinity differences and net differences are
distinct: the latter subtract the separately computed BH horizon absorption.

## 4. Independent weak-field normalization check

The leading STF orbital current is J_ij=mu*h*r_<i*z_j>. Its contracted third
derivative yields P=(8/45)mu^2*h^2*M^2/r^6*(v_t^2+4v_r^2). Averaging on a
Newtonian Kepler ellipse derives

    average P_current=(8/45)*mu^2*M^4/[a^6*(1-e^2)^(9/2)]
                     *[1+19e^2/2+69e^4/8+9e^6/16].

This is not the full Peters-Mathews mass-quadrupole power. It independently
checks our specific odd current sector. The circular limit recovers the old
8mu^2 M^4/(45D^6) result. The actual finite-compactness BH flux divided by this
leading expression is .9973800,.9898796,.9833780 for e=0,.3,.6. Finite-GR
corrections are expected; this loose leading-order check is not an exact
finite-compactness target and is not used to tune the wave amplitudes.
