## What we actually calculated

We began with the recorded MTS scalar and its original4/3 potential, not a
generic new model mislabeled MTS. The original canonical real scalar does
not change its propagation cone just by choosing a different background.

An explicit phase/amplitude extension makes a condensate calculation
possible, but it introduces a second physical scalar, a U(1) symmetry and a
charged state. With the unchanged4/3 potential, that NEW extension has a
long-wavelength instability: sound speed squared is-1/5. This is not a
stability verdict on the original real-scalar theory.

We then kept the original potential term and tested a positive quartic
addition. With `y=lambda*varrho^(8/3)/g`, the calculation gives:

- local homogeneous stability for y>1/3;
- preference over the empty state at the same chemical potential for y>1;
- a derived wave metric and `c_s^2=(3y-1)/(5+9y)`;
- a fluid-like stress, not cosmological-constant stress, on that preferred
  homogeneous branch.

The repulsive term is an explicit new hypothesis, not a coefficient obtained
from the existing parent or fitted to the previous numerical residual.

## What it points us toward

The effective metric is derived from wave propagation, while the underlying
flat geometry remains assumed. The metric's linear tensor projection
vanishes at the homogeneous background. The first calculated tensor-stress
response, including finite momentum in the low-energy phonon approximation,
is a two-particle continuum rather than an isolated gravitational tensor
mode. This identifies the next physical question rather than solving it by
inserting the Einstein action.

Next: derive the full amplitude/phase finite-momentum stress response and its
interaction kernel for this explicitly stated potential. Look for a genuine
collective tensor pole, or a controlled reason this candidate cannot produce
one. Keep the previously adopted GR/Standard-Model framework as the unchanged
comparison standard. No condensate branch has replaced it.
