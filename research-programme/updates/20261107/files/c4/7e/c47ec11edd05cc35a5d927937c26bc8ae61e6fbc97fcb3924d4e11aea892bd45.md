## 1. A bound on exterior force, not merely an escape-speed argument

Deepening a potential by an additive constant does not change a bound-state profile. The previous escape-depth bound by itself did not settle whether otherwise invisible outer matter could confine the wave. Here is a comparison that uses forces directly and does not assume a halo radius.

For a realized spherical potential \(\Phi\), its positive nodeless state in sector \(\ell\) solves the radial Schrödinger equation. Let an auxiliary potential have \(\Phi_*'(r)\ge\Phi'(r)\) everywhere, and \(u_*,u>0\) ground states with the same regular origin. With \(\kappa=\hbar/m_h\) and energies specific,
\[
 W=u_*'u-u'u_*,\qquad W'=(2/\kappa^2)(\Phi_*-\Phi-E_*+E)u_*u .
\]
The factor in parentheses is nondecreasing. \(W(0)=W(\infty)=0\) (including a Coulomb-point comparison); \(W\le0\) follows by integrating before and after its single sign change, including equality for a constant offset. Thus \(u_*/u\) is nonincreasing and
\[
 \frac{\int_0^{fR}u^2}{\int_0^R u^2}\le\frac{\int_0^{fR}u_*^2}{\int_0^R u_*^2}.
\]
The last step follows by comparing each inner/outer pair of radii in the two integrals. The same statement applies to a self-consistent solution *after* its potential is realized. It does not apply to arbitrary superpositions of excited states.

Take the analytic comparator \(u_*=r^{\ell+1}\exp[-\alpha r-\beta r^2/2]\). Its inward acceleration magnitude is
\[
 g_*=\kappa^2[(\ell+1)\alpha/r^2+\alpha\beta+\beta^2 r] .
\]
Let \(V_+\) be the measured upper total circular speed at \(R\) and \(V_s\) any proposed upper bound on circular speed outside it. For nonnegative spherical mass, use \(\alpha=R V_+^2/[(\ell+1)\kappa^2]\), \(\beta=V_s/(\kappa R)\). Inside \(R\) the point-mass term beats even concentrating all the enclosed total mass at the centre. Outside, the harmonic term beats \(V_s^2/r\). This auxiliary oscillator is deliberately overconfining, not a proposed infinite-mass galaxy. Defining \(a=\alpha R,b=\beta R^2\), the heavy squared-speed ratio satisfies
\[
 D(f)\le D_*(a,b)=\frac{\int_0^f y^{2\ell+2}e^{-2ay-by^2}\,dy}
 {f\int_0^1 y^{2\ell+2}e^{-2ay-by^2}\,dy}.
\]
Increasing either coefficient increases this ratio (the likelihood ratio decreases with radius). Invert the *lower* observed \(D\) against this upper bound. For an independent total-mass floor take instead \(b=0,\ a=G M_{\rm total}R/[(\ell+1)\kappa^2]\); putting all the mass at the origin bounds any positive spherical finite mass, no matter its actual radial extent. Neither floor is sufficient.

## 3. The expansion response is fixed by the same vertex

For a canonically normalized light fluctuation about the local background, differentiating the nondegenerate heavy eigenvalue gives \(\partial_\ell m_h^2=\gamma_{\ell hh}\) (at that background, in this potential-vertex subfamily). With \(M_R^{-2}=8\pi G\),
\[
 C=M_R\partial_\ell\ln m_h,\qquad B=2C^2 .
\]
The sign of \(C\) is not determined by attraction. For number-conserving, adiabatic, nonrelativistic heavy matter, \(\rho_h=m_h(\ell)n,\ n\propto a_{\rm cos}^{-3}\). Covariant energy exchange then fixes *both* signs:
\[
 \dot\rho_h+3H\rho_h=C\dot\ell\,\rho_h/M_R,\qquad
 \ddot\ell+3H\dot\ell+V'=-C\rho_h/M_R .
\]
The sum conserves energy. The original \(H,\ell,\dot\ell,\Omega_m\) are taken from the verified zero-Λ fixture; \(\dot\ell/(M_R H)=-0.4836531\) today. Using the same unscreened coupling here as in the dwarf produces the drift in the table. The light-source column uses the inherited illustrative \(F=0.5\) share of pressureless matter; it scales with \(F\). This is a consistency test of *keeping* those original values and force law, not the solution of a refitted cosmology. Even with an extremely slow change per microscopic oscillation, changes per Hubble time are not necessarily small. Pole mixing, rolling backgrounds or distinct environmental dressings must be derived rather than silently equating their vertices.

**A prediction for expansion that does not require choosing a full mass history:** hold \(H,\dot H,\rho_h,\ell,\dot\ell,V'\) and the other sectors fixed at one epoch. In flat GR with the same minimally coupled light potential,
\[
 \Delta\ddot H=\frac{C\,\dot\ell\,\rho_h}{2M_R^3},\qquad
 \boxed{\Delta j_0=\tfrac32\Omega_{h,0}\,C\,\dot\ell_0/(M_R H_0)} .
\]
Here \(j=\dddot a/(aH^3)\); \(q=-\ddot a/(aH^2)\). The instantaneous \(H_0,q_0\) agree, but the next derivative does not. In the order of the four rows above the minimum \(|\Delta j_0|\) values are **0.410, 0.270, 0.866, 0.581**. The sign flips with \(C\). Thus \(\Delta H(z)/H_0=\Delta j_0 z^2/2+O(z^3)\) and \(\Delta D_L(z)H_0/c=-\Delta j_0 z^3/6+O(z^4)\) with standard metric redshift/light propagation. These are local Taylor coefficients, not fits or bounds from observations; do not extrapolate a truncated series to the BAO or CMB epochs. A change to \(V'\), the scalar trajectory, cosmic share, or screening can change this test; that would be a rederived branch, not the old constant-mass fit.

Numerically: an independent energy-conserving FLRW ODE with radiation \(9\,10^{-5}\) reproduces the historical \(\Lambda=0\) \(H(0.1)/H_0=1.05894599021115\) and dimensionless comoving distance 0.0971923482508 when \(C=0\). For each sign and both angular states, two different local mass continuations (linear mass and linear squared mass, same first derivative) independently recover the analytic jerk from \(H(z)\) and \(D_L(z)\) at shrinking positive/negative \(z\). Neither continuation is selected as a global parent law. No observational holdout inspected or likelihood rerun. A universally coupled dark scalar of this kind is not a novel invention; here the coefficient, strength requirement and expansion consequence were calculated from the retained vertex and the pinned fixtures.

## From the local force back to conserved energy and GR stress

The previous stationary one-scalar analysis forbade adding a radial gradient to a rolling light field and then simply declaring the energy flux zero. The retained mediator predicts what that flux is **for**. Subtract the homogeneous background. In a local weak, quasi-static, long-range patch with constant local \(C\), \(R\ll c/H,\mu^{-1}\), the slow scalar responds to the heavy excess:
\[
 \nabla^2\delta\ell=C\,\delta\rho_h/M_R,\quad
 \partial_r\delta\ell=\frac{C\,\delta M_h(<r)}{4\pi M_R r^2}=2C M_R g_h .
\]
Here \(g_h=G\delta M_h(<r)/r^2\) is positive for an overdensity; \(\rho_h\) is mass-energy in \(c=1\) units. The extra heavy acceleration is \(-B g_h\). A baryonic test body has no such direct acceleration in this subfamily.

The field's outward energy flux in a local orthonormal frame is \(F_\ell^{\rm out}=-\dot{\ell}_0\,\partial_r\delta\ell\). Consequently
\[
 4\pi r^2 F_\ell^{\rm out}=-\frac{C\dot\ell_0}{M_R}\,\delta M_h(<r)
 =-\dot{\ln m_h}\,\delta M_h(<r).
\]
The leading surface flux supplies exactly the rest-mass change of an enclosed, fixed-number heavy excess. If there is particle flow across the sphere, add its advective term; this is not a claim of exact stationarity. Time derivatives of the local perturbation, cosmological terms, self-energy and retarded corrections were dropped in this leading balance, not set to zero in the parent. An outgoing flux accompanies a decreasing heavy mass for the retained sign of \(\dot\ell_0\) and positive \(C\). This is a source-derived exchange, not a cancellation counterterm.

For the quasistatic gradient alone \(p_r=+\rho_{\nabla},\ p_t=-\rho_{\nabla},\ \rho_{\nabla}=(\partial_r\delta\ell)^2/2\). Its contribution to \(\rho+p_r+2p_t\) is zero, even though its spatial stress and energy are real. Restoring compactness \(\nu_h=V_h^2/c^2\),
\[
 8\pi G r^2(p_r-p_t)/c^4=2B\nu_h^2 .
\]
Thus the same scalar can exert a substantial force on heavy matter without directly adding that amount of *metric* rotation or lensing. Other terms in the stress, including the light potential and changes to the heavy density, were not cancelled by this identity. For DDO154, \(\nu_h=2.01\,10^{-8}\). At the four sample force floors the gradient anisotropy is \(4.8\,10^{-7}\) to \(5.0\,10^{-6}\) **of** \(\nu_h\); attaining an anisotropy of order \(\nu_h\) through the gradient alone would require \(B\sim2.5\,10^7\), not tens. Do not identify this force with the earlier large-pressure geometric phase. ## 4. Check against the parent instead of choosing a cosmology by fiat

Stage52's specialized cubic germ has \(Z=1-r,\ \gamma_{\ell hh}=\sqrt Z\,r\,U_3,\ g_{h\ell\ell}=-\sqrt r\,Z\,U_3\). With \(0<r<1\), the same cubic gives a decay rate
\[
 \Gamma_{h\to\ell\ell}=(GM^3/2)B(Z/r)\sqrt{1-4\mu^2/M^2}
\]
when open. This is a constraint on a proposed rescue, not a derived heavy occupation. It also induces a light cubic \(Z^{3/2}U_3\). The action is not “quadratic as before, plus a freely chosen galaxy force.”

There is an exact check if the constant cubic polynomial is continued away from the germ. Write \(s=\sqrt Z\,l-\sqrt r\,h\), \(u=U_3 r s/M^2\), \(A=\gamma_{\ell hh}l/M^2\). On the zero-heavy-density static trough \(\partial_h V=0\), \(A=u+u^2/2\); the locally stable branch has \(u=-1+\sqrt{1+2A}\), requiring \(A>-1/2\). Along it
\[
 V'_{\rm eff}=\mu^2 l+\sqrt Z\, U_3 s^2/2,\quad
 V''_{\rm eff}=\mu^2+(Z/r)M^2 u/(1+u),\quad
 Z_{\rm path}=1+(Z/r)[u/(1+u)]^2 .
\]
The trough is obtained by minimizing the heavy potential, not by keeping a populated state at its minimum; it is an auxiliary vacuum check. Near zero the path metric has no linear term, so canonicalizing it cannot remove the leading light cubic. The vertex needed for confinement therefore cannot be inserted while asserting an exactly unchanged quadratic action over an open interval. Higher operators, symmetry, or small motion-coordinate light overlap can change this conclusion only with additional physical input. The truncated cubic is not asserted to be globally bounded below.

**Stronger obstruction, without a polynomial truncation.** Suppose the passive real bath is quadratic and the only extra potential is a regular \(f(s)\) of the original motion coordinate, with \(f(0)=f'(0)=f''(0)=0\). In the vacuum mass basis the full Hessian is \(D+f'' vv^T,\; D=\operatorname{diag}(\mu^2,M^2),\;v=(\sqrt Z,-\sqrt r)\). Evaluating its characteristic determinant at the **original** light pole gives exactly
\[
 \det[{\cal H}-\mu^2 I]=Z(M^2-\mu^2)f''(s).
\]
For nondegenerate modes and \(Z>0\), preserving even the light curvature eigenvalue exactly on an open interval of sampled \(s\) implies \(f''=0\), hence \(f'''=0\) and no potential-mediated exchange there. Agreement at one point does not imply agreement on an interval. This is a local Hessian theorem, not a general theorem about a rolling pole in a nonlinear dynamical medium.

There is a separate background check which does not confuse that Hessian with a cosmological equation of motion. On the adiabatic stable vacuum trough \(M^2 h=\sqrt r f'(s)\), if the first nonzero analytic term of \(f\) has degree \(n\ge3\), then \(h=O(l^{n-1})\), the path-length field \(\sigma=l+O(l^{2n-3})\), and the first nonquadratic term in \(V_{\rm eff}(\sigma)\) is exactly \(f^{(n)}(0)Z^{n/2}\sigma^n/n!\). The adjustment of the heavy coordinate, its mass energy, and the induced kinetic term first affect the pure quadratic term at degree \(2n-2>n\). Thus adiabatic elimination cannot hide that interaction in an *exactly unchanged* quadratic light background near the germ. This says nothing against an observationally acceptable **modified** background. Independent bath self-interactions, derivative vertices, and genuinely multiple coordinates are not covered. The finite cubic trough and determinant were checked on 18 signed toy cases, maximum algebra residual \(3.1\,10^{-16}\), in `germ-result.json`.
