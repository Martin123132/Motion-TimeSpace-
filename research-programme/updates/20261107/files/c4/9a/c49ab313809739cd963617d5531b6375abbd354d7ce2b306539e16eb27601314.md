# 45 — Constrained polar Hamiltonian with independent phase

6 October 2026. Private daughter of stage44. No parent files or coefficients changed.

## What was derived

Stage44 derived the inertia of one driven adiabatic profile. Here the phase is
restored as an independent canonical variable and the spatial gravity constraints
are eliminated. The resulting l=2 Hamiltonian has three canonical pairs.
Its momentum/phase block is analytically a sum of positive squares. The operator
that removes the remaining conformal metric variable has a positive ground-state
identity. The other, coupled block is explicit and ready for a continuum bound.

The numerical check is substantially broader than stage44's single profile:
eleven coupled discretizations and five flat controls, up to 96 pairs. All tested
discrete forms are positive. The independent flat mode is recovered to relative
1.287e-11; this is agreement between calculations, not an accuracy guarantee for
the underlying candidate. A mesh-sensitive smallest coupled energy eigenvalue is
reported below rather than interpreted as a certified continuum margin.

## 1. Action, branch, and energy convention

The inherited dimensionless action is

\[
 S=\int\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{R}{4\alpha}
 -\frac12(\partial h)^2-\frac12h^2(\partial\theta)^2-W(h)\right]d^4x,
 \qquad W(h)=\frac38h^{4/3}+\frac18h^4.
\]

The common dimensional prefactor is unchanged. The background is

\[
 ds^2=-N^2dt^2+F^{-1}dr^2+r^2d\Omega^2,\qquad \theta=\omega t.
\]

The R20/C0.03 background has alpha=0.00019661998915671979,
R=19.73166220910539 and geometric mass M=0.296546804477414.
G_R and the Einstein-Hilbert sector are retained, not obtained anew by this step.
The scalar symmetry is global U(1), not electromagnetic charge.

Use the rotating canonical energy E-omega Q_charge. In polar canonical variables
Q_charge is linear in p_theta; there is no extra quadratic -omega Q term to
append by hand. Lapse and shift perturbations multiply the linear constraints.
Only after varying those multipliers are the constraints eliminated. The
quadratic ADM surface variation for the specified first-order decaying fields
vanishes at infinity; the second-order mass response is accounted for by the
constrained, background-lapse Hamiltonian construction. First-order l=2 charge
integrates to zero. Fixed total charge at second order is the reason for using
the rotating Hessian rather than raw unconstrained energy.

## 2. Spatial gauge and canonical variables

Normalize a real spherical harmonic by integral Y^2 dOmega=1, with L=l(l+1)=6.
Use spatial Regge-Wheeler gauge, not a premature lapse/radial-metric equality:

\[
 \delta\gamma_{rr}=F^{-1}H Y,\quad
 \delta\gamma_{ab}=r^2 K Y\Omega_{ab},\quad
 \delta\gamma_{ra}=0,\quad G_{\rm angular}=0.
\]

Define pi^{ij}=sqrt(gamma_background) P^{ij}; describe P by covariant components

\[
 P_{rr}=F^{-1}pY,\quad P_{ra}=jD_aY,\quad
 P_{ab}=r^2[kY\Omega_{ab}+gY_{ab}^{TF}],
 \quad Y_{ab}^{TF}=D_aD_bY+\tfrac L2\Omega_{ab}Y.
\]

The omitted gauge coordinates still have momentum constraints; their momenta
must not be set to zero. The tensor harmonic norm is L(L-2)/2=12.

Scalar perturbations are u=delta h and vartheta=delta theta. If Pi is delta
p_theta per background volume, define

\[
 v=h\vartheta,\qquad \pi_v=\Pi/h,\qquad
 w_0=\frac{r^2}{\sqrt F},\qquad b=\frac{\omega^2}{N^2}.
\]

These variables are used on h>0 and continued by zero outside the compact body.
They retain an independent phase; no relation to a single profile's time
derivative is assumed.

Maximal slicing sets the linear gravitational trace momentum p+2k=0.
Introduce Q_geom=H-K. The canonical one-form reduces exactly to

\[
 \int w_0\,[p\,dQ_{\rm geom}+\pi_u\,du+\pi_v\,dv]dr.
\]

Solving for K does not add symplectic cross terms, because its coefficient in
the one-form is p+2k. The three pairs are (Q_geom,p), (u,pi_u), (v,pi_v).
The global phase/charge direction is l=0, not an extra l=2 zero mode.

## 3. Momentum constraints, solved rather than discarded

Direct covariant tensor divergence gives

\[
 p'+\frac{2(p-k)}r-\frac{L j}{r^2}
 =\frac12h'\pi_u+\frac{\omega h^2}{2N}\vartheta',
\]
\[
 Fj'+(F'/2+2F/r)j+k-\frac{L-2}{2}g
 =\frac{\omega h^2}{2N}\vartheta.
\]

On maximal slicing these determine the non-independent momenta:

\[
 j=\frac{r^2}{6}\left[p'+\frac{3p}r-\frac{h'\pi_u}{2}
 -\frac{\omega}{2N}(hv'-h'v)\right],
\]
\[
 g=\frac12\left[Fj'+(F'/2+2F/r)j-\frac p2-
 \frac{\omega hv}{2N}\right].
\]

The divergence and contraction formulas were checked independently in the
(r,cos(theta),phi) coordinate metric, not only by substituting these answers
back into equations copied from this document.

## 4. Hamiltonian constraint and the lapse identity

At fixed canonical phase momentum the matter density variation is

\[
 \delta\rho=\frac{\omega h}{N}\pi_v
 -\frac12bh^2(H+2K)-bhu-\frac12Fh'^2H+Fh'u'+W_hu.
\]

The spatial curvature variation is

\[
 \delta R_3=\frac{2F}{r}H'
 +\left[\frac{L+2F}{r^2}+\frac{2F'}r\right]H
 +\frac{L-2}{r^2}K-(6F/r+F')K'-2FK''.
\]

Use delta R_3=4alpha delta rho and the background Einstein equations. With
H=Q_geom+K the constraint becomes

\[
 \mathcal D_l K= -\frac F r Q_{\rm geom}'
 -\left[\frac{L/2+1}{r^2}-2\alpha W\right]Q_{\rm geom}
 +2\alpha[(W_h-bh)u+Fh'u'+\omega h\pi_v/N],
\]
\[
 \mathcal D_l=-\frac{\sqrt F}{r^2}\partial_r
 (r^2\sqrt F\,\partial_r)+\frac L{r^2}
 +2\alpha(bh^2-W).
\]

Crucially, the background lapse satisfies D_0 N=0. This follows by differentiating
N'/N=[(1-F)/2+alpha r^2 pressure]/(rF), then using the field and mass equations;
it was checked symbolically, not postulated. Since N>0 on this branch,

\[
 \int w_0 f\mathcal D_l f\,dr
 =\int w_0N^2\left[F\left((f/N)'\right)^2
 +\frac L{r^2}(f/N)^2\right]dr.
\]

Endpoint terms vanish for regular, decaying fields. Thus there is no nonzero
regular/decaying homogeneous l=2 solution. This is also the maximal-slicing
lapse operator, supplying the local slicing test rather than merely imposing
trace-free momentum without checking its gauge condition. For the compact
sources used here, the regular/decaying elliptic problem is uniquely fixed.
This is not a claim of a uniform spectral gap or a bounded inverse on unweighted
L2 over the whole exterior.

## 5. Exact vacuum tail for the elliptic variable

Outside the body N=sqrt(F), F=1-2M/r. Introduce isotropic radius rho,

\[
 \rho=\tfrac12[r-M+\sqrt{r(r-2M)}],\quad
 \psi=1+M/(2\rho),\quad r=\rho\psi^2.
\]

The decaying l=2 homogeneous constraint solution is K proportional to
rho^{-3}/psi. Its logarithmic derivative supplies an exact Robin condition at
the finite integration join R_b. Its gravitational energy is integrated from
R_b to infinity with r=R_b/t, 0<t<1. The quadratic radial Ricci boundary contact
is not dropped at that finite join: the interior and tail are one integral.

This is an elliptic K tail, not an outgoing radiative condition on Q_geom,p.
Those variables have compact trial support in the numerical check. Increasing
that trial support probes basis dependence; it does not turn Ritz eigenvalues
into quasinormal frequencies.

## 6. Reduced Hamiltonian: positive block and coupled block

Write x=(Q_geom,u,pi_v), y=(p,pi_u,v). There are no x-y terms:

\[
 H^{(2)}=E_{\rm even}[x]+E_{\rm odd}[y].
\]

The momentum/phase block is

\[
 E_{\rm odd}=\int Nw_0\left[
 6\alpha p^2+48\alpha F j^2/r^2+48\alpha g^2
 +\frac12\pi_u^2+\frac F2(v'-h'v/h)^2
 +\frac{L}{2r^2}v^2\right]dr.
\]

Every term is nonnegative for alpha,F,N>0. For finite-energy l=2 fields this
block vanishes only for p=pi_u=v=0, with j=g=0 following from the constraints.
This is an analytical all-profile statement within the specified quadratic
domain, not an inference from the earlier positive adiabatic inertia.
Without removing the gauge trace, the DeWitt form for p=k is -3p^2/2: that
unphysical negative conformal direction is an explicit algebraic control.

For the coupled block, first solve section4 for K and put H=Q_geom+K. Define

\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathcal R_2={}&\frac N{\sqrt F}[-H^2/4+LHK/2]\\
 &+2N\sqrt F[r^2K'^2/4-H(K+rK')/2+3H^2/8]\\
 &+4N'r\sqrt F[3H^2/8-HK/2-HrK'/4].
 \end{split}
\]

Then E_even is the sum of

\[
 E_g=-\frac1{4\alpha}\int\mathcal R_2dr,
\]
\[
 E_{\nabla u}=\frac12\int Nw_0\left[
 F\{u'^2+2h'u'(K-H/2)+h'^2(3H^2/8-HK/2)\}
 +Lu^2/r^2\right]dr,
\]
\[
 E_W=\int Nw_0[W_{hh}u^2/2+W_hu(H/2+K)+W(HK/2-H^2/8)]dr,
\]
\[
 \begin{split}
 E_Q=\frac12\int Nw_0\big[&\pi_v^2
 -2\omega\pi_v\{2u+h(H/2+K)\}/N\\
 &+b\{3u^2+2hu(H/2+K)
 +h^2(3H^2/8+K^2+HK/2)\}\big]dr.
 \end{split}
\]

These are derived from the ADM Hamiltonian, including inverse-volume factors in
the canonical phase energy. Positivity is not evident term by term in this block.
In particular its curvature contribution cannot be discarded because another
term happens to have a positive sign.

For reproducibility the nonlinear radial/angular curvature integrand before
expansion, up to its displayed radial boundary derivative, is

\[
 N\sqrt\gamma R_3 \longrightarrow
 2NA+2N D_aA D^a\ln B+2NB'^2/A+4N'BB'/A,
\]

where A=sqrt((1+epsilon H Y)/F), B=r sqrt(1+epsilon K Y).
The radial contact is derivative_r(-4NBB'/A). The local second-order coefficient
and scalar canonical terms were checked against finite nonlinear variations
using 50-digit arithmetic, independent of the constraint solve.

## 7. Flat-space gyroscopic control

For zero gravity and H=K=0,

\[
 E_{\rm even}=\tfrac12\int r^2[\pi_v^2-4\omega\pi_v u
 +(W_{hh}+3\omega^2)u^2+u'^2+Lu^2/r^2]dr.
\]

The odd block gives pi_u^2/2 and the phase ground-state gradient form. The
canonical equations yield

\[
 u_{tt}-2\omega v_t+L_+u=0,\quad
 v_{tt}+2\omega u_t+L_-v=0,
\]
\[
 L_+=-\Delta_l+W_{hh}-\omega^2,\quad
 L_-=-\Delta_l+W_h/h-\omega^2.
\]

With x,y ordered as above, the canonical sign matrix is diag(+,+,-), not the
identity. Removing the u-pi_v cross term moves the lowest flat frequency by a
factor 11.1954; that is an intentionally incorrect control, not another theory.

## 8. Domain and numerical evidence

Matter trial functions are x^2(1-x)^2 times Jacobi polynomials on 0<r<R.
Gravity trial functions are x^4(1-x)^4 times Jacobi polynomials on 0<r<R_b.
Their canonical pairs share the w0 Gram matrix. Q_geom is rescaled by sqrt(alpha)
and p by its reciprocal solely for conditioning; no coefficient is fitted.
K is solved using a piecewise-linear finite-element weak constraint, with nodes
split exactly at the scalar surface and an exact exterior Robin tail.

Near the edge h is proportional to d^3. u,v of order d^2 have finite quadratic
form energy, although v/h may diverge there. These are form-domain trial
functions, not a claim that eigenfunctions have singular physical phase; the
operator-domain edge behavior must be recovered by the dynamics. All scalar
edge terms in this form vanish. The vacuum potential is nonanalytic, so arbitrary
finite scalar creation outside the body is not represented by this Hessian.
