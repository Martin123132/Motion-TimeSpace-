# A compatible nonflat source core: derived replacement family

The source-flat construction fixes the initial source corner but cannot use the exact Gram-null shortcut while retaining nonzero native samples. Its finite shape searches should therefore inform a genuinely different construction rather than repeated searches of the same space.

## 1. Preserve a quadratic core, not a quadratic annulus

At a material label with physical source position b, set y=R-b and require, in a finite neighbourhood of b,

    q(R)=p*y+(k/2)*y^2.

The same p and k are used on both sides. Then q(b)=0, the source slope jump J=0, and the one-sided curvatures agree without forcing the endpoint-dependent opposite slopes of the previous Neumann-null annular profile.

Every native stencil lying in this core annihilates q exactly. Choose the core wide enough to include the source-hinge stencils and their template neighbours. The source-hinge image then has row support disjoint from the field image; the full native field force is zero on a smaller core buffer for arbitrary positive metric weights. Endpoint-only extra template connections must also remain outside this buffer. This is a checkable support condition, not a claim that the entire native interaction vanishes.

Outside the core, join to arbitrary resolved interior structure and zero endpoint buffers. The whole state need not be a native-null quadratic; its native action, source force and radial metric must all be recomputed.

## 2. Derive curvature from the coupled source equation

For zero initial configuration rates, smooth one-sided metric traces, and zero native field force in the core, let

    c=N*sqrt(F),  d=2*c^2/b+c*(partial_R c).

The wave equation gives q_tt(b)=c^2*k+d*p. Twice differentiating the physical condition q(t,b(t))=0 at a zero-rate slice gives

    c^2*k+(d+a_b)*p=0,
    k=-(d+a_b)*p/c^2.

Here a_b is the acceleration produced by the full coupled field/source Hessian and force at this very state. It is not a free coefficient. This is an implicit joint state equation, because a_b and the metric depend on the entire profile. The formula is valid where c^2>0; a horizon or singular coordinate chart requires a different treatment.

The old obstruction required unequal source slopes tied to two distant Neumann endpoints while imposing the same curvature. This core removes that restriction: both source traces now give the same equation. It does not solve the separate native shape constraints by itself.

## 3. Explicit C3 transition

On each side let t be the source-to-endpoint fraction. Define a weight equal to1 for t<=9/50, equal to0 for t>=41/50, and on the transition put z=(t-9/50)/(16/25) and

    w(z)=1-35*z^4+84*z^5-70*z^6+20*z^7.

Its first three derivatives vanish at both transition ends. Multiplying the physical quadratic core by this weight gives an explicit C3 field with the required source jets and zero endpoint buffers. The existing source-flat interior modes may be added without changing those jets.

The prescribed native source-buffer width must fit inside the t<=9/50 core on every label. Fields must be built on actual physical source-fitted radii, not by assigning the same reference-coordinate polynomial on sides with different Jacobians. Material interpolation and finite P2 traces require independent replay.

## 4. Next numerical calculation

Build the two core columns and the retained interior columns, then solve the source-corner equation jointly with selected native shape constraints at a fixed positive nonzero norm. Every evaluation recomputes or consistently updates the native radial metric and obtains a_b from the coupled action. Retain withheld tests and a matched reference. Before any evolution, check constraint preservation; the old null-radical quartic formula does not automatically apply to this nonzero-energy family.
