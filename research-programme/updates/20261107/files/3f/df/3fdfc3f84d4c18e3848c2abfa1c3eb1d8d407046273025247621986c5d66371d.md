# Why the pinned-source acceleration layer survives refinement

## Setting and scope

This is an exact local constant-coefficient P2 mass-projection result, followed by a parameter-free comparison with the actual coupled accelerations. It is not an alternative dynamics and does not replace the variable-metric/source solve.

The constructed Neumann kernel slice has a pinned source value. Its computed normal acceleration is therefore zero at the source node, while the smooth interior equation requires one-sided limiting values A_minus and A_plus. Those values are calculated from the coupled source acceleration, not fitted.

Near either side of the source, freeze the positive kinetic weight and use uniform P2 cells of width h. Project the locally constant required acceleration A into the space whose source-node acceleration is zero. Let e be projected minus required acceleration. Then e(0)=-A, and the remaining mass equations are homogeneous.

## Exact recurrence

On a unit cell, the consistent P2 mass matrix is

    M0 = [[4,2,-1],[2,16,2],[-1,2,4]]/30.

Write e_j for the vertex error and m_j for the midpoint error in cell j. The assembled equations give

    m_j = -(e_j+e_(j+1))/8,
    e_(j+1)-6*e_j+e_(j-1)=0.

The bounded half-line solution is

    rho = 3-2*sqrt(2),
    e_j = -A*rho^j,
    m_j = A*(1+rho)*rho^j/8.

In particular, the nearest midpoint error divided by A is

    (1+rho)/8 = (2-sqrt(2))/4 = 0.1464466094...,

and the first vertex error divided by A is -rho=-0.1715728753....

These numbers are fixed by the consistent mass matrix. There is no free decay parameter and no fitted source acceleration.

## Refinement and regularity

Halving h halves the location of a given layer node but does not halve its error. The maximum error stays finite and nonzero when A is nonzero. Yet summing the geometric series of cell integrals gives

    integral e^2 dR = A^2*h*C0/(1-rho^2),
    integral (e_R)^2 dR = A^2*C1/[h*(1-rho^2)],

where C0 and C1 are positive quadratic forms of the unit-cell mass and stiffness matrices on the fixed vector (-1,(1+rho)/8,-rho).

Thus the layer can shrink in L2 while failing uniform convergence and developing a growing gradient norm. A refinement-sensitive higher-derivative or shape diagnostic is not by itself a physical blow-up theorem. Conversely, a stable bulk acceleration is not proof of a smooth source-compatible Taylor jet.

The actual parent has variable metric coefficients, material coupling and a graded mesh. Only the first locally uniform cells are compared with the frozen-coefficient formula, without changing the full solution. The approximation is a diagnostic of the observed layer, not an exact global solution.
