## Forced energy and physical density

DERIVATION.md supplies both the continuous forced-energy inequality and the
two-step discrete Green/energy bound. The physical source injection is
t^T f_gamma+f_g, evaluated in80-digit arithmetic. The saved energy-row mismatch
has dual norm1.2518777693055558e-16 and is included as a bound contribution.
Neither that defect nor the tiny nonzero pair injection was erased.

For initial zero attributed thermal state, the result is

    Q(S)=chi integral |J_phys|
         +chi^(3/2) integral (S-u) F(u)
         +chi epsilon_E integral F(u),

where F is the actual weak-force dual norm. The physical density is bounded
by Q throughout the interval. The canonical-field response generated during
the early window is separately bounded, not silently initialized to zero at
the later tail restart.

The continuous source bound refers to an explicitly named piecewise-linear
reconstruction of the saved weak forcing, whose norm envelope follows by
convexity. It does not certify an arbitrary uncomputed microscopic function
between those nodes. The discrete window bound does not require that
interpolation. Saved source-window state comparisons pass in both resolutions.

## Derived return path

FINITE-TIME-FEEDBACK.md derives the next coupling rather than guessing it:
variation of the rate r proportional to1/H adds -xi R_bg to the thermal
equations. The gas expansion term is not multiplied by that rate.

The causal bound is

    sup |xi| <= (external plus independent incoming bound) exp(G integral h),

with h built from the reference return-force norm, its compatible energy
moment and the representation defect. A coarse gain greater than one is
not automatically a failure: causality yields the finite-time exponential
bound without a small-gain veto or damping-gap assumption.

The numerical return coefficient has not been acquired from the reference
history in this stage. Dimensionless coupled controls test this derivation;
their coefficients are not MTS fits. The next step is to evaluate the
derived return force using the already-saved physical histories.
