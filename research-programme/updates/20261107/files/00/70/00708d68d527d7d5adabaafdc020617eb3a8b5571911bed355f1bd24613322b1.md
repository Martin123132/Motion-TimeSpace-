## 1. Conventions and the two actual metric channels

N=ln a; a prime denotes conformal-time derivative. H is the conformal
Hubble rate; k>0 is comoving wavenumber. Thus X'=H X_N.
omega_j=a m_j and Omega_j=sqrt(k^2+omega_j^2), with the retained constant
masses. R and W are conformal density/enthalpy coefficients in the
existing gravitational normalization. Potentials are dimensionless.

Use the parent's physical-weighted coordinates y. For each scalar,

    phi_n = delta_phi_s + v alpha,
    pi_n = delta_phi'_s + (v'-Hv)alpha,
    X = Omega phi_n, Y = pi_n.

The background equation is v'=-2Hv-omega^2 phi. Direct substitution into
the retained perturbed scalar equation, using alpha'=Psi-Halpha and
eta=Phi+Halpha, cancels every explicit alpha term and gives

    phi_n' = pi_n + v Psi,
    pi_n' = -2H pi_n - Omega^2 phi_n + 3v Phi' - omega^2 phi Psi.

Consequently,

    [X,Y]_N = F_h [X,Y] + U_Psi,h Psi + U_Phi,h Phi_N,
    F_h = [[omega^2/Omega^2, Omega/H], [-Omega/H, -2]],
    U_Psi,h = [Omega v/H, -omega^2 phi/H],
    U_Phi,h = [0,3v].

The second channel is Phi_N, not Phi or an independently fitted scalar.
The same derivation applies to the retained slow scalar and to the heavy
scalar; no carrier phase approximation is needed.

The complete retained system has the form

    y_N = F y + U_Psi Psi + U_Phi Phi_N.

F includes the original streaming, expansion, baryon pressure, collisions
and native outgoing hierarchy closures. Scalar/rest off-diagonal blocks
of F vanish; matter-matter collisions do not vanish.

Examples of physical (unweighted) metric injections:

| Coordinate | Psi injection in its N equation | Phi_N injection |
|---|---:|---:|
| alpha | 1/H | 0 |
| delta_c,n; delta_b,n | 0 | 3 |

| massless F1,n | 4k/(3H) | 0 |

| massive-node F1,n | 4k/(3Hv_i) | 0 |

Multiply these by the parent's respective energy weights. Polarization
has no direct metric injection. Full quadrupole metric terms cancel
against their dipole shifts.

IMPORTANT: in the native high-k-tau massless closure,
F2'=-3coth(tau)F2-F0,s', so its N equation has
-4Phi_N-4Psi-4(H_N-H)alpha. Its two injections are BOTH -4 times
its energy weight. The alpha remainder belongs to F. This closure is
retained in the central/double cases, not silently replaced by the
full-hierarchy equation used in the half-k case.

## 2. Keep the finite-quadrature enthalpy defect

Let W_q be the enthalpy in the actual density source and W the retained
background enthalpy. Define DeltaW=W_q-W; do not set it to zero.

With d_i the massive-node density coefficients and the same scalar
normalization as the parent,

    W_q = R_c+R_b+(4/3)(R_gamma+R_r+sum_i d_i)+sum_j v_j^2,
    (d_i)_N = -(1+v_i^2)d_i.

In the physical coordinates,

    D = sum_species D_component + 3H DeltaW alpha,
    Q = sum_species Q_component - k DeltaW alpha.

The scalar entries are D_h=vY+(omega^2 phi/Omega)X and Q_h=kvX/Omega.
The current and anisotropic stress dictionaries are also checked against
the native implementation. No pressure or quadrature contribution is
discarded to force a standard conservation formula.

Write D=d y and let ell be its free density-rate row:

    ell = d_N+dF,
    D_N = ell y + (d U_Psi)Psi + (d U_Phi)Phi_N.

The native equations and coordinate shifts give exactly

    d U_Psi = 3 DeltaW,       d U_Phi = 3 W_q.

For a scalar the first contraction is zero by cancellation of kinetic
and potential terms. The total nonzero first contraction comes from the
retained DeltaW alpha term.

Useful unweighted entries of ell, before division by energy weights:

    CDM density:          -R_c delta_c,n
    baryon density/flux:  -R_b delta_b,n - (k/H)R_b v_b,n
    massless species:     -2R F0,n - (k/H)R F1,n
    massive node:         -(1+v_i^2)d_i F0,n - (k/H)d_i v_i F1,n
    CDM velocity/defect:   [-R_c k^2/H
                           +3(H_N DeltaW+H DeltaW_N-H DeltaW)]alpha
    scalar h:             (2omega^2 phi/Omega-k^2 v/(H Omega))X -4vY.

The baryon density-rate row here is the actual retained native
approximation. We do not add an unimplemented -3 delta P_b term by
appealing to an idealized conservation equation.

DeltaW_N is calculated from the same constant-mass background equations,
the massive-node identity, and the retained H/gravity background derivative.
Its derivative is not set to zero or fitted.

## 3. Solve the metric channels explicitly

For the spatial-Poisson branch, Psi=Phi=-D/(2k^2). Differentiating that
constraint and using the previous density equation gives

    Psi = p y,             p = -d/(2k^2),
    Phi_N = t y,           t = -(ell+3DeltaW p)/(2k^2+3W_q).

The denominator is the original implicit density-metric denominator.
It is positive on the retained positive-enthalpy branch.

Therefore the full generator is

    B = F + U_Psi p + U_Phi t.

This is a sum of TWO outer products, not a new interaction ansatz. In
particular,

    B_hr = U_Psi,h p_r + U_Phi,h t_r,
    B_rh = U_Psi,r p_h + U_Phi,r t_h,
    B_hh = F_h + U_Psi,h p_h + U_Phi,h t_h.

Both feedback directions are now explicit functions of the background,
stress dictionary and k. They are not unknown response coefficients.

For either block r,

    ||p_r|| = ||d_r||/(2k^2),
    ||t_r|| <= [||ell_r||+3|DeltaW| ||d_r||/(2k^2)]
               /(2k^2+3W_q).

The induced two-way norm bounds follow by the triangle inequality, e.g.

    ||B_hr|| <= ||U_Psi,h|| ||p_r|| + ||U_Phi,h|| ||t_r||.

All these terms have numerical implementations in metric_channels.py;
the formulas were frozen before the six cases were evaluated.

For E_h=sqrt(v^2+omega^2 phi^2),

    ||d_h|| <= E_h,
    ||ell_h|| <= 4E_h + k^2 |v|/(H Omega),
    ||U_Psi,h|| <= (Omega/H) E_h,
    ||U_Phi,h|| <= 3E_h.

These supply background-amplitude bounds without fitting the scalar phase.

## 4. An explicit bound for the remaining matter operator

A bound on the rest block must also be justified, not supplied as an
unexplained stability assumption. Remove its metric outer products,
the exact native baryon-pressure row P_b, and the alpha column from the
high-k-tau closure. Let eta_H=H_N/H.

The two pressure-free dust/baryon density-velocity blocks have symmetric
logarithmic norm

    mu_fluid = [eta_H-2+sqrt((eta_H+1)^2+1)]/2.

Full-frequency slow-scalar streaming has norm omega_s^2/Omega_s^2.
Photon, polarization and massive streaming is skew in the parent's
weights; Thomson collisions and positive-time outgoing damping are
nonpositive. The massive tail has weight rate <=-1/2.

It follows that the pressure-free, closure-remainder-free rest norm is
bounded by

    d_base = max(0, mu_fluid, omega_s^2/Omega_s^2).

P_b is a single-row operator: its row is the EXACT native synchronous
baryon pressure perturbation, expressed in y and multiplied by
sqrt(R_b) k/H. This includes any native temperature dependence.
It is read independently from the native pressure observable, not
defined by subtracting whatever would make the bound pass.

When the high-k-tau closure is active, its remaining alpha entry is

    u_ufa = -4 weight_F2 (H_N-H)/(sqrt(R_c)k).

Its symmetric logarithmic norm is |u_ufa|/2. Thus

    d_free = d_base + ||P_b,r||_2 + |u_ufa|/2,
    mu_2(B_rr) <= d_free
                 +||U_Psi,r|| ||p_r|| +||U_Phi,r|| ||t_r||.

This bound keeps baryon pressure, Thomson drag, the actual radiation
closure and G192. Cold controls receive the identical construction.

## 5. Balance the heavy scale without changing any observable

Define r=k/Omega for the HEAVY block and leave the rest unchanged.
For constant m, r_N/r=-omega^2/Omega^2. Rescale the heavy norm by r.
The internal scalar generator becomes

    [[0, Omega/H], [-Omega/H, -2-omega^2/Omega^2]],

whose symmetric part is nonpositive. The arbitrary carrier and large
physical frequency remain skew, not an exponential-growth penalty.

The balanced comparison has explicit nonnegative bounds

    a = ||U_Psi,h|| ||p_h||+||U_Phi,h|| ||t_h||,
    b = r[||U_Psi,h|| ||p_r||+||U_Phi,h|| ||t_r||],
    c = r^-1[||U_Psi,r|| ||p_h||+||U_Phi,r|| ||t_h||],
    d = d_free+||U_Psi,r|| ||p_r||+||U_Phi,r|| ||t_r||.

This is a change of comparison norm, not a smaller physical error.
For an existing propagator P,

    P_bal = diag(r_final,1) P diag(1/r_initial,1).

Initial norms, forcing, final dual readouts and primitive endpoints must
be transformed together. The saved final output bounds are restored
unchanged; this identity is numerically tested.

## 6. Which dimensionless parameters control this estimate?

Distinguish

    epsilon = H/k,
    delta = k/(am),
    kappa_Omega = H Omega/k^2 = epsilon sqrt(1+delta^-2).

In the nonrelativistic scalar regime delta<<1,
kappa_Omega is approximately amH/k^2=epsilon/delta.
It is NOT legitimate to infer a uniform response estimate from
epsilon<<1 alone while allowing am/H to diverge arbitrarily.

A sufficient well-scaled family on a fixed finite N interval has:
positive H and a nonvanishing CDM norm weight R_c/H^2;
bounded density fractions and scalar amplitudes E_j/H;
bounded H_N/H, DeltaW/H^2 and DeltaW_N/H^2;
massive-node speeds bounded away from zero in this chosen norm;
bounded slow-field omega_s/k; and bounded native pressure-row norm.
These are explicit regime conditions, not established for every MTS
solution by this calculation.

From the displayed rows, for epsilon<=epsilon_0<1,

    ||d_r||=O(k),       ||ell_r||=O(k),
    ||U_Psi,r||=O(k),   ||U_Phi,r||=O(H),
    ||d_h||=O(H),       ||ell_h||=O(H+k^2/Omega).

Substitution, not a fitted k exponent, yields

    a=O(kappa_Omega), b=O(1),
    c=O(kappa_Omega), d=O(1).

Constants depend on the listed background/regime bounds, not on an
unknown adjoint or solution difference. For a barotropic pressure law,
the density part of ||P_b,r|| contains c_b^2 k^2/H^2; if that grows
without bound this particular norm does not give a uniform estimate.
It must not be silently dropped. A pressure-adapted norm could cover
additional regimes.

If kappa_Omega and these explicit quantities are uniformly bounded,
the comparison gain is uniformly bounded on the fixed interval.
For example, with Gamma=max(a+b,c+d),

    ||u(N)||_infinity <= exp(integral Gamma) [
       ||u_initial||_infinity
       + integral exp(-integral_initial^s Gamma) ||f_bal(s)||_infinity ds ].

This is a CONDITIONAL uniform response statement from explicit equations.
It does not require kappa_Omega->0, nor gravitational feedback to vanish.
Newtonian gravity retains gravitational growth. Conversely, a divergent
upper estimate outside this sufficient regime is not evidence that the
physical solution is unstable or that its Newtonian description fails.

This parameter identifies the limitation of the present energy estimate;
it is not a derived critical Jeans threshold or a new fundamental constant.

## 7. Connect the new channels to the physical error source

Keep the parent's GR-reference current and shear sources

    M=3H Q_GR/(2k^3),      S_shear=Pi_GR/k^2,
    L=1+3W_q/(2k^2).

The same injection algebra gives

    S_energy d_primitive = -U_Phi/L,
    S_energy g = G = U_Psi - [3DeltaW/(2k^2+3W_q)]U_Phi.

Both identities are checked. With E=S_energy e, V=U_Phi/L,

    E_N = B E + G(M+S_shear) + V M_N,
    Y = E - V M,
    Y_N = B Y + (G+BV-V_N)M + G S_shear.

These are product-rule identities, retaining the nonzero initial/final
primitive terms. All coefficients now refer to explicit metric channels.

To obtain a uniform small OBSERVABLE error, the forced integral and the
original physical dual readout, including its factor1/r, must be bounded
together. A bounded response propagator alone is not that conclusion.
The next stage should derive these source/readout products from the saved
stress/background equations and test the resulting bound on both models,
not fit another k exponent or demand that gravity disappear.
