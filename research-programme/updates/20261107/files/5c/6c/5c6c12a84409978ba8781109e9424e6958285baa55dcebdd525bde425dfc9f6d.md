## Temperature, standard templates and residual

With dM=x^2 w dx/I3, I3=pi^4/15, let g=x and C=<g,g>.
The temperature tangent follows from d n_P/d ln T=xw.
The standard number-preserving mu potential is m=x/beta-1, with
beta=3 zeta(3)/zeta(2). The Compton-y potential is
y=x[x coth(x/2)-4]. These normalizations are derived in Stage109.

For standard template columns S0=(m,y), define
 h=<g,S0>/C, S=S0-g h, eta=E/C, D=Phi-g eta.
Solve the normalized weighted two-column least-squares problem
 a=argmin ||D-S a||, R=D-S a, using inherited QR and independent SVD.
Then Phi=g eta+S a+R and also
 Phi=g(eta-h a)+S0 a+R.
The signed physical amplitudes are mu=chi*a_mu, y=chi*a_y;
epsilon_energy=chi*eta, epsilon_standard=chi*(eta-h a).
Delta n=chi*w*Phi; Delta I_nu=(2h nu^3/c^2)*Delta n.
These first-order amplitudes are at the saved epoch, not today's sky.

Energy discrepancy in the independently propagated energy is retained.
Consequently <g,R>=<g,Phi>-E to input arithmetic accuracy.

squared fraction. Mu and y are correlated, not independent norm budgets.
