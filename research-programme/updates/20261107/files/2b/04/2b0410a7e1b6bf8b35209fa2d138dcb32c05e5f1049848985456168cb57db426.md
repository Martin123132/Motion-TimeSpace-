# Stage117 — finer frequency grid and a genuine higher-precision gas solve

13 October 2026. Private/local continuation of Stage116. No observational refit,
parent-history rerun, physical-parameter change, GitHub operation or subagent.

## Main result

**The frequency-grid differences contract for baseline and both MTS correction
joins, in both saved backgrounds and both matching windows.** The final
radiation difference is now about2.6–2.7%, versus about8.7–9.1% on the previous
refinement. At selected difficult gas steps, an actual higher-precision coupled
solve changes the saved gas response by at most3.16e-10 of its peak.

The remaining1% grid flags and ordinary-precision gas residual flags remain
visible; they are not cleared by a good energy ledger or by a small absolute
signal.

## What the gas precision check establishes

The checker solves the original physical block equations derived in
`DERIVATION.md`, including both photon and gas unknowns. It does not merely
re-evaluate residuals or replace the gas equation by total-energy bookkeeping.

| Quantity, over the17 selected steps and68 branch cases | Maximum |
|---|---:|
|70-digit photon relative equation residual|3.38e-69|
|70-digit gas relative equation residual|2.12e-61|
|50/70-digit photon solution difference|2.57e-49|
|50/70-digit gas solution difference|1.51e-44|
|Gas state change / saved peak response|3.16e-10|
|Gas state change / saved local response|9.17e-9|
|Photon state change / saved peak response|3.46e-14|

All high-precision equation and50/70-digit agreement checks pass the frozen
1e-35 and1e-25 gates respectively. Independent validation also reconstructs
each witness's inputs from the saved histories and re-evaluates both saved
precision solutions at90 digits.

When the70-digit solution is rounded back to binary64 and the raw gas
residual is evaluated there,12 of68 checks still fail the original1e-7
gate; the worst is3.57e-5. This is strong evidence of precision sensitivity
in that local residual calculation. The current test does not separate
state quantization from residual-evaluation rounding completely.

The four full ordinary-precision response checks also retain gas flags:
the worst across total and component columns is9.21e-5. In the wide window,
the **total** baseline and both total MTS columns actually pass1e-7; their
component columns cause the aggregate failure. In the narrow window,
total-column maxima also exceed1e-7. The detailed column breakdown is saved,
not hidden behind an aggregate pass/fail.

This comparison includes differences between the original physical mass
matrix and the inherited energy-coordinate method's rounded/replaced energy
row, as well as assembly and solve precision. It would be incorrect to
attribute the entire shift to the linear solver alone.

Matrices, rates, sources and preceding states still originate in the saved
binary64 calculation. These are local coupled-step witnesses, not a full
higher-precision trajectory or a bound on accumulated gas-history error.

The fine-grid wide-window radiation response remains approximately4.707e-9
of the saved baseline norm and1.314–1.315e-8 of each saved MTS correction's
norm. These use **different denominators** and cannot be compared as model
fit scores. The narrow-window fractions are4.658e-9 and1.307–1.308e-8.

Photon equations, source identities, photon number and the paired
energy+gas+expansion-work ledger pass unchanged gates. The ledger maximum
is6.19e-14; this is reported separately from the failed raw gas checks.

Matching-window dependence and Stage116's reconstruction-time sensitivity
are inherited limitations, not erased by a frequency-only refinement.
Both windows remain visible. This stage tests numerical implementation of
the inherited collision approximation, not a new physical collision kernel.
