# Stage73 — physical polarized finite shifts and reciprocal evolution

This extends the retained MTS/Einstein photon–plasma coupling. It does not
change the action or fit a new coupling. The microphysical input is ordinary
tree-level Compton scattering, with unpolarized, nondegenerate thermal
electrons. Electron thermalization is a kinetic regime assumption, not a
derivation of electron mass/charge or an imposed photon-temperature closure.

## 1. Exact spin channels without small-energy cancellation

Use metric(+---), electron mass1, Pauli matrices sigma, and strip the common
electric-charge factor from the tree amplitude. Initial electron p=(1,0),
photon k=(epsilon,epsilon n), final photon k'=(epsilon',epsilon' n'). Let

    h = epsilon'/epsilon = [1+epsilon(1-n.n')]^-1,
    E' = 1+epsilon-epsilon', p' = epsilon n-epsilon' n'.

For real transverse initial/final photon basis vectors e_b,e'_a, define

    B_ab = sqrt((E'+1)/2) [ (e'_a.e_b) 1
      + (sigma.p')/(2(E'+1))
          {(sigma.e'_a)(sigma.n)(sigma.e_b)
           +(sigma.e_b)(sigma.n')(sigma.e'_a)} ].

B is half the electron-spin amplitude. This follows by applying the two
Dirac propagator terms to the rest spinor sqrt(2)(chi,0), then contracting
with sqrt(E'+1)(chi^dagger,-chi^dagger sigma.p'/(E'+1)). The upper block
reduces via {sigma.e',sigma.e}=2(e'.e)1; the lower block gives the two triple
products. No expansion in epsilon is needed and no 1/epsilon cancellation
remains in this representation.

For each final/initial electron-spin pair define the photon matrix
K_(s',s),ab=(B_ab)_(s',s)/sqrt(2). Then

    G(X)=sum K X K^dagger,  G*(Y)=sum K^dagger Y K.

The sqrt(2) averages the initial electron spin. G is completely positive
because it is an explicit sum of physical channels. At epsilon=0 it reduces
to the screen-overlap Jones map. The differential angular gain carries
3h^2/(8pi) per steradian, or 3h^2/2 under dOmega/(4pi).

An independent four-component Dirac implementation matches these channels;
spin-averaged traces recover scalar and linearly polarized Klein–Nishina
cross sections. Tests also retain circular polarization and off-diagonal
coherences, rather than replacing them with a scalar intensity.

## 2. Boosted screens and thermal electrons

For electron velocity beta, gamma=(1-beta^2)^-1/2, define the spatial boost
L=1+(gamma-1)beta beta^T/beta^2. A photon direction maps to
n_*=(L n-gamma beta)/[gamma(1-beta.n)]. A lab screen frame E maps to
E_*=P(n_*) L E. Projection removes the gauge component and preserves the
two screen inner products. Boost back using the inverse direction map and
the corresponding projected screen frame.

The physical event integral includes incident flux (1-beta.n), recoil h,
both Doppler factors, the channel above, and the Bose equilibrium weight
(1+n(x'))/(1+n(x)). It is not a Gaussian redistribution substituted for the
scattering amplitude. The outgoing photon direction and polarization basis
are both transformed, not just its energy.

Use the normalized Juttner distribution in electron three-momentum.
Writing u=(gamma-1)/theta gives

    p=sqrt(theta u(2+theta u)),
    p^2 dp = theta^(3/2) sqrt(u)(1+theta u)sqrt(2+theta u) du.

The remaining thermal exponential is exp(-u). Generalized Gauss–Laguerre
quadrature thus has positive weights and strictly subluminal velocities.
Normalization and mean gamma are independently checked against scaled
modified Bessel functions, not merely normalized to pass their own sum.

## 3. Reciprocity is physical, not an imposed matrix repair

The invariant two-body measure contains
dPi_p dPi_k dPi_p' dPi_k' delta^4(p+k-p'-k'). Exchanging initial and final
states leaves it unchanged. Recomputing the reverse Dirac process with the
recoiling electron as the INITIAL electron gives the forward channel
adjoint after electron-spin summation. This is checked directly; the
reverse amplitude is not assigned by transposing a numerical matrix.

For a thermal electron weight F and Planck occupations n_i,n_j,

    F' / F = exp(x_j-x_i),
    F n_i(1+n_j) = F' n_j(1+n_i) = a_ij.

Linearize the induced, anticommutator matrix collision with delta f=w Phi,
w=n(1+n). The coefficient of Phi_i is -a_ij and that of Phi_j is+a_ij.
For a forward channel K:i->j, one reciprocal pair therefore contributes

    w_i C_i = a [K^dagger Phi_j K - {K^dagger K,Phi_i}/2],
    w_j C_j = a [K Phi_i K^dagger - {K K^dagger,Phi_j}/2].

Node phase-space measures are included with w in a discretization. The trace
sum vanishes and the quadratic form is exactly

    sum w_i Re tr(Phi_i C_i) = -a ||K Phi_i-Phi_j K||_F^2.

The general complex2x2 identity and the induced-scattering coefficients are
proved symbolically in `derive_reciprocal_form.py`. It extends to a sum over
all spin channels and physical events. Subtract x_i(r+nhat_i.v)1 from each
potential and evolve the plasma with the opposite energy/momentum moments:
the same square then gives combined quadratic dissipation at finite heat
capacity and inertia. No instantaneous temperature condition is required.

## 5. A physical finite-grid test in the previously unstable sector

Rotational symmetry makes the scalar intensity dipole an autonomous linear
collision sector: linear screen polarization starts at l=2 and an
unpolarized electron bath does not mix intensity with circular polarization.
For Phi=f(x)mu P, integrate global rotations using
<mu_i^2>=1/3 and <mu_i mu_j>=(nhat_i.nhat_j)/3.

Let m(x)=x^2w/(3 I3). For the physical outgoing-event rate R_ij including
Bose weight, the exact reciprocal dipole weak form is

    f^T K f = -1/2 integral dx_i m_i integral dR_ij
               [f_i^2+f_j^2-2 cos(alpha_lab) f_i f_j] <= 0.

The bracket equals (f_i-cos(alpha_lab)f_j)^2+
(1-cos(alpha_lab)^2)f_j^2. Positive event quadrature therefore preserves the
sign for ANY frequency resolution. Piecewise-linear frequency potentials
give the mass matrix M and stiffness K by direct integration of this form.
This is Galerkin projection of a physical integral, not smoothing or a
phenomenological drag law. An independent strong-form work integral agrees.

Since x lies exactly in this finite-element space, let b be its nodal
vector. The coupled plasma-momentum equations are

    M f' = K(f-b v),   B v' = -b^T K(f-b v).

They conserve b^T M f+Bv and dissipate
(f^T M f+Bv^2)/2. The common-velocity vector(f,v)=(b,1) is a null mode.
Energy/temperature are not independently evolved in this dipole-only test:
their first-order angular moments vanish. The earlier Stage72 full scalar
heat-capacity solver is preserved for the next general grid coupling.
