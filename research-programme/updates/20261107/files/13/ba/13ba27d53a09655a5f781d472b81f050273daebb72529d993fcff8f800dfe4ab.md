## 2. Conserved-source translated multipoles

Use center-of-mass positions z2=a*d*n, z1=-chi*d*n and
Qij=3*lambda*a*MT/d³ n<ij>, where angle brackets denote STF projection.
For the leading adiabatic even-parity quadrupole with zero internal spin,
continuity gives integral xi_i j_j = dot(Qij)/2 up to trace.
Translation of the actual moments therefore gives

    Iijk = sum_A mA zA<ijk> + 3 z2<i Qjk>
         = [eta*MT*d³*(1-2chi) + 9lambda*a²*MT/d²] n<ijk>,

    Sij_tide = STF_ij epsilon_abi
                 [Qja V2b + z2a dot(Qjb)/2]
             = (9/2)lambda*a²*MT*Omega/d² l<i n_j>.

Here l is the orbital angular-momentum unit vector. The trace part cancels
from the STF current. Intrinsic odd-parity current response is not included
in this translation identity and has not been set to zero.

The lower-order circular shift is delta d/d0=3a*t/chi. Thus, after
normalizing Iijk by eta*MT*d0³ and Sij by eta*MT*d0³*Omega,

    O = (1-2chi) + 18a²*t/chi,
    J = (1-2chi) + 9a(3-4chi)*t/(2chi).

Differentiating the actual tensors gives squared phase-derivative norms
32 for n<ij> at order3, 8202/5 for n<ijk> at order4,
and 1/2 for l<i n_j> at order3.
These are checked componentwise in `derive_transported_moments.py`.

## 3. One action and one order of circular reduction

Set s=d/d0 and w=Omega/Omega_ref, with x,t defined at the reference
frequency. Before differentiating w, hold the physical radius fixed.
The circular part of the retained action, divided by mur*x, is

    Lbar = s²*w²/2 + 1/s + A*t/s6
         + x[theta0*s4*w4 + theta1*s*w² + theta3/s²
             + A*t*(xi1*w²/s4 + xi3/s7)],
    A=3a/(2chi),
    theta0=(1-3eta)/8, theta1=(3+eta)/2, theta3=-1/2,
    xi1=a(5+chi)/2, xi3=-7+5chi.

Radial-velocity terms vanish on a circular orbit. Solve dLbar/ds=0,
then use Ebar=(w*dLbar/dw-Lbar) at w=1. The result is

    s = 1 + (eta-3)*x/3 + 3a*t/chi
          + a(-6+26chi-chi²)*x*t/(2chi),

    E/(mur*x) = -1/2 + (9+eta)*x/24
              + 9a*t/(2chi)
              + 11a(3+2chi+3chi²)*x*t/(4chi).

In the test-companion limit the tidal energy's relative x coefficient
is 44/9. This matches the independent direct-metric derivation in stage20;
it is not obtained by fitting the present coefficient.

## 4. Radiation from corrected and translated moments

Write the mass quadrupole in the form
Iij=eta*MT*d0² [N n<ij> + T vhat<ij>], with vhat the unit tangent.
The radiation-sensitive amplitude is N-T. The unreduced N,T expressions
and their attributed coefficients are saved in `pn_algebra.py`.

The standard GR radiation kernel at this order is

    P = (1/5) Iij''' Iij'''
      + (1/189) Iijk'''' Iijk''''
      + (16/45) Sij''' Sij'''.

The explicit STF contractions reduce it to

    P/P0 = (N-T)² + x[(1367/1008)*O² + J²/36],
    P0 = (32/5)*eta²*x5.

The normalization follows directly from the multipoles. Expanding yields

    P/P0 = 1 + f1*x + ft*t + fxt*x*t,
    f1 = -1247/336 - 35eta/12,
    ft = 6(3-2chi)/chi,
    fxt = (-704-1803chi+4501chi²-2170chi³)/(28chi).

Dropping the translated multipoles does not merely change the new tide:
the point-mass baseline also loses (155/112)(1-2chi)²*x.
Both controls are required. Equal masses alone would conceal that defect.

An independent direct-wave bridge is available as chi tends to1:

    deltaP/PNewton = 6t[1-(22/21)*x+...],
    P22_BH/PNewton = 1-(107/21)*x+...,
    Re(deltaA/A_BH) = 3t[1+(85/21)*x+...].

The last coefficient is the previous source-normalized field result,
not a coefficient inferred from this PN reduction.
P22_BH here means the polar l=2,m=+/-2 pair, NOT the all-multipole flux.

## 5. Energy balance, chirp and phase

From dot(E)=-P and t=lambda*x5/MT5,

    dx/dtime = (64eta/(5MT))*x5 [1+c1*x+ct*t+cxt*x*t],
    c1 = -743/336 - 11eta/4,
    ct = 6(12-11chi)/chi,
    cxt = (4421-12263chi+26502chi²-18508chi³)/(56chi).

The energy derivative weights the four Ebar coefficients by 1,2,6,7.
Ignoring the x^5 dependence of t fails the leading-tidal regression.
The normalized inverse chirp has mixed coefficient
B=2*c1*ct-cxt, not merely -cxt.

For the stationary-phase Fourier convention with the usual positive
point-mass Newtonian phase, the tidal correction is

    deltaPsi = (3/(128eta))*x^(-5/2) [psi_t*t + psi_xt*x*t],
    psi_t = -24(12-11chi)/chi,
    psi_xt = -5(3179-919chi-2286chi²+260chi³)/(28chi).

The time-domain accumulated GW-phase correction between endpoints is
the difference of the primitive

    lambda/(16eta*MT5) [-ct*x^(5/2) + (5/7)*B*x^(7/2)].

formulas. Published final circular coefficients are same-source algebra
benchmarks, not independent measurements or independent field derivations.

## 6. The residual tail belongs to both calculations

The direct saved waves retain curvature scattering that a first-PN
instantaneous formula omits. For a monochromatic quadrupole exp(-i*omega*t),
the leading universal GR tail is obtained from Blanchet2006 Eq97:
https://www2.iap.fr/users/blanchet/images/lrr-2006-4BW.pdf .

    Uij = Iij'' + 2MT integral_0^infinity
          Iij''''(t-tau)[ln(tau/(2b))+11/12] dtau.

Apply Abel damping exp(-epsilon*tau), then take epsilon to0 from above.
The Laplace derivative of Gamma(1+a)/s^(1+a) gives

    integral = -pi/(2omega)
             + i[-EulerGamma-ln(2b*omega)+11/12]/omega,
    deltaU/U0 = pi*MT*omega
                +2i*MT*omega[EulerGamma+ln(2b*omega)-11/12].

The leading flux correction is therefore 2pi*MT*omega=4pi*x^(3/2),
using omega=2Omega. The arbitrary log scale only affects phase.
This coefficient is derived from the retained propagation kernel, not fitted.
