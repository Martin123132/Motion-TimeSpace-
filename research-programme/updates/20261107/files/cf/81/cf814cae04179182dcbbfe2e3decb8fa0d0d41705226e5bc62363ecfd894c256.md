# Two-potential weak-field reduction and scope

This supplements DERIVATION.md without changing any frozen numerical input.
G is the retained Einstein-action normalization; this step does not derive
Einstein gravity from non-geometric MTS assumptions or derive G numerically.

For a flat FLRW background, the leading static scalar-gradient part of
the Einstein-Hilbert action, after discarding the explicitly subleading
time/background-curvature terms and boundary terms, is

    S_grad = M_Pl^2 integral d tau d^3x a^2
             [(grad Phi)^2 - 2 grad Phi dot grad Psi].

Nonrelativistic matter adds -integral a^4 delta rho Psi. Variation in
Phi gives laplacian(Phi-Psi)=0. With no independent harmonic boundary mode,
Phi=Psi; substitution gives -M_Pl^2 a^2 (grad Psi)^2-a^4 delta rho Psi.
Variation then gives laplacian Psi=a^2 delta rho/(2 M_Pl^2).
For a Fourier mode, -k^2 Psi=D/2, D=a^2 delta rho/M_Pl^2.

This explains the sign and coefficient used in the frozen Poisson source.
It explicitly needs the stated boundary condition and static, weak-field,
nonrelativistic ordering; it is not a derivation of those assumptions.

The dust-force branch drops the spatial-metric contribution to material
evolution at leading Newtonian order. Its zero Phi_driver is an omitted
coupling, not the physical metric solution Phi=0, and cannot be used to
predict lensing. Relativistic species remain live in the comparison. That
hybrid is deliberately a test of reduction error, not a replacement
relativistic theory. The Poisson-spatial comparison retains Phi_driver=Psi.

The retained radiation-rich interval need not make either truncation small.
The independently evolved GR control must pass the old numerical gate;
physical Poisson/Newton differences are reported rather than relabelled as
numerical failures or hidden by a fit.
