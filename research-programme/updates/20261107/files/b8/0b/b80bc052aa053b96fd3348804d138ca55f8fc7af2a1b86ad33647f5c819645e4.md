# Stage80: deriving the emitting-background response

This continues the two Stage79 parents. It does not replace their action,
background calibration, constants, finite-shift Compton kernel or earlier
empirical results. All quantities below use the retained early-time kinetic
approximation, a=1e-6 to1e-5. A density derivative at fixed reference expansion
is a source-sector calculation, not a self-gravitating finite-wavenumber mode.

## 1. The density term that must not be discarded

Write C=C_C+C_DC+C_BR per unit proper time. With fixed fully-ionized H/He
composition, the collision rates scale as

    C_C, C_DC proportional to n_e;
    C_BR proportional to n_e sum_i Z_i^2 n_i g_i.

Consequently a fractional baryon density perturbation d, holding the
instantaneous radiation and gas temperature fixed, gives

    delta_d C = (C_C+C_DC+2 C_BR) d.

The second BR factor is ion density, not a duplicate electron-opacity factor.
For gas temperature, the collision heating is divided by the number of gas
particles. Its perturbation is therefore proportional to

    -(delta Q_gamma - d Qbar_gamma)/n_gas.

The common electron-opacity density term cancels in this specific heating;
the additional BR ion-density term survives. Copying the old Compton-only
density cancellation to the whole emitting source would remove that term.
This is a physical derivative, not a new fitted coupling.

## 2. An independently testable, evolving density tangent

Use s=ln(a/1e-6), n=n_P+chi w B, w=n_P(1+n_P), T_e/T_star=1+chi b,
E=q_T^T M, N=q_C^T M and q_T(x)=x. Let K be the original finite-shift
Compton stiffness and D=D_DC+D_BR the positive absorption stiffness.
The existing equations are

    M B' = r(K-D)(B-q_T b),
    E B' + chi b' = -1-chi b.

For an independently evolved density factor f=1+d, use chi_f=f chi,
r_f=f r, D_f=D_DC+f D_BR. The Hubble history, photon reference temperature
and species composition are held fixed. This cleanly separates the source
derivative from the metric derivative that the spatial wave system must add.

Define the physical tangents

    P = partial_d[f B_f] at d=0,
    Z = partial_d[f b_f] at d=0.

Then delta n=chi w P d and delta(T_e/T_star)=chi Z d. Differentiation gives

    M P' = r(K-D)(P-q_T Z) + M B' - r D_BR(B-q_T b),
    E P' + chi Z' = E B' - chi Z.

The gas equation is equivalently

    chi Z' = -r E M^-1(K-D)(P-q_T Z)
             +r E M^-1 D_BR(B-q_T b)-chi Z.

Thus the extra ion term is visible on both sides of the photon/gas exchange.
It is not legitimate to force N P=0: number production is part of this
response. Its source is

    (N P)' = -r q_C^T D(P-q_T Z)
             -r q_C^T(D_DC+2D_BR)(B-q_T b).

The expansion-work derivative W_d'=1+chi b+chi Z satisfies

    E P + chi(1+b+Z) + W_d = chi.

The right side is the initial extra gas internal energy. The implementation
checks this evolving identity and the independent photon-number source.
Positive/negative density runs evolve the original equations separately,
without using the tangent equation. Their central differences test P and Z.

## 3. Common-temperature emission column without inventing a radiation temperature

The published near-equilibrium DC factor is G_eq(theta)=1/(1+14.16 theta).
It does not specify a general functional G[n,T_e]. Nevertheless there is a
useful, limited derivative that is determined.

Suppose the isotropic emission source is C=G R H, where H vanishes on the
whole common-temperature Planck equilibrium family. Let T denote that
family's tangent and let the background departure be chi D. For two smooth
extensions G_1 and G_2 agreeing on the family,

    Delta G=O(chi),  d_T Delta G=O(chi),
    H=O(chi),        d_T H=O(chi).

Therefore d_T[(G_1-G_2) R H]=O(chi^2). The mixed O(chi epsilon)
common-temperature source column is independent of the unknown extension.
For an isotropic perturbation h=w[x Theta+chi Y], delta xi=Theta+chi eta,
the nonthermal/slip part likewise needs G only at equilibrium to this order.
This result requires smoothness and a bounded scaled Y, eta; it does not
extend automatically to arbitrary O(epsilon) nonthermal or angular modes.

Let u=B-x b and let R be the finite-frequency Gould ambient integral saved
in Stage79. With k_DC=A R(e^x-1)/x^3, define

    L_R = delta_R[x w]/R = 5-x R'/R,
    L_G = -14.16 theta/(1+14.16 theta).

The directly derived common-temperature DC coefficient, divided by chi w,
is

    m_DC = k_DC {x(1+n_P) B + b[x^2 n_P-2x] -(L_R+L_G)u}.

The factor theta_star^2 in A is fixed in the chosen frequency coordinate;
it must not be independently varied again. Its physical temperature scaling
is already carried by the ambient integral and the equilibrium G derivative.

For BR let L_kappa be the logarithmic gas-temperature derivative of its
coefficient, with proper ion densities held fixed. The actual Gaunt tables give

    L_kappa = [sum_i Z_i^2 n_i(-g_i/2-d_lngamma g_i-d_lnu g_i)]
              /[sum_i Z_i^2 n_i g_i],

    m_BR = k_BR {x n_P B + b[x^2(1+n_P)-2x] -L_kappa u}.

The isotropic emission perturbation to retained order is consequently

    delta C/(chi w) = -(k_DC+k_BR)(Y-x eta)
                     +(m_DC+m_BR)Theta
                     +(c_DC+c_BR)delta_e+c_BR delta_i,

where c_j=-k_j u. A common fixed-composition baryon perturbation has
delta_e=delta_i=d. This formula uses no effective fitted photon temperature.

The code checks the two thermal columns at the physical chi with70-digit
arithmetic. A deliberately different smooth DC extension, agreeing on the
equilibrium family, changes the common-temperature result only at the next
heat order. That extension is a mathematical check, not a new physical model.
The already sourced BR first derivative suffices: unknown higher derivatives
multiply terms beyond retained order. The nonthermal background perturbation
is set to zero outside the finite frequency window; no uncontrolled rational
basis extrapolation is used. The common Planck thermal tangent is global.

## 4. Proper clock, rest frame and paired four-force

If proper-time collision rate C_rest is expressed in a moving lab frame,
invariance of the Boltzmann collision scalar implies

    C_lab(E,mu)=D C_rest(D E),  D=gamma(1-v mu).

To first order its velocity column is -v mu(C+x C'). For C=chi w c,

    delta_v C/(chi w v mu)=[x(1+2n_P)-1]c-x c'.

A proper-time lapse A multiplies the background rate and contributes A Cbar,
in addition to the separate proper density derivative. A full time-slicing
transformation must also transform the background profiles and metric; this
source identity alone is not a complete metric/gauge re-evolution.

Photon energy and momentum moments define the retained collision four-force;
the gas receives its negative. On a finite fixed frequency window,

    (1/3) integral x^3[-C-x C'] dx
       = integral x^3 C dx -[x^4 C]_edges/3.

The moving-window correction restores the full boost identity. In the weak
spectral form the boost row is (2V+x V')^T measure c; contracting with q_T/3
gives exactly the background energy moment. Both the weak identity and the
independent strong derivative including edges are tested on the saved spectra.

## 5. Finite-shift Compton derivative prepared for the same background

For incident/outgoing frequencies x,y, Delta=x-y and
d_e=(gamma-mean_gamma(theta))/theta, the first heat coefficients are

    left_1  = b d_e-Delta b n_P(x)+B(y)[n_P(y)-n_P(x)],
    right_1 = b d_e+Delta b[1+n_P(y)]+B(x)[n_P(x)-n_P(y)],
    thermal_1 = d_e[B(x)-B(y)-Delta b]
               +Delta{2b-b[d_e+Delta]-[1+n_P(y)]B(y)-n_P(x)B(x)}.

These follow by expanding the existing finite-shift pair coefficients, not
by replacing Compton scattering with diffusion. Tests on both actual emitting
histories compare against the old exact finite-heat expressions down to the
physical chi. The new closed forms avoid repeatedly subtracting tiny quantities
when constructing the next angular bank.
