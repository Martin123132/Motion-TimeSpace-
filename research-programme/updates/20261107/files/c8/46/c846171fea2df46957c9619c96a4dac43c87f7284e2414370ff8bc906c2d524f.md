# Cancellation explains why component accuracy is not total accuracy

Let T=B+C be the total, with B the baseline and C the MTS increment.
For any one scalar channel or the retained weighted spectral norm,

    kappa = (norm(B)+norm(C))/norm(T).

If independently established component errors obey
norm(delta B)<=epsilon norm(B) and norm(delta C)<=epsilon norm(C),
then norm(delta T)/norm(T)<=kappa epsilon. A sufficient shared component
target for a1% total tolerance is therefore epsilon<=0.01/kappa.
This is derived from the triangle inequality, not a fitted tolerance.

For the TWO saved numerical estimates, retain their measured component
representation remainders E=T-B-C. Then exactly

    delta T = delta B + delta C + E_alternative-E_reference,

and the script bounds its norm with all four terms. Baseline/MTS small
differences do not by themselves justify ignoring a larger total difference.

The bound concerns the observed discretization/history differences. It is
NOT a certified bound on the uncomputed exact solution. In particular,
dividing a half/quarter difference by3 requires verified asymptotic
second-order behavior; it is not assumed here.

The separate matching diagnosis evaluates old incoming plus new increment
near a zero crossing. Its cancellation factors describe THAT sum, not
baseline-plus-MTS at the interval endpoint. Keep the two diagnoses distinct.

Do not loosen the1% or1e-10 gates. Use these budgets to target the next
history/precision calculation where the physical total needs it.
