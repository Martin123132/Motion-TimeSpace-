## 1. Worldline and harmonics

Use geometric units G_R=c=1, companion geometric mass mu and Schwarzschild f(r)=1-2M/r. The matched monopole action `-mu integral d tau` gives the geodesic source

    T^{alpha beta}=mu integral u^alpha u^beta
                       delta4(x-z(tau))/sqrt(-g) d tau.

Its distributional divergence vanishes on a geodesic, as follows by integration against a smooth test covector. Let the particle radius be rho(t), v=drho/dt, Omega=dphi/dt, E=f(rho)u^t and h=rho^2 u^phi. Then

    v=f w/E, Omega=f h/(E rho^2),
    w^2=E^2-f(1+h^2/rho^2),
    vdot=-f f'/2+3f'v^2/(2f)+f rho Omega^2,
    Omegadot=Omega v(f'/f-2/rho).                            (1)

The orbit is stable and external to the body; numerically p>6+2e and rho_min>R_body. Specific E,h are constants of this leading test orbit. All new calculations retain the parent M=.296546804477414 and R_body=19.73166220910539, with no physical-scale calibration.

Unit-normalized scalar harmonics at the equator have

    Ybar_20=-sqrt(5/(16pi)),
    Ybar_2,+/-2=sqrt(15/(32pi)) exp(-i m phi).

The even tensor harmonic obeys `Ybar_phiphi=(3-m^2)Ybar` there. The even m=+/-1 projection vanishes for this equatorial worldline. Define

    C(t)=8pi mu u^t Ybar_2m,
    Cdot/C=-v f'/f-i m Omega.                              (2)

## 2. Stress projections and conservation

With delta=delta(r-rho(t)), the Martel–Poisson conventions give

    Qtt=C delta/r^2, Qtr=C v delta/r^2, Qrr=C v^2 delta/r^2,
    Qt=-i m C Omega delta/3, Qr=-i m C Omega v delta/3,
    Qflat=C Omega^2 delta,
    Qsharp=C r^2 Omega^2(3-m^2) delta/6,
    Qtrace=C(v^2/f-f) delta/r^2.                            (3)

Qt,Qr on the second line are vector-harmonic projections, not the corresponding rank-two stress components. These conventions are inherited from Martel–Poisson2005, equations4.17–4.20; source ownership and archived papers are in SOURCES.md.

When differentiating a coefficient with respect to the auxiliary field radius r, hold C(t),v(t),Omega(t) fixed, and only afterward set r=rho. Replacing them by off-worldline functions of r before differentiating a delta distribution changes its canonical coefficient unless the compensating terms are also included.

The projected identities are

    nabla_b Q^{ab}+2 Q^{ar}/r-3 Q^a/r^2-r^a Qflat/r=0,
    nabla_a Q^a+2 Qr/r-2 Qsharp/r^2+Qflat=0.                (4)

Using `partial_t delta=-v delta'`, all three delta-prime coefficients vanish. The delta coefficients vanish using (1)–(2). These six identities are checked symbolically, with separate numerical derivatives along the anomaly-parametrized orbit. No external force or compensating support stress is inserted.

## 3. The wave source is not just the circular source at a changing radius

Write L=2+3M/r, Lambda=2L and B_poly=8+12M/r+84M^2/r^2. Let q,qrr,qr,qflat,qsharp denote the coefficients of the distributions in (3). Substitution into the covariant polar master source gives

    (-partial_t^2+partial_x^2-V_Z) Z
         = G_raw(r,t) delta(r-rho)+F_raw(r,t) delta'(r-rho),

    F_raw=-2r^2 f^2 q/(3 Lambda)
         =C f(f^2-v^2)/(3L),

    G_raw=f {4qr/Lambda-qsharp/r
          +[-2r^2 f q_r+24M qrr/Lambda+2r f qflat
            +r B_poly q/Lambda]/(3 Lambda)},               (5)

where q_r is the radial derivative of q with the time-dependent coefficients fixed. Here x=r_star and

    V_Z=f(24r^3+24Mr^2+36M^2r+18M^3)
          /[r^3(2r+3M)^2].

The radial distributions must first be canonicalized:

    F_c=F_raw(rho,t), G_c=[G_raw-partial_r F_raw]_(rho).

The tortoise form is `S0 delta(x-xp)+S1 delta'(x-xp)`, with

    S1=F_c/f^2,
    S0=G_c/f+F_c f'/f^2.                                  (6)

The v, v^2 and vector-harmonic pieces in (5) are essential. The explicit eccentric source in Martel2004 AppendixA6 uses a different off-worldline extension involving the constants E,h. After canonicalizing each extension separately, both F_c and G_c agree identically on the mass shell. That agreement is also tested using independently handwritten numerical expressions, not merely the same generated formula twice.

## 4. Moving value and derivative jumps

Let u=dxp/dt=v/f and J=[Z]. A moving discontinuity in the wave operator has coefficients

    S1=(1-u^2)J,
    S0=(1-u^2)[Z_x]+2u Jdot+udot J.                        (7)

Thus the derived value jump simplifies to

    J=C f/(3L)=8pi mu E Ybar_2m/(3L),
    Jdot=J[-i m Omega-(L'/L)v],
    [Z_x]=(S0-2u Jdot-udot J)/(1-u^2),
    [Z_t]=Jdot-u[Z_x].                                    (8)

The timelike exterior source has |u|<1. A fixed-source recipe `[Z_x]=S0` would be incorrect for a moving worldline. Conversely, body and BH driven by exactly the same worldline have identical source jumps: their difference has no value or first-derivative jump. Its source contacts cancel before any field products are formed.

For the circular geodesic v=0,Omega^2=M/rho^3, (8) becomes the inherited source for every permitted m:

    J=C(rho-2M)/[3(2rho+3M)],
    [Z_x]=-C(12rho^3-16rho^2 M-24rho M^2-33M^3)
             /[6rho^2(2rho+3M)^2]+C M(m^2-4)/(6rho^2),
    [Z_t]=-i m Omega J.                                   (9)

These are exact regressions, including the static circular m0 component.

## 5. Fourier functional and its finite-interval boundary term

For an orbit with radial period T_r and azimuthal advance Delta_phi,

    omega_mn=m Omega_phi+n Omega_r,
    I_y=(1/T_r) integral[0,T_r] exp(i omega_mn t)
                         [S0 y(rho)-S1 y_x(rho)] dt.       (10)

Let y solve the common homogeneous Zerilli radial equation at that frequency. To check normalization against a metric, use the **dual time factor exp(+i omega t)** required by (10), rather than the physical exp(-i omega t) reconstruction without changing its sign. Define

    a(r)=(6r^2+6Mr+6M^2)/[r^2(2r+3M)],
    b(r)=(2r^2-6Mr-3M^2)/[r(r-2M)(2r+3M)],
    K=a y+y_x,
    H2=L/f(3y/r-K)+r K_r,
    H0=H2, H1_dual=+i omega r(b y+y_x/f).

For k=(1,v,0,Omega), the dual vacuum metric contraction, with its scalar harmonic factored out, is

    hkk=(f+v^2/f)H2+2v H1_dual+r^2 Omega^2 K.

Define the boundary functional on the worldline

    B_y=C r {u y_x+[i omega-Cdot/C-v/r+2v b(r)]y}.          (11)

Direct use of the radial wave equation and (1)–(2) gives the exact identity

    exp(i omega t) {C hkk+6[S0 y-S1 y_x]}
                     =d[exp(i omega t) B_y]/dt.            (12)

This is the moving-source extension of the circular source–metric reciprocity relation. It holds on a finite interval, with its actual endpoint term. On a complete radial period, `exp(i omega_mn T_r-i m Delta_phi)=1` and all remaining radial factors are periodic, so that endpoint term cancels. On a partial orbit it generally does not.

The tests use two independent real homogeneous solutions; linearity then extends (12) to complex outgoing solutions. Full-period, half-period symmetry and partial-period checks are kept separate. The largest sampled partial endpoint is .8323 on the integrated absolute scale, whereas the identity including it closes below3.88e-13 on that scale. Thus dropping it is a measurable mistake, not merely a question of notation.

## 6. Common-source response and counting modes

Let U be the actual center-regular coupled body solution, H the ingoing BH reference and V the common outgoing solution, with `W(A,B)=A B_x-A_x B`. The outgoing amplitude coefficients are

    A_body=I_U/W(U,V), A_BH=I_H/W(H,V),
    Delta A=-W(U,H) I_V/[W(U,V)W(H,V)].                    (13)

The last expression follows by expressing U in the H,V basis and applying the linear functional (10). It avoids subtracting nearby large amplitudes. The smooth difference extends through the source worldtube; the common source-dependent metric reconstruction contacts cancel as well.

Equations (10)–(12) establish compatibility with the inherited worldline/field normalization. In the canonical pericentre phase, the source functional is real on real radial test functions. With pair multiplicity g, the smooth-difference retarded response kernel therefore retains

    Fret=-3g/(16pi) Delta C I_V.                           (14)

Here Delta C uses the corresponding normalization of V. For an arbitrary source phase, use the dual-source pairing; do not blindly square a rephased complex overlap. The half-interaction factor comes from eliminating the quadratic field, as derived in the parent circular stage. Equation (14) specifies the kernel to evaluate next, not a new numerical evaluation of its imaginary part in this stage.

Mode counting for a real field is:

- m=2: include all signed n; pair with (-m,-n), with g=2. Negative omega remains in the spectrum.
- m=0,n>0: pair with (0,-n), with g=2. These modes radiate energy with zero azimuthal angular momentum.
- m=0,n=0: a single static component, g=1; solve its static response separately and assign no radiative power.

For each nonstatic representative, the inherited ell2 flux factor is `P=g*3/(8pi)*omega^2*|A|^2`, angular flux is `m P/omega`, and the stage32 radial-action weight is `n P/omega`. None of these formulas licenses dividing by zero for a static mode. The static action component must not be omitted merely because its radiation is zero.
