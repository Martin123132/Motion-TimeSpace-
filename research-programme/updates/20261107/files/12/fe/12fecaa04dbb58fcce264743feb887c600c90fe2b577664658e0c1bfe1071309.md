# Moving transmission and a concrete higher-jet repair direction

Work locally at an ordinary native interface X(t), with continuous alpha>0, beta>0 and a continuous field q. Use the wave density L=(alpha*q_t^2-beta*q_R^2)/2 and the same native potential U(values, positions, metric). Put V=X_dot, u=q_t, g=q_R and let v=q(t,X(t)) denote the common field trace. Then u_minus+V*g_minus=u_plus+V*g_plus=v_dot. The native derivatives are A=U_v and C=U_X at fixed trace values, including the sampled Eulerian metric derivative. The distinguished source and any nonsilent source-hinge covectors must still be treated separately.

## Exact moving laws

Vary the two wave domains with their moving common boundary. Their trace coefficient is [P], where P=beta*g+V*alpha*u. Their position coefficient, after holding v fixed, is -[P*g+L]. Including -delta U gives

    [P]=A,
    [P*g+L]+C=0.

Substitute u=v_dot-V*g and use continuity of alpha and beta. The v_dot term is common to both sides, so

    D*[g]=A,                 D=beta-alpha*V^2,
    D*[g^2]/2+C=0.

Consequently the same mean-slope law survives motion:

    m=(g_plus+g_minus)/2=-C/A,
    g_plus/minus=m +/- A/(2*D),

when A!=0 and D!=0. The subcharacteristic branch D>0 corresponds to |V|<sqrt(beta/alpha). There is no added support mass and no new force term. If A=0,C!=0, the pair remains incompatible; the formula is never used with a denominator floor.

## Time-symmetric second-order conditions

All following dots are TOTAL derivatives along the moving interface. At V=0 with vanishing first time derivatives, let j=[g] and a_X=X_ddot. Then

    D_ddot=beta_ddot-2*alpha*a_X^2,
    beta*j_ddot+(beta_ddot-2*alpha*a_X^2)*j=A_ddot,
    A*m_ddot+A_ddot*m+C_ddot=0.

Thus the second condition tested by the shape-form jet is not the whole moving field-transmission test: the first equation, including its velocity-squared term, is also required. It must be retained in subsequent evolution. The initial field and metric are time symmetric; the tests require smooth one-sided time jets and no additional interface action beyond the stated inherited potential.

Away from point loads the initial wave acceleration is q_tt=c^2*q_RR+d*q_R, where c^2=beta/alpha and d=beta_R/alpha. Denoting the one-sided second and third spatial jets by k and ell gives

    g_ddot = c^2*ell + ((c^2)_R+d+a_X)*k + d_R*g.

This identifies where a possible repair has freedom: the initial transmission conditions fixed values and first slopes, not the third spatial jets. A piecewise quadratic interpolation implicitly chooses ell=0 in each cell; that is an interpolation choice, not a consequence of the parent field equation. Changing the higher jets can change preservation without abandoning the native interaction.

## Explicit independent third-jet lift

For one physical interval [L,U], h=U-L>0 and x=(R-L)/h, define

    delta q = h^3*x^3*(1-x)^3*(ell_L*(1-x)-ell_U*x)/6.

At both endpoints its value and first two derivatives vanish, while delta q_RRR(L)=ell_L and delta q_RRR(U)=ell_U. This degree-seven polynomial therefore preserves the already balanced native values, first slopes and second jets, while opening two independent third-jet directions. It is not a point-load cancellation or a shrinking-grid bubble: the interval is an unchanged physical native interval and the native base field/potential remain nonzero.

This supplies a construction direction, not a finished dynamical repair. Such a lift changes bulk wave energy and hence the live metric. A_ddot, C_ddot and beta_ddot also respond to the coupled acceleration and its kinetic density. The resulting conditions must be solved together with the radial equations, source law and moving jump equation; the right-hand sides may not be held fixed and called a proof. P2 cannot represent this degree-seven lift exactly; the next implementation needs an appropriate higher-order representation or an explicitly converged projection.
