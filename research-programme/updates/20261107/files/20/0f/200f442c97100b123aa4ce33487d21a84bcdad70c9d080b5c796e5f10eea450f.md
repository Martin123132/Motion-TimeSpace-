# 40. Matched near-circular inspiral: clock, phase and eccentricity transport

Private daughter, 6 October 2026. Parent: stage39, matched finite-mass tidal balance.
This step integrates the derived equations, rather than adding a new fit or coupling.

## 1. Physical comparison and approximation

Use the previously solved Einstein-scalar condensate candidate, its inherited
Einstein-Hilbert normalization, and its static electric quadrupole deformability.
The companion is pointlike. Compare it with a point-mass binary of the SAME masses.
Both integrations have the same initial azimuthal frequency, the same initial
action eccentricity z, and the same final azimuthal frequency. Set both elapsed
times and accumulated orbital phases to zero initially.

We do not also equate the radial frequencies or the coordinate turning radii.
Matching those instead would define a different experiment. In particular,
z=2Jr/Jphi agrees with Kepler e^2 at leading small eccentricity and zero tide;
it is not an exact finite-tide geometric eccentricity.

Retain the leading weak-field, adiabatic, static electric tide, linear in lambda
and linear in initial zi, INCLUDING their product. Drop lambda^2 and zi^2 terms.
The conservative orbit and total outgoing quadrupole come from the same action
in stage39. Do not selectively add absorption, magnetic tides, dynamical response
or point-mass post-Newtonian terms to one side of this comparison.

Geometric units G=c=1 are used. M and mu have length units, lambda has length^5,
time has length units and frequency has inverse-length units. No astrophysical
mass scale, detector likelihood or seconds conversion is assigned here.

## 2. The inherited local laws in an observable-frequency coordinate

Define

    q=mu/M, S=M+mu, mr=M mu/S, eta=mr/S,
    D=(S/Omega_phi^2)^(1/3), ell=lambda/D^5,
    a=157/24, B=6(1+12q), C=837/4+20721q/16,
    H=459/19+8547q/76, n=19/6.

D is a frequency radius, not the canonical action radius d=h^2/S.
Stage39 gives, at the retained orders,

    Ddot=-(64/5) mr S^2 D^(-3) [1+a z+B ell+C ell z],
    zdot=-(608/15) mr S^2 D^(-4) z [1+H ell].

The damping coefficient changes when transforming from d to D: H is the
action-radius coefficient minus 48q. Terms proportional to z^2 in zdot are
outside the retained order, including ones generated by taking a ratio of laws.

Set Di to the common initial frequency radius, y=D/Di and li=lambda/Di^5.
Then division of the two laws gives

    dz/dy = (n/y) z [1+(H-B)li y^(-5)] + omitted higher orders.

Consequently the derived transport law with z(1)=zi is

    E=n(H-B)/5=23/2+205q/8,
    z(y)=zi y^(19/6) [1-E li(y^(-5)-1)].

There is no freely selected circularisation coefficient. At positive lambda
and q, the bracket reduces z relative to the matched point binary for y<1.
The amplitude sqrt(z) changes by half this fractional correction, to this order.
For zi=0, both z histories vanish: a fractional z difference is then undefined.
The CSV's fractional_z_difference at zi=0 is the limiting susceptibility as
zi approaches zero, NOT the quotient 0/0 or an eccentricity generated from zero.

## 3. Integrate the clock and phase without silently resumming

The inverse chirp at fixed instantaneous z is

    (1+a z+B ell+C ell z)^(-1)
       =1-a z-B ell+(2aB-C)ell z.

One must ALSO insert the changed z history from section2. Define

    A=2aB-C+aE=-(10660+35603q)/192,
    W(y)=1-a zi y^n-B li y^(-5)
         +zi li y^n [A y^(-5)-aE].

For scaled elapsed time T=(mr/S^2)t and scaled orbital phase Phi=eta phi,
with xi=Di/S,

    dT/dy=-(5/64)xi^4 y^3 W(y),
    dPhi/dy=-(5/64)xi^(5/2) y^(3/2) W(y).

Both start at zero at y=1. Define J_s(y)=(y^(s+1)-1)/(s+1). All used exponents
avoid s=-1. A common primitive is

    I_s(y) = -J_s(y) + a zi J_(s+n)(y) + B li J_(s-5)(y)
             +zi li [-A J_(s+n-5)(y)+aE J_(s+n)(y)].

Then

    T(y)=(5/64)xi^4 I_3(y),
    Phi(y)=(5/64)xi^(5/2) I_(3/2)(y).

The first two terms are the point binary and its linear eccentricity correction.
The last two are the circular tidal and mixed eccentricity-tidal differences.
All four pieces are stored separately, so small differences are not obtained by
subtracting two huge accumulated phases.

The reported fractional clock and phase differences divide those derived
differences by the retained-order point baseline. That ratio is a convenient
numerical summary, not a promotion of higher powers of zi to computed physics.

## 4. Recovery of earlier limits

### Circular limit

Setting zi=0 gives the orbital phase difference

    Delta phi= -B lambda/(32 mr S^(3/2))
                  [Df^(-5/2)-Di^(-5/2)].

Twice this equals the stage20 circular m=2 carrier formula,

    Delta phase_m2= -3lambda(1+12q)/(8 mr S^(7/3))
                       [Omega_f^(5/3)-Omega_i^(5/3)].

All four archived stage20 examples at q=.001,.01 and 20R->10R,10R->5R
are recovered with maximum relative numerical difference 2.23e-16.

### Test-mass limit and endpoint matching

The q->0 clock uses the independently archived outgoing coefficients 315/4
(stage37 action-radius shrinkage) and 459/19 (stage35 action damping).
Transforming their clock using d=D(1-z) recovers the new fixed-frequency
inverse chirp exactly. The all-q transformation d/D=1-z+12q ell+171q ell z/2
is checked separately against stage39's full action-radius law.

Stage37's quoted finite-frequency clock instead used fixed semilatus endpoints
and included a BH reference with absorption. Its eccentric clock correction
and this one need not have the same sign: the endpoint surfaces and benchmark
are different. We preserve that calculation and compare only the common
outgoing static-electric limit after explicitly transforming coordinates.
