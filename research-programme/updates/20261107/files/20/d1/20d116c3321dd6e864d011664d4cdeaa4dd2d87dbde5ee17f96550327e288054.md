# Product-form finite-element force evaluation

## Why this check was necessary

The all-native third-jet correction retains the earlier point/shape improvements but reduces the maximum moving-jump second response only from 32.5743863 to 32.3884462. Its acceleration derivative still differs markedly from the strong bulk expression. Before changing the physical profile again, compare two algebraically equivalent force assemblies on both the primary and matched reference branches.

The first comparison failed its **unchanged** relative gate of 2e-10 on both branches, at about 1.15e-9. Those runs remain failed. This discrepancy alone does not establish which assembly is more accurate.

## Stable evaluation without changing the trial space

On each reference cell, let x_j=j/7 and

    L_j(x) = product_{k != j}(x-x_k) / product_{k != j}(x_j-x_k).

The derivative is the sum of products omitting one additional factor. This is the same degree-seven nodal basis as the existing monomial-coefficient representation. The new evaluator uses the factored product directly, with exact rational denominators converted only at the final floating operation. Native coordinates, the action, physical field, source pin, polynomial degree and interaction support are unchanged. Exact Decimal coefficient transforms are retained for the native kernel.

The old evaluator differentiates/evaluates large signed monomial coefficients. Even a small relative error in this local operation can become a larger relative error in a nearly cancelling assembled force, and a still larger derivative error after inversion of the inertia matrix on small cells.

Implementation: `scripts/annular_product_basis_20261003.py` and `scripts/annular_product_live_20261003.py`.

## Independent polynomial integral oracle

For a saved cell polynomial p(x)=sum_a p_a x^a and basis coefficients L_j(x)=sum_b l_bj x^b, the constant-coefficient reference force is exactly

    -integral_0^1 p'(x)L_j'(x) dx
      = -sum_{a,b >= 1} a*b*p_a*l_bj/(a+b-1).

Evaluate this coefficient identity at 75 decimal digits, independently of Gaussian quadrature. At both the large-profile cell and the selected quiet-region cell, the old monomial quadrature differs by roughly 2.84e-12 to 3.66e-12 relative; product evaluation differs by roughly 4.03e-15 to 9.22e-14. This is a numerical comparison, not an interval enclosure. Twelve-, sixteen- and twenty-four-point rules are checked: more points alone do not guarantee better floating accuracy.

For the actual curved coefficients, compare direct weak force against the integrated-by-parts force from `DERIVATION-20261003-balanced-native-force-test.md`. At labels -0.5, 0, +0.5, the product form reduces the discrepancy from roughly 1.1e-9--1.46e-9 to 2.3e-12--1.84e-11 across those quadratures on both branches. It therefore satisfies the original 2e-10 comparison gate on those samples. The matched reference is a same-profile action control, not a fitted GR observational baseline.

## Full-coupled qualification and preservation test

The daughter live runner rebuilds the full field/source Hessian using the product basis, with the same initial physical data and each branch's own solved radial geometry. The direct product force and independently integrated force must satisfy the unchanged 2e-10 equivalence gate at the full material quadratures. Each acceleration must satisfy the same Euler residual gate; the source force and mixed inertia are not discarded.

Then the two accelerations are probed separately at three decreasing time-jet steps with fresh kinetic-inclusive radial gravity. This separates a stable force reformulation from a change in physical initial data. A successful initial force comparison alone does not establish time preservation. The measured jump, point, shape and source responses decide how far the numerical repair actually helps.

The first full-coupled driver passed the low-order force comparison (about 5.9e-12) and both Euler solves, then failed while multiplying Python lists in the output summary. The original failed runs remain unchanged. The daughter converts returned arrays explicitly, reruns the same equations and retains every original gate.

Runners: retained original `scripts/solve_annular_product_basis_20261003.py`, daughter `scripts/solve_annular_product_basis_v2_20261003.py`, and `scripts/probe_annular_product_basis_20261003.py`.
