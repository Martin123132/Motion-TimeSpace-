# Stage83 finite radiative source matching

The universal cancellation from Stage82 now has a finite, sourced vacuum
completion for unpolarized scattering. Its angular integral agrees with the
independent Lee–Schwartz–Zhang total cross section. Its energy–momentum moments
give equal and opposite photon/electron sources without discarding the created
photon. This supplies a calibration block for the same MTS radiation–gas–metric
equations; no parent action, species abundance, electron mass, charge or G changes.

## Conventions and primary source repair

Use metric(+---), m_e=c=hbar=1 and alpha=e_Heaviside^2/(4pi).
Brown–Feynman use e_Gaussian^2=alpha. Their equations27,29,35 give

    delta_virtual = -(alpha/pi) Re[U1]/U,
    U1 = P(kappa,tau)+P(tau,kappa).

For a resting initial electron, incoming energy E and hard scattering cosine c,

    E_B = E/[1+E(1-c)], kappa0=-E, kappa1=E_B,
    kappa=2kappa0, tau=2kappa1,
    y=asinh sqrt[-(kappa0+kappa1)/2],
    U=(1/kappa0+1/kappa1)^2-2(1/kappa0+1/kappa1)
      -kappa0/kappa1-kappa1/kappa0.

Both original P contributions are implemented separately from the newer
symmetric/asymmetric arrangement. The original imaginary branch is handled by
taking Re Li2 and ln|kappa|, consistently with Brown–Feynman AppendixW.
Apparent kappa=1 poles have a symmetric high-precision removable-limit
evaluation; the exactly forward angular endpoint is explicitly excluded from
the pointwise interface and is not a sampled quadrature node.

Two inconsistencies in arXiv:2005.06941v1 AppendixB are resolved by its own
integral definitions and the original paper:

    h(y) = (1/y) integral_0^y u coth(u) du
         = y/2 + ln(1-exp(-2y))
           + [pi^2/6-Li2(exp(-2y))]/(2y).

Its displayed expanded numerator therefore needs denominator12y, not y.
The original invariant is twice the newer kappa0, so the G0 factor in the
asymmetric part is G0(2kappa0), with G0 defined as in B.3, not G0(kappa0).
Both issues are visible on PDFpage30 as well as HTML equations57/63.
They are corrections to the transcribed source convention, not fitted choices.

The defining real integral for either sign of the argument is

    G0(x) = -2 integral_0^1 [ln|x|+ln(t)]/(1-x*t) dt.

For x>1 its apparent internal singularity is removable. The closed and
integral definitions agree in tests. Literal G0(kappa0) gives spurious
percent-level low-energy corrections; the corrected expression obeys the
low-energy limit and agrees with the independent total.

## The finite photon-mass conversion constant

Let p.p'=cosh(2y), v=tanh(2y), C=2y coth(2y), and

    A=(2alpha/pi)(C-1),
    V_fin=-(alpha/pi)[2Xsym+Xasym(kappa0,kappa1)
                              +Xasym(kappa1,kappa0)]/U.

The virtual correction with a photon mass lambda is V_fin+A ln(lambda).
The real soft integral uses exactly the same regulator and conserved current:

    S_lambda(Delta)=alpha/(4pi^2) integral_|k|<Delta d^3k/omega [-j(k)^2],
    omega=sqrt(k^2+lambda^2), j=p'/(p'.k)-p/(p.k).

Setting k=lambda*sinh(u), r=tanh(u), its angular integral is

    J(r,v)=4 atanh(v*r)/(v*r)-2-2(1-v^2)/(1-v^2*r^2),
    S_lambda=alpha/(2pi) integral_0^asinh(Delta/lambda) r^2 J(r,v) du.

Brown–Feynman equation39, independently checked against this one-dimensional
massive phase-space integral, gives

    S_lambda = A ln(2Delta/lambda) + C_mass + O(lambda^2/Delta^2),
    C_mass = -A/2-(2alpha/pi) C [h(2y)-1].

Thus V+S=V_fin+C_mass+A ln(2Delta). The regulator disappears, but the
hard/soft separator does not disappear until resolved real emission is included.
Dropping C_mass or changing the ln2 convention alters the physical finite answer.

## Complete ordered real phase space

Label the harder outgoing photon h and the softer s. This chooses one of the
two identical-photon sectors once, so there is no additional factor1/2.
Let a_ij=1-n_i.n_j, q=E_s, D=1+E*a_0h-q*a_hs. Exact recoil gives

    E_h=(E-q-E*q*a_0s)/D.

The upper endpoint E_h=q is

    B=2+E(a_0h+a_0s),
    q_max=2E/[B+sqrt(B^2-4*a_hs*E)].

With X from the full Mandl–Skyrme expression, the real differential rate,
divided by the Born differential rate in direction n_h, is

    R(q,n_s) = alpha/(8pi^2) X E_h*q/[E*D*(E_B/E)^2*U].

It is independently checked against the six-diagram spin/polarization tensor
from Stage82 before any integration. There is no Gould or14.16 factor here.
In the soft limit R=a(n_s)/q+O(1), where

    gamma=1+E-E_B, d=1+E*a_0s-E_B*a_hs,
    a(n_s)=alpha/(4pi^2)[2gamma/d-1-1/d^2],
    integral dOmega_s a = A.

The inclusive hardest-photon angular correction therefore is

    delta_inc = V_fin+C_mass
      + integral dOmega_s {
          integral_0^qmax [R(q,n_s)-a(n_s)/q] dq
          +a(n_s) ln(2q_max) }.

This has neither a photon-mass cutoff nor a chosen finite subtraction constant.
It is the limit of real photons above Delta plus the correctly regulated soft
and virtual pieces below Delta. Replacing the subtracted lower bound0 by
Delta leaves an O(Delta/E) interval error; the explicit slicing tests measure
that convergence instead of declaring a finite separator exact.

## Independent total cross section

Integrate the hardest-photon direction with the actual Born measure:

    sigma_NLO/alpha^3 = pi/alpha integral_-1^1
                         (E_B/E)^2 U delta_inc dc.

An independent implementation of Lee–Schwartz–Zhang equations5–8 evaluates the
iterated integrals by integrating their power/log series. It does not call the
Brown–Feynman finite correction or the double-Compton kernel.
The series is restricted to 0<x=2E<1; orders70/90 are checked through x=.2.
Several G functions are also checked against their published closed forms
and the square-root-weight integral against direct quadrature.

At threshold the independent result obeys

    sigma_NLO/alpha^3 = x^2[-16/9 ln(x)+7/15+...].

independent expression to approximately double-precision rounding after

## Finite energy and momentum source

For any weak observable F with F(soft)->0 and smooth soft limit, denote its
real collision increment by D_F(q) and its Born increment by D_F^B. Replace
the previous scalar formula by

    Q_F = (V_fin+C_mass) D_F^B
      + integral dOmega_s {
          integral_0^qmax [R D_F(q)-a D_F^B/q] dq
          +a D_F^B ln(2q_max) }.

For the full photon four-momentum, D_gamma=k_h+k_s-k_in.
The electron increment is evaluated independently with its on-shell energy,
sqrt(1+|p'|^2)-1, and recoil momentum p'=k_in-k_h-k_s.
Consequently D_gamma+D_e=0 eventwise, including the Born increments, and
the matched integrated Q_gamma+Q_e=0. The retained soft energy
integral R k_s is finite and positive. The harder-photon source alone does
not balance the electron source: the created photon must be retained.

These saved Q's are per Born differential rate, in electron rest units.
Multiplying by the incident distributions, electron density and Born rate
and taking the appropriate local-tetrad projection supplies a physical source.
For energy multiply the saved component by m_e*c^2; for momentum by m_e*c.
That distributional average has not yet been substituted into the parent run.
The identities explain how the same matched kernel can enter
div(T_gamma)=Q_gamma and div(T_e)=Q_e without a net spurious gravity source.

Photon counting is different: its real increment is1 while its Born
increment is0, so it is not infrared safe in a vacuum of freely propagating
photons with arbitrarily fine resolution. We keep its logarithmic resolved
count and do not subtract it using the virtual Compton coefficient.

## Thermal continuation and the next calculation

This is the vacuum, unpolarized calibration. Stage82 still owns the general
polarized real amplitude and thermal universal soft functional. The finite
polarized thermal remainder must use both real crossings and the same bath
occupation in the virtual insertion, including external-leg/mass terms.
Varma1992 equations5.1 provide explicit unexpanded thermal trace integrals.
Their subsequently expanded result assumes soft bath momentum relative to
both electron propagator invariants. In the retained early-universe histories,
E_hard is of order T, so T<<m_e does not itself justify that extra expansion.
Do not insert Varma's high-energy result or the vacuum scalar correction as
a universal multiplier of I/E/B/V.

Next derive/evaluate that thermal insertion at the actual E/T, with the
real absorption crossing, then build its energy/momentum moments in the
existing radiation–gas–metric convention. The observed vacuum correction's
small size is not by itself a bound on the still uncomputed thermal remainder.
The source's neglect of internal thermal fermions also needs its own density
estimate for our baryonic electron population; low temperature alone only
suppresses zero-chemical-potential thermal pairs. Preserve Ward checks and do
not mistake the dilute external electron distribution for an absent medium.
