## Background phase lift from the retained field equations

Let N=ln a, omega=a m, and H be the conformal Hubble rate. The retained background
canonical mode satisfies phi_N=v/H and v_N=-2v-omega^2 phi/H. Consequently

    A=a(sqrt(omega) phi+i v/sqrt(omega)) exp(i theta)
    A_N=(3/2) exp(2i theta) A*+i(mu-omega/H)A.

At fixed instantaneous (a,H,A), the heavy conformal density is phase independent:

    R_h=omega |A|^2/(2a^2).

Its derivative is NOT phase independent:

    R_h,N=omega/a^2[-|A|^2/2+(3/2)Re(A^2 exp(-2i theta))].

Therefore, writing H_N/H=b0+bc cos(2theta)+bs sin(2theta),

    b0=[R_rest,N-4v_s^2+omega_s^2 phi_s^2
                       -omega_h |A|^2/(2a^2)]/(6H^2)
    bc=omega_h Re(A^2)/(4a^2 H^2)
    bs=omega_h Im(A^2)/(4a^2 H^2)
    mu_N=(1-H_N/H)mu.

The small difference between this continuum carrier/background derivative and
the actual stored dense/cubic derivative is measured at the native phase.
We do not declare them identical. R_rest,N is still the retained independently
represented background input, not a newly derived closure for the rest species.

This phase lift holds the instantaneous background amplitude fixed when defining
a phase projection, but derives its directional time derivative from the parent
equations. It does not prove that every coefficient is globally slow or that the
background itself has been reduced. Those are distinct forward/averaging issues.

## Finite Laurent coefficients, not fitted curves

The previous exact Hessian has harmonics 0,+/-2,+/-4. Write

    K(theta)=sum K_n exp(i n theta)
    G_n=K_n/(i n mu), n!=0
    Acal=J G, Bcal=J G_N.

Here Acal is a matrix exponent, distinct from the complex background amplitude A.

The time derivative of each K_n has background harmonics q=0,+/-2, because its
directional derivatives depend affinely on A_N, H_N/H, v_s,N and mu_N. Their
coefficients follow by differentiating the actual action terms. No perturbation
history enters their construction. Thus

    G_N(theta)=sum_{n!=0} K_n exp(i n theta)
       +sum_{n!=0,q} [K_n,N;q-K_n(mu_N/mu)_q]
                         exp(i(n+q)theta)/(i n mu).

The code obtains the constant derivative and its two harmonic increments by
linearity of this directional derivative; this is an algebraic coefficient
operation, not regression on sampled temperatures or times. Explicit phase
evaluation independently checks the resulting Laurent polynomial.

## Action-order expansion

For Z=exp(Acal) W the exact transformed Hessian is

    Knew=exp(Acal^T) K exp(Acal)
         +J exp(-Acal) d exp(Acal)/dN.

Define L_A(X)=Acal^T X+X Acal and R_A(X)=X Acal-Acal X.
Then the exact analytic expansions are

    Knew=sum_{j>=0} L_A^j(K)/j!
         +J sum_{j>=0} R_A^j(Bcal)/(j+1)!
    lnew=exp(Acal^T) l.

The order-p candidate truncates BOTH sums at j=p. For the intended p=2,

    Knew[2]=K+L_A K+(L_A^2 K)/2
            +J[Bcal+R_A Bcal/2+R_A^2 Bcal/6]
    lnew[2]=(I+Acal^T+(Acal^T)^2/2)l.

Its mean is the zero Laurent coefficient. All products are finite convolutions,
so the mean can be evaluated directly without sampling a phase curve. The
highest harmonics of orders0,1,2,3 are6,10,14,18. Independent32/64-phase
quadrature checks the finite projections and the untruncated exponential result.

The result includes the scalar–metric coupling and -3a^2 F^2/(4H) inherited from
the joint action, rather than averaging only an isolated heavy oscillator.

## Source coupling and constraints at the same order

For l=B_rest Y, where Y is the actual rest-species perturbation vector,

    Bmean[p]=zero_mode[(sum_{j=0}^p (Acal^T)^j/j!) B_rest].

The current construction holds Y fixed during the formal fast-phase projection.
It supplies coupling coefficients; it does not supply a saved future Y(t) as a
replacement for solving the rest hierarchy.

For constraint rows C(theta)Z+c(Y)=0, use

    Cmean[p]=zero_mode[C(theta) sum_{j=0}^p Acal^j/j!].

This is calculated for both Hamiltonian and momentum constraints, with the
original rest-source constants retained. In particular

    D_h=omega_h Re(A*B)/a^2

is phase independent in rotating canonical variables, while F_h retains its
second harmonic. The native-coordinate constraint construction is independently
checked against this algebraic form.

Averaged constraint rows are not automatically propagated by an independently
averaged evolution operator: products can retain phase correlations. The next
forward interface must check that propagation, not assume it from row agreement.

## Analytic truncation bounds

Use the Frobenius norm. At a fixed retained background time let

    a_bound=sum_n ||Acal_n||_F
    k_bound=sum_n ||K_n||_F
    b_bound=sum_n ||Bcal_n||_F
    c_bound=sum_n ||C_n||_F.

These bound the corresponding norms for EVERY phase. Submultiplicativity and
the exponential series give

    ||Knew-Knew[p]||_F <= exp(2 a_bound)(2 a_bound)^(p+1)
                           [k_bound/(p+1)!+b_bound/(p+2)!]
    ||exp(Acal)-sum_{j=0}^p Acal^j/j!||_F
                       <= exp(a_bound) a_bound^(p+1)/(p+1)!.

For the source-map transpose the same map bound holds. Multiply it by c_bound
for the constraint-row pullback bound. Averaging cannot increase these bounds.
They cover truncating the exponential/commutator expansion, NOT the separate
error of replacing a time-dependent equation by its phase mean.

The inequalities are analytic. Their evaluation here is ordinary floating point,
not a computer-assisted interval proof. They apply at the65 tested background
times, uniformly in phase; no untested continuous-time envelope is claimed.

    Z_N=J K Z+J B_rest Y

    W_N=J Knew W+J S^T B_rest Y

rest-source force back on those fields. C_rest and D_rest must come from the
actual native Boltzmann operator, including collision terms, not from saved
