## What was derived

For xi=ln(theta), theta=k_B T/(m_e c^2), the finite microscopic construction is

    S(xi) = -sum W(xi) F(P(xi),h(xi)).
    D S = -sum W [(D ln W)F + F_P D P + F_h D h].

F is linear in the incident photon/gas departure h. P contains recoil,
electron velocity, boosts, thermal factors and soft integration limits.
The complete second-derivative chain rule and moving-boundary terms are
given in DERIVATION.md and SECOND-DERIVATIVE.md. Closed derivatives of the
normalized electron population, its velocity and the Bose factors pass
18 independent 90-digit checks.

The numerical tangent and curvature are evaluated from the original finite
real/control/virtual/state equations at xi offsets 0,+/-.01,+/-.005.
They are not derivatives of the fitted source temperature curve.
The second-order prediction uses the smaller stencil without fitting any
coefficient to the target. The first-order prediction was frozen too.

Native incident histories remain external inputs. They are linearly
interpolated in expansion time and can have derivative jumps at stored
epochs. Thus the moving-history result is a finite-step variation, not a
proof of a classical second derivative of that interpolant at a knot.
The fixed-incident branch isolates the kernel and population dependence.
See HISTORY-REGULARITY.md and SOURCE-NORMALIZATION.md.

is not fully step-stable under the original criterion. No gate was weakened.

prediction is at most **0.006399% of the actual baseline source** and
**0.038437% of the actual MTS source**. These are finite step differences,
not certified continuum-error bounds. The 1% derivative criterion still fails
where recorded, even though the source-level impact is much smaller.

    error_moving = error_fixed_kernel + error_incident_history.

0.052443% for baseline; 0.206125% and 0.131021% for MTS. Their cross term
is retained; these separate maxima cannot simply be added to explain one case.

history-dependent discrepancy.
