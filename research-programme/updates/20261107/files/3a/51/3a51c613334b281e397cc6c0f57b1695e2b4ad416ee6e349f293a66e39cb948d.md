# Actual radial pulsations of the compact gravitating bodies

3 October 2026. A local daughter of the completed radial-energy calculation.
The stationary profiles and all couplings are retained without refitting.

## 1. Why another operator is needed

The last stage's positive energy Hessian supplies a stability test. Its
eigenvalues are not oscillation frequency squared, because Hamilton's
equations also contain the symplectic structure and a phase/charge pair.
Here both are retained. We construct a squared-frequency operator, remove
only the conserved-charge/constant-phase pair, and reconstruct full physical
linear modes afterwards, including the interior metric response.

Conventions, dimensionless time and background:

    ds²=-N²dt²+dr²/F+r²dOmega², F=1-2alpha m/r>0,
    Xi=N/sqrt(F), A=Xi F, N(infinity)=1,
    C=h(r) exp(i w t)/sqrt(2),
    W(h)=3h^(4/3)/8+h^4/8.

The ADM Hamiltonian H=m(infinity), charge Qbar=q/(4pi), and eliminated
spherical constraints are derived in
`../radial-gravity-stability-20261003/11-coupled-radial-gravity-stability.md`.
The retained independent Einstein metric is not replaced by an acoustic one.

## 2. Use canonical polar variables to represent the exact phase symmetry

Set C=f exp[i(w t+eta)]/sqrt(2), with canonical radial momenta p_f and pi.
The rotating-frame Hamiltonian is H-w integral pi dr. Its kinetic source is

    K=f'²+f² eta'²+p_f²/r^4+pi²/(r^4 f²),
    Qbar=integral pi dr,
    m'+alpha r K m=r²(K/2+W).

At the stationary background

    f=h, eta=0, p_f=0, pi0=r² w h²/A.

Let u=delta f, p=delta p_f, sigma=delta pi. Relate these to the previous
Cartesian perturbations (u,v,pi_u,pi_v), using P=pi0/h:

    v=h eta,     pi_u=p-P eta,     pi_v=(sigma-Pu)/h.       (1)

This is a canonical linear transformation about the background. It gives
delta Qbar=integral sigma dr directly, with no locally imposed density
constraint. The transformation is used in the interior h>0; the compact
vacuum edge is handled by weighted form spaces, not by claiming polar
coordinates are nonsingular at h=0.

## 3. The even and odd quadratic Hamiltonians

The first kinetic variation, which sources the metric, is

    delta K=2h'u'+2w sigma/(A r²)-2w²h u/A².             (2)

Keep the constraint responses

    delta m=Xi^(-1) integral_0^r Xi t²(F delta K/2+W'u)dt,
    delta log Xi=-integral_r^R alpha t delta K dt,
    delta log A=delta log Xi-2alpha delta m/(rF).          (3)

The canonical energy second variation is B=B_even+B_odd, with

    B_even = integral {A r²u'²+(Xi r²W''+3r²w²/A)u²
                   -4w u sigma/h+A sigma²/(r²h²)
                   -2alpha Xi r delta K delta m}dr,      (4)
    B_odd = integral {A r²h² eta'²+A p²/r²}dr.            (5)

The perturbative Hamiltonian is B/2, since B is the second derivative.
There is no missing -w delta²Q term: pi is a canonical charge density and
Q is exactly linear in it. Transforming (4) back by (1) reproduces the
previous Cartesian Routhian Hessian, including its charge cross term.

Equation (5) represents eta=constant,p=0 as an exact symmetry zero. We do
not force a slightly negative computed eigenvalue to zero, drop the weakest
physical mode, or add an artificial stabilizing term.

## 4. Finite-element spaces and canonical normalization

Amplitude and amplitude momentum use interior, endpoint-zero linear elements:

    r u=sum_i B_i a_i,           p/r=sum_i B_i c_i.

Phase and charge use linear elements including BOTH endpoint basis functions:

    eta=sum_j T_j eta_j,         sigma=r²h² sum_j T_j xi_j.

Their sum is exactly one, so the constant phase is represented exactly.
The background weight is fixed in this linear coordinate transformation.
The two symplectic mass matrices are

    Ma_ij=integral B_i B_j dr,
    Mh_ij=integral r²h² T_i T_j dr.

Use Cholesky factors Ma=La La^T, Mh=Lh Lh^T. Define whitened even and odd
coordinates

    E=(La^T a, Lh^T xi),         Y=(La^T c, Lh^T eta).

Let He be the matrix of (4) in E coordinates. In Y coordinates (5) is
block diagonal:

    D=diag(Dp,Deta),
    Dp=La^(-1)[integral A B_i B_j]La^(-T),
    Deta=Lh^(-1)[integral A r²h² T_i' T_j']Lh^(-T).

For S=diag(+I,-I) and Z=S Y, Hamilton's equations are

    E_t=D Z,             Z_t=-He E.                       (6)

The only exact null vector of D expected on the connected positive interior
is c_Q=(0,Lh^T 1). Its dual relation is delta Qbar=c_Q^T E. Hence D c_Q=0.
This realizes charge conservation and phase invariance as one canonical pair.

Let U be an orthonormal basis of c_Q-perpendicular, constructed analytically
from this known vector. Then

    Hq=U^T He U,         Dq=U^T D U,
    e_t=Dq z,           z_t=-Hq e,
    e_tt=-Dq Hq e.                                         (7)

When Dq is positive, write Dq=Ld Ld^T, e=Ld y. The symmetric pulsation
eigenproblem is

    [Ld^T Hq Ld] y = Omega² y.                            (8)

Thus Omega, not a bare energy-Hessian eigenvalue, is the radial oscillation
frequency in asymptotically normalized time. Positivity of both Dq and Hq
implies all frequencies of this finite-dimensional system are real. We
compute the lowest five and check the least eigenvalue of Hq as well.

The radial density and metric oscillate at Omega. The complex scalar field
has the corresponding sidebands w+Omega and w-Omega. In physical units
omega_pulsation=mu_* Omega; without identifying mu_* with an empirical time
scale these are not predictions in hertz or seconds.

## 5. Reconstruct, rather than suppress, the phase motion

The quotient identifies configurations differing by a constant global phase.
It does NOT authorize setting the full spatially uniform phase RATE to zero.
That rate can be driven by the amplitude oscillation. Reconstruct it from
the unprojected equation

    eta_t=A sigma/(r²h²)-2w u/h+w delta log A.             (9)

For a mode with u,sigma,delta m proportional to cos(Omega t), integrate (9)
to obtain the complete phase proportional to sin(Omega t), including its
constant spatial component. The amplitude momentum follows its unprojected
Hamilton equation. The saved reconstructed modes include both.

As an independent check, assemble the linearized amplitude equation in weak
form from (3), the derivative flux delta(A r²h'), and the force variation.
Compare that and (9) with the matrix gradient. This checks the nonlocal
metric source and canonical signs without simply diagonalizing the same
matrix twice. At fixed charge delta m(R)=w delta Qbar=0, although interior
delta m and lapse perturbations remain nonzero.

## 6. Flat-space and uniform-bulk controls

At alpha=0 the independent Cartesian equations are

    u_tt+[-Laplacian+W''-w²]u-2w v_t=0,
    v_tt+[-Laplacian+W'/h-w²]v+2w u_t=0.                 (10)

Discretize (10) separately as a second-order gyroscopic system and solve its
first-order companion eigenproblem. It retains the charge/phase zero pair;
compare its nonzero imaginary frequencies with (8). At finite mesh the mixed
canonical momentum space and the velocity-based formulation have a projection
difference. Do not claim exact equality between unequal discrete spaces;
show that the difference decreases under refinement.

For a uniform interior and w fixed, let m_a²=W''-w². Equation (10) yields

    Omega^4-(2k²+m_a²+4w²)Omega²+k²(k²+m_a²)=0.

At coexistence h=w=1, m_a²=2/3. The sound branch has c_s²=1/7 and the
gapped branch has Omega_gap²=14/3 at k=0. These are derived bulk limits,
not replacements for the finite-body boundary-value problem. In particular
no numerical finite-body mode was assigned k=pi/R to manufacture agreement.

## 7. Numerical validation and the edge

The same compact profiles and curved cubic endpoint from the preceding
stage are inputs. Refinements vary radial cell count and quadrature order
independently. The phase null vector is controlled separately from physical
frequencies. Direct first-order Hamiltonian eigenvalues provide a further
small-grid sign and normalization control.

The weighted phase/charge spaces are deliberately explicit. A physical
charge perturbation can require xi=sigma/(r²h²) to grow near the edge, while
its weighted norm is finite. Each finite-element xi is bounded, but successive
spaces can approximate such weighted functions; this requires convergence
testing. No ordinary smooth massive-scalar vacuum theorem is transplanted.
The singular amplitude potential and free-edge tangent domain are inherited
from the previous derivation. There is no artificial exterior atmosphere.

For three gravitating cases evolve the reduced LINEAR system (7) for three
fundamental periods with implicit midpoint. Check the quadratic energy and
time-step convergence of the phase. Midpoint has its own known dispersion,
2 arctan(Omega dt/2)/dt, so separate time-discretization error from physical
frequency. This is a linear dynamical check, not nonlinear Einstein-scalar
evolution or a theorem for arbitrary exterior disturbances.

Positive refined spectra remain numerical evidence, not certified continuum
lower bounds. The present domain is weak-gravity, horizonless and spherical.
Strong coupling and the first instability are a continuation task, not an
extrapolated conclusion from nine weakly gravitating configurations.
