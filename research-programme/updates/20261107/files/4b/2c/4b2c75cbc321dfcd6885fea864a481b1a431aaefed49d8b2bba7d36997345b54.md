## 1. Action ancestry and approximation order

The original real motion field and its existing real bath are canonically
rotated in
`../motion-bath-matching-20261006/49-real-motion-envelope-and-existing-bath-matching.md`.
The rotation is constant, so it preserves the Hilbert tensor on arbitrary
metrics, not just the homogeneous density. The numerical Gaussian
specialization and the nonzero interaction vertices that would accompany
an extension are retained in
`../heavy-interaction-and-condensate-bridge-20261007/52-heavy-mode-interactions-and-condensate-coherence.md`.
No new complex fundamental field or adjustable pressure is introduced.

Use signature(-+++), c=hbar=1, physical canonical fields phi_I and

    S_fields = integral sqrt(-g) sum_I[-(grad phi_I)^2/2 - m_I^2 phi_I^2/2],
    I = slow, heavy.

The gravitational action and ordinary matter keep their inherited common
metric and normalization. This stage uses the same leading GR-connected
gravitational branch as the native calculation; it does not newly determine
G or eliminate the parent's higher-derivative matching remainders.
Nonzero Stage52 interactions are not silently turned into independent
fitted contacts: their numerical values remain zero in this inherited
Gaussian benchmark.

There are three distinct levels below: exact canonical stress identities;
leading weak-field/nonrelativistic heavy reduction; and a small-amplitude
second-order response within that reduction. Accuracy at one level does
not prove the approximations at the others.

## 2. Exact metric source, including the quadratic stress

Let n^mu be the unit future normal to an ADM slice, gamma_ij its metric,
N its lapse, beta^i its shift and

    Pi_I = (dot(phi_I) - beta^i partial_i(phi_I))/N.

Varying the SAME action gives

    T_munu = sum_I[partial_mu phi_I partial_nu phi_I
                  - g_munu((grad phi_I)^2/2 + m_I^2 phi_I^2/2)].
    E = sum_I[Pi_I^2/2 + D_i phi_I D^i phi_I/2 + m_I^2 phi_I^2/2],
    j_i = -sum_I Pi_I D_i phi_I,
    S_ij = sum_I D_i phi_I D_j phi_I
         + gamma_ij sum_I[Pi_I^2/2 - (D phi_I)^2/2 - m_I^2 phi_I^2/2],
    S_ij^TF = sum_I[D_i phi_I D_j phi_I - gamma_ij (D phi_I)^2/3].

These expressions include the slow field. Their covariant divergence is
sum_I[(Box phi_I - m_I^2 phi_I) partial_nu phi_I]; it vanishes on the
canonical equations. Exact inhomogeneous FLRW energy AND momentum Ward
identities are differentiated independently in `derive_sources.py`.
Lapse and shift variations separately recover E and j_i.

Expand phi=phi0+epsilon phi1+epsilon^2 phi2, without a factorial convention.
With N=1+epsilon N1+epsilon^2 N2 and beta=epsilon beta1+...,

    Pi0 = dot(phi0),
    Pi1 = dot(phi1) - N1 Pi0,
    Pi2 = dot(phi2) - N1 dot(phi1) + (N1^2-N2)Pi0 - beta1.grad(phi1).

For each field, the second-order energy and pressure scalars are

    E2 = Pi0 Pi2 + Pi1^2/2 + m^2 phi0 phi2 + m^2 phi1^2/2
         + (grad phi1)^2/(2a^2),
    P2 = Pi0 Pi2 + Pi1^2/2 - m^2 phi0 phi2 - m^2 phi1^2/2
         - (grad phi1)^2/(6a^2).

The covariant spatial momentum is
j_i,2=-Pi0 partial_i phi2-Pi1 partial_i phi1. In a background orthonormal
spatial frame,

    S_hat_i_hat_j,2^TF =
      sum_I[partial_i phi_I,1 partial_j phi_I,1
            - delta_ij (grad phi_I,1)^2/3]/a^2.

Thus zero LINEAR scalar anisotropic stress does not mean zero quadratic
stress. E2 also contains the second-order field and lapse: retaining only
the square of a linear perturbation is not a complete Einstein source.
The exact ADM expressions specify the additional terms instead of hiding
them inside a fitted coefficient. Metric quadratic terms in the Einstein
tensor and other species still enter the complete second-order solution.

## 3. Nonlinear heavy reduction from the real field

For the already existing mass M, insert

    h = [psi exp(-i M t) + psi* exp(i M t)]/sqrt(2 M a^3)

into its canonical action. Average over the carrier while holding the slow
envelope fixed. Integrating the a^(3/2) rescaling boundary term leaves,
at leading slow-envelope/weak-metric order,

    L_NR = i(psi* dot(psi)-dot(psi*) psi)/2
           - |grad psi|^2/(2 M a^2) - M Psi_N |psi|^2
           + U_H |psi|^2/(2M),
    U_H = 3 dot(H)/2 + 9 H^2/4.

Terms quadratic in dot(psi)/M, fast harmonics and higher metric/gradient
terms have not been retained. This is a derivation under H/M <<1,
k/(aM)<<1, weak potentials and slow envelope, not an exact new particle
symmetry. The spatially uniform U_H shifts phase but supplies no force.
This is the Stage49 map applied nonlinearly, not an extrapolation of
Stage55's linear pressure closure.

Set rho_h=M|psi|^2/a^3 and v=grad(arg psi)/(M a). Variation gives

    dot(rho_h)+3H rho_h+div(rho_h v)/a=0,
    dot(v)+H v+(v.grad)v/a =
      -grad(Psi_N)/a
      + grad[laplacian(sqrt(rho_h))/sqrt(rho_h)]/(2 M^2 a^3).

The coefficient is fixed by M. No damping function or independent nonlinear
sound speed has been chosen. Nodes of psi require the wave variables rather
than dividing by rho_h; the numerical probes explicitly stay positive.
The slow mode has no assumed heavy carrier and retains the exact ADM/KG
description. Its background is retained in the numerical expansion rate.

This reduction is established methodology, not claimed as original:
[real-field EFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.00445);
[nonlinear expanding-universe EFT](https://arxiv.org/html/2104.10128v1).
The latter derives relativistic corrections too; those corrections are not
automatically included in our leading projection.

## 4. Do not confuse momentum-flux improvement with Hilbert stress

The quantum force can be written as the divergence of

    Pi_Q,ij = [(partial_i rho)(partial_j rho)/rho
               - partial_i partial_j rho]/(4 M^2 a^2),
    partial_j Pi_Q,ij =
       -rho partial_i[laplacian(sqrt(rho))/sqrt(rho)]/(2 M^2 a^2).

This is a valid effective conservative momentum flux. But directly
phase-averaging the canonical spatial-gradient tensor gives, at this order,

    <S_hat_i_hat_j>^TF =
      [rho v_i v_j
       + (partial_i rho)(partial_j rho)/(4 M^2 a^2 rho)]^TF.

The exact average of the carrier gradients verifies the second expression.
Its density-gradient anisotropy starts at second order around a homogeneous
state. Pi_Q has a linear tracefree Hessian term; inserting that expression
as a new canonical Hilbert source would contradict the retained action.

The tensors partial_i partial_j rho and delta_ij laplacian(rho) have the
same divergence. Trading them is a momentum-flux improvement, NOT permission
to change the metric variation of the relativistic action. Subleading
isotropic stress and matching terms matter when reconstructing the full
averaged tensor. We use the exact ADM stress for that ownership.
[Nonlinear scalar stress reference](https://arxiv.org/html/1707.08568v1)
also distinguishes scalar anisotropy and effective pressure; its full
relativistic construction has its own averaging/gauge assumptions and
does not supply our unsolved matching conditions.

For cold matter plus baryons, combining the velocities too early likewise
loses the stress rho_cb f_c f_b (v_c-v_b)_i(v_c-v_b)_j. The numerical
calculation therefore evolves c and b separately.

## 5. Derived harmonic feedback equations

Take s=ln(a), y=k x and U=k v/(aH), Hcal=aH. Define

    C = 2 + d ln(H)/ds = 1 + d ln(Hcal)/ds,
    g_j = 4 pi G a^2 rho_j/Hcal^2,
    lambda_h = k^4/(4 M^2 a^2 Hcal^2), lambda_c=lambda_b=0,
    W = k^2 Psi_N/Hcal^2.

Within the stated leading Newtonian projection,

    d_s delta_j + partial_y[(1+delta_j)U_j]=0,
    d_s U_j + C U_j + U_j partial_y U_j =
      -partial_y W
      + 2 lambda_j partial_y[(partial_y^2 sqrt(1+delta_j))/sqrt(1+delta_j)],
    partial_y^2 W = sum_j g_j delta_j.

The force is solved from the evolving density, not prescribed. All species
see the SAME potential and inherited G. In the numerical projection, slow
field/radiation/neutrinos remain in the background but their perturbations,
baryon gas pressure and post-Newtonian terms are omitted. Linear native
comparisons quantify the projected response, not all nonlinear errors.

Use delta_j=A d_j cos(y)+A^2 e_j cos(2y) and
U_j=-A t_j sin(y)-A^2 w_j sin(2y)/2. The derived equations are

    d_j' = t_j,
    t_j' = -C t_j + sum_i g_i d_i - lambda_j d_j,
    e_j' = w_j + d_j t_j,
    w_j' = -C w_j + sum_i g_i e_i -16 lambda_j e_j
           + t_j^2 + 3 lambda_j d_j^2.

Both factors16 and3 are fixed, not fitted: the second harmonic has twice
the wave number, and

    (sqrt(1+delta))_yy/sqrt(1+delta)
       = delta_yy/2 - delta delta_yy/2 - delta_y^2/4 + O(delta^3).

For one cosine this gives
-A d cos(y)/2 + A^2[d^2/8+(3d^2/8-2e)cos(2y)].
Removing the 3 lambda d^2 term while retaining the linear Jeans term is
not the second-order canonical dynamics.

At lambda=0, equal cold initial fields stay equal. In Einstein-de Sitter,
C=1/2, sum g=3/2 and d=t=exp(s):
e=w=exp(2s) is the growing planar solution. With e(0)=w(0)=0,
e=w=exp(2s)-exp(s). Both are checked algebraically and numerically.
Switching off quadratic velocity feedback instead gives
e=d_initial I+I^2/2, I=d-d_initial: precisely the Stage60 transport
coefficient within the common Newtonian projection.

## 6. General mixed-mode source: not confined to a planar trick

For an irrotational nonrelativistic flow let t=-div[v/(aH)] in comoving
coordinates and k=p+q. In Fourier convolution notation,

    delta_j' = t_j + integral alpha(p,q) t_j(p) delta_j(q),
    t_j' = -C t_j + sum_i g_i delta_i - lambda_j(k) delta_j
            + integral beta(p,q) t_j(p)t_j(q)
            + integral Q_j(p,q) delta_j(p)delta_j(q),
    alpha(p,q) = (p+q).p/p^2,
    beta(p,q) = |p+q|^2 (p.q)/(2 p^2 q^2),
    Q_h(p,q) = |p+q|^2 [p^2+q^2+p.q]/(8 M^2 a^2 Hcal^2),
    Q_c = Q_b = 0.

The products on the right are first-order fields when solving second order.
Symmetrizing alpha is valid for the equal growing cold fields in the check,
not a replacement for the unsymmetrized t(p)delta(q) in scale-dependent
multi-component evolution. The quantum kernel follows by Fourier transforming
the displayed square-root expansion; it is not chosen to match a bispectrum.

The quantum kernel is symmetric, vanishes at k=0 for a folded pair, and gives
the plane coefficient 3 lambda after accounting for the real cosine's two
Fourier amplitudes. The cold growing solution recovers

    F2 = (5 alpha_sym + 2 beta)/7
       = 5/7 + mu(p/q+q/p)/2 + 2 mu^2/7,
    G2 = (3 alpha_sym + 4 beta)/7.

Here mu=(p.q)/(p q), not the slow scalar mass. The folded F2/G2 and pressure
sources vanish. These checks establish the pressure kernel's cold and
conservation limits. They do not replace a time-dependent coupled-kernel
integration with native initial matching for the actual MTS branch.
`derive_mode_kernels.py` performs the exact checks.

The full periodic nonlinear equations are also solved at decreasing

The parameter probes do not justify running through shell crossing,
density nodes, or a nonlinear halo without the corresponding wave/fluid
and metric treatment.
