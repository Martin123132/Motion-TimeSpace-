# Stage76 — transport about the actual weakly distorted bath

## What changes, and what does not

Stage75 evolves the retained gravity and two-field parent with conservative
thermal background energy, finite recoil, polarization, and gas heat/momentum.
Its collision derivative is evaluated at a Planck reference. This stage derives
the derivative at the saved distorted radiation and electron temperature instead.
The parent action, coupling calibration, initial wave, and gravity equations are
not replaced. The two saved parents receive the same treatment.

This is a finite-wave transport calculation over a=10^-6 to 10^-5, k=0.1/Mpc,
not a new likelihood fit or an observational detection. The microscopic input is
the same physical Compton channel used by Stages73–75. Electron mass, Thomson
normalization, Newton's constant and the leading shared nonrelativistic gas
equation of state remain inherited inputs. Photon production is not included.

## Matrix pair before any scalar or angular reduction

Let F_i,F_j be Hermitian photon occupation matrices, T(X)=sum K X K^dagger
the physical spin-summed channel, and T^* its trace adjoint. The induced pair is

    C_i = f_j {1+F_i,T^*(F_j)}/2 - f_i {F_i,T^*(1+F_j)}/2,
    C_j = f_i {1+F_j,T(F_i)}/2 - f_j {F_j,T(1+F_i)}/2.

All invariant event factors are positive and common to the pair. This is the
nondegenerate-electron form of the gain, loss and induced structure in Eq.(99)
of Nagirner & Poutanen (2001), not a scalar collision formula copied onto Q/U/V.
In particular the anticommutator loss is retained before the isotropic average.
Trace cyclicity proves Tr(C_i+C_j)=0. At a zero eigenvalue of either occupation,
the corresponding loss vanishes and the remaining gain is nonnegative.

For scalar background F_i=N_i 1, put n_i=(exp(x_i)-1)^-1, w_i=n_i(1+n_i),
delta F_i=w_i X_i, f_i=a/[n_i(1+n_j)], f_j=b/[n_j(1+n_i)]. Differentiation gives

    delta C_i = L_j T^*(X_j) - L_i {X_i,T^*1}/2,
    delta C_j = L_i T(X_i)   - L_j {X_j,T1}/2,
    L_i = a(1+n_i)(1+N_j)/(1+n_j) - b n_i N_j/n_j,
    L_j = b(1+n_j)(1+N_i)/(1+n_i) - a n_j N_i/n_i.

The derivative therefore supplies the distinct incoming and outgoing weights
for every Stokes sector. At N=n, a=b=1, both equal one. Away from that point
there is no general right chemical null or symmetric negative Jacobian. The
left number null survives. Enforcing equilibrium symmetry here would erase
part of the derivative we are trying to compute.

## Electron response and a cancellation-safe residual

Write N_i=n_i+w_i chi B_i and r=T_e/T_* -1. The saved background has
chi=5.755472902372619e-10. The Juttner ratios obey

    log a = (gamma_i-1) r/[theta(1+r)] + log normalization,
    log(b/a) = (x_i-x_j) r/(1+r),
    d log a/dr = (gamma_i-mean_gamma_e)/[theta(1+r)^2].

The normalization logarithm is computed by integrating its exact derivative,
not subtracting nearly equal large Bessel logarithms. Scaled Bessel functions
and expm1 remainders then preserve the order-chi and order-chi^2 effects.
The scalar forward-minus-reverse flux is

    D = a (N_i/n_i)(1+N_j)/(1+n_j)
        - b (N_j/n_j)(1+N_i)/(1+n_i),
    D_linear = chi(B_i-B_j) - (x_i-x_j)r.

`stable_pair.py` evaluates L_i-1,L_j-1, D_r+(x_i-x_j), and D-D_linear
directly. The third quantity is the temperature-column correction, not an
occupation coefficient. The fourth is the omitted nonlinear homogeneous
collision residual. A 65-digit independent evaluation tests all four.

## Weak angular/frequency assembly

Use exactly the reference event geometry, physical recoil/Fano channel, Bose
weights, angular tensors and conservative spectral basis of Stage75. Let P be
its positive event weight, t its self trace, U and V the basis vectors at the
incoming and outgoing frequencies. After isotropic angular averaging, each
diagonal self block changes by

    -1/2 integral P t [(L_i-1) U U^T + (L_j-1) V V^T].

For each angular gain correlation R_AB, the changes are

    delta K_AB += 1/2 integral P R_AB (L_j-1) U V^T,
    delta K_BA += 1/2 integral P R_AB (L_i-1) V U^T.

The monopole intensity uses the equivalent difference form directly:

    delta K_0 = -1/2 integral P t (U-V)[(L_i-1)U-(L_j-1)V]^T,
    delta g_T = -1/2 integral P t (U-V)[D_r+(x_i-x_j)],
    g_res     = -1/2 integral P t (U-V)[D-D_linear].

This preserves the left constant null without an after-the-fact projection.
Store the first two divided by chi and the last by chi^2. The l=1 velocity
column changes by -delta K_1 times the already-derived distorted-bath boost
profile. Gas energy and momentum rows are the negatives of these exact photon
moments, including the temperature and velocity columns. No source is added
only to one side of the exchange.

## Nonlinear entropy, rather than the wrong equilibrium test

Diagonalize F_i and F_j separately. For each channel and pair of eigenvectors,
define p=f_i lambda_i(1+lambda_j)|K_ji|^2 and
q=f_j lambda_j(1+lambda_i)|K_ji|^2. Photon entropy uses
S'(F)=log[(1+F)/F]. Electron detailed balance yields

    Tr[S'(F_i)C_i + S'(F_j)C_j] + (E_i-E_j)Tr(C_j)/T_e
      = sum (p-q)log(p/q) >= 0.

This is a collision-pair statement with a thermal electron reservoir; in the
finite-capacity model the reservoir's heat is deposited in the gas equation.
It is not a claim that arbitrary driven linearized perturbations decay in an
equilibrium norm. Physical spin-channel numerical tests check the transition
sum and trace expression independently.

## Propagation without subtracting nearly equal solutions

Let L_75 be the retained mass-weighted expanding-wave operator and
delta L=chi L_1 the assembled change. Evolve the baseline y and z=(y_new-y)/chi:

    M y_s = L_75 y,
    M z_s = (L_75+chi L_1)z + L_1 y.

Both implicit solves use the same time grid; no metric constraints are reset
after initialization. Separately solve the forced leading background response
with physical correction chi^2 p and electron-temperature correction chi^2 t:

    M_gamma p_s = (kappa/H)(K_mixed p + g_T,mixed t + g_res/chi^2),
    chi t_s = -E p_s - chi t.

This quantifies the residual of using the saved background, including the
finite gas heat capacity and expansion work. It is a first forced correction
on that trajectory, not a fully self-consistent new nonlinear background and
Friedmann solve. The distinction is retained in results and the next target.

## Why temperature, spectral shape and gravity need separate answers

The gas-temperature component has mass chi, whereas the photon spectral
components have order-one mass in the chosen photon-reference units. Let A be
the full mass-weighted mixed wave operator, f=L_1 y, and split the scaled
correction into z_T and its other components z_rest. The exact gas row is

    chi z_T' = A_TT z_T + A_T,rest z_rest + f_T,
    lambda_T = -A_TT/chi,
    q_T = -(A_T,rest z_rest + f_T)/A_TT,
    z_T' = -lambda_T (z_T-q_T).

When lambda_T>0 this is a restoring thermal equation. Setting z_T=q_T is its
fast-relaxation approximation, not a newly fitted closure. For differentiable
q_T the error e=z_T-q_T satisfies

    e' + lambda_T e = -q_T',
    |e(s)| <= |e(0)| exp[-integral_0^s lambda_T]
              + integral_0^s exp[-integral_u^s lambda_T] |q_T'(u)| du.

This identity explains why a correction can matter to the temperature response
without carrying comparable gravitational stress. The physical temperature
correction is chi z_T, but its gas internal-energy contribution, normalized to
the photon reference density, is chi^2 z_T. Independently, a photon spectrum
has number/energy moments and a complementary shape component. Projecting onto
the constant/temperature span separates those contributions. Small bolometric
energy alone cannot establish a small spectral correction.

The numerical diagnostic tests q_T along the full coupled solution and checks
the restoring sign. It is not an independent evolution of a reduced thermal
model or a uniform bound on unsampled q_T'. All amplitudes retain the saved
wave normalization; they are not observed CMB anisotropy amplitudes.

## The failed raw-residual insertion and its derived replacement

A diagnostic inserts g_res alone into the background density and boost
sources. Its claim of negligibility at the new tiny bolometric-response scale
fails. This must not be described as a converged new background: a forcing
residual is not the relaxed background collision term.

The forced response p,t already obeys the photon and gas equations above.
Introduce the expansion-work accumulator J'=t, J(0)=0. Adding those equations
proves the exact charge

    E p + chi(t+J)=0,
    E p' = -chi(t+t').

Consequently the actual correction to the homogeneous collision is

    delta C_bar = chi^2 H p'
                = chi^2 kappa M_gamma^-1(K_mixed p + g_T,mixed t + g_res/chi^2),

not chi^2 kappa M_gamma^-1(g_res/chi^2) on its own. Its energy moment is
order chi^3, whereas the raw forcing can have an order-chi^2 energy moment.
The induced temperature relaxation cancels that leading raw energy source.
The numerical charge test includes the expansion work; it is not simply an
assertion that the photon energy alone is constant.

For the source sensitivity test, replace the old background correction by
this relaxed p' in the density and boost terms, and by p in the redshift and
bulk-velocity terms. Include its temperature-pressure sources. Evolve that
response separately in chi^2-scaled variables rather than subtracting two
almost identical complete waves. This tests the actual source replacement.

This probe does not supply every order-chi^2 collision-Jacobian change or a
new order-chi^3 Friedmann/enthalpy solution. It therefore cannot certify all
subleading stress digits as a complete nonlinear prediction. The leading
mixed spectral and temperature response and the failed raw-source attempt
are kept distinct from that later task.
