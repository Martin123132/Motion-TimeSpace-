## 3. Reconstruct the control algebraically from saved waves

Let the outgoing surface derivative be alpha+i beta and let the real exterior
bases be Y=Re(U_out), D, with Y(R)=1, D(R)=0 and D_x(R)=1. Write the source
overlaps as O=a+i beta d, where a=T[Y], d=T[D]. The solution satisfying the
real interior slope s is Y+(s-alpha)D. With outgoing normalization N,

    w=alpha-s+i beta,
    A(s)=[a+(s-alpha)d]/(w N).

For sigma=s0-s, the exact finite replacement is

    A(s0)-A(s)=sigma O/[w(w-sigma)N].

Thus the same archived exterior bases give both amplitudes: no new wave solve
and no fitted frequency multiplier are needed. The full-minus-BH amplitude is
updated by this difference, rather than subtracting large powers afterward.
The same replacement is applied to both source legs in the retarded action.

The archive stores the normalized real bases and physical amplitudes, but not
every outgoing normalization separately. Recover beta from unit-current
normalization using c=omega |A(s)|^2/[a+(s-alpha)d]^2:

    c beta^2-beta+c(alpha-s)^2=0,
    beta=2c(alpha-s)^2/[1+sqrt(1-4c^2(alpha-s)^2)].

This is the small-current branch continuous with the low-frequency exterior.
The carrier archives independently store the complex overlap and verify beta
to1.23e-15 relative. Across the frozen controls beta/|alpha-s| remains below
1.18e-9. N follows from A(s); direct frozen amplitudes and optical identities
are independently checked, including the sidebands.

The m=0,n=0 static conservative term is not put through a radiative1/omega
formula. It is inherited unchanged from stage38. Carrier frequency derivatives
are recomputed from the archived stencils AFTER freezing the surface slope;
we do not freeze the final carrier coefficient while changing its sidebands.

## 4. Match the eccentricity and frequency definitions first

Stage37 used geometric small-eccentricity u=e_geo^2 at fixed semilatus p.
Stage40 used action eccentricity zeta=2Jr/Jphi at fixed azimuthal frequency.
At leading test-mass order the Schwarzschild action Jacobian gives

    zeta=g(p)u,   g(p)=sqrt[p(p-6)]/(p-2),
    alpha_phi=-3(p^2-10p+22)/[2(p-6)(p-2)],
    x=D/M=(M Omega_phi)^(-2/3)=p+f(p)u,
    f(p)=-2p alpha_phi/3.

Start with the archived rates

    pdot=mu[P0(p)+u P2(p)],  udot=mu Gamma0(p)u.

Changing the coordinate AND the held-fixed initial eccentricity gives

    xdot=mu[P0(x)+zeta X2(x)],
    X2=[P2+f'P0+f Gamma0-f P0']/g,
    zetadot=mu G_action(x)zeta,
    G_action=Gamma0+(g'/g)P0.

The -f P0' term moves the evaluation point; omitting it amounts to comparing
different frequency endpoints. The g'/g term accounts for transporting action
eccentricity rather than the old geometric e^2. Both are derived, not calibrated.
The Newtonian limit gives X2/P_N=157/24+(837/4)lambda/(M x)^5, exactly the
q->0 coefficient in stage40. The integrated weak-limit comparison, including
both mixed clock coefficients, agrees to1.30e-12 relative.

## 5. Propagate each contribution into timing and phase

All components are projected through the same energy/angular-action balance.
At the frequency coordinate x, let P,G,X denote the outgoing reference rates
P0,G_action,X2. Let delta P,delta G,delta X be one signed response component.
For unit initial action eccentricity, Z obeys d ln Z/dx=G/P. To first order
in the inserted rate difference,

    d(delta ln Z)/dx=(delta G P-G delta P)/P^2,
    d(delta T0)/dx=-delta P/(M^2 P^2),
    d(delta Tz)/dx=-Z/(M^2 P^2)
                  [delta X+X delta ln Z-2X delta P/P].

Here T=mu t/M^2. The corresponding phase kernels are multiplied by x^(-3/2),
with Phi=(mu/M)phi. Both binaries begin at the same Omega_phi and zeta_i;
both accumulated clocks and phases start at zero. Both stop at the same final
Omega_phi. We retain first order in zeta_i and in the inserted response rates.

The archived wave powers themselves contain |delta A|^2. Its separate column
is a DIAGNOSTIC subset of the outgoing response, not an additional term to add
to the budget. Scaling the rate difference by1,1/2,1/4 and retaining the rational
denominator verifies that the clock remainder is quadratic. Its largest size
is5.50e-5 of the first-order response coefficient over this band. This is an
order check of the retained system, not a bound on missing physical multipoles,
finite-mass-ratio corrections or unknown parent operators.
