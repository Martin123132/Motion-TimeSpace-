## 4. Positive wave energy and the interior spectral gap

For ell>=2, N,F>0, alpha>=0 and W>=0, the second expression for V_ell is
strictly positive. A real master solution has

    E_U=1/2 int [(partial_t U)^2+(partial_rstar U)^2+V_ell U^2] drstar,
    flux_U=-(partial_t U)*(partial_rstar U).

Direct differentiation gives local energy conservation. Regularity kills
the center flux. With no incident energy, outward radiation removes energy
rather than driving a growing solution. Equivalently the positive radial
operator has no negative eigenvalue. An outgoing mode with Im(omega)>0
decays spatially at infinity and would be square integrable, contradicting
that positivity. This excludes exponentially growing **linear axial modes**
under the stated assumptions. It does not exclude damped quasinormal modes
or establish nonlinear/full-sector stability.

For a quantitative interior bound, derive radial stress conservation from
the actual scalar equation:

    p_r'=-(rho+p_r)*nu'-2F*h'^2/r,
    nu'=(M+alpha*r^3*p_r)/(r^2*F).

It can be written p_r'+A(r)*p_r=-B(r), where

    A=M/(r^2 F)+alpha*r*(rho+p_r)/F,
    B=M*rho/(r^2 F)+2F*h'^2/r >=0.

The integrating-factor solution with p_r(R)=0 gives p_r>=0 in the interior.
Consequently nu'>=0 and N(r)>=N0. For ell2 and r<=R,

    V_2 >= 3*N0^2/R^2.

The regular-center, zero-surface Dirichlet operator A_D therefore has
lambda_1>=lambda_lower=3*N0^2/R^2. The singular center is handled by the
regular U~r^3 endpoint. This is a lower bound, not a measured mode frequency.

## 5. Boundary response: derive the frequency correction and its bound

Let s=omega^2. Normalize the regular interior solution by u_s(R)=1, where
u_s here denotes the full master solution, not the r^3 envelope. Define
m(s)=partial_rstar u_s(R) and I0=int_0^Rstar u_0^2 drstar.
A Wronskian identity gives m'(s)=-int u_s^2 drstar. More precisely,

    u_s=u_0+s*(A_D-s)^(-1)*u_0,
    m(s)=m0-s*I0-s^2*<u_0,(A_D-s)^(-1)u_0>.

For 0<=s<lambda_lower this gives the explicit bound

    0 <= m0-s*I0-m(s) <= s^2*I0/(lambda_lower-s).

No resonance frequency, inertia or response parameter is fitted. In units
k=omega*R and Y=R*m, the reference numerical body gives

    N0=0.970677552873,
    lambda_lower*R^2=2.82664473495455,
    Y0=2.9206856656442817,

    Y(k)=Y0-0.14698388060249917*k^2-0.0024746268887009507*k^4+O(k^6).

The k^2 coefficient is computed both by an inhomogeneous variational ODE

The analytic inequality uses the inherited numerical background parameters;
