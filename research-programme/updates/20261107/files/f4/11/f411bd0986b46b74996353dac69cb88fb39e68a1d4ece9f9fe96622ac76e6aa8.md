# Prospective mean-remainder correction, derived before judging reduced runs

The near-identity remainder is NOT purely oscillatory. Products of exp(2i theta) with the conjugate fast evolution produce a phase-independent contribution. Therefore the first b_N=S experiment deliberately tests a potentially over-aggressive truncation.

For the exact canonical/trace system, with mu=omega/H, freeze a,H,A,B,z and the other species when extracting carrier harmonics. Write x=exp(2i theta), gamma=k^2/(2omega H), kappa=k/(2H). The background and trace equations give

```text
(H_N/H)_{x^-1} = omega A^2/(8a^2 H^2)
(z_N)_{x^-1} = 3omega A B/(4kHa^2)
(A_N)* = (3/2)x^-1 A
(B_N)* = i gamma B* - kappa z A*
         + x^-1[(3/2+i gamma)B+kappa z A].
```

Substituting them in R=-x[C_N-(1-H_N/H)C]/(2i mu), its constant harmonic is

```text
R0 = i/(2mu) {
     [9/4+gamma^2+3omega |A|^2/(8a^2 H^2)] B
     + kappa z(3-i gamma) A
     + 3omega A^2 B*/(16a^2 H^2) }.
```

The last two gravitational coefficients follow from metric/background feedback, not fitted endpoint data. mean_remainder.py checks the Laurent-polynomial extraction symbolically. Under these frozen-coefficient assumptions, R has only harmonics0,1,2 in x. Dense-background, noncanonical trace and carrier-interpolation defects are separate numerical terms, not declared zero in the actual control.

This is a conditional leading coefficient, NOT yet a verified autonomous closure or a global error bound. B and z still depend implicitly on the near-identity map, and the background amplitude has its own micromotion. If the zero-remainder candidate fails, a justified next experiment is to retain the derived R0 and bound the remaining phase-dependent terms, rather than fitting a replacement pressure or repeatedly tightening the old integrator.

The explicit flux and background-pressure terms cancel; the perturbation-pressure harmonic remains. Three symbolic identities test this step separately from the R0 extraction.
