## 1. Start from the actual MTS scalar, not an invented memory of it

Checkpoint 4916 records the conservative real scalar, in natural units,

\[
\mathcal L_\psi=-\tfrac12\eta^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\psi\partial_\nu\psi
 -V(\psi),\qquad V(\psi)=\tfrac34 g_\psi|\psi|^{4/3},\quad g_\psi>0.
\]

Signature is (-,+,+,+). The earlier constant-coefficient term
`-gamma psi partial_t psi` is a total time derivative, not dissipation.
About any background where the potential is twice differentiable, the
quadratic operator has principal part `eta^{mu nu} partial_mu partial_nu`.
Changing the background changes the mass/potential term, not that principal
cone. At psi=0 this particular fractional potential is not C2; a finite
ordinary mass Hessian at zero cannot be assumed.

Thus simply calling the old real scalar a condensate does not manufacture a
new propagation geometry. Using the already independent `g(H)` instead of
eta imports that geometry from the existing metric sector. Neither statement
is a rejection of all collective or nonperturbative realizations of MTS.

The original scalar is real and has no declared particle-number U(1). The
Schwinger-Keldysh response/history variables must NOT be relabeled as two
independent physical components of a complex condensate.

## 2. Explicit minimal phase/amplitude extension

Introduce a NEW physical complex scalar

\[
\mathcal C=\frac{\varrho}{\sqrt2}e^{i\theta},\qquad
\mathcal L_C=-\tfrac12(\partial\varrho)^2+\varrho^2X_\theta-V(\varrho),
\qquad X_\theta=-\tfrac12\eta^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\theta\partial_\nu\theta.
\]

New assumptions: a second real physical degree of freedom, global U(1), a
chosen potential, and a charged/coherent state. `X_theta` is a new branch-local
kinetic scalar, NOT the old bath variable X. `varrho` is an amplitude, not
the old mass density or radial coordinate. In units hbar=c=1, amplitude and
mu have mass dimension1, X_theta dimension2, and V dimension4.

Flat eta remains an input. This calculation concerns emergent *effective*
geometry for disturbances; it does not claim to derive underlying spacetime.
Nor does a classical coherent saddle prove thermodynamic condensation or
supply a cosmological preparation history.

The exact field equations are

\[
\Box\varrho+2X_\theta\varrho-V'(\varrho)=0,
\qquad \partial_\mu(\varrho^2\partial^\mu\theta)=0.
\]

For a homogeneous rotating state `theta=mu t`, `varrho=varrho_0>0`,

\[
\mu^2=\frac{V'(\varrho_0)}{\varrho_0},\qquad
M_a^2=V''(\varrho_0)-\mu^2.
\]

These define the state relation and radial stiffness. Mu is a chosen charge
or state parameter, not a predicted clock frequency. The future-directed
phase-flow convention is `u_mu=-partial_mu theta/sqrt(2X_theta)`; the current
sign can be reversed by reversing the U(1) generator convention.

## 3. Exact two-mode spectrum before discarding any amplitude derivatives

Write `varrho=varrho_0+sigma`, `theta=mu t+pi`, and `phi=varrho_0 pi`.
Up to a linear total time derivative, the quadratic Lagrangian is

\[
\mathcal L_2=\tfrac12\dot\sigma^2-\tfrac12|\nabla\sigma|^2
-\tfrac12 M_a^2\sigma^2+\tfrac12\dot\phi^2-\tfrac12|\nabla\phi|^2
+2\mu\sigma\dot\phi.
\]

With `exp(-i omega t+i k.x)`, its determinant is

\[
(k^2+M_a^2-\omega^2)(k^2-\omega^2)-4\mu^2\omega^2=0.
\]

Let `D=M_a^2+4mu^2`. The exact roots are

\[
\omega_\pm^2=k^2+\frac D2\pm\sqrt{\frac{D^2}{4}+4\mu^2k^2}.
\]

For M_a^2>0, both roots are nonnegative: their sum is 2k^2+D and their
product is k^2(k^2+M_a^2). There is one gapless scalar mode and one scalar
mode of gap squared D. The phase momentum completes the positive square
`(p_phi-2mu sigma)^2/2`; mixing is not a hidden negative kinetic energy.

The low-momentum root is

\[
\omega_-^2=c_s^2k^2+\frac{16\mu^4}{D^3}k^4+O(k^6),
\qquad c_s^2=\frac{M_a^2}{M_a^2+4\mu^2}.
\]

The high-momentum characteristic speed tends to the assumed microscopic
speed1. These are two scalar excitations, not the two tensor polarizations
of gravitational waves.

## 4. Keeping the original fractional potential gives a definite answer

Do not replace the original potential with a familiar quartic without
checking it. For `V=g varrho^p/p`, the circular solution gives

\[
M_a^2=(p-2)\mu^2,\qquad c_s^2=\frac{p-2}{p+2}.
\]

At the original p=4/3,

\[
M_a^2=-\tfrac23\mu^2,\qquad c_s^2=-\tfrac15.
\]

Indeed, for `0<k^2<2mu^2/3` the product of the two squared frequencies is
negative, so one mode grows exponentially. The simple charged complex
promotion with the unchanged fractional potential is unstable.

**This is NOT an instability proof for the original real-scalar MTS theory.**
The new U(1), physical field component and rotating charged state are the
additional hypotheses that lead to this test. It also is not a diagnosis of
the preceding annular mesh-jump problem.

### A concrete repair while retaining that original term

Instead of just recording the failure, retain the old operator and add one
explicit repulsive interaction:

\[
V=\tfrac34g\varrho^{4/3}+\tfrac\lambda4\varrho^4,
\quad g>0,\quad\lambda>0,
\quad y=\frac{\lambda\varrho_0^{8/3}}g.
\]

Here [g]=mass^(8/3), lambda and y are dimensionless. Lambda here is a quartic
coupling, written lowercase lambda; it is NOT the cosmological constant.

Direct differentiation gives

\[
\mu^2=g\varrho_0^{-2/3}+\lambda\varrho_0^2,
\quad M_a^2=-\tfrac23g\varrho_0^{-2/3}+2\lambda\varrho_0^2,
\quad c_s^2=\frac{3y-1}{5+9y}.
\]

**Local homogeneous stability requires y>1/3.** But local stability is not
enough: at fixed mu, compare the homogeneous grand-potential density with
the empty state of zero grand potential:

\[
\Omega=V-\tfrac12\mu^2\varrho_0^2
=\tfrac14g\varrho_0^{4/3}(1-y).
\]

Preference over the empty state requires y>1; y=1 is coexistence. This is a
homogeneous grand-canonical comparison, not a proof of cosmological state
selection, uniqueness against all inhomogeneous phases or nonlinear global
stability. At coexistence c_s^2=1/7. On the preferred y>1 branch,

\[
P=\tfrac14(\lambda\varrho_0^4-g\varrho_0^{4/3}),\quad
\epsilon=\tfrac14(3\lambda\varrho_0^4+5g\varrho_0^{4/3}),\quad
w=\frac{y-1}{5+3y}.
\]

It is fluid-like, with 0<w<1/3, not a cosmological constant. This explicit
repair is a new candidate operator, NOT derived from the old parent or
fitted to any observation. The result is the stability/preference condition,
not a selected numerical lambda.

## 5. Derive the effective metric, rather than assume it

For M_a^2>0 and background/disturbance gradients small compared with the
radial scale, neglect the amplitude derivative term to leading order and
solve `V'(varrho)=2X_theta varrho` on a chosen branch. Then

\[
P(X_\theta)=\varrho(X_\theta)^2X_\theta-V(\varrho(X_\theta)),
\quad P_X=\varrho^2,\quad
P_{XX}=\frac{4\varrho^2}{M_a^2}.
\]

The linear phase equation has principal tensor

\[
K^{\mu\nu}=P_X\eta^{\mu\nu}
-P_{XX}\partial^\mu\theta\partial^\nu\theta,
\qquad \partial_\mu(K^{\mu\nu}\partial_\nu\pi)=0.
\]

Thus `c_s^2=P_X/(P_X+2X_theta P_XX)`, agreeing with the exact low-k spectrum.
For any fixed positive normalization Z_* of mass dimension2, define

\[
G_{\mu\nu}=\frac{P_X}{Z_*c_s}
[\eta_{\mu\nu}+(1-c_s^2)u_\mu u_\nu].
\]

In four dimensions,

\[
K^{\mu\nu}=Z_*\sqrt{-G}\,G^{\mu\nu}.
\]

The calculation verifies the determinant and all16 matrix entries in the
rest frame; the displayed tensor identity then applies in any frame. The
phase equation is a curved-space scalar wave equation for this effective
metric. For varying on-shell backgrounds it can describe nontrivial
effective geometry; the homogeneous state used for the spectrum is flat.
No particular curved global solution has been constructed here.

Z_* fixes the field/metric normalization, not Newton's constant. The
derivative expansion fails as M_a^2 tends to zero; use the full two-mode
system there, not the singular phase-only reduction. For a Fourier mode the
amplitude elimination requires |k^2-omega^2| much smaller than M_a^2.

For an independent normalization check, the script also evaluates the usual
massive quartic `V=m^2 varrho^2/2+lambda varrho^4/4`. It gives
`P=(2X_theta-m^2)^2/(4lambda)` and
`c_s^2=(mu^2-m^2)/(3mu^2-m^2)` on mu^2>m^2. That is a comparator, not a
quiet replacement of the original fractional operator.

## 6. Matter, vacuum energy, and the first tensor-response calculation

The homogeneous stress is a perfect-fluid stress,

\[
T_{\mu\nu}=P_X\partial_\mu\theta\partial_\nu\theta+P\eta_{\mu\nu},
\quad\epsilon=2X_\theta P_X-P,
\quad\epsilon+P=\mu^2\varrho_0^2>0.
\]

An additive vacuum constant shifts epsilon and P oppositely, leaving this
enthalpy unchanged. It cannot erase the charged state's preferred timelike
direction. Nothing in this derivation makes photons, fermions or the whole
Standard Model couple to G rather than the originally assumed eta.

At linear order around the homogeneous state the spatial matter-stress
variation is isotropic. Both traceless shear contractions vanish. Thus the
two scalar modes do not secretly supply a linear helicity-two excitation.
This does not exclude composite tensor modes nonperturbatively.

We go one step beyond counting fields. In the controlled phonon limit let
`vartheta=sqrt(P_X+mu^2 P_XX) pi` be time-canonically normalized. The leading
off-diagonal stress is `T_xy=c_s^2 partial_x vartheta partial_y vartheta`.
Define the positive-frequency, zero-spatial-momentum commutator spectrum

\[
\rho_{xy,xy}(\omega,\mathbf0)=\int dt\,d^3x\,
e^{i\omega t}\langle[T_{xy}(t,\mathbf x),T_{xy}(0)]\rangle,
\quad\omega>0.
\]

For the Gaussian phonon vacuum, with the Fourier measure d^3k/(2pi)^3,
Wick's two contractions give

\[
\rho=2c_s^4\int\frac{d^3k}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac{k_x^2k_y^2}{4(c_sk)^2}\,2\pi\delta(\omega-2c_sk)
=\boxed{\frac{\omega^4}{960\pi c_s^3}}.
\]

The independent angular integral is `int dOmega n_x^2 n_y^2=4pi/15`.
This is a leading two-phonon continuum, not an isolated tensor particle pole
in this calculated channel. Contact terms and the real response are not
fixed by this positive-frequency spectral calculation. It is low-energy,
zero-spatial-momentum, zero-temperature and Gaussian: neither finite-momentum
nonperturbative bound states nor a complete gravitational response are ruled
out. Its role is a concrete starting tensor-response calculation.

## 7. What is inherited, and what actually changed

### Finite-momentum check and the metric's tensor content

A further independent runner differentiates the composite metric at the
homogeneous rest state, allowing arbitrary first variations of both its
conformal/disformal coefficients and a normalized flow. It obtains
`delta G_ij=delta A delta_ij`, hence exactly zero transverse-traceless spatial
variation. This is a restriction of this composite metric at this background,
not a prohibition on tensor composites in a different microscopic theory.

The Gaussian phonon calculation can also be transported to finite momentum
without guessing a pole. Rescale `x'=x/c_s` and
`vartheta'=c_s^(3/2) vartheta`. Then `T_xy=c_s^(-3) T'_xy`. For momentum along
z, an acoustic Lorentz boost along z leaves the xy tensor indices unchanged.
Including the Fourier-volume Jacobian gives, for positive omega,

\[
\rho_{xy,xy}(\omega,q\hat z)
=\frac{(\omega^2-c_s^2q^2)^2}{960\pi c_s^3}
\,\Theta(\omega-c_s|q|).
\]

It reduces to the previous formula at q=0 and has a continuous two-phonon
threshold, not an isolated tensor pole. Both omega and q must remain in the
phonon EFT regime. The amplitude branch and interaction resummation remain
outside this result. A curvature term written in the effective metric cannot
by itself supply a free tensor variation: varying a composite `G(varrho,theta)`
requires the chain rule, not treating its ten components as independent.
