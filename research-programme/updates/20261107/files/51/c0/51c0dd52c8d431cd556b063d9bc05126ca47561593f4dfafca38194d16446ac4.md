# Stage60 — a conserved source density tied to the actual metric

## The bridge, and its scope

Use the retained cb matter equations in the existing spatially flat native
MTS/controls. This stage derives a conserved coordinate density from covariant
number conservation, verifies the native gauge conversion, and constructs a
positive flow solution without an algebraic density closure. It does not
assign an exact particle-number current to the real MTS scalar. The heavy
field still influences the metric through the existing coupled evolution.

The original slow/heavy action, phase matching, parameters, background,
linear hierarchy and survey fits are untouched. Flatness and negligible
baryon pressure in the density equation are inherited implementation choices;
neither is silently promoted to a general theorem about all matter.

## Start with a covariant current, not a guessed density

For a conserved, future-directed number current J^mu=n*u^mu,

\[
 \nabla_\mu J^\mu=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \partial_\tau N+\partial_i(NV^i)=0,
 \qquad N=\sqrt{-g}\,J^0,\quad V^i=J^i/J^0.
\]

This coordinate-density statement is exact wherever the current is defined.
In a3+1 foliation, N=sqrt(gamma)*n*W with W=lapse*u^0. Proper density n
and coordinate density N therefore differ by metric volume and Lorentz
weight. The lapse is not an independently fitted conversion factor.

For the linear Newtonian-gauge metric

\[
 ds^2=a^2[(1+2\Psi_N)d\tau^2-(1-2\Phi_N)d\mathbf x^2],
\]

sqrt(-g)=a^4(1+Psi_N-3Phi_N) and u^0=a^-1(1-Psi_N)+O(v^2).
Because a^3*nbar is constant for conserved nonrelativistic matter,

\[
 {N\over\bar N}=1+D,\qquad D=\delta_N-3\Phi_N,
 \qquad D'=-\theta_N.
\]

The metric-volume term is required. Replacing D by a synchronous or proper
Newtonian density without its conversion does not give the same equation.

Here eta_s=etak/k, Hcal=a'/a, and sigma is native scalar shear, not a
statistical dispersion. The source/symbolic conversion yields

\[
 \delta_N=\delta_S-3{\mathcal H\sigma\over k},\qquad
 \Phi_N=\eta_s-{\mathcal H\sigma\over k},\qquad
 \boxed{D=\delta_S-3\eta_s}.
\]

The CDM Newtonian transfer is T_v,c=-k*sigma/Hcal. For baryons, replace
sigma by sigma+v_b. With constant baryon mass fraction f_b,

\[
 T_{v,cb}=-{k\over\mathcal H}(\sigma+f_bv_b).
\]

The implemented flat equations give delta_S,cb'=-k(z+f_b*v_b) and
eta_s'=k(sigma-z)/3. Therefore, without assuming a growth-index fit,

\[
 \boxed{{dD_{cb}\over d\ln a}=T_{v,cb}}.
\]

This follows from the native constraints plus matter conservation, including
the metric response to the retained MTS fields. It is not a proof of the
unimplemented nonlinear scalar dynamics or of emergent gravity from scratch.

The initial coordinate density generally contains a primordial curvature
piece. Integrating velocity from a chosen initial time gives D(a)-D(a_i),
not D(a) unless the initial density has been specified accordingly. Setting
initial density uniform while using an arbitrary saved endpoint contrast
would lose that integration constant.

## Conserved tracer bias cannot be fixed at all times arbitrarily

For an initially biased tracer with the SAME velocity and no creation or
destruction, D_g'=D_cb'. Define its proper rest-frame relation
delta_g,S=b(k,a)*delta_cb,S. The gauge volume term is common to the two
species, not multiplied by b, so D_g=b*delta_cb,S-3eta_s. It follows that

\[
 {d\over d\ln a}[(b-1)\delta_{cb,S}]=0,\qquad
 b(k,a)=1+[b(k,a_i)-1]{\delta_{cb,S}(k,a_i)\over\delta_{cb,S}(k,a)}.
\]

Scale-dependent parent growth can create scale-dependent bias even from a
constant initial bias. A fixed bias fitted at one redshift remains a valid
single-epoch nuisance; it must not be propagated unchanged while also
asserting conserved tracer number and equal velocities. Source/selection
terms or velocity bias would modify this law and need explicit physics.

## Exact positive one-mode flow under a stated velocity approximation

Let s=ln a, y=k*x, and prescribe the inherited linear velocity mode

\[
 {dy\over ds}=-A T_v(s)\sin y.
\]

This uses the native velocity as a prescribed field; it is not a full
nonlinear force solution. Define I(s)=integral T_v ds=D_cb(s)-D_cb(s_i),
g=A*I, E=exp(-g), and initial phase q. Separation of this ODE gives

\[
 \tan{y\over2}=E\tan{q\over2},\qquad
 J={\partial y\over\partial q}
 ={E\over\cos^2(q/2)+E^2\sin^2(q/2)}>0.
\]

The exponential is the integrated trajectory solution, not an imposed
exponential density ansatz. With positive initial coordinate density
N_i(q)/Nbar=1+A*D_g,i*cos(q),

\[
 {N(y,s)\over\bar N}={1+A D_{g,i}\cos q\over J},\qquad
 N\,dy=N_i\,dq.
\]

Positivity is preserved for finite g if |A*D_g,i|<1. Total number is conserved
exactly. This is conditional on positive initial data; it does not convert
every unbounded Gaussian initial density realization into physical matter.
No tails or cells are clipped and no total mass is renormalized afterward.

Expanding at fixed Eulerian y derives

\[
 {N\over\bar N}=1+A(D_{g,i}+I)\cos y
 +A^2(D_{g,i}I+I^2/2)\cos 2y+O(A^3).
\]

Thus even its first nonlinear density coefficient is fixed by transport and
the stored field endpoints. The cosine2y coefficient is twice its complex
Fourier coefficient. Independent sampling of the exact map checks that factor.

At the final time, the additional redshift map is y_s=y-A*T_v,f*sin(y).
Its small-amplitude fundamental is A*(D_g,f+T_v,f)/2 for a LOS mode. This
is a coordinate-count Kaiser limit, not permission to replace the old
rest-frame density in a full light-cone survey likelihood. Metric projection,
selection and higher multipole window effects still require consistent treatment.

## The next force term is explicit

Keeping only linear velocity is enough to solve continuity but not Euler.
For U_y=dy/ds=-A*T_v*sin(y), the missing convective term is

\[
 U_y\partial_y U_y={A^2T_v^2\over2}\sin2y.
\]

This nonzero second harmonic cannot be discarded while calling the solution
self-consistent nonlinear gravity. The density already sources quadratic
gravity/stress changes too. The next task is to derive the second-order
force and scalar stress from the SAME parent action, rather than choose a
new density law to hide this residual. Any heavy-phase averaging at that
order must be justified separately; linear matching is not its proof.
