## What changed physically

Stage60 transported matter with a prescribed linear velocity. This stage
derives and evolves the missing second-order velocity/gravity feedback
within the retained action's leading weak-field, nonrelativistic limit.
Cold matter, baryons and the old heavy normal mode share the same sourced
potential. No new damping parameter or fundamental field is added.

The action also fixes the heavy quadratic pressure term:
the second-harmonic velocity equation contains
**-16 lambda e + 3 lambda d^2**, alongside gravitational and convective
terms. The coefficient3 is derived from the square-root density force.
The general mixed-mode pressure kernel is derived too, and recovers
the familiar cold F2/G2 kernels when the pressure vanishes.

Separately, the exact canonical metric source is derived in ADM variables.
Its scalar anisotropic stress starts at second order. We distinguish it
from an improved effective momentum flux: using the latter as a new
Hilbert source would introduce an incorrect linear anisotropic stress.
Both the slow and heavy fields remain in the exact source expressions.

## How closely does the projection follow the saved parent?

Across the nine branch/wavenumber cases, the largest linear endpoint
density/velocity discrepancy from the source-complete native calculation
is **.02842%**, below the predeclared .5% gate. Cubic versus
shape-preserving background interpolation changes the quadratic response
by at most **.0600%**; tight/loose integration changes normalized states

The numerical projection keeps the actual slow-field expansion history,
but not slow-field/radiation/neutrino perturbations, baryon gas pressure
or post-Newtonian corrections. The linear comparison is a useful test of
that projection, NOT a rigorous bound on all second-order omitted terms.
Cold and baryon velocities are evolved separately, preserving their
relative-flow contribution instead of assuming it vanishes.
