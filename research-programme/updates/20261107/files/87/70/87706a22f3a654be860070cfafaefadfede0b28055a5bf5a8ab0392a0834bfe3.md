# Native-null coupled acceleration: boundary repair and source-layer mechanism

**Date:** 1 October 2026. Private local work; no GitHub operation.

## Result in plain language

We have now calculated the actual coupled field/source acceleration on the newly constructed nonzero constraint-satisfying slice. It was not borrowed from the old trajectory or chosen to cancel a residual.

The first calculation exposed incompatible outer boundary data. Replacing that initial profile with one satisfying the parent's natural zero-flux condition removes the large endpoint-driven acceleration without modifying the action.

The replacement still cannot supply a smooth time-symmetric source jet within this quadratic family: the two sides of the pinned source demand different accelerations. That is an explicit compatibility contradiction, not a missing-number diagnosis. A further exact calculation explains the observed numerical ripple as a consistent-P2-mass boundary layer, with a parameter-free prediction confirmed on both grids.

This identifies the next construction problem: source-compatible initial data satisfying the complete native constraints, rather than another evaluation of the same quadratic slice.

## 1. Actual coupled solve

At zero field and source rates, the first rate response of the radial metric constraints vanishes. Momenta and coordinate convection vanish as well. The first acceleration therefore obeys

    H a = F_wave + F_source

on the exact native-Gram-null state. The wave/source cross block and proper-clock source inertia M/(F*N) remain in H. No extra map inertia, fitted source acceleration, or discarded endpoint equation is used.

The native Gram first force vanishes analytically on the constructed state. Its represented leakage and the inherited fixed-reference leakage are separately checked rather than silently substituted for one another.

Both grids use two independently rebuilt spatial/material integration pairs: 12/48 and 16/64. Controls include the full Euler residual, independent complex-step momentum-Jacobian action, positive field/Schur factorization, matched-reference force, independent 64-digit selected matrix rows, and replayed native shape integrals.

The source-section correction is retained:

    A_normal = a_field - n_b*a_source,
    B_i''''(0) = 6*B_i[A_normal],

for a sufficiently smooth time-symmetric jet in the derived radical family. These are finite-parent jet calculations, not an evolved trajectory.

The old choice had zero endpoint values but nonzero flux. The replacement is

    n = a*((R-L)^2-(b-L)^2), R<=b,
    n = a*((R-U)^2-(b-U)^2), R>=b.

## 3. The exact source-corner incompatibility

At the pinned source q(t,b(t))=0, zero initial rates require

    q_tt(b±) + b_ddot*q_R(b±) = 0.

On this native-null slice the one-sided interior equation is

    q_tt = c^2*q_RR + d*q_R,
    c=N*sqrt(F), d=2*c^2/R+c*c_R.

For common continuous metric traces and the equal-curvature family,

    2a*c_b^2 + (d_b+b_ddot)*p_minus = 0,
    2a*c_b^2 + (d_b+b_ddot)*p_plus  = 0.

Since p_minus != p_plus, subtraction forces b_ddot=-d_b, after which either equation demands 2a*c_b^2=0. That is incompatible with the nonzero amplitude and untrapped nonzero-lapse chart used here.

For the repaired profile, the two required source accelerations are

    required_left  = -d_b-c_b^2/(b-L),
    required_right = -d_b+c_b^2/(U-b).

At the central material label:

- Required left: approximately -0.9088173966.
- Required right: approximately +0.5646252932.
- Gap: approximately 1.4734426898.
- Actual coupled source acceleration: -0.01383613433 (fine), -0.01383613316 (third).

The gap is amplitude-independent for nonzero amplitude. At zero amplitude the division by the slopes is invalid and this obstruction does not apply.

A compensating drift jump would have to equal c_b^2*(1/(b-L)+1/(U-b)). The actual finite-collar probes approach zero drift spread as the offsets shrink; at 1e-12 the largest sampled spread is below 9.1e-12, not of order 1.47. This is a numerical check of the present finite-collar fixture, not a proof about every thin-shell limit.

The conditional theorem concerns this source pin and this quadratic family. It does not exclude weak solutions, other initial profiles, a separately derived interface action, or MTS generally.

## 4. A derived explanation of the source ripple

The actual central normal acceleration agrees with the interior expression away from the source/endpoints. At margin 0.025, maximum errors are 3.95e-11 (fine) and 1.35e-14 (third). Near the source, the necessary limiting normal accelerations are approximately 0.00420299542 on the left and 0.00252018262 on the right, but the pinned node has zero normal acceleration.

Freeze the local positive kinetic coefficient and use uniform P2 cells. The consistent mass matrix gives the exact error recurrence

    e_(j+1)-6*e_j+e_(j-1)=0,
    rho=3-2*sqrt(2),
    e_j=-A*rho^j,
    midpoint_error_j=A*(1+rho)*rho^j/8.

No parameter is fitted. The nearest midpoint therefore overshoots the required acceleration by 0.1464466094*A; the next vertex undershoots it by 0.1715728753*A.

The actual finer-grid normalized errors at the first eight nodes agree with this prediction to 5.11e-8 (left) and 6.75e-8 (right). The coarser-grid maximum first-eight discrepancies are 1.74e-4 and 2.90e-4; the frozen-coefficient approximation is not claimed exact throughout a graded variable-metric mesh.

Halving the grid spacing halves the nearest-node distance while leaving its error essentially unchanged: observed error ratios are 0.9999990 and 1.0000015.

The exact local layer also satisfies

    integral e^2 dR       = A^2*h*sqrt(2)/12,
    integral (e_R)^2 dR   = A^2*31*sqrt(2)/(12*h).

Thus it can shrink in L2 while retaining a pointwise mismatch and growing derivative norm. This explains why apparently well-behaved bulk acceleration can coexist with refinement-sensitive shape diagnostics. It is not evidence of a physical singularity, and the frozen-coefficient calculation does not establish the full parent's weak evolution.

## 5. Fair comparison with the reference

The matched reference uses the same initial state, solved metric, wave/source coupling, source pin, and endpoint space. Its first force agrees with the MTS force on the exact native-null slice. Therefore the source-corner problem is shared by this reference model; it is not a selective MTS failure.

## 7. Next construction, not another repeat

Do not rerun this equal-curvature null family in the hope that a finer grid or smaller nonzero amplitude cures the exact corner mismatch.

The next useful attempt is source-compatible initial data with independent one-sided curvatures, retaining the source acceleration from the coupled action. For a general smooth slice with native acceleration contribution g_extra, the necessary trace equations are

    c_minus^2*k_minus + (d_minus+a_source)*p_minus + g_extra,minus = 0,
    c_plus^2*k_plus + (d_plus+a_source)*p_plus + g_extra,plus = 0.

The equal-curvature restriction is removable as a trial-state restriction; the source equation and native first force are not removable. Outside the exact kernel, g_extra must be calculated from the action, and the complete boundary variations must be retained. One cannot keep the old zero-Gram force or assume that the radical-family fourth-order law automatically applies.

Build these compatibility conditions into the next joint initial-state/constraint solve, with the matched reference as a control. The purpose is to obtain a dynamically admissible starting state before spending resources on a finite evolution, not to manufacture a cancellation.
