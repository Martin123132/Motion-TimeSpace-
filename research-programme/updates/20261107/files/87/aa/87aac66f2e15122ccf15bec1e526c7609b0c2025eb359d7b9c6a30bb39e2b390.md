The dust-force branch drops the material spatial-potential driver; its
zero driver is not a prediction of zero physical lensing potential.

## Exact identity carried to the new scales

N=ln(a); H below is conformal Hubble rate. The physical reference sources are

    M = 3 H Q_g / (2 k^3),       S = Pi_g/k^2,
    P = -D_g/(2k^2),            J = Phi_g - chi P,
    L = 1 + 3 chi W_q/(2k^2),   T = 3 H J_N/(k L).

Here chi=1 for Poisson-spatial and chi=0 for dust-force. W_q is the
actual retained quadrature enthalpy. For the spatial branch the GR
constraint gives J=-M exactly.

With the coupled adjoint lambda_N=-A_n^T lambda, metric-source injection
columns b_alpha,b_z, and the original physical-density terminal readout,

    d = 3 H b_z/(k L),
    C = lambda^T d,
    C_N = lambda^T(d_N - A_n d).

Thus the temporal contribution is [C J] - integral(C_N J dN).
Both endpoints are retained. In particular w=e-dJ has w_initial=-dJ_initial:
its nonzero initial term is not discarded. Full scalar phase, velocity,
scale and enthalpy derivatives remain in d_N. The coupled hierarchy remains
in the adjoint. No slowly varying adjoint assumption is introduced.

## A direct next derivation, not another blind sweep

For the spatial branch put G=lambda^T(b_alpha+Y b_z), with the retained
source mixing Y=-3(H+g)/(kL), g=3HW/(2k^2). Substituting J=-M gives exactly

    ell e_final = -[C M] + integral((G+C_N) M + G S) dN.

Triangle and Holder inequalities therefore give the sufficient bound

    |ell e_final| <= (|C_initial|+|C_final|+||G+C_N||_1) ||M||_infinity
                    + ||G||_1 ||S||_infinity.

This makes the next physics target explicit: control the current/shear
sources AND the coupled response kernels, including the heavy scalar phase.
Small H/k alone is not enough if a response kernel grows or a normalization
degenerates. A controlled Newtonian limit follows on a common interval if
these products tend to zero with a well-defined physical preparation and
readout. The three successful k cases do not by themselves establish those
uniform hypotheses or a k^-2 law.

Next use the saved adjoints and source histories to evaluate these separate
products, then derive their scaling from the retained equations. Do not
fit an exponent to these three cases, discard the scalar phase, change the
physical observable, or start another broad parameter sweep.
