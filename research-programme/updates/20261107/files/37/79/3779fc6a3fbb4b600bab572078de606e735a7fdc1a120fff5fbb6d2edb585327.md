# 38. Conservative eccentric action and the circular-limit periapsis shift

Private daughter, completed during the night of 5–6 October 2026.
Immediate parent: `../postcircular-clock-20261005/results/stage-integrity.json`.

This step derives the orbit-averaged conservative action through O(e²), including
the static tide, and uses its action derivatives to obtain the circular-limit
radial and azimuthal frequency corrections. The same calculation yields a
body-minus-black-hole periapsis advance at fixed azimuthal frequency. No new
potential coefficient is fitted.

## 1. Physical scope and conventions

Keep the selected Einstein-scalar body, its global charge, mass M, radius R,
metric coupling, Schwarzschild exterior and G_R normalization fixed. The
comparison is the smooth response difference between that body and the same-M
Schwarzschild black hole, using the same companion source. This daughter extends
the current condensate calculation; it does not replace the parent action.

Geometric units: M and companion mass mu have length units; lambda has length⁵.
Specific actions j=J_r/mu and h=J_phi/mu have length units. Set z=e² and r0=M p.
The conservative correction is

    F(p,z) = mu² [f0(p)+z f2(p)+O(z²)].

F has geometric-energy units (length), and f0,f2 have inverse-length
units. Primes on f and h below mean derivatives with respect to dimensionless p.
All quoted frequency shifts are first order in mu/M, with the conservative
response treated perturbatively. All equatorially sourced metric ell=2 channels
are included; other multipoles and the common point-mass self-force are outside
this body-minus-BH contribution.

## 2. Time-symmetric response, not a new force ansatz

The inherited retarded action of one radiative reality pair is

    Fret_mn/mu² = -3 DeltaC_mn O_mn / (8 pi),

where O is the outgoing-basis source overlap and DeltaC the smooth exterior
response-difference coefficient. Its imaginary part supplies the optical/work
identities used previously. Its real part supplies the time-symmetric action.
These are two parts of the same linear response, not independent couplings.

The source/field elimination factor of one half has ALREADY been included in
this formula, via stage20 reciprocity. Applying it again would halve the force;
omitting it at the original worldline elimination would double the force.

For each m2 polar and m1 odd carrier, stage37 gives the geometric source
coefficient. The action correction includes both source legs:

    f2_carrier = Re{ -3/(8pi) [DeltaC2_geo O0 + DeltaC0 O2_geo]
                         + alpha_phi omega0 partial_omega Fret0/mu² }.

The derivative acts on the entire matched response. It is evaluated at fixed p,
using the archived two-step, five-point frequency stencils from stage37. It is
not replaced by differentiation of an overlap with frozen interior response.

Add the real actions of the first sidebands: polar (m,n)=(2,+1),(2,-1),(0,1)
and odd (1,+1),(1,-1). Their source amplitudes start at e, so their quadratic
actions start at e². Higher sidebands start contributing at e⁴.

## 3. Derive the non-radiating static contribution

The polar m=0,n=0 term has frequency exactly zero at every eccentricity. Its
reality degeneracy is one, not two. It must never be evaluated using a radiative
1/omega expression.

Use the inherited static master bases P,Q with W(P,Q)=-5/6 and
U proportional to P+3lambda Q. Define I=<T[Q]>_t, with the conserved source
averaged over coordinate time. Then

    f_static = -9lambda I²/[16pi W(P,Q)].

For T=(A+i v D+v²G)y-(C+v²C2)y_x, the m=0 second-order source is

    I2 = E2 T_E + h2 T_h + r0(1-a)T_r/2 + r0² T_rr/4
                              + r0² Omega_r0² T_vv/4,
    a = 2/(p-2)-2+1/(p-6).

All derivatives are evaluated at r0,v=0,omega=0 and the circular E,h; T_r,T_rr
include derivatives of the homogeneous radial solution. Stage37 supplies this
analytic source operator, now evaluated with Y20=-sqrt[5/(16pi)], m=0 and the
static decaying basis. There is no phase-wobble term and no frequency-shift term.

Writing B=-9lambda/[16pi W(P,Q)] gives

    f_static,0 = B I0²,       f_static,2 = 2 B I0 I2.

Two actual zero-frequency interior solves, one at each existing resolution,
reproduce lambda=62823.92235391781 in the fine setting. Independent finite-e
orbit integration uses the ORIGINAL canonical source and explicit metric
contraction, not the new Taylor formula; it verifies the coefficient.

In the Newtonian limit I2/I0=-3/2, so f_static,2=-3 f_static,0.
The static part decreases at fixed p even though the complete averaged tide
increases. This is why the static and sideband pieces must be combined first.

## 4. From an averaged dispersive action to a Hamiltonian

Frequency dependence cannot simply be dropped when varying the action.
The first-order averaged variational calculation makes this explicit.

Let A=(j,h) label the background torus and let Omega denote independent trial
frequencies in a local averaged variational description. Write

    Lbar = mu A·Omega - mu E0(A) + mu² f(A,Omega).

The physical canonical actions and stationary-frequency equation are

    J = mu A + mu² partial_Omega f,
    Omega = partial_A E0 - mu partial_A f.

Eliminating A in favor of J/mu, to first order in mu, yields

    Hcons(J) = mu E0(J/mu) - mu² fbar(J/mu) + O(mu³),
    fbar(A) = f(A,Omega0(A)),
    delta Omega_a = -mu partial_Aa fbar.

The derivative of fbar is TOTAL along the background torus family. It contains
both explicit orbit dependence and the dispersive term
(partial_A Omega0)·partial_Omega f. Thus the Hamiltonian expression -F at fixed
canonical actions does not discard stored dynamical energy.

This is a perturbative, order-reduced averaged description for the stationary
time-symmetric branch, with no independently excited free body oscillation.
It is used away from response poles and orbital degeneracies. It is not an
assertion that an arbitrary-history nonlocal kernel is an instantaneous potential.
The reciprocal half-weight and action first-law structure are independently
consistent with Fujita et al.2017, Eq.70 and Sec.VIII.4. We do not transplant
their full Kerr gauge construction or claim a new all-mode ADM energy.

## 5. The action Jacobian gives both circular-limit frequencies

The inherited geodesic actions expand as

    h=h0+z h2+O(z²),   h0=M p/sqrt(p-3),   h2=h0/[2(p-3)],
    j=z C+O(z²),       C=M p^(3/2) sqrt(p-6)/[2(p-2)sqrt(p-3)].

Their first law is checked exactly: E0'=Omega_phi0 h0' and
E2=Omega_phi0 h2+Omega_r0 C. At z=0 the inverse Jacobian gives

    a_phi := delta Omega_phi/mu = -f0'/h0',
    a_r   := delta Omega_r/mu   = -(f2-h2 f0'/h0')/C.

The subtraction in a_r is essential: changing radial action at FIXED angular
action is not the same as changing eccentricity at fixed p.

These are shifts at fixed canonical actions. To report periapsis advance at a
common observable azimuthal frequency instead, shift the background label by
delta p=-mu a_phi/Omega_phi0'. For K=Omega_phi/Omega_r,

    (delta K/K0)|Omega_phi =
       -mu [a_r-(Omega_r0'/Omega_phi0') a_phi]/Omega_r0.

The comparison keeps asymptotic time, M and the body parameters fixed.
The reference p is then an unperturbed frequency label, not a claim that the
perturbed coordinate radius stayed fixed. The fixed-action version is separately
saved as mu(a_phi/Omega_phi0-a_r/Omega_r0); do not confuse the two.

Important order bookkeeping: f through e² supplies the CIRCULAR-LIMIT radial
frequency, because a derivative with respect to radial action removes one power
of e². A complete radial-frequency correction through finite e² needs f through
e⁴ as well. This checkpoint does not silently claim that extra order.

## 6. Independent weak-field derivation and circular regression

Eliminating the static quadrupole from the already matched action gives
L_tide=3lambda mu²/(2r⁶). Kepler time averaging at fixed semilatus yields

    <r^-6> = r0^-6 (1-z)^(3/2) [1+3z+3z²/8],
    f0 = 3lambda/(2r0⁶),          f2 = (3/2) f0.

The spectral route independently gives the same coefficient. In units of f0,
the O(z) contributions are

    static00: -3/4;   polar01 pair: +9/8;
    polar20 carrier: -33/4;   polar2,+/-1 sidebands: +75/8.

Their sum is 3/2. The relevant tidal overlap coefficients are static -3/2,
carrier -11/2, and sideband amplitudes 3/2,-1/2,7/2 for (0,1),(2,-1),(2,1).
They are derived from anomaly integrals, not extracted by fitting numeric data.
Odd tides are subleading in this electric Newtonian limit and remain included
in the finite-radius numerical calculation.

Substituting the Newtonian actions gives, at fixed canonical actions,

    delta Omega_phi/Omega_N = 18 (mu/M) lambda/r0⁵,
    delta Omega_r/Omega_N   = -(9/2) (mu/M) lambda/r0⁵,
    delta K/K_N            = (45/2) (mu/M) lambda/r0⁵.

The last leading coefficient also holds at fixed Omega_phi since K_N=1.
An independent small-perturbation central-force calculation with
V_eff=h²/(2r²)-M/r-3lambda mu/(2r⁶) recovers these coefficients. Its residuals
after removing the derived first-order law scale quadratically when the tidal
perturbation is doubled. That calculation is a consistency test of the derived
weak-field action, not a new phenomenological force assumption.

At fixed actions the binding correction is -mu² f. At fixed circular frequency
the same Legendre transformation instead gives

    delta J = mu² f0'/Omega_phi0',
    delta E = mu² [Omega_phi0 f0'/Omega_phi0'-f0].

This reproduces stage20's polar circular energy and angular-momentum corrections
at 5R and 10R to better than 4e-12 relative, using independent radial-derivative
data. In the Newtonian limit delta E=+9lambda mu²/(2r0⁶) at fixed frequency.
The opposite signs under different held-fixed quantities are consistent.
