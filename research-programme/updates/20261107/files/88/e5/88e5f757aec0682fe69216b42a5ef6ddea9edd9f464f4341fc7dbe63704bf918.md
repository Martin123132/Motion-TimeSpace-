# Normalized static collective operator: derivation and first test

3 October 2026. Isolated condensate-origin daughter calculation.
Natural units and fields are those in the preceding tensor-response step.

## Result in one paragraph

We derived the identical-particle normalization and relative-frequency
treatment, retained all four pair-matrix components, integrated the
coincident phase logarithm without an artificial epsilon, and built the
actual leading l=2 integral operator. In the chosen weak-coupling example,
its tested finite static matrices have eigenvalues of order 1e-5 rather
than the unit eigenvalue needed for a static collective onset. Enlarging
the cutoff while keeping the infrared grid fixed hardly changes this.
Infrared refinement is still measurable; no continuum norm theorem or
finite-frequency pole exclusion is inferred from a finite matrix.

## 2. Euclidean continuation from the actual spectrum

For real relative Euclidean frequency xi,
\[
 G(\xi,k)=-K(i\xi,k)^{-1}
 ={1\over(\xi^2+k^2)(\xi^2+k^2+m^2)+4\mu^2\xi^2}
 \begin{pmatrix}\xi^2+k^2&2\mu\xi\\
               -2\mu\xi&\xi^2+k^2+m^2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
It is real, but not symmetric at nonzero xi:
\(G(-\xi,k)=G(\xi,k)^T\). Replacing it with a symmetric scalar bubble
would lose the amplitude/phase mixing.

Using the parent positive-frequency residues R_a and energies E_a gives
the direct spectral continuation
\[
 G(\xi,k)=\sum_a\left[
 {R_a(k)\over E_a(k)-i\xi}
 +{R_a(k)^T\over E_a(k)+i\xi}\right].
\]
This follows by Fourier transforming the positive/negative imaginary-time
exponentials; no blind chemical-potential contour shift is required.
For any frequency-independent 2x2 insertion F at fixed k>0,
\[
 \boxed{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{d\xi\over2\pi}G(\xi,k)F G(\xi,k)
  =\sum_{ab}{R_a F R_b^T+R_a^T F R_b\over E_a+E_b}.}
\]
Same-half-plane terms integrate to zero; opposite-half-plane terms give
1/(Ea+Eb). The identity was checked by independent high-precision
integration for every matrix-unit insertion at two momenta.

The identity is a normalization check, NOT permission to integrate the
two propagator lines first in the actual ladder: the exchange matrix
depends on the relative-frequency difference and must remain inside the
convolution. A real-frequency collective pole requires a separate
continuation with the appropriate cuts; no such search was run here.

## 3. Deriving the sign and factor instead of guessing them

Take an inverse-propagator perturbation F:
\[
 \delta G=-G F G .
\]
With Euclidean interaction \(+C_{abc}\eta_a\eta_b\eta_c/3!\), the
leading cubic self energy in inverse-propagator convention is
\[
 \Sigma_{ab}(p)=-{1\over2}C_{acd}C_{bef}
 \int{d^4k\over(2\pi)^4}G_{ce}(k)G_{df}(p-k).
\]
The sign also follows from the negative quadratic term in
one-half Tr log of the background-dependent Hessian. Differentiating
both lines and substituting deltaG gives two equal exchange terms;
their factor2 cancels the identical-particle one-half. Before imposing
a numerical cutoff, the resulting map is
\[
 \boxed{({\cal L}F)_{ab}(p)=
 \int{d^4k\over(2\pi)^4}
 C_{acd}[G(k)F(k)G(k)]_{ce}C_{bef}G_{df}(p-k).}
\]
The inhomogeneous vertex equation is \(F=J+{\cal L}F\).
This is the same even-l exchange sum obtained by retaining t and u
with their one-half factor and then using Bose reflection.
It is not an arbitrary rescaling of the parent scattering amplitude.
A finite cyclic-convolution fixture checks the two-term cancellation
exactly, including nonsymmetric field matrices.

A cutoff applied to the resulting pair equation is a declared numerical
regulator, not a proof of a regulator-consistent self-energy truncation.
In particular, shifting an integration variable through an asymmetric
hard cutoff would generate boundary terms. No symmetry-preserving
renormalization claim is based on silently dropping those terms.

## 4. Pair space and angular normalization

At zero total momentum the pair insertion obeys
\[
 F_{ab}(-\xi,\mathbf{-k})=F_{ba}(\xi,\mathbf k).
\]
For even l=2, the spatial sign disappears. Use the orthonormal real basis
\[
 B_1=E_{11},\ B_2=E_{22},\
 B_3=(E_{12}+E_{21})/\sqrt2,\
 B_4=(E_{12}-E_{21})/\sqrt2.
\]
Their frequency-reflection parities are (1,1,1,-1). The fourth,
antisymmetric field component is ODD in relative frequency and is
retained; Bose symmetry does not authorize discarding it.

For \(G_2={1\over2}\int_{-1}^1du\,P_2(u)G(\xi-\zeta,|\mathbf p-\mathbf k|)\),
the Funk-Hecke angular integral gives 4pi G2, hence
\[
 {4\pi\over(2\pi)^4}={1\over4\pi^3}.
\]
There is no remaining one-half after the exchange reduction.

Parametrize \(F(\xi,\mathbf k)=k^2h(\xi,k)Y_{2m}(\hat{\mathbf k})\)
for k>0. Then
\[
 ({\cal L}_h h)_{ab}(\xi,p)
 ={1\over4\pi^3p^2}\int d\zeta\int dk\,k^4
 C_{acd}[G(\zeta,k)h(\zeta,k)G(\zeta,k)]_{ce}
 C_{bef}[G_2(\xi-\zeta;p,k)]_{df}.
\]
On the positive-frequency half-line add the negative-frequency
contribution with h transposed and G(-zeta); this is implemented
explicitly, not replaced by twice the positive-frequency integrand.

The k² factor represents the bare tensor stress insertion conveniently.
It is not an assumption that h remains uniformly bounded as p,xi go to
zero. Nonanalytic Goldstone contributions may make that particular
norm a poor continuum norm.

## 5. Treating the phase logarithm analytically

The parent closed form gives \(G_2(\eta;p,k)=-\Delta_2(i\eta;p,k)\).
Near coincidence eta=0,k=p>0 its singular entry is
\[
 [G_2]_{\phi\phi}
 ={1\over4p^2}\log{4p^2\over(k-p)^2+\eta^2/c_s^2}
 -{3\over4p^2}+o(1).
\]
This follows either from the small-transfer phase propagator
\(G_{\phi\phi}\sim1/(Q^2+\eta^2/c_s^2)\), or the parent Q2 expression.
Other entries do not contain an unsuppressed logarithm at coincidence.

For a centered integration cell of radial half-width a and frequency
half-width b, put B=b/cs. Its logarithm integrates exactly:
\[
 I_{\log}=4c_s\left[
 aB\log{4p^2\over a^2+B^2}+3aB
       -a^2\arctan(B/a)-B^2\arctan(a/B)\right].
\]
Mixed differentiation and a zero-width limit check the primitive;
independent one-dimensional high-precision integrals check it numerically.

In the h operator the singular coefficient, before 1/(4pi³), is
\[
 S(H)={1\over4}C_\phi G(\xi,p)H G(\xi,p)C_\phi^T,\qquad
 (C_\phi)_{ac}=C_{ac\phi}.
\]
Subtract S times the logarithm from the self-cell integrand, integrate
the finite remainder, and add S times Ilog. No small diagonal epsilon,
ad hoc phase mass, or discarded self-cell is used.

Two full 4x4 self-cell fixtures were refined from order4 to order256.
At order128 the subtracted results differ from order256 by
1.33e-8 and8.94e-9 relatively. Plain integration at order128 differs by
1.05e-4 and8.54e-5. Thus the analytic treatment materially improves
convergence. These comparisons are numerical, not interval certificates.

## 6. Actual regulated operator and independently varied resolutions

The unknown h is piecewise constant on cells in positive relative
frequency and radial momentum; its reflected matrix supplies negative
frequencies. Collocate at cell centers and integrate G, the k weights
and the exchange inside each cell rather than freezing them at centers.

The first eleven runs use frequency/momentum cutoffs 2,4,8 times mu,
4 through12 cells per positive axis, and cell quadrature orders4,6,8.
Changing the cutoff with a fixed number of cells also changes infrared
resolution, so those runs alone cannot diagnose a cutoff effect.

A second five-run pass fixes the complete low region0..2:
ten cells have edges \(2(j/10)^{3/2}\). Extend to4 with edges
2.5,3,3.5,4 and to8 with5,6,7,8. Both relative-frequency and radial
grids use these edges. The first center stays .0316227766016838.

The spectral-radius eigenvalue is negative in these runs; it must not

eigenvalue is positive and is the relevant displayed comparator to+1.

For any finite matrix L and strictly positive weights w,
\[
 \rho(L)\leq\|L\|_{\infty,w}
 \leq\max_i{(|L|w)_i\over w_i}.
\]
Saved positive weights give the last column; the inequality does not
require that they be exact Perron eigenvectors. The evaluated bounds
apply to the saved floating-point FINITE matrices, not to an unbounded
continuum operator. Large margins do not erase discretization scope.

The naive h infinity norm grows under infrared refinement
(.00359 at inner10/cut4 to.00941 at inner14/cut4/order8).
This is not hidden as a successful continuum norm test. The positive-weight
bounds are far more stable, but identifying an admissible continuum
weight is a separate derivation.

Cutoff extension at fixed infrared grid changes the largest positive
eigenvalue by about .104% from2 to8. Infrared refinement10 to14 changes
it by about4.77% at cutoff4/order6, while order6 to8 at the finer grid
changes it by about.032%. The comparison is therefore not being sold
as a high-precision continuum eigenvalue.

Zero cubic strength yields the zero operator. Doubling epsilon at fixed
mu,y doubles this bare operator to numerical precision, as the two
cubic vertices require. Extrapolating that linear law until an eigenvalue
reaches one would leave the tested weak regime and is NOT a prediction.

The unit stress-source vertex equation (I-L)h=h0 was also solved in all
eleven first-pass runs with h0=(1,1,0,0). This is a normalized vertex
diagnostic, not an observable stress susceptibility or a pole residue.

## 7. Scope of the negative result and the constructive next route

Within this bare leading-exchange, finite-cutoff STATIC calculation,
no tested matrix approaches a unit eigenvalue. Merely retaining the
virtual phase exchange does not create a static collective onset in
the chosen weak-coupling example. This is stronger information than
the preceding on-shell Born amplitude alone.

It does NOT exclude finite-energy resonances, other states, a nonuniform
continuum limit, or nonperturbative collective behavior. It does not
fix or replace the original framework. We have not inserted a freely
adjustable tensor coupling to force an onset.

The candidate supplies useful exact symmetry constraints on the next
step. For the radial grand potential
\(\Omega=V(\sqrt{(\varrho_0+\sigma)^2+\phi^2})-\mu^2
[(\varrho_0+\sigma)^2+\phi^2]/2\), U(1) gives
\[
 \Omega_{\phi\phi}={\Omega_\sigma\over\varrho_0},\qquad
 \Omega_{\sigma\phi\phi}
  ={\Omega_{\sigma\sigma}\over\varrho_0}
   -{\Omega_\sigma\over\varrho_0^2}.
\]
At a stationary state, therefore,
\[
 \Gamma_{\phi\phi}(0)=0,\quad
 C_{122}={m^2\over\varrho_0},\quad
 D_{2222}={3m^2\over\varrho_0^2},\quad
 D_{1122}={C_{111}-2C_{122}\over\varrho_0}.
\]
These identities also constrain a symmetry-preserving effective potential
where the regulated derivatives exist; tree identities alone do not
prove that all infrared loop derivatives exist.

On the preferred tree branch,
\[
 {2\epsilon\over3}<{C_{122}^2\over m^2}<2\epsilon,\qquad
 3<{C_{111}\over C_{122}}<{13\over3}.
\]
The soft exchange strength cannot be varied independently of stiffness,
state amplitude and the other vertices while claiming the same action.

NEXT: use these identities to derive the infrared behavior and a consistent
weight for the pair equation, including the background/tadpole and
Goldstone constraint in the same loop truncation. Check the tensor-source
Ward identity with regulator boundary terms accounted for. Then determine
whether the weak branch admits a continuum bound, before spending effort
on real-frequency continuation or proposing a distinct strongly collective
state. This is a concrete calculation target, not license to tune a new
coupling or infer a pole from a cutoff artifact.
