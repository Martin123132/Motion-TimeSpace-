# Material representation for the C0 cancellation test

The initial reference-fine complete Eulerian result differs between
rules by 3.64292e-17, just outside the unchanged2e-17 gate. Its material-face
term has magnitude0.01329 while the final force column is6.43e-7. This
motivates testing the equivalent representation with smaller cancelling
terms; it does not establish the cause by itself.

Keep the original Eulerian result unchanged. For the new candidate,
freeze the material coordinate in the complete moving packet, vary the
physical width and radius, and integrate delta L+U_R L. Add only
the +U[L] atoms at fixed radial polynomial cuts. Outer motion is already
in the material map and measure. No Eulerian material-face/outer terms
are added to this representation.

For the represented functions, delta Y=P+(derivative of the stored
primitive)U. The stored radial coefficient has variation P_R+Y_RR U,
where Y_RR differentiates its own stored radial polynomial. Likewise
the time-response polynomial is differentiated as stored. The gap between
primitive derivatives and stored radial coefficients is measured, not
silently set to zero. This distinguishes differentiation of the actual
represented functions from an assumed exact radial identity.

Evaluate both one-sided metric and metric-time traces in70-digit Decimal
arithmetic. Form the complete four density rows on each side, subtract
before converting to binary64, and integrate the small resulting jumps.
This preserves nonlinear trace jumps instead of replacing them with a
first-order estimate or subtracting two already-rounded large densities.
Material kinematic inputs remain the inherited represented inputs.

Independent tests compare the new packet derivative against the earlier
explicit material product rule. Tiny1e-25 trace jumps must survive the
Decimal evaluation and must be lost by a deliberately rounded control.
The earlier independently rebuilt moving-family and cut-sign tests remain
required. No new physical term or tolerance is introduced.

The bounded fine-grid candidate first uses wave24/28,32/36, requiring its
paired comparison to satisfy2e-17. A16/20 rule can supply a second pair
later; a two-rule run does not claim both pairs. Reuse complete original native/source event
terms, matter48/64 and gravity24/32; do not double-count their corrections.
The third-grid repair and reaction-sensitivity propagation remain separate
until this numerical comparison has established the selected method.

The original MTS attempt also exposed a Decimal zero-array type error
after both fine wave rules were saved. Version2 fixes only that zero
array and reuses both wave archives. The failed attempt remains preserved.
