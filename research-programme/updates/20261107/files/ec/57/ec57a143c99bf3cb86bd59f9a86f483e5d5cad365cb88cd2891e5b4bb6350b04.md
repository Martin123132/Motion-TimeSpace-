## A precise distinction, without overreacting to tiny tails

Density weighting changes a Gaussian relative-velocity function into a
Gaussian times a quadratic. We derived its exact nonnegativity condition:
for B=1+xi_g>0, S>0 and C nonzero, it is nonnegative everywhere exactly when
xi_g>=C^2/S. The C=0 and S=0 limits are handled separately.

Each physical branch has30 rows whose formal inverse transform is signed.
This is a limitation shared with the controls, not an MTS-specific instability.
All saved covariance matrices are positive semidefinite; pair normalization
is positive and central variances are nonnegative. Those checks alone do
not establish a globally nonnegative distribution.

Crucially, in the tested rows the negative tails are extraordinarily remote:
the largest integrated-negative-weight upper bound is below2.2e-70. The
largest directly represented negative mass is4.41e-71 for the heavy SGC
case at80 Mpc/h. At more remote tails, floating-point mass underflows;
the logarithmic bound and analytic sign test remain saved. This is not an
observational exclusion or a reason to discard the derived infall curves.
It is a reason not to call the Gaussian linear tracer construction an exact
physical probability law. The bound applies to these sampled rows and
closures, not arbitrary biases, separations or nonlinear fields.

A deliberately constructed valid Gaussian covariance gives a much larger
3.49% negative weight, and the verifier detects it. That example is synthetic,
not an MTS prediction. No negative tail was clipped or renormalized away.

## Which additional physics matters

The derivation now isolates the exact missing contributions beyond Gaussian
pair weighting: a mixed density-density-velocity third cumulant for the
mean, and two density-velocity-velocity third cumulants plus the connected
density-density-velocity-velocity fourth cumulant for the raw second moment.
Their coefficients and disconnected subtractions are derived explicitly.
No physical value for these higher correlations is inferred from a
two-point spectrum alone.

This matters more for predictive accuracy than the minuscule negative tails.
The next calculation should obtain the source density from conserved flow
and work toward the parent nonlinear correlations, rather than choosing a
new damping/PDF shape just because it fits the survey. Keep all three
branches on the same approximation and numerical rules.
