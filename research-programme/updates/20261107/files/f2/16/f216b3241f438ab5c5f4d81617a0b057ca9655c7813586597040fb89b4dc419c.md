gravitomagnetic quadrupole response**. We derive its equation from the

not electromagnetic charge or an electromagnetic magnetic moment.

## 2. Derive the matter response before choosing an equation

Use the existing normalized scalar action

    Lmatter = -(partial h)^2/2 - h²(partial theta)^2/2 - W(h),
    W(h) = 3 h^(4/3)/8 + h^4/8,
    theta = -omega*time,
    ds² = -N² dt² + dr²/F + r² dOmega²,
    F = 1-2alpha*m(r)/r, N=exp(nu), alpha=4pi G_R vstar².

The background sources are

    rho = [F h'² + omega² h²/N²]/2 + W,
    p_r = [F h'² + omega² h²/N²]/2 - W,
    p_t = [-F h'² + omega² h²/N²]/2 - W.

An odd-parity perturbation is delta g_tA=q(r) S_A, with transverse axial
vector harmonic S_A. There is no scalar harmonic of this parity on the
spherical scalar background. Thus delta h=delta theta=0 in this sector.
The induced inverse metric is off-diagonal, delta sqrt(-g)=0, and the
scalar equation's putative angular source vanishes because D_A S^A=0.
No extra vortical matter variable is added.

Importantly, this does **not** mean zero matter or metric response:

    delta T_tA = p_t delta g_tA,
    delta(T_tA - g_tA T/2) = W delta g_tA,
    beta^A = gamma^AB delta g_tB,
    delta j^A = -beta^A j^t.

The angular momentum density measured by the slice-normal observer is
zero: T_tA-beta^B T_BA=0. Its coordinate current follows the shift.
This distinguishes the derived scalar state from a fluid artificially
kept at zero coordinate velocity.

## 3. Two derivations of the same axial equation

The stationary extrinsic curvature is
Kij=(D_i beta_j+D_j beta_i)/(2N), with trace K=0.
The scalar gives no Eulerian angular momentum source, so D_j K^j_A=0.
Expanding this constraint gives, for multipole ell>=2,

    q'' - A q' + [2A/r - 2/r² - (ell(ell+1)-2)/(r² F)] q = 0,
    A = nu' - F'/(2F) = alpha*r*(rho+p_r)/F.

The independent four-dimensional check starts with the perturbed
connection, builds delta R_tA and verifies
delta R_tA=2alpha W delta g_tA with the same equation. Remaining Ricci
components vanish for this stationary odd-parity ansatz. Explicit angular
ADM checks use ell=2 and3. See `results/derivation/status.json`.

This independently reproduces the minimally coupled boson-star form in
Cardoso et al., EqC13, after Gamma=2nu and Lambda_g=-ln F:
https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.01116 ,
https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.95.084014 .
Their smooth scalar-tail boundary conditions are NOT transferred to our
compact-support potential.

### Static uniqueness

Set f=q/r². The equation becomes

    (p f')' - V_l f = 0,
    p = r^4 sqrt(F)/N > 0,
    V_l = [ell(ell+1)-2] r²/(N sqrt(F)) > 0.

For an everywhere regular horizonless body with N,F>0, a solution with
no applied growing tide is regular at the center and decays at infinity.
The boundary term [p f f'] then vanishes at both ends. Integration gives

    integral [p f'² + V_l f²] dr = 0.

Hence that homogeneous static solution is zero. The stated boundary
value problem has no freely adjustable regular decaying zero mode.
This is static uniqueness, not a new time-dependent stability theorem.

## 4. Center, compact edge and vacuum basis

The quadrupole's regular center expansion is

    q = r³ [1 + alpha*(11rho0/3+p_r0)*r²/14 + O(r^4)].

At the existing compact edge r=R, h=c_e(R-r)^3+... with
c_e=(12F_s)^(-3/2). The density starts as
(R-r)^4/(192F_s²), while the leading order4 radial pressure cancels.
There is no odd-parity radial scalar displacement and no linear thin shell.
The metric coefficients are regular there, giving continuous q and q'.
The numerical background uses the previously derived compact edge, not an
exponential scalar tail.

Outside, with M=alpha*m(R), the equation is

    q'' - (6r-4M)/(r²(r-2M))*q = 0.

Choose asymptotically normalized solutions

    P = r³(1-2M/r),
    Q = r^-2 * 2F1(1,4;6;2M/r),
    Wronskian(P,Q) = -5,
    q = a P + b Q,  response R_B = b/a.

R_B has units length^5. The exterior coefficient is independent of the
extraction radius. At zero coupling the regular solution is r³ and R_B=0.
The horizon-regular Schwarzschild solution is P: the Q contribution makes
q/F singular in an ingoing chart and is excluded, giving the BH control0.

## 5. A weak-gravity response law, not a fitted sign

Expand q=r³(1+alpha*y) and use the flat fixed-charge body at alpha=0.
The axial equation gives

    (r^6 y')' = r^6(rho+p_r) + 8r³m,
    m'=r²rho.

Outside y=constant-2m(R)/r+(R_B/alpha)/r^5. Integrating the interior

## 6. Match the current convention explicitly

Use the Cartesian current moment
Sij=STF integral epsilon_ab i x_j x_a j_b d³x.
The retained weak-field source conventions are

    g_0i = -4U_i,
    U_i(current) = (G_R/3) epsilon_ijk S_jl partial_kl(1/r),
    B_ij = (1/2) epsilon_kl(i partial_j)k g_0l
           in the stationary body frame.

These are the conventions of Banihashemi and Vines, Eqs20,23,25:
https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07266 ,
https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.101.064003 .
The distributional source is checked to reproduce our integral definition
of Sij. For aligned STF tensors B_ij=diag(-B/2,-B/2,B) and
Sij=diag(-S/2,-S/2,S), the actual angular metric terms are

    g_tphi(applied) = B r³ sin²theta cos(theta),
    g_tphi(induced) = -6 G_R S r^-2 sin²theta cos(theta).

Consequently

    Sij = sigma_bar Bij,
    sigma_bar_geom = G_R*sigma_bar = -R_B/6,
    sigma_BV_geom = sigma_bar_geom/2 = -R_B/12,

where Banihashemi-Vines Eq33 uses Sij=2sigma_BV Bij with c=1.
The matched local quadratic term is

    L_B = sigma_bar_geom B_ij B_ij/(3G_R).

It follows by eliminating S from (2/3)S:B-S:S/(3sigma_bar), including the
half-interaction factor. This is a matched static effective coefficient:
the auxiliary quadratic representation is not independently a claim of a
healthy free oscillator. The negative susceptibility is not, by itself,
a dynamical-instability test.

Other common dimensionless conventions are different:

    Cardoso k2B = -R_B/(3M^5) = 2 sigma_bar_geom/M^5,
    Landry-Poisson K2mag = -R_B/(96M^5).

The latter follows their Eq5.8:
https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.06606 ,
https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.91.104026 .
The initial numerical label incorrectly equated sigma_bar_geom/M^5 to
Cardoso k2B, missing the S_l/l factor in their EqB10. An independent
published-formula check detected the exact factor-two mismatch.
`results/validation/FAILED.md` preserves it. Authoritative v2 labels
separate all conventions; R_B, sigma_bar, the ODE and gates are unchanged.
