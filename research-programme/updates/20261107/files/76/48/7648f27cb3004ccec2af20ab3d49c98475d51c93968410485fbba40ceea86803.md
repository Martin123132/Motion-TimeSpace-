# Stage59 — pair weighting derived from the retained fields

## Scope and ancestry

This is an observable/source-mapping extension of the existing MTS branch.
The native action/evolution, Stage56 density and velocity transfers, field
fraction, masses, primordial spectrum and background remain unchanged.
The calculation uses the equal-time plane-parallel, subhorizon tracer model
already used for Stage57/58, not an exact relativistic survey observable.

We derive and evaluate a known Gaussian-field construction rather than claim
that Gaussianity follows from the full nonlinear MTS equations. The established
generating-function and cumulant identities can be checked against
[Scoccimarro (2004), Eqs.32,44,45](https://arxiv.org/pdf/astro-ph/0407214v2).
Our contribution here is its explicit use with the retained scale-dependent
MTS/control transfers, numerical predictions, positivity domain and exact
identification of the correlators needed to go beyond it. This is not a new
discovery of the streaming identity. The saved PDF pages9 and12 were inspected.

## Definitions and field integrals

Let delta be the inherited cb density field, v its peculiar velocity and
u=v/(aH). Fourier conventions are delta(x)=integral exp(ik.x)delta(k)/(2pi)^3
and u(k)=i*k*T_v(k)*R(k)/k^2. Thus minus the normalized velocity divergence
has transfer T_v; a positive cross spectrum gives infall, not outflow.
For a separation r=x2-x1 and LOS n define

\[
 d_1=b\delta(\mathbf x_1),\quad d_2=b\delta(\mathbf x_2),\quad
 U=(\mathbf u_2-\mathbf u_1)\cdot\mathbf n,
\]
\[
 \xi_g=\langle d_1d_2\rangle,\quad B=1+\xi_g,\quad
 C=\langle d_1U\rangle=\langle d_2U\rangle,\quad S=\langle U^2\rangle.
\]

C is a density-displacement cross covariance, not a fundamental coupling or
the speed of light. Its units are length; S has units length squared.
Homogeneity, isotropy and potential flow give the equal cross covariances.
With nu=rhat.n and P_ab=P_R*T_a*T_b,

\[
 \xi_g(r)={b^2\over2\pi^2}\int dq\,q^2P_{dd}(q)j_0(qr),
\]
\[
 C(r,\nu)=-{b\nu\over2\pi^2}\int dq\,qP_{dv}(q)j_1(qr),
\]
\[
 S(r,\nu)={1\over3\pi^2}\int dq\,P_{vv}(q)
 [1-j_0(qr)+2j_2(qr){\cal L}_2(\nu)].
\]

These two-point relations require the inherited two-point spectra, not a
Gaussian assumption. The exact finite-amplitude pair generating function
below does require that assumption. At r=0 the relative displacement is zero.
For the numerical units used here r_physical=r_h/h, C_h=h*C_physical and
S_h=h^2*S_physical. Velocity in km/s is (aH/h)*U_h. Every integral is over
the explicit retained physical band1e-5..5 Mpc^-1 at z=.38.

## The weighted generating function

Assume (d1,d2,U) are zero-mean jointly Gaussian with the covariances above.
Let D=Var(d1)=Var(d2). Their joint moment generator is

\[
 J(j_1,j_2,\lambda)=\exp\{[D(j_1^2+j_2^2)+2\xi_gj_1j_2
 +2C\lambda(j_1+j_2)+S\lambda^2]/2\}.
\]

Act with (1+partial_j1)(1+partial_j2) and set j1=j2=0. This derives

\[
 Z(\lambda)=\langle(1+d_1)(1+d_2)e^{\lambda U}\rangle
 =e^{S\lambda^2/2}(B+2C\lambda+C^2\lambda^2).
\]

For B>0, the normalized generator is M=Z/B. Differentiating log M gives

\[
 m={2C\over B},\qquad
 \langle U^2\rangle_w=S+{2C^2\over B},\qquad
 V_w=S+{2C^2\over B}-{4C^2\over B^2},
\]
\[
 \kappa_{3,w}={4C^3(1-3\xi_g)\over B^3},\qquad
 \kappa_{4,w}=-{12C^4(1-6\xi_g+\xi_g^2)\over B^4}.
\]

Thus Gaussian underlying fields generally do not produce a Gaussian
density-weighted relative-velocity function. The distinction is derived by
the density weighting; it is not an extra fitted shape parameter. The
one-point density variance cancels from this formula but still controls
whether the underlying linear tracer density can be interpreted physically.

## Positivity is an explicit theorem, not a numerical clip

For S>0, invert M(it). With z=U/sqrt(S), alpha=C/sqrt(S), and the unit normal
density phi, the normalized inverse transform is

\[
 p_w(U)={\phi(z)\over\sqrt S}
 \left[1+{2\alpha z\over B}+{\alpha^2(z^2-1)\over B}\right].
\]

For C nonzero its bracket equals

\[
 {\alpha^2\over B}(z+1/\alpha)^2+{\xi_g-\alpha^2\over B}.
\]

Consequently, given B>0 and S>0,

\[
 p_w\ge0\ \hbox{everywhere}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \xi_g\ge C^2/S\qquad(C\ne0).
\]

If C=0 the normalized function is simply Gaussian regardless of xi_g as
long as B>0. If S=0, physical covariance requires C=0 and the distribution
is a point mass at U=0. B<=0 does not define a positive pair normalization.
The b=0/C=0 case cannot be inferred by taking the quadratic's vertex limit.

When xi_g<C^2/S, the negative interval is

\[
 z_\pm=-1/\alpha\ \pm {\sqrt{\alpha^2-\xi_g}\over|\alpha|}.
\]

This is a signed weight, not a probability distribution. The origin is that
the Gaussian linear contrast permits 1+d<0. A positive covariance matrix,
positive B and positive central variance alone do not cure that problem.

We preserve the negative part and separately bound its integrated magnitude:

\[
 0<N_-\le {\alpha^2-\xi_g\over B}
 [\Phi(z_+)-\Phi(z_-)].
\]

The code evaluates this bound in logarithmic form, so floating-point zero
mass in a remote tail is not mistaken for exact nonnegativity. Strict
positivity failure must also not be exaggerated into an observable failure:
the bound is tiny in the actual saved benchmark rows. No clipping or
renormalization is performed.

## Linear Kaiser recovery

Scale the power spectra by epsilon; then xi_g,C,S each scale by epsilon.
The unnormalized generator expands to

\[
 Z(\lambda)-1=\epsilon(\xi_g+2\lambda C+\lambda^2S/2)+O(\epsilon^2).
\]

Use lambda=-i*k_parallel in the Fourier pair map. The transforms of C and
the displacement covariance are i*b*k_parallel*P_dv/k^2 and
k_parallel^2*P_vv/k^4. S=2*sigma_u^2-2*psi_parallel; its constant first term
is a zero mode and must not become an extra finite-k damping contribution.
For nonzero k the result is

\[
 P_g^s(k,\mu)=b^2P_{dd}+2b\mu^2P_{dv}+\mu^4P_{vv}+O(P^2).
\]

This recovers the retained Stage57 coherent spectrum without a new growth
normalization. Independent deterministic number-conserving plane-wave maps
check the sign and angular coefficients as amplitude tends to zero. Finite
Gaussian resummation beyond this order is not the full nonlinear dynamics.

## What the actual nonlinear calculation must supply

Do not call the Gaussian truncation exact for the parent. For general
zero-mean fields define C1=<d1 U>, C2=<d2 U>, S=<U^2> and

\[
 T_3=\langle d_1d_2U\rangle_c,\quad
 T_{21}=\langle d_1U^2\rangle_c,\quad
 T_{22}=\langle d_2U^2\rangle_c,\quad
 T_4=\langle d_1d_2U^2\rangle_c.
\]

The exact first two weighted raw moments are

\[
 m={C_1+C_2+T_3\over B},\qquad
 \langle U^2\rangle_w=S+{2C_1C_2+T_{21}+T_{22}+T_4\over B}.
\]

Subtract m^2 for central variance. Here the fourth cumulant excludes the
three disconnected pair contractions, including both C1*C2 terms. If the
same two-point statistics are retained, let dm=T3/B and
dR=(T21+T22+T4)/B. Then the central-variance correction is
dV=dR-2*m_G*dm-dm^2. This identifies the required bispectrum/trispectrum
projections without smuggling their amplitudes into a free constant.
No bound on those physical higher correlators follows from the linear
two-point spectra alone. A synthetic non-Gaussian quadrature example tests
the decomposition but is not a measurement of their MTS values.

Simulation-supported treatments distinguish nonlinear mapping from nonlinear
velocity dynamics; those are not interchangeable corrections. See
[Reid and White (2011)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.4165). Their accuracy claims
are not transferred to the MTS construction or our synthetic checks.

## Constructive route after this calculation

Keep the moments just derived. For a positive source density, use the
continuity/number-conservation law to transport an initially positive measure,
rather than exponentiating or clipping b*delta by fiat. A flow map x(q,t)
pushes n_initial(q)d^3q forward; its integral is conserved and its measure
stays nonnegative. Before crossing, n(x,t)*det(dx/dq)=n_initial(q); after
crossing, all streams must be counted. This is a consequence of transport,
not a new galaxy-bias action.

The next task is to derive that map from the existing, gauge-consistent
parent density/velocity evolution and verify its linear limit for controls
and MTS. A first-order displacement approximation would still require its
own stated domain; it must not be advertised as solving the missing
nonlinear parent correlations. Resolve the higher-ell survey response before
using any finite-amplitude completion for a sharper observational ranking.
