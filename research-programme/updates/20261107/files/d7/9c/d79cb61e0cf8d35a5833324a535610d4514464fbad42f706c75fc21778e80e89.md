# Differentiating the force when an artificial cut moves

Let the material test N(xi) be fixed, and let an artificial internal cut
xi_c(s) move inside a material element. The two represented flux traces
need not be equal. For

    F(s)=-integral_inner^xi_c(s) Phi_minus*N_xi dxi
         -integral_xi_c(s)^outer Phi_plus*N_xi dxi,

Leibniz differentiation gives

    delta F=-sum_cells integral H*N_xi dxi
            +[Phi]*dot(xi_c)*N_xi(xi_c),
    H=partial_s Phi at fixed material coordinate,
    [Phi]=Phi_plus-Phi_minus.

Integrating the regular part by parts then gives the already derived
volume term, [H]*N jumps, and the outer endpoint terms, PLUS the moving-cut
term above. These are distinct terms. For a fixed physical radial cut
and R_s=U, R_xi=J, its material speed is dot(xi_c)=-U/J. If the physical
cut itself has speed c_s, replace this by (c_s-U)/J.

If Phi is genuinely continuous across that cut throughout the family,
the baseline [Phi] term vanishes. It does NOT follow that [H] vanishes:
differentiating trace equality instead gives

    [H]+[Phi_xi]*dot(xi_c)=0.

A continuous family with a changing spatial slope can therefore require
the [H]*N term even while it needs no baseline moving-cut term. The two
cases are checked independently by exact polynomial manufactured families.
This is not yet a trace-continuity certification of the stored MTS metric.

The weak field-force precision diagnostic does not use this integration-
by-parts transformation. Before an actual flux-form implementation replaces
it, test the represented metric traces and retain any nonzero moving-cut
term; do not assume numerical traces vanish just because the continuum
equations require continuity.
