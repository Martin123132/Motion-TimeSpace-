# Derived old-field reaction and the combined local response

## What moved forward

The missing response of the existing fields has now been derived and
solved, not prescribed. It satisfies the independently recalculated old
field/source Euler equations on both reference and MTS snapshots at both
resolutions. We then inserted it into the actual support force and all
three inherited weak-current conditions.

The combined result is informative but mixed. The MTS velocity-current
column is stable to about1.5% between these two grids. However, holding
position fixed and balancing force with velocity/acceleration does not
satisfy all four conditions within the recorded sampled uncertainties.
The finer MTS case requires a much larger acceleration coefficient and
leaves worse current residuals. The matched reference also fails this
restricted test, but less severely. This identifies the limitation of
the tested two-column construction, not a rejection of MTS as a whole or
of continuum GR.

Everything is private. No GitHub operation, subagent, empirical refit,
new evolved trajectory or original-workbench edit was made.

## 1. The reaction actually solved

Using the existing radial metric constraints inside the old momentum and
force definitions, calculate their position and velocity derivatives:

    Ppos = D_position p_hat[e,eta],
    Fvel = D_velocity F_hat[e,eta],
    L_live = M + P_metric A_old.

The constructive solution is

    b1 = L_live^(-1)(Ppos-Fvel),
    additional old acceleration = -edot-b1,
    total old acceleration variation = edot-b1,
    corrected metric-time variation = X+Vdot-A_old(b1).

This does not postulate a new reduced action, a support inertia, or a
screening coefficient. It uses the old equations and their live metric
feedback. Native field/edge adjoints, the source-clock terms, moving
material measure and the changing source momentum basis are included.

All figures here are code-normalized quantities for the specified modes,
not SI bounds or measured astrophysical accelerations. The Euler error
below is the maximum of the residual after the inherited fixed-metric
inverse; it is not a universal physical error norm.

At zero position amplitude the tested system is

    residual_base + C1*sdot + C2*sddot = 0,

with four rows: the support force and the three weak currents for
h=1,(R-6),(R-6)^2. C1 includes the newly solved reaction, instantaneous
metric change and corrected metric-time change. C2 is the existing
acceleration channel, re-evaluated in its stable separated form.

## 3. Numerical correction and retained limitations

The first combined attempt stopped when the reference third-grid C2 h0
replay differed by2.1243e-24, outside its unchanged tolerance. A first
algebraic simplification still differed by2.1019e-24. Re-evaluating the
original separated formula reproduced the entire64-label moment array
exactly. Preserving its reference-gradient multiplication/division order
and separate fixed-metric/metric integrals reduced the h0 replay
discrepancy to1.204e-35. That last number measures reproduction of the
same discrete expression, not physical accuracy to35decimal places.

The main v2 run reuses the two completed reference baseline/C1 arrays
after hash verification, recomputes their C2 consistently, and calculates
both MTS cases with the same algorithm. Failed attempts remain unchanged.
Raw complex C2 replay flags remain false for both third-grid cases; their
differences are retained in the sampled uncertainty, not relabelled as
passes. The aligned C2 comparison passes the original tolerance.

Four inherited position-density relative warnings and four inherited
mixed-time off-grid warnings remain. The independent oracle propagation
does not erase them. Sampled quadrature/algorithm differences are not a
total error bound, and three weak moments do not establish the full
spatial conservation law. Large and grid-sensitive response amplitudes
make a finite-history check essential before using them physically.

## 4. Next constructive calculation: the position column

Reuse X, edot and the solved C1/C2 rather than redo them. Derive the
missing position-dependent force/current column C0 from the same action
and radial constraints. A useful formulation avoids a separate, expensive
derivation of eddot merely to cancel it against an old-field reaction.

Write q,v,a for the old variables, velocities and accelerations, and
s,u for the independent support amplitude and velocity. The support
profile eta is included in the meaning of s. At the background u=0 and
support acceleration=0, let D0 denote the directional derivative
(delta q,delta v,delta s)=(e,edot,1), with a held fixed. Then define

    B0 = D0(Y_q*v + Y_v*a),
    r0 = D0(p_hat,q*v + p_hat,v*a - F_hat),
    k0 = -L_live^(-1) r0.

The instantaneous position metric variation is already
X+A_old(edot). The required total old-acceleration variation is k0, and
the completed metric-time variation is B0+A_old(k0). This is a chain-rule
construction for the next numerical solve, not a claim that B0, r0 or C0
have been numerically evaluated here. It incorporates the forced total
acceleration directly rather than presuming a transported trial history
already obeys the old equations.

Once C0 is calculated and checked, test

    residual_base + C0*s + C1*sdot + C2*sddot = 0

on the same four controls, without guessed coefficients. If a compatible
and controlled candidate exists, test its finite-history consistency;
otherwise the residual tells us which existing equation needs further
construction. This is the next stage toward the local gravity limit.
