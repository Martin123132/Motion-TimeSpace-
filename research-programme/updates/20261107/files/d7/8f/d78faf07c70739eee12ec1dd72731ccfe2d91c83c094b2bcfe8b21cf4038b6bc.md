## What is actually available in the action?

Stage49 diagonalizes the retained positive real bath in the same metric as ordinary matter. Its quadratic subfamily has action densities \(-[(\partial h)^2+m_h^2 h^2]/2\) and \(-[(\partial l)^2+\mu^2 l^2]/2\) for the fast and rolling modes respectively. The same orthogonal change of variables diagonalizes the Hilbert stress: the Einstein equation therefore sources the metric with their sum, not with an adjustable galaxy coupling. On shell each quadratic mode is covariantly conserved; interactions omitted in this subfamily can exchange energy. For a two-pole example, writing \(s\) for the fast pole's residue in the old motion coordinate, \(m_b^2=(1-s)\mu^2+s m_h^2\). Pole matching fixes a relation, not a populated state: even zero motion-coordinate residue does not cancel a populated bath mode's metric stress. The normalization of \(G\) is the retained one, not a new derivation of its value.

In a weak, bound potential, restoring ordinary units, the envelope and Newtonian metric obey
\[
 i\hbar\partial_t\psi=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m_h}\nabla^2\psi+m_h\Phi\psi,\qquad
 \nabla^2\Phi=4\pi G(\rho_h+\rho_b),\qquad \rho_h=m_h\langle|\psi|^2\rangle.
\]
Here \(m_h\) is a mass, the stored eV value is \(m_h c^2\). This is the low-velocity, small-binding limit; the real-field change of variables is not a new fundamental complex field. See `condensate-origin-20261003/motion-bath-matching-20261006/49-real-motion-envelope-and-existing-bath-matching.md`; the effective real-field method is established [Namjoo, Guth & Kaiser](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.00445).

**The flux obstruction has a specific escape.** A real standing wave \(h=A(r)\cos(\omega t+\theta)\) with constant spatial phase has \(\langle T^r{}_t\rangle=0\) while \(\langle p_r-p_t\rangle=B(A')^2/2\) on a spherical static mean metric with inverse radial component \(B\). It need not have zero instantaneous flux or pressure. Unlike the earlier rolling ansatz, this is a second *existing* fast normal mode; the rolling light mode need not acquire a static radial gradient. Localization must be solved, not postulated. Nonrelativistic gradient stresses are of kinetic order; this does not supply the negative, order-rest-density radial pressure demanded by the earlier half-occupation flat-interface construction.

## Solving the wave and its own Newtonian potential together

Let \(M\) be total wave mass, \(a_B=\hbar^2/(GMm_h^2)\), \(v_B=GMm_h/\hbar\). In \(a_B,v_B\) units, for a normalized reduced radial function \(u\),
\[
 [-\tfrac12\partial_r^2+\ell(\ell+1)/(2r^2)+\Phi]u=Eu,\quad
 \mathcal M(r)=\int_0^r u^2 ds,\quad
 \Phi=-\mathcal M(r)/r-\int_r^\infty u(s)^2 ds/s.
\]
Regular origin, negative ground eigenvalue, no confining wall in the physical model. Solve the lowest radial state for each angular sector, with \(\int u^2=1\). The \(\ell=0\) standing state needs one real mode. The \(\ell=1\) spherical source requires an angularly balanced *mixed* state. A coherent amplitude \(\mathbf d\cdot\mathbf n\) contributes density \(\mathbf n^T\mathrm{Re}(\mathbf d\mathbf d^\dagger)\mathbf n\); its real rank is at most two, not three. A single coherent dipole cannot give this spherical nonzero density. Averaging rapidly over the carrier does not erase cross terms between degenerate magnetic substates. Use a density matrix diagonal in equally occupied substates, or justify independent carriers. The earlier multi-field existence/stability theorem applies within its stated symmetry class, not to formation or stability of the full MTS bath [Chávez Nambo & Sarbach](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27529).

The ground states were obtained by a tridiagonal eigenvalue solve and iteration of the spherical Newton potential. The independent virial identity is \(2T+W=0,\; W=\int\Phi\,d\mathcal M/2\). On the finest, extended box:

## Can baryons deepen the well enough? A necessary bound without prescribing its shape

Let \(R_{\ell}=r^\ell y(r)\) for a nodeless bound state in **any spherical, finite-centre, nondecreasing** Newtonian potential with \(\Phi(\infty)=0\). Here \(\epsilon<0\) is specific binding energy and \(v_e=\sqrt{-2\Phi(0)}\). The radial equation is
\[
 (r^{2\ell+2}y')'=\frac{2m_h^2}{\hbar^2}\,r^{2\ell+2}(\Phi-\epsilon)y .
\]
Regularity, decay and a single sign crossing of \(\Phi-\epsilon\) imply \(y'\le0\): once the derivative of the weighted slope turns nonnegative, the slope itself cannot become positive and still decay at infinity. Before the crossing it is nonpositive by integration from the origin. Since \(0<y\le y(0)\) and \(\Phi-\epsilon\ge-v_e^2/2\), integration twice bounds \(y(r)/y(0)\ge1-(m_h v_e r/\hbar)^2/[2n]\), with \(n=2\ell+3\). Use only its positive part before squaring. This yields, for \(t=(m_h v_e R/\hbar)^2/(2n)\),
\[
 f^{n-1}\le D=\frac{V_h^2(fR)}{V_h^2(R)}
 \le \min\{1/f,\ f^{n-1}/d_n(t)\},\quad
 d_n(t)=
 \begin{cases}
 1-2nt/(n+2)+nt^2/(n+4),&0\le t\le1,\\
 d_n(1)t^{-n/2},&t>1 .
 \end{cases}
\]
The lower bound uses monotonic \(y\), the upper integrates its positive lower envelope for the outer mass and its central maximum for the inner mass. \(1/f\) is simply mass positivity. The bounds use the *total* confining potential, but refer to the *wave component's* force. Therefore extra spherical baryons can deepen the well; they cannot evade this width test at fixed depth. It is a necessary condition, not a sufficient construction.

Freeze one interior datum near \(0.7R\) and vary measured speeds by ±2 quoted errors and stellar M/L over the saved corners; allow independent extremes to **widen**, not shrink, the ratio interval. 51 galaxies have an eligible point. All 51 satisfy the coarse positive-mass inequality under that envelope. For the pure standing state 12 require a central escape speed above \(2 V_{\rm flat}\), 5 above \(4 V_{\rm flat}\). For the mixed dipole the counts are 28 and 23. **DDO154** needs at least 570 km/s for the standing state, against a measured flat speed of 47 km/s; for the dipole the necessary floor is 932 km/s. None exceeds the earlier deliberately generous 1,341 km/s ceiling. Escape speed is not circular speed: these are targets for independent potential measurements, not measured exclusions. No distances, inclinations or disk thickness were marginalized. Six independent regular spherical Bessel-function controls satisfy the bound.

## A local GR consequence that survives the mean-flux cancellation

In an orthonormal freely falling frame the trace of the timelike tidal tensor is \(R_{\hat0\hat0}=4\pi G(\rho+p_r+2p_t)\) in \(c=1\) units (Einstein convention \(R_{00}=\nabla^2\Phi\) in the static Newtonian limit). For the fast real mode \(\rho_h=m_h\langle|\psi|^2\rangle\) while \(p_{\rm osc}=-m_h\mathrm{Re}[\langle\psi^2\rangle e^{-2im_h t}]\) to leading nonrelativistic order. Hence its oscillatory *Ricci* trace amplitude divided by its mean matter trace equals \(3\zeta\), where \(\zeta=|\langle\psi^2\rangle|/\langle|\psi|^2\rangle\le1\). Neither an anomalous pair amplitude nor its phase is implied by a diagonal occupation matrix alone. A coherent standing wave saturates this locally, a phase-balanced ensemble has zero mean line, and a single realization can still fluctuate. The neglected gradient and binding corrections are of relative kinetic order relative to the mean density scale, not necessarily relative to a cancelled line. Oscillatory potential from a real scalar is known [Khmelnitsky & Rubakov](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.5888); the aperture normalization here connects the MTS benchmark to the *same* inferred force, not a second fitted matter coupling.

For a spherical halo, the leading Newtonian volume-averaged \(c^2 R^{(h)}_{\hat0\hat0}\) inside the calibration radius is \(3V_h^2(R)/R^2\) in \({\rm s}^{-2}\). The coherent oscillatory amplitude of that trace average is at most \(9 V_h^2(R)/R^2\), with equality for the common-phase coherent state. This is a spatial mean of the physical tidal trace, **not** a clock reading at the Solar System or a pulsar timing signal. The carrier gives \(f\simeq m_hc^2/(\pi\hbar)=0.464\) nHz (period 68.36 years), up to binding and motion. In these apertures the ceilings range from \(3.50\,10^{-32}\) to \(2.54\,10^{-30}\ {\rm s}^{-2}\), median \(4.25\,10^{-31}\). Direct stress averaging at two phase preparations checks the coefficient 3 and the phase cancellation. Connecting a line to actual clocks requires local density, phase coherence, and source/observer propagation; no clock comparison has been made.
