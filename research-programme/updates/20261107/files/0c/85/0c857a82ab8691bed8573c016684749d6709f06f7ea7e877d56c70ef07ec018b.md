# Stage57 — a real survey-response test of the retained fields

## What moved forward

Stage56's already calculated density and velocity fields now predict actual
BOSS DR12 low-redshift clustering measurements through the released survey
window, wide-angle response and covariance. Both North and South Galactic Caps
are included. No new field or fitted suppression curve was inserted.

We freshly acquired the data and response matrices from the
[author's release](https://fbeutler.github.io/hub/deconv_paper.html), recording
URLs and hashes. The numerical data rebinning agrees exactly with the author's
reader. An incorrect orientation label on the landing page was resolved from
the actual matrix shapes and the paper's definition, not patched by guessing.

## The data-facing result

The primary test uses the monopole and quadrupole over .02<k<.10 h/Mpc,
32 measurements in total. Each branch receives the same four fitted tracer
parameters: bias and residual white noise for each sky cap. H0=70, primordial
normalization, native field parameters, mass and the illustrative F=.5 are fixed.

| Branch | Raw chi2 | Finite-covariance corrected chi2 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Retained slow field + heavy benchmark | 38.8057 | 38.4834 |
| Same slow field + cold matter | 41.6276 | 41.2819 |
| Matched LCDM | 45.3890 | 45.0120 |

The heavy benchmark scores6.52865 lower than LCDM here, and2.79847 lower
than the same slow-field cold control. It scores lower in each cap separately;
no fitted nuisance value hits its predeclared bounds. This is an encouraging
fixed-parameter, leading-linear diagnostic, not an established preference for
the full theory or a fit of each cosmology's best parameters.

The predeclared scale and multipole checks give:

| Comparison | Data count | Heavy | Slow/cold | LCDM |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| kmax=.08, P0/P2 | 24 | 20.7486 | 22.6434 | 25.2288 |
| kmax=.10, P0/P2 (primary) | 32 | 38.4834 | 41.2819 | 45.0120 |
| kmax=.12, P0/P2 | 40 | 58.1669 | 63.5475 | 69.2802 |
| kmax=.10, P0/P2/P4 | 48 | 57.1901 | 60.0800 | 63.8698 |

All columns use the same data and nuisance policy within each row. These tests
overlap: their scores must not be summed. The trend survives the declared cuts,
but both primary branches' residual per nominal remaining degree of freedom
increases toward the broader k range. A shared nonlinear/tracer-model check is
therefore more useful next than tuning the field parameters to improve a score.

This is not the same comparison as Stage56's four growth points. It uses one
redshift, different observables and a leading tracer model. The change of score
ordering does not invalidate the previous calculation or establish that its
compression caused the difference. Do not add the overlapping scores together.

## Does a single growth number hide the changed spectrum?

A separate noiseless injection experiment passes the true field predictions
through the actual response, then recovers a single growth amplitude using
an LCDM-shaped density template. The geometry is held to the truth in both
calculations so this isolates shape compression, not geometric mismatch.

For the primary heavy example the recovered growth amplitude is biased by
-.45% in NGC and -.49% in SGC relative to its calculated velocity rms. Those
shifts are -.077 and -.050 of the respective linearized forecast errors.
The two cold controls have shifts around+.01%. Across the declared scale cuts,
the heavy shifts remain below .5% in magnitude and .08 forecast errors.

Thus this particular leading-order compression does not produce a large
growth-amplitude bias. That useful result is narrower than validating the
published SDSS nonlinear estimator: its full bias, velocity and geometry
treatment was not reproduced. The injections are synthetic theory signals;
the observational scores in the preceding section use real measurements.

## Checks and remaining approximation

189 checks pass: data hashes and covariance, matrix order, correct h/AP units,
analytic Kaiser limits, angular quadrature, transfer interpolation, nuisance
minima, and injected parameters recovered from starts different from the truth.
Numerical tolerances were not changed after viewing the scores.

At the primary cut, removing the survey window changes the predicted vectors
by about1.3 covariance-weighted sigma per cap. This response cannot simply be
ignored. Angular convergence is much tighter than the observational errors;
cubic-versus-shape-preserving interpolation differs by at most .0051 sigma
in the primary comparison. Doubling or deleting input power above .2 h/Mpc
changes the primary prediction by under .0011 sigma per cap. These are tests
of the implemented response, not total theoretical uncertainty estimates.

AP-generated ell6/8 are small but nonzero and not propagated by this ell<=4
release operator. The calculation also lacks a calibrated nonlinear galaxy
velocity/bias treatment. The plotted quadrupole residuals make that worth
testing on both models before interpreting the relative scores as evidence.

## Next calculation

Derive the next-order redshift-space velocity/bias terms from the tracer
mapping, then test a common controlled correction on both MTS and LCDM,
using source-backed mock/catalog information where available. Distinguish
coefficients fixed by field equations from nuisance properties of galaxies.
Check recovery and scale stability before fitting heavy abundance or cosmology.
Do not add an arbitrary MTS-only suppression factor or demand that only MTS
pass a test the baseline fails.
