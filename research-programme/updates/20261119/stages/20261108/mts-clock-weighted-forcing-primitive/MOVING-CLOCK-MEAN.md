# The moving-clock correction to the slow mean

The clock-normalized primitive obeys integral w J dtheta=0 at each N. Differentiating at fixed phase:
    integral(w J_N+w_N J)dtheta=0.
M is phase-independent in the saved response equation, so its clock-weighted mean acting on J is zero. Therefore
    mean_w(M J-J_N)=mean_w((w_N/w)J)=-mean_w((Omega_N/Omega)J).
Thus the transformed mean drive is fbar - mean_w((Omega_N/Omega)J), not automatically fbar alone.

This correction follows from the existing moving background clock, rather than a fitted coupling. The full remaining oscillatory residual M J-J_N is retained in the numerical test. Its weighted RMS is compared with the original phase-dependent forcing, separately for normal-form/map components and both gravity branches. A ratio above one is not hidden or turned into a failed identity: it means this first primitive does not reduce that component and suggests a cell equation retaining the stiff M operator (rather than dropping the residual). A ratio below one is finite-domain evidence of reduction, not a uniform gain bound.
