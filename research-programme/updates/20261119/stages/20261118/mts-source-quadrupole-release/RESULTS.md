# Two neutral clouds, one over the pole and one over the equator
**Private · 18 November 2026 · same common-metric sector, two saved radial grids, 2×10⁻⁵ tolerance.**

The rotating material produces a quadrupolar radial tide even though its total angular momentum is zero. At a round launch radius of 6 the ordinary charged reference gives **+4.55447×10⁻⁸** in pole-minus-equator radial curvature per squared source spin. Relative acceleration has the opposite sign: the initially radial pole ruler shortens relative to the equatorial ruler. The full scalar changes that launch curvature by −2.26482×10⁻¹³ (4.97 parts per million of the ordinary signal) for the label-fixed preparation. The two independent constraint inversions agree on the absolute radial observable to 4.35×10⁻¹⁰; the two original grids agree on the scalar differences themselves to 2.14×10⁻⁷. These are classical calculations in reduced Compton units, not measured strains or seconds.

**The decisive control was changing what “the same initial scalar” means.** Both choices below agree on the original spherical data. Both solve the nonspherical constraints. Nothing was fitted to a crossing.

All volume differences are coefficients per ε², not changes of this magnitude in an arbitrary astrophysical system. The zero is **which cloud is larger switching**, not reversal of collapse. On a measured round shell the original label-fixed scalar samples a different local h, whereas the shell-fixed preparation does not; this changes the Ricci focusing from the first instant. The crossing is a prediction of a particular physical state, **not a universal signature of MTS**. A separately added free even gravitational deformation also changes the answer at identical matter input; we constructed constraint-compatible examples.

The shell-fixed branch's absence of a negative early volume was verified for **each** amplitude. Subtracting the ordinary curve from the weak shell-fixed curve asks a harder question: its early-window difference is only about 1.59×10⁻³³ per ε². That difference does not meet the unchanged tolerance across numerical boxes and meshes; its numerical magnitude is not promoted. The full-strength shell-minus-ordinary difference passes on its own scale. See shell_vs_ordinary.csv.

**How this faced independent checks**
- One local calculation evolved the Einstein, scalar and Maxwell fields and integrated the expansion; the other evolved the spatial geometry to tenth order at 180-bit precision and read its determinant, without using the expansion law. They agree on the short and whole volume waveforms to 1.93×10⁻¹⁰ of the respective peaks for the original preparations, and on scalar-minus-ordinary differences to 2.90×10⁻⁸. The shell-fixed check agrees to 3.22×10⁻¹¹. Both parent grids, changed numerical boxes and different viewing angles were tested. These are controlled pre-front calculations: the moving matter's new emission has not yet reached the detector.
- A genuinely different balanced spin law gives **23.0724%** of the first profile’s early amplitude in the full-scalar example. We computed the transfer from the initial spatial constraint at radius **14**, before running the second source at **6**. It reproduced all five preparations’ peak-normalized volume curves to 2.15×10⁻¹¹ and preserved both crossing times. Interior source changes cannot alter the shape of the one allowed decaying exterior solution for a fixed background without extra exterior data; the result need not hold after wave arrival. The independent spin cost also calibrates the tide without fitting ε.
- Eight directional Kerr/Kerr–Newman checks on physically round surfaces agree to 1.63×10⁻⁸. A separate neutral vacuum wave calculation follows the time-dependent radial tide up to Schwarzschild time 6.4 where the causal cone permits: at R=14 it changes by −1.214×10⁻⁵ of its initial anisotropy. This fixed-sphere readout is distinct from the freely falling volume experiment.
- Numerical misses are kept. Ordinary double-precision curvature/volume subtraction did **not** verify the weak crossing on its own tiny early scale; its Hamiltonian error could even hide the zero. Reconstructing the initial constraint and evolving the high-precision metric determinant provided the independent check. A raw scalar interpolation outside its compact support also produced a tiny fictitious trace; the saved input is exactly zero there. The corrected physical trace uses that support.
