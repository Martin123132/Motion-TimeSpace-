# What fixes the polar initial field, and what does not

Use the earlier Einstein metric, minimally coupled Maxwell, ordinary charged dust and neutral massive scalar in its retained long-wavelength normalization. Mass/charge are geometrical, scalar mass is one, energies below include 4π. Source amplitude ε multiplies the saved counterrotation; the calculation is linear in ε² and angular coefficient P=(3cos²θ−1)/2. The monopole is the **previous** solved preparation. Omit no square of the first-order axial sector. Initial radial momenta, scalar normal velocity and transverse photons vanish; no initial-boundary data for an even gravitational wave are inferred from that statement.

**Particular initial geometry.** Use the spatial metric (1+ε²uP)⁴ times the given spherical metric dr²/f+r²dΩ² for the l=2 part, zero trace of the extrinsic curvature and zero free even momentum. The scalar h and densitized baryon/charge per coordinate label are held fixed initially, as is the densitized electric radial flux (not the local electric energy). The alternative shell preparation is derived below. This uses the conformal method of [Ó Murchadha and York](https://journals.aps.org/prd/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevD.10.428); the source and observable here are the specific retained MTS initial conditions, not a new existence theorem.

Write D for conserved coordinate baryon density, d=D√f, Q=ηN, N'=r²D, ℓ for the signed angular momentum profile, I=∫ℓdN. The initial axial momentum constraint gives K_(rφ)=2I sinθ/r³ per spin in the sign convention K=−£_n γ/2. Indeed √f ∂r(2I)/r²=2dℓ. It vanishes outside this *balanced* emitter. Its square enters the Hamiltonian constraint with a definite sign even though I changes with radius. With G_h=f h'²/2, V_h=h²/2, e_E=Q²/(2r⁴), the conformal response of local energy is (−6d−4G_h−8e_E)uP plus dℓ² sin²θ/(2r²). Add KijKij=8I² sin²θ/r⁶. Using R₃=4e on the initial unspun slice gives
    [f ∂r²+(2f/r+f'/2)∂r−6/r²−V]u=S,
    V=d+Q²/r⁴−h²,             S=(4I²/r⁶+dℓ²/r²)/6.
The forcing has no unknown coefficient. Regular centre u∼r²; decaying exterior u∼C/r³, without a growing applied tidal field. (r²√f u')'−(6+r²V)u/√f=r²S/√f proves uniqueness of THIS scalar constraint solution when 6+r²V>0 and fixes u<0 for nontrivial nonnegative S. It does not prove uniqueness of the tensor data. The bound is comfortably satisfied on the saved grids. **Observable.** To linear order in ε² the coordinate position of an intrinsically round sphere of specified area 4πR² is r=R−2ε²RuP. This follows by perturbing its induced metric; derivatives of the height contribute quadratically to that metric. Misidentifying a coordinate shell as a measured round sphere would contaminate a small directional signal with a radial gradient of the large spherical tide. Outside the material the radial tidal section at launch, per P ε², is
    E₂ = [−12/r²−4m/r³+2Q²/r⁴+2h²+(6f+2rf')h'²+4rfh'h'']u +4f u'/r.
Here f=1−2m/r; E_R=R_(nR nR), relative acceleration =−E_R. The pole-minus-equator difference is (3/2)E₂. Derive this either by conformally varying the 3D Ricci tensor and subtracting 2S_ij+(e−tr S)γ_ij in the Gauss equation, or by direct spatial sectional curvatures of the deformed metric. The physical trace instead obeys Ric(n,n)=−h²+Q²/R⁴ at launch. Therefore Ric₂=4ru h h'. The charge contribution is unchanged at a **fixed round radius**, not set to zero in the field equation. When h=0 one can have an initial radial tide without an initial volume-acceleration difference. In the far vacuum, ΔE_R≈−36C/r⁵ per ε². Dividing by the initial spin cost δADM=(2/3)∫[αDℓ²/2+2F I²/r⁴]dr removes the unknown ε²; it does not remove the radial source-profile dependence or equate δADM to radiated energy.

**Physically different states on exactly the same spherical input.**
- Hold h fixed on the *measured round shells*, rather than the conformal labels. The required Eulerian perturbation is ξ=2ru h'. It gives δe_extra=f h'ξ'+hξ; subtract half this from the constraint right side before solving. Equivalently add rf h'²u'+(f h'²+rf h'h''+rh h')u on its left. With μ=exp∫r h'²dr the flux for the modified radial problem is μr√f u' in log radius. This satisfies the constraints and gives δh|round=0. It predicts exactly zero quadratic-in-time volume difference even for nonzero scalar. The two states are not gauge names for one another: a local scalar measurement on the initially constructed round sphere distinguishes them.
- Independent even spatial geometry: add a compact fractional radial seed g(r)P on the reference metric between 5.3 and 8.3, at unchanged h, flux and baryons. In the seed's vacuum-of-dust region the additional right side is [fg'/r+(f'/r+(3+f)/r²)g+f h'²g]/4. Solve again, not just add an unconstrained strain. Its additional direct term in the reduced E₂ formula above is (f h'²−f/r²)g. The compact seed changes the measured release tide with no change in the material spin law; its amplitude is an independent tensor preparation, not a force coupling. The control uses unit coefficient to exhibit linear independence, not to fit a measured value.
- Independent even momentum: in background proper radial length x, place K_xx=U P, K_xA=r v D_A P and K_AB=r²[−U P Ω_AB/2+w(D_AD_BP+3PΩ_AB)]. Compact U is arbitrary. It leaves the linear even Hamiltonian unchanged but has a nonzero curl (magnetic Weyl) and changes the vacuum master velocity. These compact examples are not data in the main prediction. Retarded formation or independent measurements must select the extra exterior data to turn a conditional response into a unique source history.

**Independent exact check.** The stationary [Kerr–Newman metric](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.1704351) has a different spatial geometry and is not the conformal material source. At fixed round R the Boyer–Lindquist coordinate shift per Kerr a² is [(1+2k)cos²θ−1−k]/(2R), k=2M/R−Q²/R²; the polar angle shift to round coordinates is (1+k)sinθcosθ/(2R²). Derive by matching the two angular metric components. In the aligned principal frame the radial curvature's angular term is (12M/R⁵−10Q²/R⁶)cos²θ, obtained by expanding its exact section. The stationary slice normal differs by an azimuthal boost of squared speed a² f sin²θ/R²; add this times (−3M/R³+4Q²/R⁴) and the shift times (6M/R⁴−12Q²/R⁵). Direct 4D metric differentiation and finite-spin/angle extrapolation check that sum, including the physical shell shift. At large R the vacuum coefficient is 18M/R⁵ per Kerr a², consistent with a Newtonian mass quadrupole. Do not assign the Kerr multipole to the material star.
