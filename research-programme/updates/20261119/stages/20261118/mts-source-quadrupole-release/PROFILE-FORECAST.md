# The predicted pre-front crossing is independent of an interior spin-profile normalization

At a fixed spherical background and with no independently added exterior tensor, scalar or electric quadrupole, the radial Hamiltonian quadrupole outside r=5 solves a homogeneous second-order equation with a single decaying solution. Two balanced spin profiles confined inside r=5 therefore differ in the exterior only by ONE multiplicative constant, fixed by their distinct interior integrals. Their initial time derivatives are both zero. Because the even exterior perturbation equations are linear and causal and the axial wave is still absent there, every linear even tide and cloud-volume waveform from the two preparations must have that same ratio until a signal from the material arrives. Their nontrivial zero times must agree. This does not apply to the later emitted waveform, to an independent exterior shape or extrinsic curvature, or across different spherical scalar backgrounds.

Test against the second already-defined counterrotation law as a genuinely different source: no change of mass scale, efficiency or coupling. Freeze the relative transfer from the global constraints at a radius other than 6 (R=14), then independently run the short local proper-time experiment at 6 in the second profile, both scalar preparations and ordinary controls. Test the weak crossing on its own early time window, not on the much larger late amplitude. Separately divide the far-field radial tide by the cost in initial ADM mass as a calibration independent of the common spin amplitude. An available radiative energy or measured light fluence is not that mass increment.
