# First nonlinear wave–gas forest sightlines — 18 November 2026

**The previously calculated force survives an actual gas evolution.** The periodic wave–gas–Poisson solver initialized from the retained z=99 field reproduces force, baryon density and velocity at all four high-k modes and all seven saved epochs to within **1.64×10⁻⁷** relative in the small-amplitude limit. This includes wave-on MTS, matched pressure-off, the rolling-field all-cold branch and conventional ΛCDM. The criterion was 2×10⁻⁵. Neither the density nor the velocity was normalized to a late result. The free wave also propagates through an interference node, and a nonlinear planar dust collapse matches its analytic, pre-shell-crossing density to 7.77×10⁻⁸ on the finer grid.

**An actual correction, not a looser test.** Keeping the photon velocity in its *original CDM frame* produced an 8.32×10⁻⁵ gas-velocity error in the pressure-off prediction. Converting the photon dipole to Newtonian gauge before carrying it into the altered matter flow reduced this below 1.5×10⁻⁷. Two additional redshifts (6.3 and 5.15) were frozen before the direct native comparison; all four arms pass there, including the dust intervention predicted without a dust-run radiation history. Pure clustered Newtonian evolution without the linear surrounding fields is less accurate. The nonlinear metric is live for massive matter; the radiation, neutrino and rolling-field corrections are imported linear responses, not a nonlinear Einstein solution. See frame-linear.json, new-epoch-receipt.json and events-*/truth.npz.

**What changes in mock absorption.** At z=4.2, with the retained primordial normalization, a common thermal history and the same sparse set of 3D modes, the mean over a sign-reversed phase pair gives:

| Matter dynamics | Mock first-mode flux power (km/s) | Difference from its own warm linear prediction |
|---|---:|---:|
| MTS, wave pressure on | 0.006498 | −0.037% |
| Same MTS initial state, pressure off from z=99 | 0.015746 | −0.098% |

Suppressing only the wave pressure increases modeled flux power by **2.42×**. The much larger all-cold contrast also includes the different cosmological preparation; it is not a measure of what late pressure alone does. These are **conditional mock powers**, not measured values, likelihoods or a goodness of fit. The readout has uniform ionization and fixed optical-depth normalization; changing just the readout temperature from 10,000 K to 5,000 or 20,000 K changes the controlled MTS mock power at this redshift by +39% or −49%. The heating-history intervention and this change of the final line profile are separate tests. The radial-only calculations cover seven instrument entries at their actual four redshift/wavenumber pairs; the full 3D benchmark keeps k_parallel fixed rather than pretending it is the same observed velocity bin at every redshift.

**Where the missing physics now shows up.** With five independent positive-k Fourier vectors plus their conjugates and inherited P_R/V per vector, adding the *second* pair of transverse modes still adds **46.1%** to MTS power and **42.4%** to cold power at z=4.2. In contrast, refining the same seeded modes from 18³ to 24³ changes flux power by less than one part in a million there in each arm. This is input-mode coverage, not a slow numerical mesh masquerading as a signal. It is not evidence that increasing the physical cutoff further leaves the MTS/cold ratio unchanged. The fixed-volume checks test two paired phase choices, not a cosmological ensemble, and do not include long-wave density environments. Both sightline-level powers and the analytic warm linear mode sum are retained.
