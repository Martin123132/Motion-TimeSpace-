# Additional paired source-coupling test, frozen before the new basis runs
A difference in the ruler at fixed prescribed currents is not automatically a difference when the observer uses magnetic measurements to prepare or identify the source. Test that question at the same η=.8, comparing the ordinary branch to the full label-prepared scalar branch. Use the actually propagated signed polar field of the ordinary minus current at times 3 and 4 as the only calibration data. Do not assume the previously extremal calibration produces an exact zero here. Never use the ruler or a later field to select weights.

Evolve the second independent parent source on both branches. Use the saved mass-form first-source packets for the full scalar; calculate the missing identically prepared ordinary first-source packets. For each branch use its own two basis fields at those two times. Within the specified two-current family invert them for the weights, and form that branch's own quadratic ruler matrix from its two pure runs and its direct minus run. Fix the full scalar branch's weights and predicted ruler *before* running that calibrated scalar source from its actual material constraint. Repeat on both grids with the fine calibration weights held fixed; compare to the same ordinary initial magnetic observations and later ordinary ruler. Score the fractional accuracy of the difference itself, not just the two large signals, and show how much of the original scalar contrast is absorbed by re-identifying the prescribed source with the two field measurements. Report ambiguities demonstrated by arbitrary current shapes separately. No observation, parameter/posterior fit or changes to the parent sector.
