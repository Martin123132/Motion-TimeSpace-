# What can field measurements tell a freely falling clock and ruler?
**Preparation.** In the saved positive-density, slowly rotating spherical material let x=N/M∈[0,1] label the enclosed material number. Write I(x)=(32/3)x³(1−x)³ and u=2x−1. Prescribe the angular-momentum primitive J=M I(x)(a+b u+d u²) and the material specific angular momentum ℓ=dJ/dN. Both J and ℓ vanish at either end for any a,b,d. This changes neither the base mass nor charge nor net angular momentum. It changes where the interior moves. For d=0 this is exactly the parent's two earlier sources; there is no free metric coupling in this experiment.

**Why one must solve a new constraint.** The same parent even constraint has rotational forcing S=(4J²/r⁶+ρℓ²/r²)/6. With J=J₁−c J₂ its mixed piece is −2c(4J₁J₂/r⁶+ρℓ₁ℓ₂/r²)/6; outside the initially spinning material there is still the decaying homogeneous metric solution. The experiment solves the boundary-value problem for the actual combined current, cross-checks it against an independent radial Green integral, and evolves the matched even geometry, dust, electric perturbation, twist, scalar where present and the axial Maxwell wave. It is not a subtraction of old metric solutions. The first-order axial field is linear in (a,b,d); the leading directional volume, normalized by squared infinitesimal spin, is quadratic. This follows because the second-order even equations are linear in the second-order unknowns with forcing quadratic in first-order fields and current. Higher spin orders, nonminimal couplings and other incoming perturbations are not included.

**Invertible calibration within a specified source family.** On d=0 let v=(a,b), b(t) the two signed polar-field responses on the common falling clock and K(t) a symmetric 2×2 matrix. Then B(t)=b(t)·v and L(t)=vᵀK(t)v. The same quadratic form construction applies separately to the determinant readout and each integrated focusing contribution. The pure runs determine the diagonals. The run with v=(1,−c) determines K₁₂=[L₁+c²L₂−L₋]/(2c). This is polarization of the field equations, not a fit to a rotation curve. Later source tests cannot modify K or c.

For the two signed early measurements stack b(3), b(4) into A. If det A≠0 the weights are v=A⁻¹B and the ruler prediction is L=BᵀH B, H=A⁻ᵀK A⁻¹. The error identity is exact to this spin order: δL=2δBᵀH B+δBᵀHδB. The worst absolute error for a common Euclidean magnetic error bound s is at most 2‖H B‖s+‖H‖s². Report the common-unit conditioning and row-normalized conditioning separately; rescaling away a faint early field must not hide noise amplification. If K is positive definite, one signed measurement B₁ gives the sharp conditional minimum L≥B₁²/(b₁ᵀK⁻¹b₁), not a unique L. No such positive lower bound is assumed when the form is indefinite.

**Out-of-family falsification.** The two calibration clocks need not fix a third independent source shape. Test the prescribed u² primitive directly and compare its late result with what the two-weight inverse would impute. As a stronger constructive check, before running an alias source read only those two measured values to specify its two weights; evolve it from its own quadratic constraint on both grids. This asks whether two actual prepared systems can give the same early magnetic measurements yet genuinely distinguishable later forces or volumes. A positive result is a non-uniqueness statement about these readouts, not new astrophysical data, not a failure of the parent stress coupling, and not proof of non-uniqueness given every possible observation.
