# Calculating the launch susceptibility without fitting finite amplitudes
Set t=ln r, z=A², h=A H and v=r b u'. The ordinary constrained fields obey u_t=i v, v_t=−v+q u+F with i=1/b, q=(6+Q²/r²−r²h²)/b+r²D, F=Dℓ²/6+2J²i/(3r⁴). First integrate the spherical mass response Y=(μ_z−μ₀)/z using its integrating-factor equation in SCALAR-TO-ORDINARY-LIMIT; it has a perfectly finite equation at z=0. Define
    i₁=2Y/[r(b_z+b₀)b_z b₀],
    q₁=(6+Q²/r²)i₁−r²H²/b_z,      F₁=2J²i₁/(3r⁴).
These are exact divided differences at finite z and exact derivatives at zero. For X=(u_z−u₀)/z, Z=(v_z−v₀)/z,
    X_t=i_z Z+i₁v₀,               Z_t=−Z+q_z X+q₁u₀+F₁.
Fix regular inner branch Z=2X at the small Frobenius matching radius; shrink that radius as an independent test. At the outside radius Z=β_z X+[(β_z−β₀)/z]u₀ where β is the decaying exterior logarithmic flux. At zero use the high-precision derivative of that boundary response with respect to asymptotic mass multiplied by Y(48); do not hold β fixed while changing the interior. This system only needs the ordinary solution and specified H at z=0. It does not measure its coefficient by subtracting two small finite-amplitude curves.

Freeze the zero-amplitude predictions for the minus and second sources on the η=.8 background. Test A=1,1/2,1/4,1/8,1/160 using the exact finite-z forcing. The predicted radial pole-minus-equator launch curvature differences per A² are −3.01550834×10⁻¹³ and −5.22875685×10⁻¹⁴ per squared spin. At full amplitude the coefficients differ from those forecasts by 2.62×10⁻⁷ and 3.18×10⁻⁷, and the finite-amplitude difference shrinks by approximately a factor of four per halving. Do not infer 11 significant figures from the weakest-arm residual: the alternate inner radius/mesh changes the zero-amplitude radial slopes by about 2×10⁻¹¹ relative, and subtraction of independent full radial tides at 1/160 only checks the divided difference to 1.1×10⁻⁶ or better. At A=1, the distinct archived mixed-packet data agree on the difference to 1.6×10⁻¹⁰ relative or better. The analytic scaling bound and the numerical coefficient answer different questions: existence of a limit versus the signed response for a preparation.

These numbers apply at release. To propagate the susceptibility through the *observed time* of the radiated burst, the next test must differentiate the actual time-dependent fields and source terms, not apply the release coefficient to a late-time observable.
