# Two matching magnetic readings, different gravity — and a derived ordinary limit
**What was tested.** The saved common metric, charged material, Maxwell and neutral scalar equations; initially round falling detector at R₀=6; same physical-time history through 6.4. Every new current was put into the material stress and squared constraint *before* solving. Twenty-four independent burst packets, two radial resolutions, and the previous bank, early signed normalization and 2×10⁻⁵ accuracy target were kept. These are forward calculations for prepared systems, not observations.

## What survives magnetic calibration?
At charge ratio .8, introducing the full scalar on matched material labels lowers the directional log-volume response by **0.37636%** at the final clock for the prescribed minus current. Now ignore the current weights when comparing what would be observed: choose the full scalar source weights using *only* the ordinary source's two signed magnetic measurements at 3 and 4. Freeze the gravitational prediction, solve that calibrated scalar current directly, and compare with ordinary. The decrease is still **0.37218%**: **98.89%** of the fixed-current contrast survives. The measured ordinary and scalar values are 1.13044247×10⁻⁹ and 1.12623517×10⁻⁹ in log-volume per squared spin. The two early fields match to 2.2×10⁻¹³ on the reference channel scales. Their later difference is checked at 5.9×10⁻⁹ by radial refinement and at 1.35×10⁻⁶ by the independent metric readout, relative to the *difference*. See matched-scalar-contrast.csv. This conditions on the specified two-current family; it is not a detection of a new scalar.

## What the fields do not fix
The previously specified opposing current cancels the time-three polar field to 1.6×10⁻⁸ or better on the first field's scale on the fine grid. Its final polar field is 1.408637×10⁻⁶; its direct electromagnetic log-volume term is 2.165082×10⁻¹⁰, agreeing with the previous independent prediction to 5.9×10⁻⁸ relative. Its *full* ruler is 1.269420×10⁻⁹. A fourth numerical experiment, not used to calculate the two-current response matrix, reproduces the frozen whole-trajectory ruler prediction to 5.6×10⁻¹² on its peak scale. Using only its early signed fields to infer the two weights still predicts the later ruler to 1.4×10⁻⁸. The absolute-field condition number is 248, not the row-rescaled 4.31. Measurement noise and source freedom cannot be ignored.

The two-current inference fails outside that family. Two explicitly evolved, balanced sources match the same two magnetic samples to **1.2×10⁻⁷ relative or better**, yet their final ruler signals are **3.822011×10⁻¹¹ versus 2.744104×10⁻¹⁰** — a factor **7.18**. Their difference is a real material current invisible to both early field readouts; directly evolving it gives a final gravitational response of 3.166669×10⁻¹⁰. No measurement of the final ruler was used to choose either current. See direct-degeneracy.csv and PHYSICS.md.

## The scalar-to-ordinary link
For the retained preparation h=A H, the spherical mass excess obeys a positive linear integrating-factor equation. The kinetic part of the even constraint forcing is *exactly unchanged* at fixed baryons/current; the remaining coefficient changes are controlled at order A². The physically matched release **volume acceleration** tends to ordinary uniformly over balanced profiles of bounded launch kinetic energy. The **radial sectional tide** also tends to ordinary for each fixed regular current (or uniformly with the stated stronger source moment bounded). This is a limit of the actual measurable relative accelerations in this sector, not a fitted recovery of a number. The conservative bounds in scalar-launch-limit.csv are generally not tight; they are not observational exclusions.

We also derived the signed *leading coefficient*. For the minus current, the predicted scalar-induced change of the launch radial tide is −3.01550834×10⁻¹³ A² per squared spin, versus the ordinary tide +5.98405502×10⁻⁸. For the second current the coefficient is −5.22875685×10⁻¹⁴. Both were calculated at zero amplitude, including variation of the exterior boundary, then tested at five nonzero amplitudes; even at A=1 the radial coefficients are within 3.2×10⁻⁷ relative of those predictions. The weak direct subtraction is not accurate enough to resolve arbitrarily many digits. See SCALAR-TO-ORDINARY-LIMIT.md and LEADING-SCALAR-COEFFICIENT.md.
