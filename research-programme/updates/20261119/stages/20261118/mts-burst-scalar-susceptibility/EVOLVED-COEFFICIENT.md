# Matter-to-ruler susceptibility on the actual ordinary burst
Take the prepared scalar amplitude A as a label on initial data, not a variable cosmological parameter. Hold the common action, baryon number, charge, and initially matched round detector fixed. On the scalar-free trajectory changing A to −A leaves the metric, material, Maxwell field and ruler unchanged and reverses the scalar and its canonical momentum. Write these as U=U₀+A²U₂+…, h=Aφ+…, Π=Aπ+…. The coefficient U₂ is the derivative with respect to z=A² at z=0; it is one half the second derivative in A. The first-order spin and even spin-squared angular sectors present in every prior trajectory are retained *inside* each coefficient. The scalar expansion is not a replacement for the spin expansion.

For the regularized, discretized common-metric equations denoted by ẏ=F(y), the ordinary state solves ẏ₀=F(y₀), the odd state solves ẏ₁=DF(y₀)y₁, and the even state solves ẏ₂=DF(y₀)y₂+½D²F(y₀)[y₁,y₁]. This last term includes scalar stress and energy transport; treating the scalar as a passive field in an otherwise fixed final geometry would omit the driven response of shear, radiation, charge and material. For example, in the spherical mass form the explicit scalar radial pressure at onset is (π_proper²+φ_x²/a²−φ²)/2 and its flux is −π_proper φ_x/a. They enter the same R̈=−m/R²−Rp and ṁ=−R²(pṘ+jR'/a) as ordinary matter. The scalar contribution to Ric(n,n) is 2π_proper²−φ². Thus even the sign of the net accumulated ruler change is not supplied by “extra positive density”.

We differentiate the *existing* spatial evolution and all four stages of its time step with coefficients of the formal variable A. Multiply polynomials mod A³; the even product coefficient is f₂g₀+f₁g₁+f₀g₂. Differentiate inverses, square roots, angular projections, radial derivatives, the connection-momentum replacement, the central parity/regularity filter and the independent spherical mass evolution. This produces a derivative of the saved numerical scheme, not a finite-difference estimate from two completed signal curves. The regular radial cap projects each coefficient separately because its weights do not depend on A. The ordinary determinant and the integrated focusing variable give separately differentiated readouts.

Launch matters. Compute φ from the saved scalar preparation. Use the previous mass and conformal-constraint sensitivity, including differentiation of the asymptotic exterior boundary, to initialize the spherical and angular metric, the enclosed mass per cubed coordinate radius and auxiliary connection. On the matched sphere the radial label displacement is ξ=−2ru; its derivative −2r∂_z u contributes to a directional perturbation even when the spherical observable is nonzero. Holding that displacement fixed by mistake would be a different experiment. Scalar is fixed on labels in these tests; a different shell preparation is not silently substituted.

Field calibration changes the prediction. Put v=(1,−c) at z=0. The two-row ordinary polar magnetic map is D, with rows at the fixed proper clocks 3 and 4. Let β₂ be the *actual tangent* of the two fields at fixed v. To preserve the two measured values, v₂=−D⁻¹β₂ is fixed, with no late-volume data. For any clock, a quadratic spin observable with ordinary matrix K has calibrated derivative L₂,matched=L₂,fixed+2v₂ᵀKv. For a linear polar field with ordinary row b the corresponding derivative is B₂,matched=B₂,fixed+bv₂. This adjustment changes the preparation, not the field equations or gravitational coupling. The inversion is conditional on the two-shape family; the already established magnetic degeneracy for unrestricted sources still applies.
