# Predict the mixed tidal history, not just a subtraction of four simulations

Let b scale the fixed initial baryon rest-mass profile D and let y scale the scalar energy (amplitude √y). Expand about flat vacuum; D and the free scalar carrier already contain the chosen finite reference amplitudes. The coefficient of by gives an independent weak-source prediction. Neither a halo density nor a new force law is inferred. All variables below are in the units of EQUATIONS.md. This construction is defined before evaluating the variational solver.

Write the scalar field, canonical normal momentum as √y(F+bU,...), √y(P+bV,...). F_t=P and P_t=ΔF−F. The first dust geometry is a_b=M_b/r, l_b'=M_b/r², l_b(∞)=0 (a−1 and log lapse). With B_b=l_b−a_b the field perturbation obeys
    U_t=V+B_b P,
    V_t=(Δ−1)U+B_b ΔF+B_b' F_r−(l_b+a_b)F.
Use the derivative of the actual preparation for initial U,V, or zero induced field amplitude for the bare state. A spatially constant lapse is included in both the field clock and observer clock; omitting the latter can make a pure relabeling appear physical.

Compute the flat carrier's energy, radial pressure, transverse pressure and flux and the corresponding first-order scalar mass and lapse. Ordinary dust initially at rest develops v_y,t=−∂r l_h and D_y,t=−div(D v_y). Its mixed proper energy is D_y−a_hD; the second term converts conserved baryon number per areal shell to proper density. Its mixed flux is D v_y, with no mixed pressure at this order. Scalar mixed stress is
    e=P V+F_r U_r−a_b(P²+F_r²)+FU,
    s=e−2FU, t=P V−F_r U_r−a_b(P²−F_r²)−FU,
    j=−P U_r−V F_r+2a_b P F_r.
The mass integral uses total mixed energy. At a turning point, in a flat part of the initial envelope, the leading mixed radial and tangential tides are −D M_h/r−2M_mix/r³ and M_mix/r³−A²χ. Here M_mix'=r²(A²χ−D M_h/r), M_h=A²r³/6. The separately integrated exact initial constraint tests these leading terms without evolving a numerical perturbation.

For any first-order stress the normal radial and tangential tidal coefficients are e−s+2t−2M/r³ and s+M/r³. At mixed order the tangent gets the frame correction −2v_b j_h. Add r_b∂r E_h+r_h∂r E_b+t_b∂t E_h for each component to compare events along the same initially specified geodesic at the same elapsed **proper** time. The omitted term t_h∂t E_b is zero here because the first-order ordinary dust source is static. Here r_X''=−∂r l_X, t_b=−∫l_b dt, with zero initial observer displacement/velocity. The Weyl part is read independently from the Misner–Sharp mass and anisotropy. The geodesic separation satisfies J_mix''=−E_mix−E_b J_h−E_h J_b where J_b,J_h denote first-order separation perturbations. Initial radial separation rate for initially normal geodesics is −r a j; its mixed part must also be expanded.

This predicts which terms drive the non-additive response, including ordinary motion, field stress, enclosed mass, boost and the observer clock. Compare with the finite-amplitude four-arm evolution and its source-halving controls; do not fit coefficients or infer higher-order equality from small total curvature. The sharp physical null is the exterior shell: the bare scalar sees a constant interior lapse perturbation. Its coordinate phase shift must cancel the readout clock shift before the shell can influence the interior.
