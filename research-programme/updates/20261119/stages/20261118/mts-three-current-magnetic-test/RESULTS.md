# Can a different ordinary source imitate the scalar burst?

**With two magnetic readings, yes. With the third reading at clock 5, no—not anywhere in the tested three-current family at exact calibration.** In the saved charge-.8 burst, every nonzero mixture sharing the three early fields has a smaller final directional log-volume with the full scalar than in ordinary charged gravity. The suppression ranges from **0.2670% to 0.4101%** of the ordinary response. This is a statement about a prepared system at the retained spin order; the three profiles are not assumed to exhaust physical sources. No late gravity data calibrated the three-clock sources. Nothing here is a galaxy fit or an observation.

**An actual false positive.** On the ordinary branch a balanced current with weights (0.99898916, −1.16078842, −0.00542172) matches the scalar branch's polar magnetic readings at clocks 3 and 4 and its final gravitational ruler at 6.4. We deliberately solved for that final value to construct a counterexample, froze the resulting weights, and evolved the ordinary material and electromagnetic field independently. The mismatch is only 5.91×10⁻¹⁰ of the nominal scalar gravity difference on the fine grid, and its rotational kinetic cost is *lower* than the nominal source's (99.523% of reference). It cannot be rejected by assuming excessive energy. Its independently predicted third magnetic reading at 5 is off by **0.6163% of the reference magnetic channel scale**. The sign and magnitude are reproduced on the other grid. The difference is not a percentage of a nearly cancelled reading. See direct-two-clock-impostor.csv.

**What the third reading fixes.** The triplet of signed polar fields at clocks 3, 4 and 5 has full rank in this family. In solving for the three source weights the row-normalized condition number is 4.30, absolute condition about 199; raw measurements with unequal errors cannot be treated as equally precise. For the ordinary opposing source (1,−1.1666214,0), the scalar solution inferred from magnetic fields alone is approximately (1.00000810, −1.16656563, −0.0000723426). Its directly evolved final ruler is 1.12617838×10⁻⁹ versus ordinary 1.13044247×10⁻⁹, a difference of **−4.26408971×10⁻¹² (−0.37721%)**. The two-source/two-clock case gave −0.37218%. The three field matches close to 5.32×10⁻¹³ on the row scales. Across the complete history the predicted ruler *difference* agrees with the independent evolution to 3.18×10⁻¹⁰ relative; changing grid changes it by 5.68×10⁻⁹. Independently computed determinant and stress-based readouts agree to 1.23×10⁻⁷ and 2.16×10⁻¹¹ on that difference's peak scale.

**Why “every mixture” is justified here.** Write the ordinary source as v. The three field readings force the scalar source to be Tv, so the difference in the ruler is vᵀCv with C=TᵀK_scalar T−K_ordinary. These matrices were obtained from coupled evolutions and polarization, not a regression. The extremal ratios of the two quadratic forms give the interval in the opening paragraph. Independently, exact rational arithmetic on both grids' *represented input numbers* proves the outward bounds −0.4102% < vᵀCv/(vᵀK_ordinary v) < −0.2669%: the metric denominator and both difference inequalities have strictly positive leading minors. This certificate covers all vectors, not a sampling of source weights. It is exact about the saved numerical response matrices, not an exact continuum solution. Grid disagreement in the fractional bound is about 2.1×10⁻⁹ in fractional units. See exact-final-fraction-certificate.json and fractional-all-current-bounds.csv.

There is a second interpretation which does not divide by a gravitational response. At the independently calculated ordinary launch cost of the nominal source, the final differences lie between −7.108×10⁻¹² and −2.922×10⁻¹². We directly evolved both partners of the source giving the *smallest absolute* separation at that cost: measured −2.921930616×10⁻¹² versus predicted −2.921930615×10⁻¹². Its scalar partner's cost was computed, not forced to equal the ordinary cost. This extremal pair is distinct from the independent prespecified (.45,−.70,.85) validation mixture: its own prospective coarse-only forecast predicted the fine-grid mixture rulers to 1.62×10⁻⁷ or better in both arms.

**When the sign is useful.** At every saved nonzero clock from 0.02 to 1.68 the scalar-minus-ordinary correction is negative for all matched sources. From 1.70–1.84 both signs are possible, then from 1.86–3.80 all are positive. From 3.82–4.44 both signs are possible again; from 4.46 to 6.40 all are negative. This uses an eigenvalue sign screen charging both grid disagreement and the original comparison tolerance. It specifies sampled clock ranges, not precise continuum transition times. Thus the reversal found previously for one source is shared, but assigning one universal crossing time would be wrong.

**Measurement precision matters.** Let the half-widths on the first two magnetic fields be identical in absolute units between the old and new experiments; normalize each by the ordinary first-two-unit source channel norm and give the third reading the same fractional precision on its own reference scale. For the nominal pair the independently movable gravitational prediction intervals first touch at ε=0.03962% (two sources, two fields) versus **ε=0.06703%** (three sources, three fields), before ruler noise. Scaling by all three unit currents instead gives 0.06429% for the latter. For *all* sources at the nominal kinetic cost, a conservative common sufficient early-field precision is ε below 0.03635% on the native scales. These are sensitivity requirements in the model, not claims of available detectors.

A separate prospective coarse-grid design allowed a linear combination of the 6 and 6.4 gravitational clocks: the best sign-separating observable was simply minus the **final** ruler. The three withheld magnetic readings at 5.5, 6 and 6.4 also cannot all coincide for any nonzero source in this subspace in the saved equations. But the uniform lower bound at reference cost is only 1.38×10⁻⁶ in normalized vector norm; accounting for the amplification of early field errors gives a sufficient ε of only 3.09×10⁻⁷ *before late field noise*. Magnetic measurements alone are a much more demanding way of making the distinction. See late-source-discrimination.csv for both grids and scales.

**Verification and limits.** 24 fresh coupled trajectories plus the previously sealed histories. All 111 prediction/refinement checks and all 144 per-packet Green, mass, and physical ruler checks pass at 2×10⁻⁵. Of 192 auxiliary momentum/connection checks, 124 pass. On the fine grid 12 miss, all the central radial connection; its worst normalized defect drops from 3.88% coarse to 0.0676% fine. This is an open numerical limitation, not a reason to loosen the tolerance or call every component converged. The two grids and independent readouts are evidence for the measured quantity, not a rigorous continuum error bar or proof of the underlying action.
