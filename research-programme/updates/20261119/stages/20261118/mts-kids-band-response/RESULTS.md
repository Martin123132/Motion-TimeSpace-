# What KiDS can distinguish, and where the heavy wave first departs from dust

**Decision:** keep the measured shear vector blind. We can construct combinations of real KiDS band powers dominated by large spatial scales, but the predicted heavy-component signal in those combinations is much smaller than their statistical uncertainty. A fit to the raw high-frequency bands with a linear calculation would give a misleading impression of sensitivity. The next dynamical calculation now has an explicit nonlinear source rather than a fitted power-suppression function.

## Put the three parent calculations through the survey

The [KiDS-1000 cosmic-shear public release](https://kids.strw.leidenuniv.nl/DR4/KiDS-1000_cosmicshear.php) supplies the five source histograms, 120 E-mode row coordinates and E covariance used here. No VALUE column or posterior chain was decoded. The whole 17.28 MB compressed release passed through memory; only the allowed numerical fields and provenance were retained. A public paper summary is available, so “blind” refers specifically to not looking at these measurements to choose a model or a cut. Cite the collaboration's data and methods, including its prescribed acknowledgments, before publication.

We reconstructed the eight finite-angle E/B windows from the release pipeline settings and the [KCAP reference implementation](https://github.com/KiDS-WL/kcap/tree/36b40c55394caf26021408e71ff74538bfa3d51e/cosebis). All integrals use the signed E window; the fraction on a physical scale uses the absolute window to prevent positive and negative contributions from cancelling. Independent KCAP first-band E/B reference maximum absolute differences were 1.35e-4 / 1.50e-5 in window units, while our own quadrature refinement differed by 2.2e-12. Do not conflate internal precision with exact replication of that external table. All 399 original qualified k modes through 5/Mpc were evolved or reused in 121 epochs out to z=4 for heavy, same-background cold and matched ΛCDM, retaining all nonzero source support. Source fractions above z=2 reach 0.976%; they were included. The gravitational shear calculation uses the leading flat-sky Limber response, not a full intrinsic-alignment likelihood.

**Ordinary bands:** zero of 120 have at least 90% of their calculated absolute response at k≤0.1/Mpc in each model. Even the best gives only 67.96%. An apparent 2.01σ heavy/cold separation using the fixed covariance and all 120 linear band powers relies on scales where this calculation does not predict the nonlinear spectrum reliably; this is not an observed preference or a calibrated power-spectrum prediction.

**Observable combination:** weight the highest three source maps by (0.3507474,−1.3507474,1) and use their six corresponding auto/cross band powers. No measured amplitudes set the weights. Because source distributions overlap, foreground cancellation is not exact over a redshift interval. In the following table the absolute response fraction is the minimum of all three cosmologies. Changes are in the signed model band powers, and all ratios are restricted to the computed k range.

| Effective multipole | Calculated response at k≤0.1/Mpc | Heavy vs cold | Heavy vs matched ΛCDM |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 118.44 | 99.27% | −0.0521% | −4.765% |
| 166.16 | 97.56% | −0.0997% | −5.538% |

The other six combined bands fail that cut. The two listed bands together separate heavy from cold by **0.000107σ**, or from matched ΛCDM by **0.00741σ**, for the published covariance and fixed source calibrations. Five freely varying calibration derivatives span both surviving measurements; there is no remaining calibration-independent separation in those two numbers. This is a statement about this combination and covariance, not all available lensing data.

The 90% screen is necessary, not sufficient. For an unknown heavy-minus-cold change on 0.1<k≤5/Mpc bounded in absolute size by a fraction f of the cold linear power, the uncertain band contribution is bounded by f times its absolute cold tail weight. A worst-case perturbation of just 7.57% or 4.39% of that tail can match the respective calculated large-scale contrasts. Nothing here bounds power at k>5/Mpc. Source-redshift uncertainties, alignments, baryonic feedback and low-ell curvature corrections have not been marginalized. Numerical refinements of the signed survey contraction are below 2e-5 in units of the respective absolute band response; this is not a physical accuracy claim. See scale-coverage.csv and selected-bands.json.

## What the retained field predicts beyond linear order

NONLINEAR-SOURCE.md derives the comoving conservative stress, the second-order coupling of density waves and the extra stress a mixed state can carry. A single coherent weak-field mode has a pressure coefficient determined by the **existing** heavy mass, not by the shear data. For a standing cosine perturbation with the same initial matter density and gravity on both sides, the wave-induced second-harmonic density acceleration divided by the ordinary quadratic Newtonian term begins at 2(k/k_J)^4. In this controlled preparation k_J=6.863/Mpc today; the ratio is 5.63e-5 at k=0.5/Mpc. This does not undo the retained early-time transfer suppression: an instantaneous force balance is not the accumulated growth history.

For this preparation the response can be derived **at finite contrast** without truncating the density expansion. At initial contrast 0.9, the exact wave-driven second harmonic is 84.57% of the leading small-contrast prediction. The first six harmonic amplitudes agreed with independent 50-digit wave-Hamiltonian derivatives at all four tested contrasts. Two independent grid sizes also passed crossed-mode and equal-density/opposite-stream controls; in the latter, identical initial density and net current did not imply identical subsequent density. No galaxy occupation or initial random phases were inferred from those fixtures.

**Next toward the observable:** evolve the derived second-order density, gravitational and advective sources together on the retained heavy/cold backgrounds, with matched linear initial histories and an explicitly specified initial second-order state. Start with a two-mode triangle and its pressureless counterpart. Check the evolved force and phase before attempting a nonlinear spectrum or scoring shear measurements.
