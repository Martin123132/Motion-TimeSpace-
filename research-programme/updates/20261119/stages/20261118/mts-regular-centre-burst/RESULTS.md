# What changes the falling ruler during the burst?
The experiment follows an initially round, neutral, freely falling cloud at radius 6 all the way to proper time 6.4, including the part that depends on what crossed the regular centre. Same parent Einstein–Maxwell–material–scalar equations, two conserved rotations, and the previous time-three magnetic calibration. No retuning, astrophysical data or new force constant. The result is a prediction conditional on an independently specified source and preparation.

## The scalar makes a distinguishable change
At the final clock the ordinary charged source gives a pole-minus-equator logarithmic volume of 8.61074×10⁻¹⁰ per squared spin. Adding the full scalar on the original material labels lowers it by **0.35201%**; preparing the same scalar on round shells lowers it by **0.35511%**. These are differences from the matched ordinary source, not from zero. For the first preparation, explicit scalar focusing contributes −3.07629×10⁻¹² and the other changes return +4.52506×10⁻¹⁴, mostly through spatial shear. Integrating the differences of the instantaneous sources reproduces the volume difference to 2.1×10⁻¹¹ on its own scale. The separate metric determinant checks that difference to 4.4×10⁻⁷; the radial refinement is 3.1×10⁻⁹ or better for both preparations. See scalar-versus-ordinary.csv.

The two scalar preparations have the same emitted dipole but their full-amplitude final ruler signals differ by −2.67512×10⁻¹⁴ (shell minus label). A separate evolution of just their difference agrees to 5.7×10⁻⁹ on that difference's scale. The independent weak-preparation difference is about −1.04497×10⁻¹⁸; subtracting the two ordinary-precision emitting solutions misses the 2×10⁻⁵ target here. Likewise subtraction of the weak scalar and zero-scalar determinants cannot establish a precision measurement of their difference. The scalar stress is even under sign reversal, so leading amplitude-squared scaling is natural; these tests do not establish exact amplitude-squared scaling at the stated tolerance. See state-difference.csv.

## Why Newton appears and where the scalar changes it
The independent spherical calculation evolves geometrical mass with pressure work and energy flow; it does not add a Newton force to the metric. For uncharged cold dust, ṁ=0 and R̈=−m/R² follows exactly before shells cross. The initially resting neutral detector goes from 6 to 5.99886266869. Extremal charged matter is a different check: the matter is in electrostatic balance but neutral test bodies fall, and the exterior R̈=−M/R²+Q²/R³. The smooth mass implementation also recovers the exact *change* of the central scale in both cases, where the original mass quadrature did not. This cancellation of pressure/flux for neutral dust is a limiting mechanism; the numerical value of G is an input.

In the scalar case the pressure and energy flux are calculated from the field itself. At the last clock the local mass attraction is about −5.62205×10⁻⁵ and the scalar pressure force +1.91077×10⁻⁶ in the chosen units. More telling than the total acceleration: subtracting the known Coulomb tail by forming m+Q²/(2R), the enclosed energy changes by −1.19817×10⁻⁸; about −1.14914×10⁻⁸ comes from scalar transport, the remainder from scalar pressure work. A fitted rescaling of Newton's attraction would not determine that energy movement. See WHY-NEWTON-APPEARS.md and mass-force-ledger.csv.

## Magnetic calibration survives the centre, not as a universal conversion
With both source strengths fixed by equal signed polar fields at time 3, the ratio of the second to first ruler signal is 0.3088973 at time 4 and **0.2884878 at 6.4**. The late signed magnetic-field ratio is instead **−0.1322331**. The change through the centre is verified separately against the independent emitted-wave bank, regular Maxwell and transverse-traceless gravity waves, and two different readouts of volume. All eight absolute source/state cases meet their applicable accuracy tests.

The Poynting-flux lower bound accounts for 99.99497% and 99.99929% of the **direct dipole contribution** at the end for the two currents. The dipole itself accounts for just **15.61%** and **8.21%** of the total directional signals. This is what a flux measurement can bound without assuming that radiation accounts for all of the tide. The tightly resolved positive remainder uses quadrature split at changes of sign, not an interpolation through absolute-value cusps. See resolved-flux-bound.csv and focusing-ledger.csv.

A third prescribed current, the difference of the first and the previously calibrated second, has a magnetic null at time 3 but predicts a final polar field **1.1322331 times the first source's field**, and a direct electromagnetic volume contribution of 2.16508×10⁻¹⁰. This is a coherent prediction from known first-order fields; we have not obtained its full second-order ruler by subtracting the two completed experiments. That mixed response is the next distinct test. See MAGNETIC-NULL-SOURCE.md and magnetic-null-predictions.csv.

## Numerical limits worth keeping
The direct geodesic ADM spatial discretization at the origin was unstable even in flat tests. Replacing the problematic derivative using the physical momentum constraint and independently evolving the connection fixed the measured burst and exact-wave tests. The smooth realization of the *same* initial source brings the spherical core Hamiltonian residual from about 1.3×10⁻⁴ to below 1.8×10⁻⁹ on the fine grid. The radial auxiliary-connection residual, measured against that small connection within the core, still reaches 0.154%; it does not meet 0.002%. Do not infer uniform componentwise accuracy from agreement at the detector. The weak-source subtractions and original exact-dust core misses are kept in the accuracy receipt. The raw unstable prototype is retained as a negative control.

Across the 48 saved numerical packets: 168 structural checks; 285 of 305 numerical comparisons pass the original relative tolerance. The 20 misses comprise five central auxiliary-connection comparisons, seven dust/centre comparisons on the original quadrature seed, five weak-subtraction comparisons and three coarse-grid manufactured Maxwell comparisons. All controlled mass-equation comparisons pass. On the fine scalar run, the radial mass constraint and geometrical reconstruction differ by 2.4×10⁻¹² and 3.2×10⁻¹² respectively or less on their specified scales; scalar energy balance after removing the Coulomb tail closes to 2.9×10⁻¹⁰ of the actual change.
