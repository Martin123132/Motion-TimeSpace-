# What local Poynting measurements can and cannot tell the ruler
On the same falling proper-time clock let T and S be the signed azimuthal electric and meridional magnetic amplitudes of the axial dipole at the equator, and Z the radial magnetic amplitude at the pole. In the parent's geometrical-field units the equatorial radial Poynting amplitude is F=−TS. The direct Maxwell energy contribution to pole-minus-equator focusing is Z²−T²−S²; the negative of this drives the directional logarithmic volume. Therefore there is an **amplitude-calibrated lower bound on that part of the signal**:
    L_dip(τ) ≥ ∫₀ᵗ (τ−s)[2|F(s)|−Z(s)²] ds,
    L_dip(τ) − lower bound = ∫₀ᵗ (τ−s)(|T(s)|−|S(s)|)² ds ≥ 0.
This follows from the Maxwell stress and the geodesic focusing equation, not a fit. It does not require purely outgoing radiation, a far-zone approximation, or identifying all of the gravitational tide with electromagnetic energy. The dipole contribution is initialized with zero volume and velocity; the propagation kernel is the twice-integrated acceleration. At this order the spherical background stretches and the induced geometry contribute through the other focusing terms in the same free-fall slicing. The two emitted currents may be compared with the previously fixed magnetic normalization, but the inequality itself requires no knowledge of the unknown spin amplitude if the fields are measured.
