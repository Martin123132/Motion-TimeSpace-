# What could actually be measured?
The released test is a comparison of two clouds of neutral freely falling neighbours, with synchronized local proper times, at the pole and equator of the same initially round sphere. The ratio of their volume changes is independent of the arbitrary small spin normalization when the two specified sources have been matched by the early magnetic measurement. The late ratio is a **prediction for those two prepared sources**, not for every emitter with the same magnetic amplitude. Its applicability requires independently knowing the mass/charge distribution, preparation and common scale.

There is also a dimensioned check which does *not* require knowing the toy spin: measure signed equatorial flux, the two transverse field magnitudes and the polar magnetic field over the proper-time exposure. The Maxwell contribution can then be calculated or bounded in SI by the local squared-field relations in POYNTING-AND-RULER.md. In the ideal null-wave limit with negligible polar field and constant equatorial flux S, the direct directional fractional volume at one exposure duration t is 4πG S t²/c³. As a scale example only, 1 kW/m² for 1 second gives approximately 3.1×10⁻³². No apparatus or observation is being claimed. A larger total volume signal need not be excess electromagnetic coupling: the geometric shear and induced field have their own terms in the ledger. Resolving the MTS scalar would demand a source and noise model plus independent constraints on those terms.

To assign seconds to the synthetic clock, specify a physical length unit L; time is L/c, mass is c²L/G in kg and charge is L c²√(4πε₀/G) in coulombs, all multiplying their tabulated geometrical values. If the retained scalar mass term is fixed, its code value μ=1 corresponds to L=ℏ/(m_scalar c); L cannot also be tuned arbitrarily to a prospective source. No SI scalar mass or real source is inferred from these simulations. G is used here as the calibrated gravitational coupling, not claimed as derived by this experiment.
