# Two input waves now predict a third on the retained backgrounds

**Main result.** With the same incoming primordial amplitudes and no initial sum mode at z=2, p=0.25 and q=1/Mpc at cos(angle)=0.6 generate a present-day clustered-matter density coefficient **0.32724 in MTS versus 1.22664 in the same-background all-cold control** (matched ΛCDM: 1.27857). These are transfer coefficients, not measured density contrasts; multiply by the two small input amplitudes. A separate direct wave-plus-dust calculation reproduces the self-consistent second-order trajectory within 1.4e-7 of peak response. Rotating the waves reverses the sign for the obtuse triangle in all three models. The large-scale pair instead has a heavy/cold ratio 0.99772. Using the same denominator for both models matters.

The main ratio 0.26678 is nearly the product of the two inherited linear amplitude ratios (0.98314 and 0.27137). Turning off pressure **only after z=2** while retaining the original heavy initial fields changes the autonomous coefficient from 0.327236 to 0.327287. The large loss in this triangle is principally inherited structure, not a newly discovered late nonlinear force. Native-driven and autonomous present-day heavy coefficients agree to about 1.2e-5 fractionally for this preparation, not identically in all tests.

## A three-point contrast not just due to power reweighting

Four complete native triangle shapes, five starting epochs and three measurement epochs were evaluated with all cyclic terms and two explicitly different initializations; see tree-bispectrum.csv and attribution.csv. The (0.075,0.1,0.125)/Mpc triangle at z=2 passes the common Δ²<0.2 screen. Its conditional reduced heavy/cold three-point contrast is +0.0926%, but almost all of that is reweighting the existing powers. The difference attributed to its individual mode-coupling kernels is below our numerical comparison target. Don't advertise that triangle as new independent evidence.

Equilateral triangles eliminate this particular ambiguity exactly. At **z=8, k=1.98096/Mpc**, the maximum Δ² across the three backgrounds is 0.144. For a matter-era growing initialization at z=99 the predicted reduced three-point amplitude is **Q=0.64636 heavy versus 0.57242 cold**, +12.92%. The direct, finite-amplitude Schrödinger/dust replay of the corresponding autonomous system gives 0.6463603 against the autonomous kernel's 0.6463603; the native-driven result is 0.6463619. The larger *normalized* response is not a stronger absolute signal: B is 5.02e-5 versus 0.229 Mpc⁶ because the heavy linear power is much smaller. This is a real conditional prediction of the specified equations, not a measurement or a claim to 13% observational precision. The Δ² screen does not bound loops, astrophysical bias or unmodeled species.

## What the starting state costs

Moving the growing initialization from z=99 to 49 changes the high-k contrast from +12.92% to +9.95%; imposing zero second order at z=99 instead gives +8.97%. Starting as late as z=19 changes it further. These are comparison preparations, **not an uncertainty interval**. An adjoint calculation supplies the row mapping arbitrary initial errors to this test. In the specified dimensionless initial-density/velocity norm a joint change of 0.497 across both branches can exactly erase the 0.07395 gap in Q (0.785 if only the heavy state changes). No prior says such a change is likely or unlikely. This gives a quantitative target for an early-time derivation instead of assuming the EdS choice.

A separate density-only test combined 13 intrinsic matter epochs between z=8 and 3 to remove independent unknown homogeneous seeds in both models. The six observable initial-state directions remove essentially all the raw difference in this construction; at the largest knot the remaining norm is 9.7e-7 in Q units and changes by 1.4e-6 upon thinning the saved histories. **That residual is not resolved.** This is not a survey signal-to-noise estimate, and it does not mean every high-redshift test is degenerate.

## Checks and continuation

- The common-metric Ψ was recovered separately from the curvature potential Φ; no no-slip axiom was imposed. For the primary modes over z≤2, max |Φ−Ψ| in units of peak |Ψ| is about 1.4e-7. This is a cosmological weak-field check, not a local Solar System precision estimate.
- The two conservative/primitive formulations agree after the explicitly derived first-order projection residual. The production EdS dust test agrees with its analytic finite-start transient to 2e-15; the adjoint agrees with the independently integrated primary response to 5e-11 fractionally. A direct two-dimensional replay checks the primary and high-redshift triangles including their second-order velocity prescription.
- Native linear inputs include neutrinos, radiation and the rolling source. These species' *second-order* responses and baryon pressure are not supplied by the dust-plus-wave calculation. For the main modes the cumulative baryon Euler residual relative to peak baryon flow reaches about 1.1e-4 from z=99 in the heavy branch. Differentiated single-precision transfer samples can look worse than their integrated balances. These physical and input limitations are separate from numerical convergence.
