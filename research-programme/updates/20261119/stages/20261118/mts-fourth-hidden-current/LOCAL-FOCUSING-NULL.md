# Change what is measured, not how many currents are guessed

Three magnetic snapshots and one gravitational clock do not exhaust what the local field equations predict. Let L be the directional pole-minus-equator log-volume of the initially round geodesic ruler. In the retained spin convention write its sampled forcing as G (even geometric expansion/shear), X (axial metric contribution), P (polar electric stress), D (direct electromagnetic wave stress), M (material term), and H (direct scalar stress). The actual equations give L''=G+X+P+D+M+H. These are signed directional contributions, not six positive energies. H=−contrast(2π_proper²−h²) in the normalization of this action. The matched ruler has zero initial L and L'. Hence

    N(τ)=L_metric(τ)−∫₀ᵗ(τ−s)[G+X+P+D+M](s) ds = ∫₀ᵗ(τ−s) H(s) ds.

On the ordinary branch, H=0 identically, regardless of the radial current profile, not just for four polynomials. This is conditional on the retained equations and availability of **local kinematics and non-scalar stress**; it is not a consequence of the early magnetometer readings alone. A scalar can also give a zero at a particular clock or for a special preparation. Other unmodelled matter could give a residual, so a nonzero N is not uniquely a discovery of an MTS scalar. Importantly the GR null is an equation identity, not a fitted baseline and not a magic way to derive G.

Test it on the newly evolved impostors and cost-matched pair. Use the independent metric determinant, not just the variable propagated by the focusing equation. Reintegrate the time-weighted stress with independent Simpson quadrature and use the actual measured B_polar, E_equator and B_equator to reconstruct D=E_equator²+B_equator²−B_polar² rather than squaring an inferred source profile. Retain the separately computed polar electric response P: first-order magnetic measurements do not supply it. The shear and polar-electric channels are geometry/field readouts in the simulation, not independent telescope measurements. For the general test retain the axial and material contributions. The exterior reduction may replace X by 2(Q B_polar/R)² only where J_internal vanishes: the code has X=2[2(Q A_phi−J_internal)/R³]², B_polar=2A_phi/R². Test this replacement with the measured spherical Coulomb flux (sampled every 0.2 from the mass packet) and areal radius, and test the effect of omitting residual local material. Do not assume that reduction for an arbitrary environment.

Preserve both the zero-ordinary false-positive floor and the scalar recovery error on the nominal scalar-channel scale; do not divide by the much larger total ruler or by an expected zero. Compare the two grids and both ray and determinant. This is a diagnostic designed using known field equations and existing simulations, not an astronomical detection or a blinded observational test.

**How accurate would the local channels have to be?** The endpoint tables give conditional error allowances, not observational error bars. If each signed G and P readout, each of the three field amplitudes, Q and R has fractional error at most e<1 relative to its true value at each sample, the absolute error of the stress subtraction for an exterior ray is at most the time-weighted integral of

    e(|G|+|P|) + e(2+e)(E_eq²+B_eq²+B_p²) + [(1+e)⁴/(1−e)²−1] X_ext.

This follows by squaring the amplitude intervals and using the positive time kernel, not by assuming statistically independent errors. On the saved regular clocks the composite Simpson weights are positive. The bound is conservative since B_p enters two terms. Add both arms’ allowances and two absolute errors for the directional ruler. The budget reserves the observed coarse/fine readout change and both fine single-arm identity residuals first; the reserve is a numerical stress test, not a proved continuum truncation bound. Report two different margins: resolution of the sign and recovery of its amplitude to 2e−5. Zero allowance at the latter means numerical error alone exhausts the target. Allowances concern inferred local channels, not raw instrument calibration on a much larger common background; no independence of shear and the metric has been demonstrated in data. Unresolved intervening material would require an additional independently bounded term.
