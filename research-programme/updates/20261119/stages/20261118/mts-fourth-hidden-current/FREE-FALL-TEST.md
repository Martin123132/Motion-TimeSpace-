# A second, physically different observable: proper-time fall without knowing the mass or charge

Use the unspun spherical backgrounds already in both grids’ ordinary and scalar packets, not the spin-squared directional ruler. Do not count the four current trials as independent spherical samples: their spherical equations are identical on the same arm. This is a retrospective test on controlled numerical histories, not blinded observational data. The final radius and velocity of the two backgrounds have been seen; freeze the times and rules here before computing the shape scores. No new evolution or fitted scalar parameters.

For spherical electrovacuum in the retained normalization, neutral, initially stationary, freely falling rulers obey R''=−M/R²+Q²/R³ with constant exterior mass M and charge Q. This follows from the ss Einstein equation and the Maxwell mass balance derived in the retained WHY-NEWTON-APPEARS.md, not a new force. One first integral gives, at positive proper time on an inward portion,
    
    x=(R0−R)/(R0 R),   Y=(R')²/x,   Y=a+b x,
    b=−Q²≤0,   a=2M−2Q²/R0.

Thus ANY constant M and real Q must put the observations on one nonincreasing straight line. Q=0 gives a horizontal line with ordinate 2M, the first integral of Newton's inverse-square fall. The measured observable uses only areal radius, proper time and an established rest release. No oscillatory-current weights, Einstein-RHS acceleration, enclosed-mass history, pressure subtraction, or magnetic inputs enter the primary score. A charged test particle, nongeodesic support, a nonzero launch velocity, radial flux, material near the detector, a cosmological term or significant multipoles would change the null. This does not assert that arbitrary ordinary matter is excluded. In a real experiment inclination, light travel, areal-radius calibration, rest preparation and any extra stresses would need independent treatment.
