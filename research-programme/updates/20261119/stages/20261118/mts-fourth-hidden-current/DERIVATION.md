# What one unmeasured component can hide

Use the previous regular common-metric, charged-material, Maxwell and scalar field equations without changing the force or adding a scalar coupling. Change only the radial formation profile of the balanced material current. For x=N/M let J=(32M/3)x³(1−x)³∑ₖ₌₀³ vₖ(2x−1)ᵏ, ℓ=∂J/∂N. Every basis current has zero end-to-end angular momentum because both endpoint values of J vanish. The kinetic cost below is the rotational cost at retained spin order, not the total rest or gravitational energy of the system.

**An exact magnetic null in the preparation space.** On each arm the three early signed polar readings obey d=D v[0:3]+p v₃. Obtain p by actually evolving the fourth law and recording only the field; concurrently propagate a passive second axial wave on the same spherical RK stages and validate it against the locked second current. No late gravity selects the null. If D is invertible, all weight vectors giving the same d can be written c+a n, with n=(−D⁻¹p,1) and c the previously calibrated source with no fourth component. These nulls need not be identical on the two physical arms. Freeze the fine weights and carry them unchanged onto the coarse grid; the small coarse residual is checked, not labelled exactly zero. The weak-spin approximation is an expansion in overall rotation. Extremely large shape weights can invalidate a finite-rotation interpretation; kinetic costs help expose this.

**The full allowed late ruler along the line.** On either arm solve the pure null and c+n as actual material currents including their initial squared stress. B(a)=B(c)+a B(n). For every clock and each quadratic focusing/readout component,

    L(a)=L(c)+a [ L(c+n)−L(c)−L(n) ]+a² L(n).

Use a separate directly evolved impostor which did not construct these coefficients. A target L* outside the parabola's range has no real solution; a target inside normally has two, not necessarily comparable in cost. The branch through a=0 obeys a=2(L*−L(c))/[b+sign(b)√(b²+4q(L*−L(c)))] when the discriminant is positive. This need not minimize energy among real roots.

**Physical energy accounting.** At the retained order the launch rotational cost W=vᵀEv has Eᵢⱼ=(1/3)∫D(r)ℓᵢℓⱼ dr, D=D₀(1−r²/25)⁶ inside r=5, zero beyond. N/M is a known polynomial in r/5, so all four-mode Gram entries are obtained by exact rational polynomial integration, then independently checked by radial quadrature; its 3×3 block must equal the previous exact result. Along a null W=W₀+2a nᵀEc+a² nᵀEn, with positive last coefficient. A known absolute cost fixes up to two states per arm, a cap restricts to an interval, and merely requiring both arms to have the same unknown cost is a different question. No claim that the kinetic minimizer is selected by formation.

For two freely adjustable hidden amplitudes a and b, let gₒ(a)=gₛ(b) be the final-ruler equality and wₒ(a)=wₛ(b) a further equality (launch cost or time-6 ruler). In the generic case define G=gₒ(a)−gₛ₀, W=wₒ(a)−wₛ₀, d=wₛ₂gₛ₁−gₛ₂wₛ₁. Then b=(wₛ₂G−gₛ₂W)/d. Substitution into G=gₛ₁b+gₛ₂b² gives a polynomial of degree at most four. Isolate *all* real roots of the polynomial over exact rationals representing the computed coefficients. Each root is substituted back into both original equations. Certifying root counts for numerical response coefficients does not certify a continuum solution; check both grids with unchanged source weights and independently propagated states.

**A time measurement that projects out hidden amplitudes.** The vector of fourteen late ruler readings on each arm is m+a u+a²v. For a pair of arms with independent amplitudes, choose weights orthogonal to all four nuisance vectors; a weighted model difference then depends only on the earlier field readings, not on either hidden amplitude. The projection of the nominal difference onto that orthogonal complement determines the weights without detector data. For three late magnetic samples the corresponding space has only two nuisance columns. Compute the projection rationally on the saved binary data and scale by the sum of absolute weights: correlated sample errors never enjoy an automatic √N reduction. A coarse-only design tests transfer to fine; a separate ruler design nulls both grids' columns simultaneously. A very small algebraically nonzero projected gap can still be unusable. Direct full runs, alternate readouts, grid disagreement and explicit finite-cost leakage are the relevant resolution checks. A gravity-only statistic conditioned on the early fields is not by itself a tolerance on those fields. The EM statistic also admits a calculated sensitivity to independent errors in the early magnetic calibrations since it is linear.

**Link to the ordinary limit.** See WEAK-LIMIT.md: differentiating the three-field calibration and final ruler with respect to z=A², at zero scalar amplitude, predicts the ordinary hidden current needed to imitate the scalar. The derivative of the residual *after* that matching is fixed at every other time. This distinguishes convergence of the dynamics toward ordinary charged gravity from distinguishability of the chosen instruments: a final clock can be degenerate even while the equations and the rest of the time history differ.
