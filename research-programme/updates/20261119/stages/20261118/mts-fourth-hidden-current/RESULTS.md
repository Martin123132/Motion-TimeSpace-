# What a hidden current can imitate, and what the clocks still tell us

Private local stage, 19 November 2026. Same regular η=.8 preparation, same scalar and ordinary common-metric equations, both original grids, unchanged 2×10⁻⁵ target. There are two different observables below: a directional spin-squared ruler, and a spherical freely falling ruler at zero spin. They are not interchangeable. Neither is an observation of a galaxy.

## A real counterexample to the three-current inference

Within three current shapes the previous sign certificate still stands. Allow both models the next independent balanced current and hold exactly the same three signed early magnetic fields fixed. An ordinary current can now imitate the nominal scalar final directional ruler. It costs **0.9966384 times** the ordinary reference rotational energy, not an enormous or fitted extra energy. Its directly evolved final log-volume differs by −1.10×10⁻²¹ on the fine grid. Its reading at proper time 6 instead differs by +7.1925×10⁻¹³ and its final magnetic field by −7.7355×10⁻¹⁰. A finite ambiguity is not a claim that every source is physically selected.

Even enforcing equal but unknown cost on both arms leaves a directly verified imitation pair (both 0.5008921 times reference): final ray difference +1.44×10⁻²¹, end magnetic difference +1.5230×10⁻⁸. That pair matches its own two endpoints, not necessarily the nominal scalar endpoint. By contrast an independent measurement that fixes cost exactly at the ordinary reference leaves an absolute final-ruler separation 4.0733×10⁻¹²; a symmetric cost half-width of 0.16708% about this reference first lets the allowed bands touch, before ruler or early-field measurement error. Do not assume anyone measured this cost. See nominal-imitation-roots.csv, joint-clock-results.csv and launch-accounting.json.

The tuned source varies continuously through the ordinary limit: the derived coefficient is da/d(A²)=−0.01163804, with three early magnetic channels held fixed. The predicted final scalar ruler change per A² is −4.2641395×10⁻¹². Direct A=1/8,1/2,1 checks of this latter coefficient differ by 1.60×10⁻⁷, 2.92×10⁻⁶, 1.17×10⁻⁵ respectively. The simple linear prediction for the compensating source misses the unchanged tolerance even at 1/8 (2.56×10⁻⁵); retaining the known ordinary quadratic response in the inversion reduces the respective errors to 1.60×10⁻⁷, 2.92×10⁻⁶, 1.17×10⁻⁵. The full time-dependent magnetic residual is not uniformly accurate at that tolerance. See WEAK-LIMIT.md and near-ordinary-limit.csv.

## A local measurement independent of the guessed current

The determinant of the directional ruler minus the time-integrated local geometric, Maxwell and material terms measures the remaining scalar focusing in the retained system. The ordinary equation gives zero whatever the internal current is; measuring three earlier fields does not by itself give all these local channels. For the directly evolved ordinary imitation versus nominal scalar, the independently reconstructed final contrast is −4.312265×10⁻¹²; the direct scalar stress gives −4.312284×10⁻¹². Dropping shear or even just the polar-electric response produces a spurious ordinary signal much larger than this. The separately integrated material at this exterior probe is negligible, not presumed zero everywhere.

The paired recovery benefits from cancellation: the absolute ordinary false signal is about 5.7×10⁻¹⁵. Accounting for both individual errors and the grid change consumes 0.265% of the predicted contrast (0.297% for the common-cost pair), enough to resolve its sign in these runs, not the unchanged relative precision goal. For the ordinary imitation versus nominal scalar and an exact ruler, about 0.169% uniform fractional channel allowance remains for **sign** resolution under the assumptions in LOCAL-FOCUSING-NULL.md. This is not a measured instrument precision. Pure projections of fourteen directional clocks or three late magnetic clocks can also separate particular simulated pairs, but on much smaller scales and not uniformly to tolerance; see detector-stress-test.csv.

## A clock test that does not need the launch current, mass or charge

For a neutral spherical free-fall probe released at rest, let x=1/R−1/R₀ and Y=(dR/dτ)²/x. The ordinary spherical electrovacuum equations give a straight, nonincreasing line in x for **any** constant mass and real charge; zero charge gives Y=2M. Six saved radii and speeds derived from radius versus proper time test the line. The best physical line's largest ordinate error is 6.94×10⁻¹² on the ordinary control but **2.82×10⁻⁵ (0.693% of the peak ordinate)** on the scalar preparation. This is minimization over all such constants, not a mismatch at somebody else's chosen parameter values.

Permitting an additional arbitrary constant vacuum stress and even allowing unphysical signs for mass and charge-squared still leaves a best possible maximum discrepancy of **2.2512×10⁻⁵ (0.553% of the peak ordinate)**. The same calculation on the ordinary arm gives 4.32×10⁻¹². Four times (3,4,5,6.4) suffice to certify the scalar discrepancy; its combined numerical stress from the two grids, the alternative velocity reconstructions and the ordinary floor is 2.09×10⁻⁷ of the discrepancy. Independently, taking only the first three readings to predict time 6.4 gives Y=0.00445267 versus 0.00404081 measured on the scalar radius history. The analogous ordinary prediction misses by 2.96×10⁻¹¹. The numbers here are in retained solver units, not redshifts or astronomical distances.

Can a measurement resolve it? With independent bounded errors of 3.06×10⁻⁷ in each radius (initial radius 6) and 0.0696% in each speed, the four-clock bound retains about 49.8% of its nominal size. If the same noisy radii are also differentiated to get speeds, the conservative radius-only limiting half-width is much tighter, about 4.62×10⁻⁹ in these units. Assumes accurate proper times and a known rest release. The analytic error propagation and its zero-noise check are in FREE-FALL-TEST.md and free-fall-input-precision.csv.

**What supplies the signal?** Using only release data and the saved local pressure and energy-flux histories as inputs, a reduced forward mass-and-radius evolution predicts 2.25123077×10⁻⁵ versus the geometric 2.25123219×10⁻⁵. On that trajectory the exact energy integral assigns **99.984%** of the signed four-clock signal to direct non-Coulomb radial pressure and 0.016% to the changing charge-corrected enclosed mass. Holding the flux history but removing the pressure leaves only 3.42×10⁻⁹. Removing both recovers the electrovacuum null. These deletions are conditional diagnostics, not new self-consistent matter trajectories. The primary quintic reconstruction agrees within 6.28×10⁻⁷ relative; cubic interpolation of the same 33 saved times and quintic at doubled spacing miss the 2×10⁻⁵ target (2.92×10⁻⁵, 3.73×10⁻⁵). Do not infer an independent uniform source-interpolation guarantee. See CLOCK-SOURCE-BALANCE.md and clock-source-balance.csv.

**Interpretation:** the scalar preparation produces *time-dependent radial stress*. The fall history distinguishes it from every tested static mass/charge/constant-vacuum combination, not from GR with a matter source carrying the same stress. Nor does one radial trajectory rule out an arbitrary radial mass or pressure profile. The geometry and source readouts are different extractions from the same simulation; there is no sky detection here.
