# What drives the fall test in the parent equations?

A shape difference is not yet a source explanation. In the spherical common metric let R be areal radius, v its proper-time derivative, m the Misner–Sharp mass, Q the locally measured constant charge, p the non-Coulomb radial pressure, and j_eff=j R_x/a the non-Coulomb energy flux with outward sign. Write M_e=m+Q²/(2R). Subtracting the radial Maxwell stress exactly from the parent mass/force equations gives

    R'=v,    v'=−M_e/R²+Q²/R³−Rp,    M_e'=−R²(pv+j_eff).

At release v=0, M0=M_e(0). Consequently the clock ordinate has a source integral: Y−[2M0−2Q²/R0−Q²x] equals (2/x) times the integral of v[−(M_e−M0)/R²−Rp] in proper time. This explains why changing the constant mass or charge cannot in general imitate a time-dependent stress/flux. For p=j_eff=0 the ordinary line follows; if also Q=0, this is the Newton fall law. These are consequences of the retained metric action, not a derivation of its absolute coupling.
