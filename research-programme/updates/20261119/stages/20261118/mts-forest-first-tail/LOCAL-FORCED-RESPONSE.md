# What does the retained matter do in a weak static potential?

Use the positive quadratic real pole already diagonalized in ../MTS-HEAVY-BATH-COUNTERFLOW-AND-GALACTIC-STRESS.md. Work to first order in an externally prescribed lapse perturbation Ψ at a **nonzero** physical Fourier wavenumber K; c=ℏ=1. The background patch is locally uniform, the carrier is h₀=A cos(mt), and expansion and background curvature are neglected over the patch. We are deriving a fixed-metric material response, not solving the complete stationary Einstein problem in a medium. Ordinary sources and the rolling field have not been assigned a galaxy population.

From (□−m²)h=0 in ds²=−(1+2Ψ)dt²+(1−2Φ)dx², the spatial perturbation obeys δḧ+(m²+K²)δh=(Ψ̇+3Φ̇)ḣ₀−2m²Ψh₀. The static forced particular solution is δh=−2(m/K)² Ψ h₀. This is exact in K/m at linear order for this patch: it does not extrapolate a Schrödinger equation through the Compton scale. The expansion requires both |Ψ|≪1 and 2(m/K)²|Ψ|≪1. Uniform K=0 is excluded; a constant lapse also changes the reference clock. Rapid metric corrections from the oscillating background stress are excluded in this imposed-metric calculation.

For a local orthonormal static observer, δρ=ḣ₀δḣ+m²h₀δh−Ψḣ₀², δp=ḣ₀δḣ−m²h₀δh−Ψḣ₀². Anisotropic stress vanishes at this order because the unperturbed spatial gradient vanishes. With ρ₀=m²A²/2, the one-carrier-period means are
    δρ/ρ₀ = −[1+4m²/K²] Ψ,       δp/ρ₀ = −Ψ.
In particular ∇δp=−ρ₀∇Ψ, as required by static mean stress balance. The unperturbed carrier pressure averages to zero. A strictly empty quadratic mode (A=0) has **no linear stress response**; occupation is not an adjustable force coupling. For equal homogeneous density and different carrier phases the stationary susceptibilities are identical. A gas of particles with a different momentum distribution is not thereby covered.

The low-velocity Schrödinger number-density response accounts for −4(m/K)²Ψ. The fraction of the full static *energy-density response* it misses is K²/(4m²+K²); the two densities must not be equated exactly. The missing part is the proper kinetic-energy response to the lapse. The same fraction equals the static ratio δp/δρ. It agrees only to leading small-K order with the propagating envelope pressure coefficient K²/(√(K²+m²)+m)². A forced susceptibility is not a dispersion relation. In particular the slow beat frequency is √(K²+m²)−m rather than K²/(2m) in general. Do not infer arbitrarily strong local suppression by taking K/m→∞ inside the nonrelativistic theory.

There is a preparation test, not a hidden equilibrium assumption. A source switched on at a carrier maximum with unperturbed initial field and velocity gives the linear response proportional to cos(mt)−cos(√(m²+K²)t), with the same coefficient as the static particular solution. The homogeneous part persists without damping. Other phases or switch histories can excite additional initial velocity; the static quotient alone does not determine them. Matter response can be computed for a specified history, but no galactic occupation is selected in this calculation.

For comparison, *within* the nonrelativistic and Newtonian domain, linearize the common-metric Schrödinger–Poisson system about a uniform occupied patch. For physical frequency ω and wavenumber K define S=ℏ²K⁴/(4m²), g_h=4πGρ_h. Then δ̈_h+(S−g_h)δ_h=F_b, where F_b=−K²Φ_b is the imposed baryonic tidal divergence. The measured divergence is F=F_b+g_hδ_h. For static forcing with no free oscillation, (F−F_b)/F_b=g_h/(S−g_h). For |ω|≤η√S and g_h<(1−η²)S, the frequency-domain induced correction is bounded by g_h/[(1−η²)S−g_h]. This is a bound on the **particular response**, not a bound on incoming waves, persistent homogeneous solutions, modes near the Jeans scale or local background tides. A suddenly applied constant source with zero initial perturbation can reach twice the static correction. At resonance there is no such bounded inverse. This separates a universal metric coupling from a preparation-dependent total force.

The relativistic material susceptibility does not by itself supply a high-K effective Poisson coupling. Computing an order-Gρ₀Ψ correction there also requires metric/background curvature terms at that same order, constraints, an actual environment and boundary conditions. The finite spectral bank tests the regime of these approximations for its own conditional population; transporting that population into a galaxy or the Solar System is not justified.
