# What survives more general gas physics?

The finite-support cancellation does not require a deterministic relation between density and velocity. At each large k a stochastic density and velocity give angular power P₀(k)+P₁(k)x+P₂(k)x², positive for the actual angular bins; their cross-spectrum is allowed either sign. Orthogonality removes all three terms. Even a wildly different unresolved density spectrum cannot by itself spoil this identity.

There is a useful conditional extension: suppose the nonnegative high-k contribution at each k is an angular polynomial of degree ℓ times exp(−u(k)x). The robust seven-bin weights annihilate exp(−u₀x)x^j through j=5. Multiplying the angular polynomial by the Taylor polynomial of exp[−(u(k)−u₀)x] through p=5−ℓ still gives zero. For s=max|u(k)−u₀| and 0<x≤1 this yields the pointwise bound e^s s^(p+1)/(p+1)! times the sum of the actual absolute-weighted high-k contributions. Positivity allows replacement of that unobserved sum with Σ|w_i|P_i(total) after integration. The same argument permits positive mixtures of such components if their widths satisfy the bound. At 10% thermal-width-parameter variation s≈0.0521: for quadratic angular **power** the guarantee is 3.23e-7; for quartic power it weakens to about 0.00143. This is not a bound without a uniform width/degree assumption. An unbounded velocity dispersion, arbitrary velocity-angle dependence or correlated components with different damping cannot simply be put under it.

Because the measured wavenumbers are in velocity units, x is common across cosmologies and u does not contain an extra Hubble calibration. A redshift distribution that is identical for all bins can be integrated after applying the argument at each redshift above the largest radial threshold. Unequal redshift weights or survey bin convolution generally break the point-center identity. The proper nuisance matrix is built from the actual window acting on the columns; using centers and calling that correction calibrated is not justified. Simulated log-bin window tests in window-controls.csv are sensitivity tests only.

In the ideal continuum the third derivative of exp(u x)P(x) eliminates a common quadratic exterior power, but numerical differentiation of noisy data would amplify its noise. The preselected linear contrasts do not require differentiation of observed spectra. With seven point bins and a known width one has four independent linear contrasts. One may use their shape independently of an overall flux normalization if they have measurable nonzero amplitudes. With two distinct specified widths six nuisance columns are generally independent: there is only **one** simultaneous exact contrast left, not two for an amplitude-free ratio. In particular a single number cannot by itself distinguish arbitrary thermal history, normalization and field preparation.
