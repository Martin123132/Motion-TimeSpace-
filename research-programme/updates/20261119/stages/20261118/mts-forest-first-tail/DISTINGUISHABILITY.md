# What the contrast can actually measure

Let p_i be the projected power, C the published covariance, and A the matrix of nuisance columns for the unresolved angular power. Select columns of U spanning the left null of A. Then y=Uᵀp has covariance V=UᵀCU. For known normalizations the largest possible predicted signal-to-noise from these linear tail-independent contrasts is the norm of Uᵀ(p_off−p_wave) in the V⁻¹ metric. It is invariant under every nonsingular change of contrast basis. It is **not** the significance of any observed result. Both model branches are compared to the same assumed error matrix, not to measured fluxes.

There is a stricter question than whether two fixed predictions differ. If the true wave amplitude is held at its fiducial value but the rival has unknown nonnegative flux-power normalization r, minimize ||y_wave−r y_off||². The stationary solution is r=(y_offᵀV⁻¹y_wave)/(y_offᵀV⁻¹y_off), clamped to zero if negative. Reverse the roles separately. This does not fit observations. It tests whether the calculated difference is *shape* information after allowing a normalization. If both true and alternative amplitudes can go to zero, there is no unconditional positive lower bound on distinguishability.

At identical temperature there are four independent point-bin contrasts in seven bins. At unequal specified temperatures the simultaneous exact null is generically one-dimensional. If the two resulting signed contrasts have the same sign, the single surviving signal is exactly degenerate with a free positive amplitude of the rival. If the signs differ, a nonnegative rival amplitude cannot match the sign. The case with a common nuisance normalization is different. A degree-five robust single-temperature null also has only one linear contrast; its controlled thermal leakage is not a second measured shape. No assumed bound on the size of cosmic small-scale power enters the exact column cancellation.

This argument relaxes the allowed nuisance moments to a whole linear space: positive-semidefinite Gram matrices would be more restrictive. Hence this is only an assessment of *linear guaranteed-null* tests; a joint positive-power likelihood could conceivably use additional information. Redshift distributions, true spectral bin windows, nonlinear forest physics, flux normalization and thermal history are not calibrated by these calculations.

# Newtonian and common-metric connection

The saved relativistic variables are number-density coordinates δ_i−3Φ for the leading wave/off/cold equations. At the initial epoch use the returned metric to restore δ_i, leave the peculiar velocities unchanged, and evolve the Newtonian continuity and Euler equations in the same expansion. Test density-only Poisson sourcing including the supplied external perturbations and slip; compare also a clustered-density-only potential and a separately labeled comoving-density correction. Do not count 3Φ twice in the density output. Photon drag is retained in every arm. These are subhorizon cosmological reductions on the same physical initial data, not local galaxy boundary conditions.

One can see the mechanism without relying on a parameter fit. Freeze a, remove external forcing, expansion drag and photon friction just for an instantaneous eigenmode diagnostic. In conformal-time units write g_i=4πG a²ρ_i and S_i=(0,k²c_b²,k⁴/(4a²m²)) for ordinary dust, gas and the leading heavy wave; set S_h=0 in the matched off branch. Density accelerations obey δ_i″=Σg_jδ_j−S_iδ_i. Conjugation by diag(√g_i) gives a symmetric rank-one gravitational matrix minus a nonnegative diagonal. For g=Σg_i, f_i=g_i/g and s_i=S_i/g, the positive dimensionless eigenvalue L uniquely solves Σ f_i/(L+s_i)=1 if an unpressured component of positive mass is present. The sum strictly decreases from infinity to zero, and a negative semidefinite matrix plus a rank-one correction has at most one positive eigenvalue. This diagnoses an instantaneous attractive mode; it does not assert a constant growth exponent over cosmic history.

With d=Σ_{s_i=0} f_i one has d≤L≤1. Rewrite the dispersion relation L=d+Σ_{s_i>0} f_i L/(L+s_i); substituting d and 1 in the increasing fractions gives tighter bounds. Relative to dust, the heavy mode's density in this eigenvector is L/(L+s_h): when s_h≫1 the wave contribution to attraction is suppressed without changing Newton's constant. When all S_i vanish, L=1, the relative number modes do not source gravity, and all massive species accelerate in the same potential. The full evolving comparison, not this frozen-time spectrum, tests the accuracy of dropping relativistic terms. Weak metric amplitude and nonlinear density variance are different quantities; report both.
