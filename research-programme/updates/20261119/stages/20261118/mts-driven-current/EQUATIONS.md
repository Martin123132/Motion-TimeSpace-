# Derivation in a moving charged source, not just the electrovac exterior

**Input and order.** Use the same common Einstein metric, canonical Maxwell action and convectively charged dust as in the signed parent. The minimally coupled particle action is −∫dτ+η∫Aμ dxμ per rest mass. This charged-dust current is the usual one, proportional to rest density times four-velocity (e.g. [Rumler, Kleinwächter & Meinel, charged dust discs](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10714-023-03086-8)); no stationary disc solution is assumed. Here the retained unperturbed motion is spherical, with normal radial velocity v, W=(1−v²)^−1/2, F=αa, B=α/a and V=Bv=dr/dt. Let N_r=r²D=w, D the parent's conserved baryon density, Q=ηN, N(0)=0 and (∂t+V∂r)N=0. All angular fields and l below are first-order coefficients per unit of initial spin. Energies are second-order coefficients, divided by the angular normalization 2/3 of the previous stage. The ordinary electric monopole is present initially where η≠0; 'empty' means no incident *transverse* field. The neutral scalar stays in the spherical metric, without assigning it an electric current.

**How the current is fixed.** Write Aφ=A sin²θ, dφ−ωdt−βdr in the metric and C=ω_r−β_t. Axial symmetry makes (uφ+ηAφ)/sin²θ=l(N) constant on the dust worldline. Thus m=l−ηA is the *mechanical* specific angular momentum coefficient, H=α/(Wr²), and Ω−ω−Vβ=Hm is the angular velocity relative to horizontal transport. The axial current is the angular component of the very same jμ=ηρuμ as the monopole; ∂φ of the perturbation vanishes and the original radial charge continuity is unchanged. The current relative to the shifted frame sources the Maxwell wave with η w H m. It is not an additional freely fitted current or a new charge sector. In particular, changing A transfers mechanical angular momentum: (∂t+V∂r)m=−η(A_t+V A_r). The V A_r term is required even for free charges.

**Constraint inside the source.** The enclosed axial gravitational surface angular momentum K obeys K_r=(2/3)(w m−Q A_r): the first term is mechanical and the second is the azimuthal Poynting momentum from the crossed monopole and dipole fields. Integrating from the regular centre gives
    I(N)=∫₀ᴺ l(n)dn,    K=(2/3)(I−QA),    C=4F(QA−I)/r⁴,
    K_t=−(2/3)(V w m+Q A_t).
K_t includes both advected material angular momentum and stress flowing through the surface. The nonzero *interior* twist is part of the correct initial data. Outside the compact source I returns to zero for the chosen counterrotation. There the parent current-free law K=−2QA/3 is recovered without assigning a second exterior spin.

**Maxwell equation with varying Q and moving material.** The electric/rotational Maxwell cross term per angular normalization is Q(ω A_r−β A_t). Vary A before integrating Q as though constant. The derivative gives −QC−Q_r ω+Q_t β, whereas the convective current adds Q_r Ω. Since Q_t=−V Q_r, only the horizontal current and C remain:
    A_t=BP,     P_t=(B A_r)_r−2F A/r² + ηwHm−QC.
After solving the rotational constraint the restoring potential is 2F/r²+η²wH+4FQ²/r⁴. The forcing from the prepared *source*, independent of the unknown emitted A, is S=ηwH l+4FQI/r⁴. It is consistent to prescribe the initial angular momentum; prescribing l−ηA as fixed during the run would silently add an actuator. In the time-domain solver use A=r²b, P=r²p to avoid subtracting singular terms. The regular equation is
    b_t=Bp;
    p_t=B(b_rr+4b_r/r)+B_r b_r−[2F(e−s)+η²wH+4FQ²/r⁴]b+S/r².
Here e−s=ρ+h²+Q²/r⁴ in the retained normalization and the radial Laplacian in brackets tends to 5b_rr at the centre. This uses the radial Einstein constraint; it is not a flat-space approximation to the source. The separately frozen FEM calculation instead solves for A itself and uses neither this central cancellation nor the time-domain stencil.

**Energy and recoil.** Write Y=QA−I. At fixed canonical label the three reduced energies per dr are e_EM=[B(P²+A_r²)+2F A²/r²]/2, e_m=wHm²/2, e_rot=2FY²/r⁴. Radial photon energy flux j=−B²P A_r. The EM energy satisfies
    ∂t e_EM+∂r j = W_EM + (ηwHm−QC) A_t,
    W_EM=B_t(P²+A_r²)/2 + F_t A²/r².
The mechanical energy satisfies ∂t e_m+∂r(Ve_m)=(w/2)(∂t+V∂r)H m²−ηwHm(A_t+V A_r). For the rotational energy differentiate its displayed square; at fixed r, Q_t A−I_t=Vw m. Thus the material and geometry transfers proportional to A_t cancel exactly against photon gain. The remaining total work is W_EM+2F_tY²/r⁴+(w/2)(∂t+V∂r)H m²+Vwm(C−ηH A_r). This includes changes in radial motion and the time-dependent common metric. In static ordinary extremal equilibrium V=0 and F,B,H are stationary: total axial-sector energy is conserved without an external driver. That equality is for the quadratic reduced system, not a claim that its second-order spherical backreaction has been evolved.

For stable arithmetic subtract the *known* advected reference wHl²/2 and 2FI²/r⁴, keeping their own flux and work; this is only an algebraic energy origin. The differences contain factors ηA(ηA−2l) and QA(QA−2I). The numerical integrals report EM transfer, mechanical recoil and the constrained-geometry work separately. Changing η→−η at fixed l flips A but leaves QA, m, K and the energy even; this is different from the parent's independently prepared photon pulse. Integrating K_r or K_t from the numerical stress offers an independent check on signs and normalization.
