# The cloud now emits the pulse

**The advance.** Instead of supplying a wave and checking its propagation, we started with no transverse electric or magnetic field. Counterrotating layers of the *existing charged dust* generate the current. Their initial angular momentum and the previously specified charge fix the forcing, recoil and metric response. The spherical cloud and scalar initial data were not replaced; all ten radial evolutions match their archived endpoints exactly. No emitted amplitude or phase was fitted. This is a classical first-order rotation experiment (quadratic energy) on the common metric, not a determination of microscopic electric charge.

**Did it predict the signal?** In the static charged ordinary control we froze an independent normal-mode prediction for two different initial counterrotations before running the time-domain experiment. At four radii each, all 32 fine-grid comparisons of magnetic potential, time derivative, spatial gradient and radial energy flux pass the unchanged 2e-5 limit. The largest frozen-prediction discrepancy was 3.36e-6 of the waveform peak. Several original mode-resolution differences were above threshold; doubling the final mode resolution gives differences no greater than 3.11e-6, without replacing the original forecast. With neutral dust the same preparation produces interior frame dragging but exactly zero electromagnetic wave. Reversing the dust charge at fixed initial rotation reverses the emitted field but leaves its energy flux and the exterior rotation unchanged: the latter depends on Q times a wave itself proportional to the signed source.

**Where did the energy come from?** In the static primary control, at t=6.5 the normalized transverse EM energy gained 7.04746e-9, mechanical rotational energy fell by 7.04419e-9, and constrained rotational metric energy fell by 3.26961e-12. The whole-domain net change was −1.78e-19. These are energy coefficients per squared initial rotation, not joules or all energy radiated to infinity. In moving runs the ledger separately includes advected energy, magnetic torque and work on/from the time-dependent spherical background. Along independently tracked baryon labels the electric plus motional torque recovers recoil; omitting the motional term misses up to 0.227% in the moving source. No external actuator maintained the current.

**What MTS changed for the same source.** In the η=0.8 comparison a held receiver at radius 6 reaches the common reference fluence at proper time 4.02056255 in ordinary charged GR. The 1/160 scalar delays that event by 4.59694e-10 in the reduced Compton time unit. The coordinate delay alone is 9.85257e-9; the receiver clock offsets most of it. Full scalar strength gives a proper delay of 1.17682e-5. All 32 two-mesh tests of the *waveform differences* pass on those differences' own scales; the differenced proper shifts also agree across meshes. These are synthetic predictions for the given preparation. No change of local light speed or distinctive nonminimal photon law is implied.

**A burst need not leave a large permanent field.** On the extremal static reference the ideal Newtonian dipole vanishes: opposite layers have identical charge/mass ratio and exactly zero total spin. Nevertheless, for a nonnegative cumulative spin profile, the exact relativistic stationary dipole has a definite positive sign. SIGN-AND-SIZE.md proves this from a positive radial integral, without a small-compactness expansion. For the primary fixture its moment is 4.07531848e-8 per unit spin amplitude in the parent geometric units. The leading compactness-squared term misses by 0.118%; the independently derived cubic term reduces the relative discrepancy to 3.40e-7. Both profiles at four compactness scales pass the original gate with the cubic law. This sign theorem is about the specified stationary Einstein–Maxwell–dust subfamily; it is not an MTS-only signature. The initial energy above that stationary minimum is 3.52396e-9 for the primary fixture. Distinguish this from the *total magnetic-field energy* and from finite-time outward fluence.

**What had to be repaired.** A low-order enclosed-mass interpolation left a spurious exterior remainder when almost opposite angular momenta were integrated separately. Integrating the same saved baryon density more accurately reduces the fine-grid stress-integral error to 5.15e-8 or less against the original metric; no waveform was refitted. A global subtraction also missed the very small MTS-induced exterior rotational work (14.0% of the difference). Forming the difference locally before integrating the source-free slab reduces that fine-grid residual to 4.93e-8. Raw failures remain in momenta.csv and paired_energy.csv. The four fine raw mechanical failures in the exterior slab have residuals below 2.3e-67: no material there in the continuum, so a relative null error is not an accuracy measure.

The next step toward the local GR limit is then to feed the measured angularly averaged stress, **including the source's loss**, into the second-order spherical metric and test the response of neutral rulers.
