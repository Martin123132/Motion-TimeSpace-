# Differentiate the retained evolution, not a fitted temperature curve

N=ln a, Y=(Y_c,Y_b,Y_h,V_c,V_b,V_h). The three variables V are velocity potentials in the saved integrator convention, not velocities in km/s. At each epoch D_b=Y_b+3Φ and U_b=k²V_b/(aℋ). The metric potentials are affine in the six variables with contributions from the saved external species. The latter and the initial conditions are kept fixed on each branch. At z=99 the wave and pressure-off share the same physical preparation. Rolling cold does not.

Let t=T/T₀−1; T₀=10,000 K is the normalization of the retained smooth heating history, not a measurement or a new particle parameter. For L=L₀+t V the only explicitly temperature-dependent gas propagation coefficient is V[velocity_b,density_b]=−(a/ℋ)c_hot,0², with the original ramp and composition. Constant term s does not change. Substitution of Y(t)=ΣtⁿYₙ into the original equations gives Y₀′=L₀Y₀+s and Yₙ′=L₀Yₙ+VYₙ₋₁ for n≥1. All higher coefficients have zero initial data. The first coefficient is also ∂Y/∂ln T at T₀. These are inhomogeneous evolution equations, not polynomial coefficients fitted to tests. DOP853 advances through the two heating breakpoints; a separate Gauss collocation code evolves the unexpanded finite-temperature dynamics from the same sources. Only the changed coefficient in that independent code is linearly interpolated between the existing heated and unheated operators. The solution itself is not interpolated in temperature.

At fixed radial mode put R=B D_b+μ²U_b, μ=k_parallel/k, B=2−(γ−1)(0.7+d), d proportional to temperature. Thus R_λ=B ∂λD_b+μ²∂λU_b−(γ−1)dD_b for λ=ln T. A simple root has dk*/dλ=−R_λ/R_k. For power the local derivative is prefactor×[2RR_λ−2dR²]. All three terms (gas evolution, density-to-flux conversion and Gaussian broadening) were kept separate. Log-wavenumber power integrals and signed-root interpolation use the retained knot prescription; derivatives of the numerical interpolant are evaluated along the analytic nodal tangent. No division by R at a zero is needed to predict its movement. The cubic forecast uses Y₀ through Y₃ and the known readout at the target T. No series remainder bound is assumed.

## Optional common-metric pressure consistency test

This is a **separate effective-stress comparison**, not a replacement of the archived source or a claim that the intergalactic gas has zero physical background pressure. Take the dust-background limit for the added component, with zero background pressure, zero shear, and a rest-frame entropy pressure perturbation Π=δp_hot/ρ̄=c_hot² Δ_b. The standard linear fluid conservation equations and the distinction between the background equation of state and rest-frame sound response are external theory, not MTS derivations: [Ma & Bertschinger, §5](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9506072), [Hu, §II, eqs. 8–10](https://arxiv.org/html/astro-ph/9801234). What is evaluated here is their implication in the saved variables. The gas, radiation drag and other original nonthermal source terms are not switched off.

In the code's sign convention θ_b=−ℋU_b. The linear comoving energy-density contrast is Δ_b=D_b+3ℋθ_b/k²=Y_b+3Φ−3ℋV_b/a. The additional rest-frame stress contributes −3Π in dY_b/dN and −aΠ/ℋ in dV_b/dN. This follows from energy conservation (expansion work) and momentum conservation (pressure gradient) respectively. The old added heat acted on Y_b in the Euler row alone. A separately conserved particle number obeys dY_number/dN=k²V_b/(aℋ), so the specific incremental **number minus gravitating energy contrast** is +3∫Π dN when their initial contrasts agree. The gas transfer used by a number-density tracer must include this term, rather than simply relabeling the effective energy as atom number. Even this test does not derive the neutral fraction history.

Instead of subtracting two nearly equal solver histories, stress_test.py evolves the original state and its forced difference together: ΔY′=L_old ΔY + (L_new−L_old)(Y+ΔY)+(s_new−s_old), including the constrained metric response; it also integrates the number spectator. The difference coordinates are rescaled for accurate integration. The photon and slow-sector source histories, temperature history, background and flux conversion remain prescribed. These equations test the relativistic form of the **added** thermal perturbation. They do not close the background radiative heating, ionization kinetics, pressure of real baryons, or a full autonomous radiation transfer calculation.
