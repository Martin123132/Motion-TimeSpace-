# Temperature can copy the cancellation; it does not copy the measured-band response in this experiment

**Direct degeneracy test.** At z=4.2 we asked the leading wave-pressure branch and the same prepared state with heavy pressure removed to cancel at exactly the same existing total wavenumber, 25.33437 Mpc⁻¹. The derived temperature response selected 9,381.03 K for wave-on and 10,517.30 K for pressure-off. Independent gas evolutions on both sides of each temperature, only 0.1 K apart from its prediction, confirmed opposite signs. At the central temperatures the residual signed response was below 6.82e-8 of its respective peak. These are theory-to-theory diagnostic temperatures chosen deliberately to match one mode, not measurements or fitted data.

Matching that feature did **not** match the rest: wave/off partial flux powers were **0.030969 / 0.062002 km/s**, a factor **2.0021**. With the same temperatures carried to z=4.6 the cancellation locations also split: 29.030 versus 28.730 Mpc⁻¹ on the existing grid. This is a prediction for a specified heating history, cosmic preparation and linear model. The radial bin is 0.0794328 s/km and only total wavenumbers from its radial minimum up to 37 Mpc⁻¹ are integrated. A 3D signed-response zero does not make 1D observed flux vanish.

## Are there signatures beyond amplitude?

- Two-redshift test: we fixed weights (0.694393, −0.719596) on log powers at z=4.2 and 4.6 orthogonal to the wave thermal tangent. At the three tested temperatures wave stays within 0.000213 of its reference value, whereas pressure-off spans 0.01627–0.01707 and rolling cold −0.10443 to −0.10360. The weights do not exactly cancel arbitrary flux normalization. Three temperatures do not bound all possible thermal histories.
- Seven sightline bins on a common k band: the squared linear density/velocity response is an exponential damping factor times a quadratic in squared radial wavenumber. Its positive Gram determinant measures the velocity contribution that cannot be represented as a multiple of the density. The normalized statistic J removes an overall amplitude and the explicit density-to-flux thermal shear. At the matched zero J is **0.019484 vs 0.028165**; the log-curvature Q, separately cancelling a common amplitude and Gaussian broadening, is **−0.002725 vs −0.003915**. See ANGULAR-DERIVATION.md. These are different diagnostics: width cancels automatically in Q, but measuring J from damped data would require separating the width. An arbitrary independent calibration at every bin would defeat this test.
- When two bands are joined, their normalized J is a density-power-weighted mean of the two J values **plus** the nonnegative variance of the two coherent velocity/density slopes. Algebraically, for weight w=I0_left/I0_total, J_total=w J_left+(1−w)J_right+w(1−w)(c_left−c_right)². This identity is verified on divisions at 12 and 24 Mpc⁻¹. It implies the unnormalized residual I0 J cannot decrease when a band is added. Holding the finite-band wave target fixed, the matched pressure-off branch would need at least 44.55% of its current density weight in unseen modes to lower its own J that far. The actual wave tail may change as well: **no infinite-band discrimination has been proved**. Raw Gram matrices, not separately averaged J values, should be carried into the next band.
- For the primary 10,000 K wave, a check on common-metric coupling distinguished gravitating gas energy from conserved particle number. Replacing only the added thermal Euler force by the rest-frame pressure including expansion work, with a number spectator and metric feedback, changed the number-tracer partial power by 5.98e-9 relative at z=4.2. Across all five branch/temperature preparations and four redshifts, the largest effect was 8.02e-9. This is a *paired model difference*, not the total error. It is much too small to account for the factor of two. The effective-stress assumptions, external references and equations are identified in THERMAL-EQUATIONS.md. This is not a solution for the ionizing radiation field.

## Numerical and observational limits

On existing modes, two of 1,240 relative time-step kernel checks initially missed 2e-5, both at pressure-off k=31.4561, z=4.6, 9,000 K. Further halving gave 1.48e-5 then 1.03e-6. At that same near-zero kernel, switching the heavy matching surface still changes its tiny pointwise power by up to 4.45e-4 relative. Across the whole acquired band the corresponding partial-power changes stay below 1.0e-7 for matching and 1.67e-9 for time stepping. Do not use peak-normalized or integrated errors to certify relative precision at a zero. The cubic kernel passes 1,181/1,240 pointwise relative tests; this is consistent with the signed forecasts without replacing the failed checks. At the independently chosen near-zero centers the time-step change in tiny relative kernel can reach 0.00389, whereas the bracketed root moves by less than 3.40e-8 Mpc⁻¹. Discrete k sampling changes some partial powers by up to 0.0614%, much more than the time error. All are retained, not adjusted to pass.

Recomputing the two-redshift contrast on the thinner k grid with its own reference changes it by at most 0.000172 across all three branches. That does not erase the 0.016-scale wave/off split, but this check is not a bound on omitted modes.
