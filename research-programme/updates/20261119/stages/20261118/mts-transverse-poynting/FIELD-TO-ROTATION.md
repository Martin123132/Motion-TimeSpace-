# From measured charge and magnetic field to a local metric quantity

A result independent of how the dipole was launched follows from the same constraint. In the orthonormal frame normal to the spherical slice, denote the solid-angle average by brackets. Taking the first angular moment of Br=2A cosθ/r² gives r²〈Br cosθ〉=2A/3. Therefore for this canonical axial dipole, in the charged-current-free exterior at linear order,
    K(r)=J−Q r²〈Br cosθ〉,                  K=−r⁴ C/(6F).
No scalar occupation, pulse amplitude, time rephasing or current-to-light conversion is chosen by fitting rotation. Even the unknown total-angular-momentum constant drops out of a two-sphere measurement:
    K(R₂)−K(R₁)=−Q [R₂²〈Br cosθ〉₂−R₁²〈Br cosθ〉₁].
These are spheres on the specified common slice, with the axial orientation and sign of magnetic field retained. A positive intensity measurement alone loses that sign. The scalar and ordinary branch modify which field arrives and the lapse/length conversion for reading the metric, not the coefficient in this constraint. The quantity on the left is the axial gravitational surface angular momentum (extrinsic-curvature rotation charge), not a particle's angular momentum or a new local spin density. Nonconstant enclosed charge, exterior material torques, higher multipoles, and nonlinear electromagnetic or higher-derivative gravity actions require new terms or separate matching.

**Dimensional calibration.** With physical radius in metres, geometric conversions are Q_g=√(G/(4πε₀))Q_SI/c², B_g=√(4πG/μ₀) B_SI/c², J_g=G J_SI/c³. Their combination gives the same displayed two-sphere equation directly with coulombs, tesla, metres and kg m²/s, since ε₀μ₀c²=1. When converting the measured metric curl back to SI angular momentum the usual G remains; this cancellation is not a derivation of Newton's constant or electric charge. For example, reversing either supplied charge or wave phase flips the linear induced rotation without changing wave energy. The compact pulse and J=0 employed in this calculation are fixtures, not a state-selection law.
