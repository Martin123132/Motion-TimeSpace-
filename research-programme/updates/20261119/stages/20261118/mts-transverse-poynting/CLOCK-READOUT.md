# A detector observable rather than coordinate flux alone

Before extracting arrival scores, fix a static areal-radius detector at R=23 and compare its elapsed proper time to crossing one tenth of the **same flat initial pulse energy coefficient** per angular normalization. Use proper luminosity L=j/α² (angular integral of the normal Poynting vector times proper area), proper time dτ=αdt, hence cumulative local fluence ∫j/α dt. It is an externally held observer; it is not the freely falling neutral ruler in the previous stage. For sign or multiple crossings use the first positive crossing; an absent crossing remains missing. Fix the threshold for both theories, both charges and both pulse calculations; don't renormalize each by its own eventual received fluence. Separately compare equal-proper-time flux waveforms on the baseline coordinate interval 0…6.375. This is an ideal detector calculation, not a physical choice of absolute source brightness or a telescope measurement.

At fixed r, write B=B₀+s b, F=1+s d, s=λ². Then δα/s=(b+B₀ d+sbd)/(α+α₀). Let K=∫δα/s dt. At fixed proper time τ=α₀t₀ the new coordinate displacement sθ solves θ=−K(t₀+sθ)/α₀. For source fluence G the matched difference in a crossing time follows instead from equal G: θ=−[(G_s−G₀)/s](t_s)/average(G₀') on [t₀,t_s]. The proper-time arrival shift per s is α₀θ+K(t_s). These quotients avoid subtracting times that coincide to nearly machine precision. Local luminosity and total canonical energy stored in the geometric perturbation are different readouts.
