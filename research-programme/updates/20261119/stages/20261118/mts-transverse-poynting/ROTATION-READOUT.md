# Angular momentum and a sign-odd metric observable

This test is fixed after the first radial-flux results, before scoring rotation. In the exterior the normal electric Coulomb field is Q/r². Its cross product with the wave's polar magnetic field carries *azimuthal* momentum: jφ(normal)=−Q A_r sin²θ/(4π a r²). Integrating on a shell gives angular-momentum density per coordinate radius −(2Q/3)A_r. The independent Maxwell off-diagonal spatial stress gives outward angular-momentum flux +(2Q/3)BP from −E_r Eφ/(4π). Their continuity is just Faraday evolution; neither quantity is the positive outward wave-energy flux. The enclosed gravitational rotation charge K=−r⁴C/(6F) therefore has K_r=−2Q A_r/3 and K_t=−2Q BP/3, at total-spin constant J=0. The Maxwell torque on the geometry is first order in probe amplitude at nonzero Q. Its effect on the propagating Maxwell wave is even in Q, whereas K itself is odd. For a static radial Coulomb field alone BOTH transports vanish.

Use a separate staggered-grid Maxwell evolution: electric momentum and axial potential on nodes, magnetic gradient on faces, and K evolved from the angular-momentum current. Initialize K by integrating the independently sampled initial magnetic gradient, not by setting it equal to A times a constant. Feed measured K into the dipole Ampère equation as +6Q F K/r⁴, with no prescribed RN potential for this term. Compare K both to integrated radial angular momentum and to the action prediction. Compare the induced-wave correction on its own scale with the collocated action solver. Reverse charge without changing the pulse, and retain an actual neutral-source null. Angular momentum in the material interior is not simulated; compact initial pulse, no initial core spin, and causal isolation set the integration constant.
