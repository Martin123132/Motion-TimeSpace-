# A transverse photon pulse carries energy and sources an axial metric response

**What changed.** Starting from the retained spherical matter preparation, a divergence-free Maxwell pulse now has a checked nonzero Poynting flux and a calibrated relation to a metric observable. The previous electrostatic test could not test either transport. The result uses the canonical Einstein–Maxwell terms already in the parent action, not a fitted photon coupling. The ordinary GR part is known physics; the comparison here is the response on the same specified core with and without the retained scalar. These are controlled field calculations, not observations.

**Energy and arrival.** At radius 23 an observer held at fixed areal radius measures the time to receive one tenth of the common initial flat pulse energy per angular normalization. On the uncharged ordinary exterior that proper time is 3.57376125. With the retained 1/160 scalar it is later by 7.07315e-9 in the model's reduced Compton time units. At full scalar amplitude the delay is 1.81086e-4. The scalar alters both propagation and the receiver clock: at full amplitude the coordinate delay is 3.24188e-4, and the clock contribution offsets about 1.43e-4. At 1/160 the peak change in equal-proper-time received luminosity is 1.79731e-8 of the ordinary peak. These are fixed-pulse results, not a change to local c. The charged runs give almost the same scalar timing shifts. Fourteen two-mesh comparisons of the small flux and timing signals pass on the difference's own scale. The analytic flat dipole's fine-grid energy flux agrees to 6.52e-9 on the waveform scale.

In the measuring shell [17,23], loss of stored energy equals flux out minus flux in, less the work done on the wave by the time-dependent metric. For the 1/160 neutral run the metric work is −1.14518e-9 in the normalized units. The matched scalar minus ordinary balance closes to 9.75e-6 or better of peak work on the fine mesh. Directly subtracting large stored energies misses this small term; the raw closed-domain static energy defect is 2.77e-10 at the end. Its exact expected change and flux are zero, so a ratio against that change is not a meaningful static control. Both the raw and paired rows remain in budgets.csv. Angular quadrature of the local electric and magnetic fields independently recovers the canonical radial flux and energy in angular.csv.

**What charge does.** Changing the sign of the source charge reverses the induced axial rotation but preserves the outward wave-energy flux. For the charged full scalar preparation, the closed-loop axial rotation across radii 17–23 at time 6.5 is +8.65074e-8 radians per unit of wave amplitude at positive charge and its negative at negative charge; it is exactly zero on the neutral control. This concerns rotational response to an axial dipole, not a free spin-2 radiation mode. The induced geometry changes radial wave-energy flux at radius 23 by only 8.22e-11 of the fixed-metric peak here. A fixed-metric Maxwell solver therefore approximates the total flux extremely well in this fixture, but would miss the sign-sensitive geometric observable altogether. A second solver evolving angular momentum from electromagnetic stress rather than imposing the extra potential recovers the induced wave on its own scale. The stationary charged-GR comparison uses the physical dipole, not a radiative gravitational dipole; see [Burko](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.59.084003) and AXIAL-ACTION.md.

**An input a detector could supply.** The difference in axial gravitational surface angular momentum between two spheres is the measured charge times the negative difference in R² times the first angular moment of the radial magnetic field. FIELD-TO-ROTATION.md gives the precise signed equation, the SI conversion, and the restrictions. The unknown total spin constant cancels. Against the separately evolved angular momentum, all twenty two-sphere comparisons (including exact neutral controls) pass; even the scalar-induced changes alone pass at the small preparation. An intensity-only measurement cannot reconstruct this signed effect.

**Smallest energy term.** The interaction between the scalar-induced change and the charge-induced rotational feedback was initially unresolved on its own work scale in [17,23]: 5.40e-5 at the fine mesh, above 2e-5. Keeping the *identical* final evolved fields, evaluating mixed products without nested subtraction, and separately improving the space and time integrals gives 8.41e-8 or better there for both amplitudes. The original miss remains saved. mixed_precision.csv shows which change mattered. No pulse shape, source parameter or acceptance threshold was changed.

**Next physical step.** Replace the supplied initial radiation by a conserved, specified transverse current. Predict the waveform, source work and angular-momentum transfer from that current and its material recoil; keep the present free pulse as the source-free control. This would distinguish a sourced Maxwell coupling test from another successful propagation test. The present construction fixes neither a microscopic charge nor a galactic state, and does not include the higher photon operators or the pulse's own second-order change to the spherical metric.
