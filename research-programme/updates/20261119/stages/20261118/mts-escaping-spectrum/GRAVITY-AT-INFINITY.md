# What the emitted light fixes about gravity

Use the parent's Einstein–Maxwell normalization G=c=1, areal distance R, polar axis along the prepared spin, infinitesimal amplitude a_s. The source produces an axial l=1 field and a constrained l=1 metric rotation, not l=1 gravitational-wave news. With no independent first-order gravitational wave or scalar radiation on this static reference, the gravitational-news square first contributes above order a_s². The fixed Coulomb monopole adds no radial light flux.

At future null infinity the outgoing transverse electric and magnetic fields both have strength a_s v(u) sin θ/R. Restoring the angular factor that was divided out of the matter/field calculation,
    dE/(du dΩ)=a_s² v(u)² sin²θ/(4π),
    dM_B/du=−(2/3)a_s² v(u)².
Here u increases toward the future. E_rad=(2/3)a_s² E* for this problem. This is a *decrease* of the Bondi mass, not a decrease of the conserved ADM energy on a complete spatial slice. No initial spin energy has been added twice. It fixes a mass difference, not an absolute mass for the rotating initial data. The net linear momentum radiated vanishes by reflection symmetry. For the general GR relationship see [Bieri, Chen & Yau, Einstein–Maxwell null asymptotics](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.2267). We use the framework's specified Einstein–Maxwell subfamily; we do not import their global nonlinear existence assumptions for a material interior.

**An angular prediction for a distant neutral ruler.** The time-integrated outgoing energy per solid angle is F=E_rad(1−P₂(cos θ))/(4π), so the electromagnetic contribution to null displacement memory is not spherical. To fix both normalization and sign, write the electric-parity change in Bondi shear as ΔC_AB=(D_A D_B−q_AB D²/2)Φ on the unit sphere. Splitting off any change of non-monopole endpoint mass aspect, the flux part obeys ½D²(D²+2)Φ=16π F with the l=0,1 components projected out. The l=2 eigenvalue is 12: Φ₂=−E_rad/3. In the meridian/azimuth polarization basis (plus is theta minus phi), this yields
    R Δh_plus^(EM null)=−½ E_rad sin²(i),       R Δh_cross^(EM null)=0.
A pair of distant freely falling rulers aligned with those axes has opposite fractional displacement ±Δh_plus/2 from this component. It vanishes on the axis and is largest in the equatorial plane. In SI replace E_rad/R by G E_physical/(c⁴ R_physical). Nothing here chooses an astrophysical mass, spin amplitude or observer distance.

**Independent angular check.** In the transverse-traceless integral the plus kernel for null direction n' and observer N is 2[(e_theta·n')²−(e_phi·n')²]/(1−N·n'). In the observer's basis this equals 2(1+cos θ')cos 2φ'. Apply it directly to the time-domain energy sky; use the frequency-domain total energy in the analytic l=2 prediction. Code integrates both polarizations at nine sightlines per preparation, including null directions, and checks the monopole, net momentum and higher harmonic leakage. These are two evaluations of the *same physical law*, not independent observational evidence.

Only the EM null component follows from the escaping flux alone.
