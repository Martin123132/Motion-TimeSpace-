# From two counterrotating charge profiles to an escaping spectrum

**Domain.** This is the same linear axial dipole about the parent's regular ordinary static, extremally charged dust sphere. Radius 5, total geometrical mass and charge 0.002, no horizon. Source spin is infinitesimal with amplitude a_s. Divide energy by (2/3) a_s² until reinstating it for gravity. The eta=0.8 ordinary and scalar-on arms are time dependent and are tested separately on their saved interval. No fit or observational spectrum enters.

**Why there is a burst.** In optical radius x=∫dr/B (x(0)=0), the parent equation is A_tt+H A=b, H=−∂x²+V, V=BU, b=BS. On this branch B=F(1−N/r)², α=F(1−N/r), U=2F/r²+Dα+4FN²/r⁴, S=Dα l+4FN I/r⁴, l=dI/dN. The prepared shells carry opposite canonical angular momenta; A and A_t start at zero. The steady response A*=H⁻¹b is not zero. The freely moving displacement is A−A*, initially −A*. Its conserved energy is E*=½〈b,H⁻¹b〉, not the total rotational energy. In particular, the nonzero final magnetostatic field is not continuously radiating. Requested observable is news v=A_t at fixed retarded time u=t−x, not A at a finite radius. v has homogeneous evolution with v(0)=0, v_t(0)=b.

**Outgoing construction.** Use the retarded transform with positive exponential exp(ikt) and boundary value from Im k>0. Let y be real regular, y/r²→1, and f the unit outgoing solution f exp(−ikx)→1. Set W=y_x f−y f_x. For r≥5 the transform of v is f(r) T(k) with T=∫yS dr/W. This follows from the unit derivative jump of the resolvent; it fixes the sign, phase and absolute normalization. In particular the escaping energy spectrum is |T|²/π for positive angular frequency k, in the above energy units. With y=r²c, the action-derived interior equation is
    c''+(4/r+B'/B)c'+[k²/B²−3D/g−6(N/(r²g))²]c=0,  g=1−N/r.
Integration by parts with I vanishing at both endpoints gives ∫yS=∫I(3FN c/r²−α c')dr, including at finite k. Compare to direct overlap, not just to a rescaled mode. Outside, B=(1−M/r)². For z=kr, ε=kM and f=exp(ikx)h, the equation is
    B h_zz+(B_z+2i)h_z−(2/z²+4ε²/z⁴)h=0.
The outgoing series starts h=1+i/z+… at large z; where ε=0 this is exact. The higher coefficients follow by inserting powers of 1/z into the equation. The exterior phase uses the exact integral of 1/B, including the logarithm. Dropping that logarithm would change a timing observable. We start the asymptotic series at two different outer arguments, not at a reflecting boundary.

**Why energy is not disappearing into another mode.** Near the regular centre V=2/x²+O(1); at infinity x=r+2M log r+O(1) and V−2/x²=O((1+log x)/x³). Thus the perturbation q of the l=1 half-line operator has ∫x|q|dx finite. The positive-axis absolutely continuous spectral result for this assumption is stated in [Kostenko, Teschl & Toloza, Introduction and §2.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1504.03015). We do not invoke their dispersive decay theorem, with its stronger moment hypothesis. The quadratic form ∫(|A_x|²+V|A|²)dx is strictly positive on nonzero square-integrable states (V>0). This excludes negative and zero eigenvalues. The regular zero-energy solution cannot also decay as 1/r: its integrated quadratic form would vanish. There is no absorbed central port. For the specified operator the continuum therefore accounts for the energy above equilibrium, not for the steady energy itself. Indeed Im(f/W)=k y/|W|² by the outgoing Wronskian; the spectral measure of b per dk is 2k²|T|²/π. Integrating dμ_b/(2k²) gives E*. This is an analytic sum rule; the computed spectra do not take E* as an input.

**Unresolved frequency range.** Positivity gives E(k>K)≤||Hb||²/(2K⁶). Here b has compact support with more than the required surface/central smoothness. Evaluate Hb via the displayed regularized radial operator applied to b/r² at 320 interior quadrature nodes, differentiating two independent polynomial orders (96,144). The inequality is analytic; the quoted numerical moment values are quadratures, not interval-arithmetic certificates. The direct band sum from 24 to 48 avoids subtracting almost equal total energies. No assertion of an exact time after which the tail vanishes.

**A physical long-wavelength discriminator.** The l=1 free outgoing solution is exp(ikx)(1+i/(kx)); the normalized threshold solution (−ik)f tends to the real zero-energy decaying mode d with r d→1. This is the usual continuity of the scaled radial Jost solution under the above first-moment assumption (same source, §2.3); x/r→1 does not change the coefficient. Meanwhile y→y₀, ∫yS→W[y₀,d] μ∞. Thus T=−ik μ∞+o(k). The slope is the independently obtained signed exterior magnetic moment, not total angular momentum. In the ideal flat control T_flat=∫(kr)j₁(kr) D l dr/(ik). The k term vanishes because ∫r²D l dr=0 for either preparation. Its first surviving term is i k³∫r⁴D l dr/30. The low-frequency energy densities therefore scale as k² and k⁶ respectively. Both bursts can still have almost the same total energy. Equivalently the zero-frequency news integral vanishes and the first signed time moment gives −μ∞ in the transform/regularized sense. A finite record is not the infinite moment; signed_tail.csv measures the miss instead of presuming saturation from the energy budget. These are reduced angular frequencies, not a prediction in Hz without a physical time scale.

**Numerical conditioning.** The exact outward mode current is Im(conj(f) B f_r)=k. The complex representation no longer computes this small determinant accurately at the softest auxiliary probes. Independently write f=exp(h+iθ); conservation gives θ_z=exp(−2h)/B. In the real amplitude equation the large-phase cancellation can be evaluated as expm1(−4h)/B². soft_flux.py integrates the envelope and phase correction from a separate outer start, forms W from logarithmic derivatives and retests the actual complex fields, slopes and emitted amplitudes. The identity evaluated in polar variables is an algebraic check, NOT independent validation; the independent results are the mode and spectrum comparisons.
