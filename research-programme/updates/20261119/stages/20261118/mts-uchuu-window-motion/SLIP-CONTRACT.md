# A boost-invariant candidate for the missing current (prepared on low-z cone 0 only)

The absolute host-velocity linear control made the low-z power errors worse. Its prediction for host-relative motion changes by βU under a common bulk boost U with the calibration held fixed. A relative local flow is a better-defined input. This is a candidate tracer-state closure, not a term added to the gravitational action.

In physical km/s, let N have unit sky sight lines of neighbouring centrals as rows. Given diagonal inverse-distance-squared weights W, project their radial velocity vector v into the field of radial velocities that a constant 3-vector would generate. Solve weighted least squares for V. The local radial estimate is n_i·V, and the surrounding velocity variance is the weighted mean of (v−NV)². If N has rank three then v→v+N U gives V→V+U exactly. Both host-minus-environment velocity and surrounding variance are invariant; this argument does not assume a fixed line of sight across the galaxy survey. For exact coincident entries, exclude them as environment probes, not as galaxies. Count and retain diagnostics of geometry and noise amplification. This is local Newtonian boost covariance at fixed selection, not a redshift-space invariance of the observed catalogue.

Freeze before any higher-redshift power measurement: use 8 and 32 nearest noncoincident unique different-family centrals in each cap. Matched satellites use their actual host vector projected along their own LOS; unresolved satellites use the eight-central projection. For all satellites define slip = host/proxy minus the 32-central projection. For each cap and satellite class take the zero-intercept parity-odd moment γ=〈(v_sat−v_host) slip〉/〈slip²〉. Only mock 0, 0.2≤z_obs<0.5, using the usual positive weights. Approximate remaining residuals by the symmetric ± empirical law around γ slip within four frozen training-weight quartiles of the 32-central residual variance. Retain positive 12-node quadrature for each law. Do not infer planar transverse velocities as physical observations, adapt weights to the power scores, remove difficult test rows, or silently clip extrapolations. Test the mean and width stages separately in the same estimator against the present independent control. The physically measured line-of-sight predictions should be mapped to displacements with the saved nonlinear χ relation. Upper-z multipole transfer is for the next stage; no claim is based on these calibration moments.
