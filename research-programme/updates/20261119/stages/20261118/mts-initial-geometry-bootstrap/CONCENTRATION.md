# Small integrated energy is not uniformly small central curvature

Keep the same ordinary cloud and rest data, but use a different **negative control** for the scalar smoothness implication: h=0, Π=δ^(−1/4)(1−r²/δ²)_+^8. This is canonical radial data, not a proposed galaxy population. It has no initial radial flux, and no fitted parameters. Let f(x)=(1−x²)^8 on 0≤x≤1, zero beyond. At regular r=0 the radial metric is one. The added scalar energy there is δ^(−1/2)/2; the trace-reversed scalar term raises the normal-normal Ricci curvature by 2δ^(−1/2) in the parent's units. Ordinary proper central density is held fixed.

For the parent's u=r h and p=rΠ initial norm, only p,p_r contribute. Since B≤1 one has H≤δ^(5/2)∫x²f²dx/2+δ^(1/2)∫[(xf)']²dx/2. Thus H tends to zero. The enclosed mass difference is positive and bounded by the scalar energy integral, which is at most the first term of this inequality. Before the edge, 2ΔM/r≤δ^(−1/2) r²/3 and beyond the edge it remains ≤δ^(3/2)/3 by the same constraint and decreasing upper bound with r. The small packet therefore hides neither a large ADM mass nor a trapped sphere; it **does** hide arbitrarily high central curvature. It also raises V(0) without bound via the enclosed mean. A small H and small compactness cannot by themselves certify the earlier global metric derivative assumptions. This does not contradict small ruler effects away from the centre, or the fixed-shape λ→0 result.
