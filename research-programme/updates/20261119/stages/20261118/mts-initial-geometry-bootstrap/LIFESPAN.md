# Why a loss of untracked smoothness is a separate issue, and how to continue this branch

The first-exit calculation proves pointwise inequalities on a classical development. A lifespan argument is needed before treating it as an initial-data guarantee through the requested time. This note restricts to the regular isolated spherical data of the experiment, with smooth parity at the centre and the stated finite differentiability of the joined profiles. It does not extrapolate past a shell crossing or apply to a nonspherical fluid with pressure.

**Local input, and what is new here.** Choquet-Bruhat and Friedrich, [Motion of Isolated Bodies, Theorems 2.1 and 3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/gr-qc/0605096), treat dust and charged dust with Maxwell matter, respectively, not MTS. Use their wave-gauge dust reduction as follows. Differentiate reduced Einstein once along the timelike dust flow. Continuity and the geodesic equation remove the density derivative. The added trace-reversed scalar source is proportional to ∂φ⊗∂φ+(m²φ²/2)g; its flow derivative contains at most two derivatives of φ, and no derivatives of dust density. Append the massive KG wave equation, whose coefficients use at most first metric derivatives. In the Leray indexing of that construction take unknown orders (g,φ,U,ρ)=(3,3,2,1), equation offsets (Einstein differentiated, KG, geodesic, continuity)=(0,1,1,0). The principal diagonal is transport×wave, wave, transport, transport. The new couplings are strictly lower in this grading. Each diagonal symbol has simple time roots (timelike flow and light roots distinct in the cubic); repeated cones across different diagonal equations are not off-diagonal principal couplings. The standard local hyperbolic existence/restart argument therefore applies at sufficiently regular data; choose s=5 in three spatial dimensions. The initial undifferentiated reduced Einstein equation fixes the extra metric time derivative. Its residual is transported from zero; the harmonic constraints propagate by the contracted Bianchi identity since both dust and KG stress are conserved. This is a specialization of the hyperbolic argument, not an imported theorem about a quantum measure.

**Continuation estimates.** One can avoid a derivative loss in pressureless density by transporting physical shell variables instead of commuting the naive density-flux equation. In polar coordinates write H=UR/R, Z=UJ, Y=(A,H,J,Z), χ=r/A, W=sqrt(1+(arH)²), v=arH/W, d=Bv and f=α/W. Then on the physical development,
    (∂t+d∂r)Y=f(AH, −H²−M/r³−s'_h, Z, (2M/r³−Q)J−ρ₀(r/A)/A²).
Coefficients use Y, field and its first normal/spatial derivatives, a,F, and the regular enclosed mean M/r³=∫₀¹ y² e(yr)dy; ρ=ρ₀(r/A)/(JA²). H and Z are bounded by integrating the already bounded accelerations. In particular d/r is bounded. A further needed Lipschitz coefficient is d_r. If η=atanh v, the polar orthonormal radial-frame connection obeys ω(e₁)=a_t/(αa), ∇_U U=0 and the radial expansion n·∇_n U=Z/J. Boosting e₁=W(n−vU) gives
    η_r=aW Z/J−a_t/α=aW Z/J+r a²j.
Thus d_r=B_rv+B(1−v²)η_r is bounded without a higher derivative of the matter. It is not assumed in the characteristic estimate.

Here are details of the derivative bookkeeping used for restart. On a bounded causal region about the material support, at level k≥1 use sup norms of ∂ʲw± through j=k and of ∂ʲY through j=k−1 plus r∂ᵏY. Treat r=0 by regular even/odd extension. Keep u as well. At level one, N already controls the wave variables. Applying r∂r to the Y transport generates (d/r−d_r)rY_r plus r times the source derivative. The latter is bounded by a constant times 1+sup|rY_r|: rΠ_r,rX_r are controlled, r(M/r³)_r=e−3M/r³, and r a_r and rF_r are bounded. Gronwall therefore supplies the full level-one norm from initial values. This last step does not presuppose the density-gradient gate; that gate was closed independently.

For k≥2, the transport estimate for level k is at most linear in that level, with coefficients depending on lower levels and the finite domain:
- From parity and the fundamental theorem, derivatives through order k−1 of Π=p/r and X=(u_r−h)/r and their r-weighted order-k derivatives are bounded by the wave norm. For example ∂ʲ(p/r)=∫₀¹ yʲ p^(j+1)(yr)dy and r∂ᵏΠ=p^(k)−k∂^(k−1)Π. Use h=∫₀¹u_r(yr)dy for X. These formulas are regular at the origin.
- Products and smooth compositions in the material variables obey the same mixed derivative bound. In an r-weighted product of differentiated factors put r on a largest-order factor; if k=2, the apparently quadratic rY_r² is bounded by the *previous* rY_r bound times the new unweighted Y_r. At higher k the other factors are lower order. A positive lower bound for J,A,1−2M/r controls division. The mean integral preserves these bounds by the powers of y in differentiation. There is no implicit top-order metric loop: a_r=r a³(e−M/r³) and F_r=rFa²(e+s) supply **unweighted** order k geometry from previous-level, r-weighted order k−1 matter and lower derivatives of a,F. The given ρ₀ and finitely many of its derivatives enter as known functions.
- In the kth wave characteristic equation, the highest new source terms are ∂ᵏB_r times w and ∂ᵏV times u. B_r=rFK and u=rh supply the needed r weight on ∂ᵏK. Other terms need at most unweighted order k−1 matter. The commutator with B∂r uses at most ∂ᵏB and lower wave derivatives apart from the bounded Lipschitz coefficient times ∂ᵏw.
- In the dust equation d is r times a smooth function of Y,a,F,r. Hence ∂ᵏd requires only the mixed order-k bound. In its highest new commutator it multiplies rY_r, already bounded. The unweighted order k−1 commutator is lower or linear in that level. The r weight itself contributes only d/r. Source derivatives of s'_h and Q have the same mixed bound by the first item.

Characteristics and Gronwall now bound each of these finitely many higher norms up to the finite time, successively; this argument does not require their values to be small. It can first be applied to smooth even/odd profiles. The experiment's compact joins have bounded weak derivatives to the needed order (positive-part sixth powers; quintic-order endpoint flatness in the beta taper). Smooth regularization with uniform bounds through level five, or piecewise differentiation with matching traces to the required lower order, gives the same finite-order estimate. Where derivatives are only essentially bounded the estimate is on their essential suprema.

Level five controls at least the local restart data g_space in H⁵, extrinsic curvature and scalar normal velocity in H⁴, φ in H⁵, dust velocity in H⁴ and density in H³. In checking Cartesian components at the origin use regular radial parity, subtract the appropriate constant Taylor terms before division by r, and use a²−1=O(r²), rj=O(r²), U^r=O(r). For example K_radial/a²=r a j, K_angular=0 in polar slicing. Away from material and the signal the metric is the isolated Schwarzschild exterior; equivalently use compact overlapping domains with a vacuum collar for restart. The lower lapse, positive Jacobian and compactness gaps keep these spacelike charts nondegenerate. These uniform finite-order bounds allow a local restart with a time independent of approach to a putative finite endpoint: one cannot end the regular solution inside the improved first-exit box solely by blowing up an untracked high derivative. No numerical values of high-norm lifespan constants or computer-assisted interval validation of an arbitrary galaxy are claimed. The path to this conclusion uses spherical symmetry, pressureless common-metric dust, the retained canonical pole and the chosen finite interval.
