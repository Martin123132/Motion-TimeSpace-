# When can the early amplitude transfer be exact?
Let U_i be the axial field plus conserved source of preparation i, Z_i its quadratic even response, and T the ratio fixed by the decaying exterior launch solutions. Both preparations use the same unspun spacetime, hence the same linear operator L governing even perturbations, and obey Ż_i=LZ_i+S_i. The state difference W=Z₂−TZ₁ obeys Ẇ=LW+(S₂−TS₁). Initially W vanishes in the source-free exterior because the fixed conformal preparation selects only one decaying radial solution there. With no independent incoming data the retarded domain-of-dependence then explains the early transfer. In a source-containing past cone the solution is
    W(τ)=U_L(τ,0)W(0)+∫₀ᵗ U_L(τ,s)[S₂(s)−T S₁(s)]ds,
where U_L is evolution by the homogeneous even equations. This is not a claim that the interior initial W vanishes. The conserved alternative material angular momentum ℓ₁=(2N/M−1)ℓ₀+2I₀/M is not a constant multiple of ℓ₀ across the baryon shells. Neither its squared mechanical stress nor its emitted wave is fixed by one exterior launch multipole. The later fixed-clock residual tests this physical statement without redefining T.

In contrast, holding the same material rotation but moving the initial *scalar preparation* between round shells and label spheres leaves the axial solution identical. In that subtraction S₂−S₁ is zero everywhere, so the remaining signal is transported preparation data, including induced polar matter/current/geometry. Compare these two physically different reasons for a late difference before attributing a feature to a new interaction. The reported logarithmic volume differences are dimensionless per squared source spin. They are neither likelihood scores nor observational detections.
