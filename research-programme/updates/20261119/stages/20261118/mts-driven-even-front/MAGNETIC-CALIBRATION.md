# Use a magnetic measurement to eliminate the unknown rotation amplitude
This is a conditional source calibration, proposed during this stage, not an astronomical observation or independently held-out dataset. Compare the two specified η=1 balanced launch profiles at the same physical mass and length unit. Match their **signed polar dipoles** at proper time 3 at the round-launched falling detector, using the independent saved radiation bank, not either late volume. Use that *same* relative source strength for the later time 4; do not refit. Since B_pole=2ε A/R² and the common spherical areal radius is identical, the required amplitude ratio ε₂/ε₁=A₁(3)/A₂(3). It may have either sign. Both physical amplitudes may be made small together. Test it using the two directly evolved waves at 3. Then the predicted ratio of pole-minus-equator logarithmic volume differences at 4 is [V₂(4)/V₁(4)] [A₁(3)/A₂(3)]². Use independent metric-determinant and direct Maxwell values as an alternative numerical readout. A result other than unity is not a failure of the metric coupling; equating one real magnetic measurement need not equate the source stresses or their histories. No division by any field in the nonemitting neutral control, or by a pre-front zero. These are calibrated synthetic sources — no sensitivity of a real detector is assumed.
