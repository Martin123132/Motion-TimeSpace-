# Regular centre for the next, longer observation

A smooth axisymmetric scalar field has quadrupole O(x²); Aφ=A sin²θ is regular for A=O(x²), N=O(x³), Q=O(x³). For the retained first rotation law I=O(N³), ℓ=O(N²). Hence C=4a(QA−I)/R⁴=O(x) or smaller. No physical core cutoff or hand-picked inner angular momentum is required.

Importantly, **do not set every even metric component to zero at the origin**. Its rr component is H P₂; rθ/x is H ∂θP₂/2; θθ/x² is H(1/2−P₂); φφ/(x² sin²θ) is −H/2. These four limits have one common amplitude, not four independent reflective conditions. They also hold for a regular covariant extrinsic-curvature perturbation with its own amplitude. Corrections to these normalized metric components are even powers of x. A regular polar covector can have a leading gradient of x²P₂, proportional to (2xP₂,x²∂θP₂). Optical twist, wave energy and the charge density must be formed *after* imposing regular variables.
