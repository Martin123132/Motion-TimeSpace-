# The sign change was predicted before the interior modes were opened

**Result.** From the retained regular adiabatic initial conditions, the positive quadratic heavy field gives a *signed*, scale-dependent gravitational density, not a sharp cutoff at the previously estimated local crossover. At a≈0.000265 its density supplies **32.56%** of the removed cold perturbation at k=0.710497 Mpc⁻¹ and **23.38%** at k=0.749894 Mpc⁻¹. Those two cold comparison histories were computed from the early equations with their own matter content and matched at the same scale factor; no early cold archive at those k was assumed. The total Weyl potentials are respectively 58.25% and 48.17% of their cold controls. The illustrative heavy fraction, mass, gravity and initial normalization have not been refitted.

**Where this comes from.** Evolving passive, separately forced copies of the heavy perturbation alongside the actual matter/metric system gives 81.05% and 75.25% of the endpoint heavy density from the state inherited before epoch 3038. The remaining 18.95% and 24.75% are generated in the subsequent window. These are signed contributions: the kτ=1 to 2π interval contributes −34.29% and −54.01%, whereas 8π to 16π contributes +57.78% and +71.01%. Starting just with the initial scalar perturbation and omitting the gravitational drive contributes negligibly here. Fine trajectories sum to the true window density within 1.0e−13 of peak. The late window's mass-only beat phase is only 0.088 and 0.098 radians (versus 4.34 at k=5); that short interval cannot by itself determine the inherited phase. These fractions allocate the source on the actual metric; they are not independent feedback interventions.

**New frozen sign test.** Previous work had already seen the signs at k≈0.75 and k=1; we did not rediscover their existence blindly. We solved for the position and derivative of a simple zero between them, then opened *all five* other archived interior modes, not a selected subset. The predicted zero is k=0.890817 at 3038 and 0.896651 Mpc⁻¹ at 4252. At both epochs the numerical archive brackets the sign change between **0.881683 and 0.930572 Mpc⁻¹**, as predicted. All 6,075 held-out mode/epoch heavy-density signs agree. These epochs on five simulated histories are highly correlated, not 6,075 independent trials. All five peak-normalized density discrepancies nonetheless exceed 2e−5; the largest is 3.22e−4 on the small near-zero mode. Sign accuracy and precision accuracy are separate results.

The archived grid is too sparse to validate the precise root or its drift: the two tested interpolation choices differ by 0.00271 and 0.00547 Mpc⁻¹ at the endpoints. The physical solution predicts a positive instantaneous node velocity of 1.33436e−5 and 1.37710e−3 Mpc⁻² there. Differentiating the governing equations and independently differencing nearby readouts of the integrated solution agree to relative 2.33e−6 and 1.93e−7. At the frozen roots the heavy density drops out of the instantaneous Weyl source, **but gravity does not disappear**: the total Weyl signal is 31.65% and 32.01% of matched cold. Past metric feedback is still present. See node-brackets.csv and gravity-at-frozen-node.csv.

The conditional forecasts scored the 1,214 epochs after the seed. In the physical-background replay both pass at full source resolution, but dropping alternating source knots changes them by 3.18e−5 and 2.78e−5. There are gaps up to 1.067 Mpc in conformal time. The k=5 conditional heavy replay and the tiny nonempty cold slow scalar also miss the strict threshold. The empty cold heavy component is an identity, not a success metric. By contrast, halving the fully forward step changes near-crossover densities by at most 2.88e−9 of peak, and changing integrator changes them by at most 1.11e−9. The archived five-momentum and streaming choices do not restore agreement in the heavy density. The streaming treatment matters more for the Weyl readout (changes up to 8.52e−5). The physical slow pressure differs greatly from the archive relative to its own tiny peak. All comparisons, including failed gates and same-sign counts for pressure, remain in the CSVs. The k=5 early independent control agrees on nonempty density, CDM and Weyl; its near-zero trace-minus-constraint diagnostic does not pass its own-scale check.
