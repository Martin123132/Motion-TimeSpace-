# A real outer-Galaxy test of the single bound state

**Question.** Can the same retained nodeless heavy-field state, at any occupation allowed by the outer Cepheid speed, account for the inferred Milky Way mass at much larger radii? This is a test of an **isolated spherical single-state assignment**, not of every state of the MTS action. The inner ordinary force is assumed inward or zero; no ordinary mass budget is assigned, not even for the hot halo.

**Prediction frozen first.** The ordinary and heavy matter source the same Newtonian potential. Using the [outer Cepheid speed at 17.58 kpc](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.21780v1), reserve *all* that speed for the wave to maximize its allowed mass. The preceding monotone mass law M(<R;L) ∝ H(R/L)/L, H′≥0, makes the smallest allowed L the maximizing occupation at **every fixed aperture**. With the retained pole that gives L≥6.898 kpc and R_half≥13.130 kpc. No choice of normalization in the allowed family puts more than **2.7802e11 solar masses** in the entire wave; at 100 kpc the bound is effectively the same. Even raising the speed by three quoted errors gives only 2.8514e11. Dividing by the particle rest mass gives effective nonrelativistic N≤3.2352e101 at the nominal speed, not an exact conserved count of a real field and not an independent formation prediction. The original illustrative normalization gives 1.4813e11 solar masses at 100 kpc.

These are not five independent votes. Gibbons and Vasiliev model the same stream under different assumptions. Gibbons fixes solar constants; its paper quotes slightly differing summaries, so both radii and errors here consistently use **Table 3** (68% widths 0.4 and 0.7). Orphan and Sgr are different streams but share Galactic and LMC modelling issues. Deason excludes likely Sgr stars, approximately corrects LMC reflex motion, assumes tracer slope 4, and uses a potential-slope prior informed by NFW. Its adopted 100-kpc result includes a bias correction, a separate ±0.29 statistical and ±1.21 systematic error; using both lower errors leaves 1.79e11 solar masses at the nominal speed. Correa Magnus & Vasiliev rewind tracer orbits in a moving-LMC model. Use the authors' MW aperture masses, not virial extrapolations or an added LMC mass. Catalog and reduction independence has not been audited. Do not combine errors into a Bayes factor. The illustrative profile is lighter than even the maximized case.

**Like-for-like check.** An ordinary singular isothermal halo also allowed the entire 224.03 km/s at 17.58 kpc can supply up to 5.83e11 and 1.17e12 solar masses at 50 and 100 kpc respectively. This is an upper envelope, not a fit, and that untruncated profile has no finite total mass. The bound state runs out of mass because its *profile* falls off, not because the data pipeline forbids every halo from supplying mass at large radii. Neither model's upper envelope predicts a total mass without the ordinary source.

**Where the extra mass would have to live.** Subtracting the *two aperture maxima* is not a valid upper bound for the 50–100 kpc shell. Its actual maximum generally occurs at a different occupation. Write s=50 kpc/L; shell mass is K s[H(2s)−H(s)], with K=ħ²/(G m_h² · 50 kpc). On [a,b] a valid upper bound in units K is b[H(2b)−H(a)]; midpoint evaluation gives a feasible lower bound. Start with the entire allowed interval including zero, prune only intervals whose upper bound is below an achieved value, and bisect until the gap is below 1e−6 relative. Cross-check on a separate domain-42 solve versus the saved domain-30 shape, and pad by the original 2e−5 numerical tolerance. This brackets the search on the numerical profile; it is not an interval-arithmetic proof of continuum solver error.

The resulting shell upper bound is **3.302e10 solar masses**, reached near L=27.36 kpc. At the much more concentrated state maximizing *total* mass the shell contains only 3.70e7. Gibbons's central mass difference implies 1.20e11 in the shell, hence at least 8.70e10 outside this single wave there at those central values. But taking opposite ends of the two marginal error intervals permits zero extra shell mass; their joint posterior was not available in this calculation. This is a real target for the next model, **not** a statistically established shell deficit. This shell maximization is an exploratory corollary after the freeze, not a preregistered statistic.

**Interpretation and next test.** Turning up the occupation alone cannot make this isolated mode the entire *gravitating mass* on the central distant-tracer estimates without breaking the observed inner speed. To test whether it could nevertheless be the entire *nonbaryonic* component, compare against an independently measured baryonic mass budget at matched apertures, including hot gas, with a disk-aware wave solution. Do not invent an extended component or fit a tail to these same numbers. A mixed or formation-populated state may give a different shape; that would need its own equation, preparation and prediction. The separate, already-frozen θ=π/4 clock experiment remains the next time-evolution test.
