# What moves relative to matter on the saved satellite orbit?

**The compact ordinary population does not, in general, follow the wave centroid.** We used the same five-Gyr extended LMC forcing and two previous occupations, this time letting both the ordinary orbits and the wave deform and exchange gravity. No new coupling, tether, fitted displacement or rotation-curve data. These are **first-order responses** evaluated at the reference source mass. The earlier full-strength numbers were for an *isolated* wave; they are not full-strength answers for the newly coupled system.

| Previous isolated wave, linear / full strength (pc) | 62.92 / 61.22 | 278.42 / 257.46 |
| Loaded wave center in the point-free-fall frame (pc) | 62.92 | 278.41 |

Distances are vector norms: subtract **positions**, not the two tabulated lengths. The new separation is about 36% and 99% of the corresponding linear isolated offset. The loaded wave mass is 2.7802e11 / 1.4813e11 solar masses by preparation. The compact ordinary object stays physically the same: radius 33.36 pc, mass 278,809 solar masses, initially circularly supported. Adding it and solving for the equilibrium again changes the circular speed at 17.58 kpc by +2.12 / +10.04 ppm relative to each unloaded profile. We did not reset the Cepheid ceiling or impose a new fit. Neither this cloud nor its centroid is a model of the entire Galactic disk.

**Why the rigid calculation misses.** Poisson fixes an attractive restoring coefficient from the overlap of the two densities. But the orbit of the satellite also changes the shape of the wave, and that changes the local pull on the ordinary center. On the final displacement direction, the changing wave supplies **64.0%** (compact) and **28.7%** (diffuse) of the separation; the direct differential forcing supplies the remainder to the displayed precision. These are signed projections of vector contributions, not energy fractions. A changing ordinary shape has a negligible effect on this center measurement. For these states that conclusion follows both from the live orbits and from a bound on the variation of the wave's tidal field across the compact cloud. A uniform acceleration drops out of the relative equations.

**Independent checks.** Finite differences of the mutual energy reproduce the derived restoring slope. Equal-and-opposite forces were checked with independent volume integrals. Direct individual Cartesian orbits reproduce the linearized circular-orbit displacement and velocity to 0.15–0.51 ppm at the smallest signed forcing. A separate inertial normal-mode calculation keeps the *wave* live but translates the small ordinary profile without deforming it; it uses a different satellite angular integral, unprojected translations and a retarded-time convolution instead of the production evolution. On refinement it agrees with both final live separation vectors to 1.0 ppm or better. All 65 saved states per preparation include fields and orbital variables; restarting independently at 2.5 Gyr reproduces the last 33 states exactly.

**What failed and what is being claimed.** The first direct shell discretization showed fast growth that moved with resolution. Subtracting the common force on each enclosed mass group removes that cancellation algebraically, and consistently subtracting the harmonic field reduces the spurious growth further without changing the measured separation. We preserved the failed packet. Center results pass the 20 ppm spatial, time, source, and larger-domain checks. The tiny ordinary internal RMS is not resolved to 20 ppm in every refinement (one compact comparison changes by 58 ppm of roughly 0.0000046 pc). The first independent diffuse ordinary-position comparison also missed at 26.8 ppm; a higher-resolution calculation brings it to 3.13 ppm. This validates these center readouts over the forced history, not arbitrary long-time stability. The orbit is an inherited best-fit environment, not data measuring an offset. A finite-strength offset relative to a real stellar population will require testing the finite-amplitude response and specifying that population.
