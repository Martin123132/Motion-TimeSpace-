# Fixed population-source experiment

Use the source table associated with Lian et al., ApJL 990 L37, and their 2026 erratum, ApJL 1003 L51 (doi:10.3847/2041-8213/ae6e40). Do not use their rotation decomposition, fitted dark halo, or any MTS/Gaia velocity or off-plane force residual to set the stellar source. Use the current pinned radial living/WD/NS/BH columns, corrected vertical profiles, and corrected local columns and integrated masses. Show the raw table as well as all conversions. Compare a source normalized by each published global component mass to one normalized by each local component column at the authors' 8.2 kpc. Do not apply both normalizations or add the four components on top of the Imig stars. Separate finite sampling, unobserved material and stochastic/systematic errors. These reconstructions share APOGEE/Gaia inputs with earlier work; not blind tests.

Use linear surface density between published half-integer radii, constant between the centre and 0.5 kpc, and explicitly compare a continuation with published outer stellar slope 2.1 kpc against the measured-domain mass. In the primary models use this tail to 30 kpc, not an observed outer map. Smooth the reflected local vertical shape by two exponential components at the erratum's averaged living-star heights and let the measured height distribution determine each class's thick fraction. Check the vertical model against observed cumulative fractions, propagating the local shape radially only as a named assumption. Retain local north and south separately in the source diagnostic; do not introduce spurious disk asymmetry from the superseded selection function.

Compare flattened and spherical treatments of the same central population mass without double counting. No additional inherited spherical bulge in either: outer disk gas and compact field retained. Preserve the projected stellar density in the second case by Abel-deprojecting the central excess above a disk continuation: join continuously to the observed profile at 4 kpc, extrapolate with the linear trend of the 4.5,5.5,6.5-kpc annuli, flatten inside the innermost sample. This is a geometry sensitivity, not a direct 3-D bulge measurement. Solve the same massive wave and compare to the fixed-scale spherical halo at the same one-point calibration. Hold the two previously inferred occupations as counterfactual fixed controls when testing amplitude dependence. Reuse both latest saved stellar sources as baselines. Compare the radial and polar forces on both sides, solar quasar readout, and the exposed velocity points. Numerical checks are distinct from empirical residuals.
