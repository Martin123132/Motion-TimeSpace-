# From observed stellar populations to the common metric

**Authority.** [Lian et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.13665v1) describe the local Gaia cylinder and APOGEE radial profiles. Use the [authors' published 2026 correction](https://doi.org/10.3847/2041-8213/ae6e40), not the earlier claim of significant vertical asymmetry. Negative heights were looked up incorrectly in a selection-function table. The current [pinned data release](https://github.com/lianjianhui/Source-data-for-MW-mass-paper/tree/78ba3c4e8e92a2d3b6e03842e437d6e95df6e9fd) supplies the 17 radial density bins and 357 height bins. Corrected local component columns (solar masses/pc²) are 25.438, 4.599, 0.682 and 1.560; global component masses are 2.031, 0.435, 0.058 and 0.132 in \(10^{10}M_\odot\). There is an actual bookkeeping discrepancy: the printed local components sum to 32.279, not the reported aggregate 32.378. The 0.099 difference is too large for last-digit rounding. Likewise the printed global components sum to 2.656, versus 2.659 in aggregate. Use the four stated components and record both differences; do not invent a fifth component or distribute an unexplained remainder. The latter has reported systematic and stochastic uncertainty 0.353 and 0.095 in the same units. The remnants are **population and stellar-evolution estimates**, not counted compact objects at each position. Brown dwarfs are not a fifth measured class in this release. An independent source construction is not an independent galaxy sample: underlying APOGEE/Gaia data overlap earlier work.

**Height-coordinate check.** The current README refers to a superseded FITS basename. The corrected primary and ERROR images have shape 357 by 6. Multiplying their sixth column by \(10^9\) matches *every* height in the archived original [coordinate witness](https://github.com/lianjianhui/Source-data-for-MW-mass-paper/blob/a4f8fe81f97e7012600b8dcf3a1c5416aa4ecd5d/density-dtr-sn-250615.fits) to floating-point precision; the resulting inner 0.01-kpc spacing, outer bins and ~3-kpc span correspond to the paper. Do not multiply **densities** by that factor: integrating the corrected values over physical pc independently recovers the local columns approximately. The ERROR image's last column is coordinates, not coordinate errors. Superseded density values are not loaded into the potential. Both archival and current bytes are preserved locally.

**The radial table is a shape input, not a mass-calibrated volume.** Applying the raw annular values directly does not return the erratum's global component masses or Gaia local column. We use either each published global mass divided by \(2\pi\int R S_j(R)dR\), or each published local column divided by \(S_j(8.2)\), never both. This is a reconstruction with explicit choices for interpolation and endpoints; it does not reverse-engineer the authors' exact calibration pipeline. The reported solar coordinates in the radial paper and in the Gaia force-map study differ: 8.2 kpc fixes source amplitude; 8.122 kpc is the readout for the quasar test. No shift of data points was used to improve agreement.

**Vertical and central geometry.** Let \(H\) be the common extent of the north and south local bins. Fold the corrected observations, integrate their first absolute-height moment using linear interpolation, and write the normalized profile as a mixture of \(e^{-|z|/h}/(2h)\) at \(h=0.265,0.795\) kpc (means of the erratum's two north/south living-star scales). The thick fraction \(w_j\) is obtained by equating the observed truncated mean to
\[
 \frac{\sum_i w_{ji}h_i[1-(1+H/h_i)e^{-H/h_i}]}{\sum_i w_{ji}(1-e^{-H/h_i})}.
\]
This is a reconstruction from **stellar data** rather than a force fit. The measured remnant profiles choose their own fractions. The component column then multiplies this normalized density. The same vertical shape at all radii is *not* independently measured; flaring, warp, disk bar and stellar-population kicks are not captured. Centered symmetry and the two heights are model approximations.

The radial table already contains central stars. One control flattens the entire stellar source into disk profiles. The other removes a nonnegative inner projected excess \(\Sigma_b\) from each disk and uses the spherical deprojection
\[
 \rho_b(r)=-\pi^{-1}\int_r^{4\ {\rm kpc}}\Sigma'_b(R)\,dR/\sqrt{R^2-r^2}.
\]
Piecewise-linear slopes integrate analytically as differences of arcosh; the spherical mass and potential then follow radial quadrature. Cumulative mass, forward projection and nonnegative density are tested independently. Both cases have exactly the same projected stellar mass by construction and no extra old spherical bulge. The inferred central mass is compared to the authors' bulge-to-total ratio, not forced to agree. Gas and the separate inherited compact source remain as in the previous stage.

**Common-metric response.** At fixed pole and occupation the retained stationary real envelope obeys \(-\kappa^2\Delta f/2+(\Phi_\mathrm{baryon}+\Phi_f)f=\epsilon f\), \(\Delta\Phi_f=4\pi Gf^2\). A is the amplitude of the comparison spherical NFW (16-kpc scale); it has exactly zero polar acceleration. Both receive the same source and their single outer calibration when matched. The two previous nominal occupations/amplitudes are also carried unchanged as controls. The matched solution is approached independently from both earlier stationary states; equality is a numerical branch check, not proof that there are no other physical states. Distinguish empirical stellar population models, fixed shared physical coupling, and calibrated wave occupation.

For each saved wave, integrate its angular Poisson moments independently. For \(\Phi_f=\sum_\ell\Phi_\ell(r)P_\ell(\mu)\), \(\mu=z/r\), its polar acceleration is
\[
 a_{\theta,f}=\sqrt{1-\mu^2}\,\sum_\ell P_\ell'(\mu)\Phi_\ell(r)/r.
\]
A spherical mass cancels identically. North and mirrored south constrain the same predicted magnitude if the state is reflection symmetric. Subtract the ordinary-source polar force from the overlap of their outer bands. The resulting interval concerns **additional angular gravity**, not microscopic pressure and not a unique mass-density inversion. The effective quadrupole divides the result by \(3\mu\sqrt{1-\mu^2}/r\); the code also evaluates all higher modes, rather than labeling this effective value a pure quadrupole.

**Derived susceptibility to disk flattening.** Hold the actual radial monopole, common coupling and full state's norm fixed, and set the common external potential to its monopole plus \(\lambda\) times its measured-source angular contrast. At \(\lambda=0\) the relaxed state \(f_0\) is spherical. Its first variation solves
\[
 P (H_0-\epsilon_0+f_0\,\delta\Phi_f[\cdot])P\,u=-P\,(\Phi_{\rm ext}-\langle\Phi_{\rm ext}\rangle)f_0,\qquad
 \Delta\delta\Phi_f[u]=8\pi G f_0u ,
\]
where \(P\) projects onto the fixed-norm tangent. Rotational invariance makes the independent \(\ell\) channels decouple at first order. In particular changing the spherical norm adds **no first-order polar force** there; this is not a general statement about a deformed or excited state. Freeze the linearization, check at two signed step sizes and continue the same wave to the full exterior angular contrast. This separates a weak physical response from a numerical linearization that simply missed a large nonlinear effect. The shell-averaged external field is a theoretical control, not another measured galaxy.

**Input uncertainty versus fits.** Forces are linear functionals of each calibrated radial knot \(p_{ij}\). For quoted raw values \(s_{ij}\), marginal errors \(e_{ij}\), and width \(w\), express the set of allowed, recalibrated shapes *exactly* by \(t_i\max(0,s_{ij}-we_{ij})\le p_{ij}\le t_i(s_{ij}+we_{ij})\), \(t_i>0\), together with the published mass or local column equality per class. This uses a common multiplier within each class. In the spherical-bulge option require admissible nonnegative disk and Abel excess. Stellar and gas direct forces, the matched NFW amplitude and arbitrary spherical enclosed-mass inequalities are affine in these unknowns. Max/min at each site bound the ordinary polar force; a separate simultaneous feasibility test uses a single source for all sites. For general spherical positive material require increasing enclosed mass through the two sites and the calibrated outer point; for nonincreasing density require decreasing shell means per increment of \(r^3\); for \(r\rho(r)\) nonincreasing use increments of \(r^2\). Zero/one/two raw-error boxes are **sensitivities**, not joint confidence regions, and do not include scale-height or common survey/calibration systematics. A force-selected feasible witness is not an independent MTS prediction. The cone does not re-solve nonlinear MTS for every vertex.
