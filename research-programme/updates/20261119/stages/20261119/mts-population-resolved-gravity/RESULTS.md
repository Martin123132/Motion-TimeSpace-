# Where the independently reconstructed stars help — and where the wave falls short

**With the Lian population profiles, the inner Milky Way needs appreciably more gravity toward the plane than the retained standing wave produces.** At cylindrical R=6 kpc and |z|=1.5 kpc, the central reconstruction gives ordinary polar acceleration **0.416**. Even the nearer edge of the north/south [Gaia force bands](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.13358v3) requires **0.573**. The MTS wave adds **0.00849**, not the **0.158** needed. Units here are 10⁻¹⁰ m/s², with the south reflected. On this source, the minimum extra directional force is **18.6 times** the wave contribution. At R=8.3 kpc both the wave and a spherical halo fall within the *polar* bands. The signed radial components are a separate miss. This is a conditional test of a specific populated state and ordinary-source reconstruction.

## Why this is a different test

Four radial surface-density profiles from [the authors' data release](https://github.com/lianjianhui/Source-data-for-MW-mass-paper/tree/78ba3c4e8e92a2d3b6e03842e437d6e95df6e9fd) distinguish living stars, white dwarfs, neutron stars and black holes. The old Imig stars were replaced, not added to. The authors' [erratum](https://doi.org/10.3847/2041-8213/ae6e40) removes the earlier claimed vertical asymmetry. We checked all 357 vertical coordinates against the archival source, preventing a billion-fold error in the scale of the disk. Remnants are inferred from stellar populations, not directly mapped dark objects. The printed local components sum to 32.279 rather than the printed aggregate 32.378; we did not allocate that difference to a fictitious population.

Using the four corrected **global** masses predicts a local stellar column of **32.02** solar masses/pc². Independently normalizing to the four **local** columns yields **26.77 billion** solar masses globally, versus **26.56 billion** from the printed global components. The local and global reconstructions are not independent catalogues. The smoothing model's cumulative vertical discrepancy at the tested heights is at most 1.69% of each class's full column; transplanting these heights away from the solar cylinder is a larger unmeasured assumption. Only ~0.35% of the adopted stellar mass lies in the extrapolated tail past the final radial knot. Its direct inward acceleration at the outer anchor is ~0.95 (km/s)²/kpc; this last number is not a self-consistent no-tail wave solve.

Placing the central projected excess into a sphere instead of the disk moves **7.35–7.41 billion** solar masses between geometries without changing any projected mass. Forward projecting it recovers the original excess; a second inherited stellar bulge was not added. Neither geometry, nor the independent choice of local versus global normalization, removes the inner force mismatch. We are testing the response to this source, not declaring it the uniquely correct Galactic mass map.

## What the two competing models actually do

Global-component masses, spherical central excess; both models matched only to 224.03 km/s at 17.58 kpc. Source choices are independent of the force values, but these velocity tracers and Gaia force bands are already exposed.

The spherical comparison is closer on both rotation tracers and on the [Gaia quasar dipole](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.02036) in this stage. That did not hold across the previous Imig source conventions. At the 6-kpc site the preceding census-scaled catalogue model pulled too strongly inward; this reconstruction instead pulls too weakly there. The data therefore do not justify picking a dark-sector law while silently switching the stars. Both inherited fixed-occupation controls, signed forces at the tested sites in both hemispheres, and aggregate comparison scores are saved. The new matched wave norm corresponds to 2.159 × 10¹¹ solar masses; this is a one-point-calibrated mass, not a formation prediction. No joint significance is inferred from overlapping pointwise bands.

## Did we overlook a nonlinear response of the field?

We derived the angular response from the stationary heavy-field and Poisson equations on the spherical control, then turned on the actual disk's angular gravity in steps **with its radial monopole and wave mass held fixed**. At 6 kpc the linear prediction was **0.0084923** and the full relaxed solution gave **0.0084876**, only **0.055%** different. At 8.3 kpc the difference was 0.113%. The leading quadrupole accounts for about 91% of the physical wave force at 6 kpc. Changing the norm of the spherical background cannot produce this angular force at first order. The small response is in the parent equation for this equilibrium, not something a looser numerical tolerance or another iteration will amplify eighteenfold.

We also allowed every one of the 68 radial class/bin measurements to move inside its printed marginal error box, while maintaining physical nonnegativity and its global or local component normalization. Even letting the bins vary by **twice** their printed errors leaves a minimum extra polar force of **0.081–0.092** at 6 kpc across the four geometries/calibrations. A spherical halo of *any* mass profile supplies exactly zero there; its radial structure alone cannot fix this comparison. The radial bands alone permit a spherical nonincreasing density profile here, unlike the prior census-scaled catalogue case. These are conditional, deliberately permissive input boxes, **not** joint confidence intervals and not limits on unmodeled flaring, gas or disequilibrium.

For the global/spherical case we then **actually re-solved MTS** at the source profiles maximizing the *ordinary* polar force: the total rises to **0.4591** at one width and **0.4966** at two, still below **0.5734**. The spherical totals are 0.4502 and 0.4874. These are stress tests, not observationally preferred populations or proofs of a global nonlinear optimum. Across the nominal numerical refinements, the largest scaled change in the 180 reported velocity/slope/vertical-force/quasar readouts was 2.94 parts per million; the force deficit is far larger.

**Decision:** carry the directional-force requirement into an actual alternative state of the same retained action, not a fitted new coupling. A number-conserving mixture of oppositely rotating first excited modes can be tested against it while preserving zero net spin, and flaring/bar structure at 5–7 kpc remains an independent ordinary-source alternative.
