# Preparation, energy flux and proper timing

## Initial data and conserved homogeneous response
Use the predecessor's areal metric −N²dt²+dr²/F+r²dΩ², F=1−2m/r, B r²dr fixed particle count and γ₀=√(1+J/r²) for its stationary circular orbits. H=A cos(ωt+π/4). Fix its *local* time derivative Π initially, so the canonical field momentum W=Π/√F changes with the loaded metric. The exact excess energy at launch consists of z δF H′²/2 and the change in B√F√(1+J/r²+FP²). The latter is evaluated with differences of squares, rather than subtracting two nearly identical square roots. Solve δm′=r²δρ; evolve the mass with the independently evaluated scalar plus matter momentum flux. For the prepared case use the frozen P=γ₀ V/(NF); do not re-optimize P in the finite target. Inverting the particle label r−d(t,r)=r₀ gives ξ=d/(1−∂ᵣd) through second order in the displacement. Angular pressure from counterrotation is retained, while the radial kinetic stress is initially quadratic in V. For a material clock dτ=Ndt/γ along its trajectory; a clock held at a prescribed radius is different. For the actual pressure marker include the spatial derivative of the equilibrium scalar pressure as well as the moving excess. Regions with B=0 have probe orbits only.

For the same stationary mean the linear shell equation is ξ̈+κ²ξ=−a sin(σt), σ=2ω, V=aσ/(σ²−κ²). The particular solution is ξ_p=(V/σ)sin(σt). The difference is a free oscillator. In variables Z=(κ(ξ−ξ_p), ξ̇−ξ̇_p)/V, its propagator is R(κt) with rows (cos,sin) and (−sin,cos). This preserves the norm and phase area: gravity does not relax arbitrary radial data onto the particular orbit in this leading collisionless equation. The finite histories test both initial energies, not an exact full-field conservation law for this reduced quantity. The early drift of a free-start preparation is approximately linear only for a small fraction of the epicycle; extrapolating it indefinitely is unjustified.

## Deriving the conditional initial velocity from earlier forcing
Let the *net shell force* be −a W(t)sin(σt), W rising from 0 to 1 before t=0, constant thereafter, while κ and the mean are fixed. Start from rest before the drive. Superpose step responses dW=g(s)ds. Each newly switched-on fraction needs a free correction opposite the particular position and velocity at s. Thus, at the end of assembly,
```
Z(0) = −∫ g(s) R(−κs) [ (κ/σ) sin(σs), cos(σs) ]ᵀ ds .
```
Converting to complex exponentials gives |Z(0)|≤(1+κ/σ)(|ĝ(σ−κ)|+|ĝ(σ+κ)|)/2. For a stretched fixed smooth rate distribution those Fourier components vanish (off resonance); then and only in the sense of negligible free response the retarded solution approaches the periodic launch. Phase cancellations may also occur for a particular end phase; the sufficient bound is not a necessary condition on each sideband separately. Long duration by itself is insufficient. For the examples g is proportional to 30u²(1−u)²[1+e cos(σs)] on −D≤s≤0, u=(s+D)/D, with integral normalized to one. Compare a direct quadrature of this identity with integrating W, ξ and ξ̇ from scratch (1, 8, 64 cycles). Tolerances reference V. W is a diagnostic for an actual net force if known; simply multiplying a scalar stress tensor by W does not give a conserved parent source. No assumption about the original unmeasured assembly history enters the finite experiment.

## A clock contrast that does not subtract two large numbers
At first order the periodic-minus-rest displacement is Δξ=V sin(κt)/κ. At fixed radius Δm=−m_b′Δξ and ∂ᵣΔν=(1+2rν′)Δm/(F r²). The pressure perturbation is identical at this order. Outside the material, Δm=0 and Δν depends only on time, even though the **common** oscillation at that point is not zero. A change of time coordinate removes this contrast to first order. At finite order neither a changed initial kinetic energy nor the backreaction of the ambient real field is forbidden.

The paired simulation uses four numerical coefficients for mean, preparation contrast at mean (zero), rest-start real-minus-mean, and periodic-minus-rest at finite wave stress. Both compared solutions contain ordinary matter. The analytic static supported branch is identical; compute the tiny difference directly in products, inverses, roots, exponentials and the real field and matter equations. The contrast lapse can be integrated from the exterior clock rather than adding and subtracting a time-dependent spatial constant. At that clock the two lapse histories agree in the chosen coordinate. The other worldlines and null rays must still be propagated. We ran original-central comparisons, a paired outer-emitter gauge, a paired outer-receiver gauge, and an independent half time-step and half sampling-step replay. Matching proper times, not comparing coordinate fields between gauges, is the observable.

Independent linear check: write S=1/(N√F) for the background radial light-time density, T=∫S|dr| for a leg, d=τ_emit/N_e and a(r) for the background travel time to a point along a ray. Temporarily set Δν_e(t)=0 by referencing each lapse perturbation to the emitter. Then the perturbed one-way receiver proper time at fixed launch is
```
δτ_r(d)=N_r [∫path S (Δm/(rF)−Δν)(d+a(r),r)|dr| + ∫₀^(d+T) Δν_r(t)dt].
```
For an echo use both legs and the emitter as destination. Subtract the same expression at d=0. Frequency is obtained from the inverse arrival-time derivative, δ(f_r/f_e)=−(N_e/N_r) d(δτ_r/N_r)/dd. Evaluate temporal differences of sine/cosine analytically before spatial integration; compute the lapse difference as a *local* radial integral. Static profile derivatives follow from the stationary Einstein equations and scalar radial stress conservation p′=−(ρ_s+p)ν′−zF A′²/r. The resulting calculation in clock_identity.py uses no evolved optical data. In the exterior both kernels vanish identically. Inside, a displacement nearly proportional to time gives an almost constant echo-frequency offset, which cancels again when referenced to launch. This is why the remaining echo-frequency tests are much more ill-conditioned than echo delays.
