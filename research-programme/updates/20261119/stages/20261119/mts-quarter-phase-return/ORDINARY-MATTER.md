# Do ordinary baryons fill the aperture budget?

The preceding sealed test provides separate upper bounds for the isolated scalar's total mass and its mass in the 50–100 kpc shell. It did not subtract baryons. Here we credit observations of ordinary matter using their actual apertures. We do not claim a complete census, an independently prepared Galactic wave or a statistically independent holdout.

**Inventory scenarios.** [Licquia & Newman](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.1078) give 6.08±1.14e10 solar masses in disk plus bulge (Kroupa IMF, exponential disk); [Deason et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02763) infer 1.4±0.4e9 in halo stars out to 100 kpc including Sgr, with a Kroupa light-to-mass conversion. We allow both entire masses even in the 50-kpc inventory. [Nakanishi & Sofue](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.08877) report about 8e9 in HI + H₂ inside 30 kpc. The generous scenario substitutes [THOR's extrapolated optical-depth-corrected HI upper estimate](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201937095) of 1.05e10 plus 0.85e9 molecular; we allow 1.4 times the reported hydrogen components for associated material. This is an explicit allowance, not a new measurement of gas at 100 kpc. [Miller & Bregman](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.3116) infer hot mass 3.8±0.3e9 at 50 kpc and 4.3 (+0.9/−0.8)e10 at 250 kpc from O VIII emission, conditional on a metallicity/ionization and density model. Positivity lets us credit the **entire 250-kpc hot estimate** at 100 kpc as a generous comparison; that is not a measurement at 100 kpc.

For the larger ordinary scenario these allowances sum to 1.4189e11 solar masses at 100 kpc. Even using the +3 quoted-error inner-speed wave ceiling (2.8514e11), [Vasiliev et al.'s 100-kpc stream estimate](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.10726) leaves 1.330e11 at its central value or 0.930e11 at its lower reported endpoint. The corresponding lower-end remainder is 0.300e11 for [Deason et al.'s halo-star estimate](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.13801), allowing both statistical and systematic errors downward. [Gibbons et al.'s lower stream mass](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.2243) needs **no** deficit in this scenario. These are sensitivity budgets, not confidence levels or evidence that an uncounted component is nonbaryonic. Uninventoried ionized phases, cold material outside the mapped disk, satellites, geometry, dynamical assumptions and halo deformation matter. Do not put a satellite's inferred total dark mass into the ordinary census.

**Independent gas-column inequality.** A radio burst beyond the Galaxy measures an electron column. It does not directly measure a shell mass. For a *spherical* ionized distribution with proton-mass-to-electron conversion μ_e=2/(1+X) at fully ionized hydrogen mass fraction X (we bracket 0.70 and 0.75), put a=50 and b=100 kpc, observer radius d=8.275 kpc. Then
```
M(a,b)/(4π μ_e m_p DM) ≤ b²                              positive radial density
                         ≤ (b³−a³)/(3(b−a))              if nonincreasing within shell
                         ≤ (b³−a³)/(3(b−d))              if nonincreasing also from Sun to shell.
```
These are alternative upper bounds with consistent length units, not measured equalities. On the outward part of a line of sight dl≥dr, giving the first inequality directly. For decreasing density write it as a positive superposition of uniform radial layers extending from a (or from d) to varying outer cutoff s. Each has shell-mass/column ratio bounded by [min(s,b)³−a³]/[3(s−a)] or by [min(s,b)³−a³]/[3(s−d)]. The maximum occurs at s=b: the numerator/denominator increases for a≤s≤b and the denominator alone then increases. For the second expression the derivative sign follows from 2s³−3ds²+a³>0 here. Unknown density inside the solar radius can be discarded. The outward-radial LOS attains these geometric ceilings for suitable profiles (a thin outer layer in case 1). Five orientations check the calculation. In a nonspherical halo a single low column need not bound the mass over other directions.

Separate the measured total from a halo decomposition. [Kirsten et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-04354-w) localize FRB 20200120E to the M81 system; its total DM is about 87.8 pc/cm³ and their inferred Galactic halo upper allowance is roughly 32–42. [Ravi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.01000) report total DM about 111 for FRB 20220319D; alternative nearby-pulsar disk estimates give halo limits 28.7 or 47.3. We round the totals upward and never average alternative subtractions. For X=0.70, even the stronger radial monotonicity gives a 1.02e11 solar-mass shell ceiling using the M81 total alone, which can accommodate the *central* 8.70e10 requirement of the earlier stream comparison. Using M81's 42 halo allowance gives 4.88e10, or 5.50e10 using Ravi's more permissive 47.3. If monotonicity is dropped, even these foreground-subtracted columns permit the central requirement. The X-ray component alone can contribute at most 3.92e10 to this shell from the central 50- and 250-kpc measurements (4.85e10 combining their opposite reported endpoints). Radio and X-ray probe overlapping material: **do not add** the two ceilings. The dynamical shell requirement itself can vanish across marginal mass intervals, whose covariance is unknown.
