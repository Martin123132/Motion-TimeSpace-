# Two results: the Newtonian contact and a delayed material response

**What changed.** On the retained spherical charged preparation, a calculation from the initial fields predicts a later central density pulse that is absent at launch. Independently, the material gradient term that looked capable of ruining the cold local limit is the already-counted Newton-potential contact. No galaxy fit, measured clock event, new pressure term or adjusted coupling was used.

## Frozen, held-out pulse

A compact difference of two scalar modes (k=9 and 15) has zero central field and density shift at launch. Propagate the scalar on the ordinary charged GR geometry, then let its stress change the central material density. Freeze all four prediction files before comparing with separate, fully coupled real evolutions at two resolutions, two amplitudes and both signs. Only the scalar's feedback on its own propagation geometry is omitted in the prediction. No measured histories are inputs to it. “Held out” means a new numerical initial disturbance, not an observational dataset.

These four budgets cover the full 201 saved samples from τ=0 to 0.8, not just the peaks. Each sums model difference, independent prediction and target grid changes, and the finite-amplitude correction, all divided by the peak of its own target. All are below the unchanged 2e−5 criterion. This estimates numerical and approximation sensitivity; it is not a statistical confidence interval. In the η=1 control the ordinary charged material is exactly force-balanced: comparing the induced response there isolates focusing by the massive scalar rather than ordinary collapse.

The independent launch calculation predicts a **negative** quartic density coefficient, −0.00130186209, before the later positive peak. The extrapolation from τ=0.024–0.192 passes for both charge values (1.05–1.30e−5 relative); the 0.032–0.256 extrapolation misses (2.89–3.20e−5). Do not call this an all-window measurement of the coefficient. It does not change the separately frozen waveform test.

## How much room is left for a contact correction?

- At the centre, the number and scalar accelerations follow from the constrained mass, electric flux, material chemical potential and scalar stress. Across the ordinary and massive preparations the independent conditioned calculation resolves all 406 nonzero matrix entries; 140 exact zeroes and 14 absolute-only null comparisons remain separately labelled. All eight sign-change locations agree within 1.4e−7 relative for the deliberately large negative test contact.
- In the first-order reduced theory the gradient coefficient obeys (γ₁+γ₂)k²/2=(d₀−4d₂)/3. Requiring each parent insertion below 1% **at the momentum being used** bounds the new term to 1/60 of the Newtonian term, versus an ordinary residual of 0.36 of that term at η=0.8. The independently bounded mixed scalar contribution at launch does not overturn this conclusion. This is not an all-wavelength stability theorem.
- The ordinary shell derivation predicts how the correction evolves without putting in a sound speed. For η=0.8 and 1, both contacts, and k=4 and 24, all eight refined waveform comparisons pass through τ=3.24 (worst 5.92e−9 relative). All eight separately conditioned k=0 comparisons pass (worst 6.73e−6); the original ill-conditioned zero-mode results remain visible.
- At τ=3.24 on the retained η=0.8 ordinary branch, the first-order absolute correction to the central density transfer for **any** allowed k is ≤6.164e−5; the no-contact transfer is 1.001330914. For k=24 under the saved coefficient caps the tighter bound is 2.27e−45. These are conditional theoretical bounds on this response, not errors on a measured Newton constant. Longer shell extrapolations are explicitly labelled.

**What did not work.** Exponentiating the instantaneous acceleration matrix cannot replace evolving the massive scalar. The raw evolved comparisons passed 41/52 mesh and 15/20 real-amplitude checks; the +4 density trial moved by 94% on refinement and is not evidence of physical growth. Direct real initial slopes originally missed 28 nonzero tests at the finest grid. A regular-axis flux and conditioned directional derivative distinguish numerical subtraction from actual mode coupling. Flat spacetime scalar transport missed by up to 3.77e−4 on the single-mode controls; using the independently evolving ordinary GR geometry brought the worst residual down to 8.12e−7 there before the mixed pulse was frozen.

**Next physical test.** Take the pulse beyond the central readout: derive the first scalar-sourced metric correction on the ordinary background, including constrained initial mass, propagate it into a finite-radius two-clock light signal, and freeze the predicted event-minus-quiet signal before comparison with a full calculation. Use the same matter preparation on both sides, retain the pure ordinary control, and distinguish fixed total mass from fixed material number. Do not count this pulse as a measured celestial source.
