# Resolved Milky Way stars in the retained MTS weak-field equation

**The new input is a spatial stellar density, not another adjustable disk radius.** The 186 fitted population profiles from Imig et al.'s APOGEE release give two materially different Newtonian source reconstructions. Normalizing each profile to its published surviving mass gives **37.01 billion solar masses** in the adopted finite cylinder. Normalizing its shape to the reported solar column convention instead gives **31.93 billion**. The catalogue-normalized half-mass radius is 2.915 kpc. We replayed every archived population mass to within 5×10⁻¹⁵ relative. The release calculation and the physical cylinder integral use different radial measures and the local-column calculation also uses a different parameter summary. This establishes the bookkeeping, not which source is uniquely correct. See DERIVATION.md and normalization-replay.csv.

**What happens in the field equations:** the stationary heavy wave was re-solved against each source in the retained common metric; the comparison halo was given *exactly* the same ordinary gravity and outer-speed calibration. A shape-only experiment holds the original analytic disk mass fixed. The other two use the source's own stellar amplitude. Neither gas nor a self-consistent bulge decomposition is silently included in these replacements. The inherited compact source and bulge remain common, possibly double-counting central stars. These are controlled Milky Way calculations, not new SPARC predictions.

The circular-speed scores split by tracer rather than giving a blanket winner. They are exposed diagnostics, not a new independent sample. The heavier wave occupations in the latter rows were inferred at the common 224.03 km/s outer anchor, not predicted from formation. Keeping the old wave occupation instead gives 233.93 / 202.39 / 195.48 km/s there for the three rows; the corresponding fixed-amplitude NFW values are 227.90 / 209.50 / 207.18. This separation is important: changing a source and recalibrating the state are not the same physical operation.

At R=8.3 kpc, z=1.5 kpc the catalogue-mass case gives vertical acceleration −1430 / −1459 (km/s)²/kpc, less downward than the inherited −2104 / −2117. Relative to the two existing red-clump central values, the new MTS discrepancies are 1.65 and 0.92 quoted widths; neither tracer separates the models. The known annular stellar mass inside 7–9 kpc and |z|≤1.5 kpc is 2.062 billion for the first *unscaled* reconstruction and 2.348 billion for the second, despite the second's smaller integrated Galaxy mass. Total mass alone does not specify local gravity.

## A derived, but difficult, discriminant

At the same off-plane position, T = g_z+(z/R)g_in cancels *any spherical* potential, including an NFW halo of any scale or amplitude. The nonspherical stationary MTS wave adds **−16.02 / −11.01 / −12.12 (km/s)²/kpc** in the three cases. This survives two calculations (force vs angular derivative of potential) and the refined states. The ordinary stellar term is roughly 34–43 times larger. A spherical halo plus disk does **not** predict zero *total* T, and a flattened halo need not give zero excess.

We then froze three radii (6, 8.3, 12 kpc, all z=1.5 kpc) and derived weights **(+0.1800, −0.6415, +0.7457)** from just the two stellar vectors. Their weighted sum removes any global linear mixture of these exact stellar templates, in addition to spherical gravity. Unfortunately it removes most of the wave signal too: the refined remaining accelerations are only **+0.511 / +0.285 / +0.221 (km/s)²/kpc**. More digits are not warranted by the numerical or physical uncertainty. The result was not used to choose new radii. This is a way to test a field prediction, not evidence that the sky shows it.

**We tested the principal missing foreground rather than merely naming it.** Using only the two published gas templates from [McMillan, equation 4 and Table 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1608.00971), with no gas, halo or wave fit, the direct contributions to the *same* weighted observable are **−20.58 (atomic-associated gas) and +4.43 (molecular-associated gas)**. Net **−16.15**, opposite in sign and roughly **32–73 times** the wave residual. At the single near-Sun point the two gas terms add to −251.22. These are conditional smooth models, not precision maps; the atomic normalization already includes associated helium. This direct foreground does not include gas-driven wave rearrangement or an outer recalibration. It is stored separately, never retrofitted into the stars-only scores. The primary gas inputs give a physical reason not to promote the three-point residual as a clean observation.

## Numerical checks and boundary of interpretation

- The independent Newton force-flux and mass integrals agree to 7.5 parts per billion on both physical stellar reconstructions. The analytic finite Plummer control includes its boundary potential offset. Readout forces use direct rings rather than relying on multipoles where their derivatives fail the 20-ppm criterion.
- All 18 calibrated response derivatives frozen before testing (three sources × two models × three observables) agree with extrapolated nonlinear perturbations to at worst 1.16×10⁻⁹ relative on the same discretization. This tests the calculation, not the catalogue's observational accuracy.
- Coarser parent discretizations missed 20 ppm on five of 18 endpoint comparisons. We solved the calibrated endpoints again with 256/24, 320/28 and 384/32 radial/angular basis cutoffs in both inherited preparations/boxes. At 384/32 all three slope, vertical force, single-position angular excess, wave occupation and annular-mass comparisons between boxes agree within 20 ppm. The main sequence's last slope changes are still 22–45 ppm; the small three-radius residual differs by 23–95 ppm between the final boxes and more on some successive comparisons. Do not claim universal 20-ppm convergence or change tolerance. All signs and the gas-to-signal size hierarchy survive.
- This is a local, conditional test of an already derived common gravitational coupling. It changes neither the pole nor the action, does not promote an empirical galaxy branch, and does not use timing outcomes.
