# The two finite changes and their different signatures

The field perturbation is to calibrated ordinary living-star density on the *same* metric as the retained smooth wave and the spherical NFW comparison. At fixed cylindrical radius multiply the pooled vertical density by 1+a f(R). Inside 4–7 kpc, f=1. In 7–8 kpc, f=0 for the added family and −M_inner/M_donor for the transfer family. Elsewhere it is zero. The reference annular masses are 2π∫RΣ(R)dR. Total stellar mass changes by a(M_inner+f_donor M_donor); its change vanishes identically for transfer, including at finite amplitude. The donor choice, reference ground state, vertical shape, halo profiles and common outer normalization are retained assumptions, not consequences of the field action. Source occupancy is not independently derived by this exercise.

**What the density data permit.** At fixed calibration each interior fractional margin is width×σ/Σ. With a shared calibration t, let L_i=max(0,1−width σ_i/Σ_i), U_i=1+width σ_i/Σ_i. A feasible t must lie above every (1+a f_i)/U_i and below every (1+a f_j)/L_j for L_j>0. Eliminating t gives linear inequalities
a(L_j f_i−U_i f_j) ≤ U_i−L_j.
Intersect them with 1+a f_i≥0. A unit-width artificial calibration constraint L=U=1,f=0 recovers the fixed calibration case. An independent two-variable linear program checks both endpoints. This uses no kinematic data. The half-mass radius is tested by solving the finite equation M(<r₅₀)=M_final/2, using the calibrated projected total living profile (including its central component) plus the same annular changes. Use the corrected Table 2 living mass 2.031±0.094 ×10¹⁰ M☉ and r₅₀=4.24±0.20 kpc; the widths are not independent of the radial catalog and are not multiplied as likelihoods. The aggregate systematic budget for all populations is not assigned wholesale to living stars.

**How the dark response enters.** Compute the direct radial force at the retained outer anchor and the vertical force at the actual SEGUE sites by finite-thickness elliptic ring integration. Project the *same* ordinary sources on the retained Poisson grid. For MTS solve the stationary self-gravitating wave again at each finite amplitude, adjusting its conserved-number parameter *between stationary solutions* until the reference outer acceleration is restored. No particles are asserted to be created dynamically by this continuation. Report wave overlap, eigen-equation residual and anchor closure; keep derivative-only predictions as checks. The fixed-shape spherical control is simpler: if F_A is the original halo radial force at the anchor and δF_* is the new stellar force there, the exact halo factor is 1−δF_*/F_A. The positivity of this factor is checked. At each fixed radius its resulting finite force is therefore affine in a, not merely a tangent. Means are taken over the same fourteen R<6.5 kpc abundances/sites as the predecessor; this is a descriptive, previously exposed discrepancy, not a new likelihood.

**Light propagation when the source of gravity has both signs.** Write h(D)=(4πG/c²)D[ρ_inner(D)−(M_inner/M_donor)ρ_donor(D)] and A(s)=∫₀ˢ h, B(s)=∫₀ˢ D h. For a source at distance s, the transfer changes ideal optical depth by a T(s), with T=A−B/s. Since T′=B/s², extrema occur at the allowed source-distance endpoints, or at a zero of B. Split the ray at every ring crossing and stellar-profile knot: B is monotone within each sign-definite segment. Bracket sign-changing zeros and explicitly check zero-valued segment boundaries. Beyond the last disk intersection A,B are constant and a source at infinity has T=A. The two ring contributions share a *single source distance*. Independently minimizing each positive contribution and subtracting the other's maximum is a wider valid enclosure, not generally the attainable minimum. The same distance intervals, including unrestricted unmatched stars, as the frozen optical pilot apply. For negative a reverse the endpoint order. Different source stars' permitted ranges may be varied separately to bound the unweighted pilot mean; no assumption of independent errors or of a common red-clump luminosity is needed beyond the original simultaneous error contract.

**Specific physical consequence.** A star entirely behind at least some positive donor column but entirely in front of the 4–7 kpc annulus is a sign test. Adding mass in the annulus alone changes its depth by zero. Transferring that mass out of the near donor changes its depth *downwards* if a>0. This is a prediction for ideal lensing of that source, not an observation of a microlensing event, nor a signature which separates smooth wave matter from smooth NFW matter.
