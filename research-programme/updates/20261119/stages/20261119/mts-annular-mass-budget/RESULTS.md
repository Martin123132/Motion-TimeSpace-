# Moving the observed living stars cannot erase the inner force shortfall in these tests

The missing force is much bigger than the leverage of these two specific changes to the stellar disk. That is true for **both** the retained stationary MTS ground state and the spherical NFW control. Mean inner SEGUE force is 183.65 in the retained units. The largest positive added-mass case tested moves MTS from 97.20 to **102.22** and NFW from 110.28 to **115.12**. Moving already-counted mass from the adjoining ring instead gives **97.95** and **111.01**. We did not choose the amplitudes to make these numbers look good or bad.

## What set the budget

The [corrected Lian et al. mass measurements](https://doi.org/10.3847/2041-8213/ae6e40) supply radial living-star columns and a separate published integral living mass of (20.31 ± 0.94) billion solar masses. The retained reconstruction uses the table as a *shape*: its original numerical profile integrates to 28.84 billion and the retained global calibration multiplies it by 0.7042. We do not treat the raw absolute normalization and the published mass as independently exact. Marginal errors were transported with that scale. Free calibration below means allowing one **additional common comparison scale** across the raw bins, not allowing every radius to float its own calibration. Radius-specific population bias or a different disk/bar decomposition is not covered.

The inner living ring (4–7 kpc) holds 5.459 billion solar masses; the adjacent donor (7–8 kpc) holds 1.559 billion. The selection of the donor is explicit, not an inferred formation history. The 17 measured radial knots bound the positive amplitudes below, with no force or event outcomes involved:

Positive change in inner ring | Fixed calibration, one marginal width | Free common calibration, two widths
---|---:|---:
Add new living mass | +237.6 million M☉ | +942.7 million M☉
Transfer from 7–8 kpc at fixed total | +65.1 million M☉ | +197.2 million M☉

For the first column, the 6.5 kpc knot limits the addition and the 7.5 kpc donor knot limits the transfer. In the wider column the 9.5 kpc exterior measurement also limits calibration in the added family; the 6.5 and 7.5 kpc pair limits the transfer. Negative amplitudes are not assumed symmetric when calibration is free. Closed inequalities and an independent linear program agree on all endpoints. All endpoints satisfy the corresponding marginal-width checks on corrected integral living mass and projected half-mass radius; these are related measurements, not two independent likelihoods. For either family the cumulative mass at a fixed aperture and the total mass are affine in amplitude, so the two half-radius endpoint inequalities also hold between corridor endpoints. If the published total is imposed as an exact equality, only the transfer family allows a nonzero change. See independent-budget.csv and global-shape-check.csv.

## What enters the common gravitational field

The force means use the same fourteen R<6.5 kpc [SEGUE population entries](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.0809), at height 1.1 kpc. Units are |K_z|/(2πG) expressed in solar masses/pc², **not** a stellar mass density. At the generous added-mass endpoint the change closes **5.8%** of the reference MTS deficit and **6.6%** of the NFW deficit. With compensated transfer it closes **0.86%** and **1.00%**. Every one of the fourteen predictions remains below its corresponding measurement minus one printed width in both models at every tested amplitude. These populations share observational/modeling uncertainties; this is not a fourteen-independent-sigma rejection.

The wave was solved again at six nonzero amplitudes per family, including both signs and interior points, retaining the same outer speed. These are finite stationary solutions, not a tangent extrapolated to a new mass. The NFW counterpart is affine in amplitude here, so endpoint bounds cover the whole interval. The sampled ground-state means increase smoothly across the test points; they do not prove a theorem excluding a bifurcation or an untested occupation. Direct force was the dominant change. The earlier grid comparison missed the induced-wave tolerance in one family; the finer basis brought the maximum scaled induced-force change down to 5.15×10⁻⁶ against the unchanged 2×10⁻⁵ threshold. The failed comparison was retained, not averaged away.

## A light-propagation sign test using the same mass

For the previous 3,760 EROS pilot IDs, the widest positive added case increases ideal optical depth by **[4.49×10⁻¹⁰, 6.88×10⁻⁸]** on the saved simultaneous parallax contract. For the widest positive transfer case the interval runs **from −1.21×10⁻⁹ to +8.51×10⁻⁹**. The sample mean does *not* have a fixed sign for redistribution. The extrema use one allowed distance per star for both rings, not two distances selected independently. Their stationary points follow from the signed line-of-sight moment in DERIVATION.md; separate order-8/order-14 and independent positive-kernel integrations test the calculation.

Two individual stars give a sharper, opposite result. At cg6104l11414 and cg6104l31652, adding inner mass alone gives exactly zero change. Removing the same mass from the adjacent donor makes the predicted ideal depth **decrease throughout both allowed source-distance intervals**. In the widest positive transfer case their intervals are respectively [−4.41,−1.08]×10⁻¹⁰ and [−5.44,−1.08]×10⁻¹⁰. They are in front of the inner ring but behind some of the donor. These are conditional predictions, not observed events. The result uses the earlier Gaia ID, error and crowding assumptions; it is shared by both smooth halo models.

## Which part of the force remains missing?

At cylindrical R=6 kpc and |z|=1.5 kpc, project the acceleration perpendicular to the Galactocentric radius, toward the plane. A centered spherical force has **zero projection for every radial mass law**. The [Gaia acceleration bands](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.13358v3), intersecting the north and reflected south bands under the retained symmetry, start at 0.57340 in units of 10⁻¹⁰ m/s². The ordinary stellar-plus-common foreground is affine in the allowed stellar amplitude. Over the **entire** generous added-mass interval, at least **0.12945** extra angular acceleration is still needed; with compensated transfer, at least **0.15695** is needed. No spherical halo adjustment can supply it. This is conditional on these two ordinary-source families, their axial symmetry and the tracer reconstruction. At R=8.3 kpc, zero *extra* angular acceleration is allowed throughout both intervals: the same test is capable of accepting a spherical contribution.

The finite ground wave supplies **0.008732** at the largest positive added endpoint: the minimum requirement is **14.8 times** that response. Direct integration of the saved wave density gives 0.007924 from its quadrupole and 0.000808 from higher even multipoles; the monopole gives exactly zero. Roughly **80%** of the wave's angular acceleration there comes from material *outside* the 6.185 kpc spherical radius of the measurement, not just the inner stars' neighborhood. A redistribution with the same wave normalization or the same enclosed mass need not give the same angular force. At the compensated positive endpoint the wave supplies 0.008478. These are computed ground states, not caps on all populations. All thirteen packets are retained; reconstructing their full angular force from interior and exterior density moments agreed with the separate force readout to better than 8×10⁻¹⁴ in the stated units.

This gives the parent theory a quantitative target: e.g. an effective pure quadrupole potential of at least **3,500 (km/s)²** at the inner site in the generous added family. The bound is on the sum of angular force components; 3,500 is not an identification of a pure physical quadrupole. The immediate derivation to try is the even-mode response to the independently sourced Galactic bar, preserving the measured radial component and the solar-radius control. Simply increasing spherical mass does not address this observable.

## Boundaries of the result

There is no new fitted galaxy parameter or independent blind sample here. The amplitudes are conditional on the calibrated star table, its center-knot convention, axisymmetry, vertical profiles and the specified donor. Other radii could supply mass, formation could populate a different wave state, and the tracer accelerations themselves are inferred. These results identify which physical explanations are still worth testing; they do not rank full theories from one Galaxy.
