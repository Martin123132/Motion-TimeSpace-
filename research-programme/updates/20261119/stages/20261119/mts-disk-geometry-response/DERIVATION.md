# Fixed disk mass, calibrated occupation and a response to arbitrary ordinary sources

**Geometry.** Write ζ=√(z²+b²), c=a+ζ, d²=R²+c². The ordinary disk is Φ_d=−GM/d, GM fixed numerically rather than renormalized at the Sun, b=0.28 kpc. Then ∂aΦ_d=GM c/d³. Its gradient drives the direct accelerations, while the potential itself drives the Schrödinger stationary equation. The original compact object, bulge, particle pole and Newton coupling are unchanged. For a change in a the potential *and* its dilation/virial term inside the operator are updated before solving. The old numerical baryon-normalization derivative would not be the shape derivative.

**Projection independent of the kinematics.** Poisson gives the positive density
`ρ/M = b²/(4πζ³) [a/d³ + 3ζc²/d⁵]`.
With t=z/ζ for z≥0, dt=b²dz/ζ³. The projected mass fraction inside cylinder R and the surface density per unit mass are
`F(R)=∫₀¹ [1−a/d−ζc²/d³] dt`, `Σ/M=(1/2π)∫₀¹ [a/d³+3ζc²/d⁵]dt`. Independent direct integrations of the 3D density recover the mapped half mass. We do not equate any exponential scale with a. For the IR proxy the exponential half-light radius is 1.67835 times its scale; for the total stellar half-mass proxy solve `(1−B/T)F+(B/T)F_b=1/2` with the retained normalized spherical bulge projected into the same *cylinder*. B/T in this equation infers a normalized stellar shape, not a changed dynamical bulge mass.

**Shape obstruction.** At fixed ζ, either summand in the positive surface integral has logarithmic downward slope p R²/(R²+c²) with p=3 or 5. Hence for any a≥0, b>0, R₂>R₁>0:
`Σ(R₂)/Σ(R₁) ≥ [(R₁²+(a+b)²)/(R₂²+(a+b)²)]^(5/2) > (R₁/R₂)^5`.
Integration and positive mixtures preserve the universal floor. It applies to *this representation*, not to galaxy matter generally. From 8 to 14 kpc the universal floor is 0.06093, above the 0.05743 ratio of an exact 2.1-kpc exponential. The much larger discrepancy for the size-mapped profiles is more informative than this modest universal difference. The observed living-star slope is approximate, not a theorem about the total gas+stellar profile.

**Response.** Let c now denote the finite wave coefficient vector (not the geometric c above), u=ln M, and let T be the inverse stationary Hessian on variations preserving norm. The linearized Poisson field sourced by 2 f δf is part of the Hessian. For an added potential δΦ let bΦ be its wave-multiplied projection, divided by the square of the inherited velocity unit. The fixed-occupation response is δc=−T bΦ. Let ℓᵢ be the gradient with respect to c of a selected readout, hᵢ its *direct* ordinary-force change, and t=∂c/∂u the constrained mass tangent. The calibration readout (*) is the squared circular speed at 17.58 kpc. Then
`δu=−(h_*+ℓ_*δc)/(ℓ_* t)`,
`δOᵢ=hᵢ+ℓᵢδc+(ℓᵢ t)δu = hᵢ−γᵢh_*−⟨T(ℓᵢ−γᵢℓ_*), bΦ⟩`,
with γᵢ=(ℓᵢ t)/(ℓ_* t). Self-adjointness gives the last equality. A single stored row thus measures the linear response to any weak, axisymmetric, midplane-even, resolved input on this background. In particular a spatial constant shifts the chemical potential but induces no acceleration or density response. This does not assume a creation or destruction law for particles: fixed occupation and the observational recalibration are distinct operations. The NFW comparator has only its direct and one-amplitude terms.

**Finite volume and physical units.** At z>0, Kz is negative for downward acceleration; g_in=∂RΦ is inward positive. The annulus between 7 and 9 kpc and |z|≤1.5 kpc is measured by integrating the force on both cylindrical walls and both faces, not by spherical enclosed mass. The wave density and its first variation were also integrated independently over the volume. The local circular slope is the centered spatial derivative in km/s/kpc at 8.3 kpc. The linear shear readout differentiates V² then divides by 2V at each neighboring radius. The two algebraic decompositions in center-jacobian.csv (fixed-M then recalibration) and source-susceptibility.csv (eliminate calibration first) differ term by term but have the same total.

**Sharp environment test.** Transfer a nominal billion solar masses from one thin spherical shell to another: δΦ=−G·10⁹[1/max(r,r_in)−1/max(r,r_out)]. Its direct field vanishes inside the inner shell. The wave tail outside it can still respond. For precision, subtract the constant interior value and insert a radial integration boundary at each shell. The external spherical matrix in each wave angular block is ∫r²dr B_j B_k δΦ/v_unit². In the 9-kpc example the annulus intersects the shell: the exact ordinary mass fraction is 1.5/9, not a smooth-grid estimate. Shell responses from the split integration supersede the raw grid for these discontinuous forces. Both the forward response and the adjoint reproduce the corrected values. Finite-strength controls re-solve the nonlinear wave and Poisson equations with the shell operator and the original outer-speed constraint.
