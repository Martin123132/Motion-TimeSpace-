# Does the near-Sun slope come from the wave or the disk?

**It is not a fixed sign of the field theory.** On the same positive stationary branch, at fixed ordinary disk mass and the same measured outer speed, the MTS circular-speed slope at 8.3 kpc crosses zero at effective MN radius **a=2.3232 kpc**. The matched fixed-scale NFW halo crosses at **3.7679 kpc**. These are diagnostic thresholds, not measurements of the disk. The second physical discretization gives a ~0.00033-kpc shift in the wave crossing by local linearization. Continuation is local to this branch.

Both models recalibrate one amplitude at the single 17.58-kpc speed; no additional rotation measurement sets a. The observed curves remain exposed. The IR size mapping gives MTS slopes −0.13 to +0.49, crossing zero. The total stellar mass mapping gives +2.31 to +4.11 on the corrected endpoint choices. These are different observables and component assumptions, not contradictory direct measurements of a or bounds on the possible gas+star disk. No variant is promoted based on the score. At the two central proxies the calibrated wave masses are 1.666 and 1.748 × 10¹¹ solar masses; the vertical accelerations MTS/NFW are −2031/−2030 and −2148/−2176 (km/s)²/kpc.

**Why a moves the result:** at a=3, the wave slope changes by +2.345 km/s/kpc per kpc increase in a. Contributions are +2.177 from moving the ordinary disk, −0.170 from wave rearrangement at fixed particle number and +0.338 from recalibrating the occupation. NFW gives +2.093. The slope, vertical force and annular-mass derivatives in both models agree with full symmetric re-solves to better than 0.94 ppm at the smaller step. This is a strong foreground dependence; it neither rules out the wave nor establishes it by choosing a compact disk.

**There is a physical reason to stop tuning a.** At their matched half-content radii the alternatives leave about 20–23% as much projected disk density at 14 kpc as at 8 kpc. A 2.1-kpc exponential, used by Lian et al. to describe the *outer living-star* profile, leaves 5.74%. A positive MN mixture even has an analytic floor of 6.09% for that ratio. An approximate stellar slope is not an exact constraint on the whole ordinary disk, but matching one radius cannot make this surrogate a measured mass map.

**New coupling calculation:** we obtained a linear response to an arbitrary small, resolved axisymmetric ordinary potential and its force, not just disk scale. A uniform potential induces zero response within numerical precision. Moving a nominal 10⁹ solar masses between spherical shells from 40 to 25 kpc exerts *zero direct force* near the Sun. Through its extended state the wave adds +0.000897 km/s/kpc to the local slope at fixed occupation; enforcing the outer calibration cancels most of this, leaving **+0.0000784 km/s/kpc**. Far too small in this perturbation to repair the slope. NFW here is a prescribed shape, not a dynamically responding halo. These examples do not set a galaxy's environment or its occupation from formation.
- The split shell operator and full nonlinear check at ±0.25 × 10⁹ give the same derivative within 0.061 ppm on the main grid.
- Across the different state discretizations that specific shear derivative differs by 373 ppm; the still smaller 60→40-kpc response differs by 0.51%. Retain the inherited 20-ppm goal: these tiny signals are not all certified to it. Signs/orders agree. A sharp shell needs split integration; raw weights alone need not resolve it.

**An actual source is ready for the next calculation:** pinned 27,881,280-byte Imig et al. APOGEE DR17 stellar-density table, 360 population cells, 186 fitted cells. Decoding the FITS and summing independent mass columns reproduced **5.2703×10¹⁰ solar masses originally formed and 3.7013×10¹⁰ now in stars/remnants**. These are inferred catalogue quantities, not wave parameters; do not use the initial mass as today's stellar gravity or silently add gas/bulge twice. Neither mass was fitted or inserted in this stage. No other chat, public repo or timing measurement was touched.
