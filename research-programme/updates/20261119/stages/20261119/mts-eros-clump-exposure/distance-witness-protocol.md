# Discriminating two explanations without fitting any lensing data

A common-marginal control and a fixed-intrinsic-brightness example may give very different responses to the source cut. Test whether the measured sightlines, membership, and independent clump modulus moments alone even identify the **sign** of the change. Use exactly the union of reference-photometry stars in the printed/drawn cuts with a unique positive-variance OGLE rectangle. Compute no lensing residual and do not optimize any measured or predicted depth.

For each cell, use the same multiset of distance moduli for two assignments. With even n use half of each mean ± rms; with odd n preassign the lexicographically smallest ID to the mean and put half the remainder at each mean ± rms sqrt[n/(n−1)]. These are finite exact mean and population-variance witnesses, not proposed bimodal stellar populations. Give the far distances to printed-only before overlap before drawn-only, with a fixed ID tie break. Reverse the order for the second realization. Use these identical two assignments in both stellar geometries and both smooth-halo models. A strict sign reversal in confirmed converged optical depths proves non-identification under these input constraints; no distributional extremum or empirical distances are implied.
