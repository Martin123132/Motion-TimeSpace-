# From the measured angular footprint to a test of source-distance assignment

The previous sealed action/optics result fixes the weak-field point-source kernel, including its source normalization; no Galactic coupling is changed here. For an individual source at distance S on ray n define T_n(S) = (4πG/c²) ∫ ρ_compact(n,D) D (1−D/S)_+ dD. This is nonnegative and nondecreasing with S. Both retained smooth halo models use identical ordinary compact foregrounds. Source radius, blending, stellar variability and motion-dependent event efficiency are not in this ideal kernel.

## Positive-density all-direction ceiling

Uncertain exposure weights and distances cannot defeat a pointwise ceiling. Over each integration interval [u,v] and latitude/longitude band, enclose the cylindrical radius using h=D cos(b) and R²=(h−R0 cos l)²+R0² sin² l. Minimize over h at the clipped closest approach and smallest |l|; maximize at endpoints and largest |l|. The height satisfies |z| ≥ max(0,u sin|b|_min−|z_sun|). Bound each stellar surface density over R at both endpoints and every enclosed linear knot (the outer exponential falls to the retained truncation). Bound the measured thin-star height by enclosing the interpolated knot values and the two smooth transition factors; exp(−z/h)/h as a function of h peaks at h=z. Keep thick and thin components positive separately.

For each spherical deprojection shell [a,b], its positive density coefficient multiplies acosh(max(1,b/r))−acosh(max(1,a/r)). This rises up to r=a and falls thereafter; evaluate at a clipped to the enclosed r interval. The zero-slope central shell gives no term. For the triaxial alternative, the K0 argument is at least [(R_min/a_long)^4+(|z|_min/a_vert)^4]^(1/4), independently of the bar orientation. K0 decreases. Integrate the resulting constant density ceilings against the *exact* positive integral of D(1−D/S) on each interval. At 52 kpc all non-bar compact support has ended in these directions; the analytic K0 tail uses K0(x) ≤ exp(−x)sqrt(π/(2x)) and x ≥ (D−|r_sun|)/(2^(1/4) max_axis). This follows from the K0 integral and cosh(t) ≥ 1+t²/2. Floating-point enclosures have a small numerical pad; not interval-arithmetic certification. Nested grids at 0.04 and 0.01 kpc and direct rays test the implementation.

The unconditional angular box is 0.5 ≤ |b| ≤ 9 degrees, |l| ≤15 degrees: the 65 vector field polygons and modern quarter corners lie inside; the off-chart field is checked in the catalog. A conditional refinement uses the five published latitude counts with each band padded by 0.5 degrees; this assumes the bin location associated with each star is within the padding. Put the unallocated 6,756 stars in the full footprint bucket. Each group has at most N_group × 2,314 calendar days, regardless of seasonal cadence. With fixed total star-days, a linear objective is maximized by allocating days to the group with the largest ceiling first, to capacity, then to the next. Use 6.615 billion total days at the low end of the printed rounding interval, maximizing the possible average. No inferred field star counts/observing efficiencies are needed for this conditional inequality. CSV quantities named living_annulus are the contribution to ideal depth of the *existing* 4–7 kpc annular living disk, not a mass estimate.

## Why an unlabelled distance distribution cannot decide this comparison

Let P and F be the source IDs admitted by the printed and figure rules. Then Δ = mean_P T − mean_F T = Σ_i a_i T_i, with a_i = 1_P(i)/N_P − 1_F(i)/N_F. Stars in only one cut have opposite signs of a_i. A measurement of the distribution of distance moduli in a sky cell does not specify its association with a_i. Transferring light from the foreground to a smooth halo is not the issue.

For each of 12 independently matched cells let n be the number of distinct stars in P union F, μ its supplied modulus mean and σ² its supplied variance. For even n use n/2 copies of μ−σ and μ+σ. For odd n keep the first archived ID at μ and use (n−1)/2 copies of μ±σ sqrt[n/(n−1)]. The empirical distribution has exactly mean μ and variance σ² (population convention). Sort the remaining IDs by category drawn-only, overlap, printed-only, breaking ties by ID. Assign the high distances to the upper half; mirror the assignment for the other witness. Nothing in the sorting uses observed events, a fitted model, or even the lens distribution. Calculate at the actual ray for every star, not one ray per cell. This is a constructive failure of point identification under the stated inputs, **not** an assertion about true bimodality, nor an optimization over all possible skies.

In the simpler conditional controls the per-star fixed-intrinsic relation is μ_i=μ_center+ΔR_i−1.9Δ(B−R)_i (distance in kpc = 10^[(μ_i−10)/5]). This is distinct from using each star's uniquely matched independent Gaussian clump-distance distribution. The magnitude and sign of selection sensitivity differ because one prescription ties inferred distance to brightness and the other doesn't. The exact-moment witnesses use neither prescription. All kernels use the same original mass distribution and numerical constants.
