# What can be said about occupation without choosing it?

**Theorem (isolated Newtonian ground-state family only).** Fix the quadratic pole mass m_h. Write the normalized nodeless stationary Schrödinger–Poisson shape u(s)>0, potential Φ(s), energy E<0, Φ(∞)=0. The equations are (s²u′)′=2s²(Φ−E)u and (s²Φ′)′=s²u². Every radial rescaling of this shape is another solution. In physical units let L be that free length (not the Compton wavelength) and s=r/L. Then
```
ρ(r;L) = ħ² u(s)²/(4πG m_h² L⁴)
M(<R;L) = ħ² H(R/L)/(G m_h² L),    H(s)=s²Φ′=∫₀ˢ u(t)²t²dt .
```
These follow by substitution; no observational normalization occurs in them. At fixed R, d ln M/d ln(1/L)=1+s H′/H>0, so an enclosed mass fixes exactly one L. At fixed nonzero r, density is proportional to [s²u(s)]²/r⁴, **not** monotone with occupation.

To count the branches let Y=s²u and B=2(Φ−E−1/s²). Then (Y′/s²)′=BY/s². B increases strictly since Φ′>0, and ranges from −∞ to −2E>0. Y′/s² begins positive like 2u(0)/s and tends to zero from below on the exponentially decaying tail. It decreases up to the unique zero of B and increases thereafter. Hence Y has exactly one maximum. A positive density below that maximum permits two scales; at the maximum one, above it none. This proves the envelope, not just its appearance on a grid. It applies to this homologous family, not to excited/mixed modes or a self-consistent disk potential.

Numerically, at r=8.27504 kpc and at the retained pole the density ceiling is **2.38208 GeV/cm³**, reached at s=2.52486, L=3.27742 kpc. Changing numerical domain and mesh changes the maximizing s or density by at most 5.3e−11 fractionally. The ceiling scales as m_h⁻²r⁻⁴. For example, if this state must supply all of 0.36 GeV/cm³ at this location, a necessary pole-energy ceiling is 2.466e−24 eV. This is conditional, not a general particle-mass limit.

**A sharper bound from a measured speed.** [Feng et al., Tables 1–2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.21780v1) give 224.03±1.91 km/s at 17.58 kpc from their Cepheid sample. If the halo is spherical, the reported speed traces circular balance, and the net radial acceleration from the other components there is inward or zero, then GM_h(<R)/R ≤ v_c². This uses no fitted NFW density or detailed baryon subtraction. At the central speed M_h≤2.05143e11 solar masses. The monotone mass law gives L≥6.8984 kpc; this is on the diffuse side of the density maximum, so **ρ_solar≤0.6842 GeV/cm³** at this pole. Raising the speed by three published bootstrap errors gives 0.7350, a sensitivity test rather than a confidence limit. Disk forces are signed; one must not assert the sign for every possible disk. The full signed force required without this sign assumption is saved.

Inverting the four tabulated *NFW-inferred*, baryon-dependent local density values (0.33–0.40) gives diffuse masses 1.196–1.386e11 solar masses or concentrated masses 1.152–1.189e12 inside 17.58 kpc. The latter give halo-only speeds 531–539 km/s. Reconciling them with 224 km/s would need v_other²≈−(2.32–2.41)e5 (km/s)², a large **outward** contribution. The sign conclusion persists across the three density levels in each row and the high-speed sensitivity test. The diffuse solutions leave room for ordinary inward gravity. They are not fits to the rotation curve.

**Fair normalization control.** Conversely anchor the *one* free length to Table 2's spherical halo masses inferred by baryonic subtraction from the measured outer bin, not its NFW masses at 18 kpc or its different averaged-RC mass row. For Pouliasis/McMillan/Ou/Mean respectively we obtain local densities 0.342/0.386/0.429/0.386. The singular isothermal comparison, also given just the same outer mass, gives 0.309/0.339/0.366/0.339. Both are close to the conditional tabulated local estimates. All variants use overlapping data, and “Mean” is not a fourth independent measurement. The quoted error-unit differences omit correlated systematics and are not significances. The original *frozen* 0.1 at 40 pc yields 0.084 at the Sun and 3.86e10 solar masses inside 17.58 kpc: it misses the empirical normalization. Retain that miss. Neither the mass anchor nor the two-root calculation is an independently derived population or a held-out test.
