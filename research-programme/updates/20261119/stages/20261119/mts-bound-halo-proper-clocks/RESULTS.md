# Which signals survive preparing the halo and matter together?

**Main result.** Changing from the earlier transient scalar profile and baryon-only orbital preparation to a bound profile with its own mean gravitational support removes almost the entire one-cycle drift in this experiment. At 20.01 pc, turning and kinetic launches reach radial coordinate speeds of 3.1701e−5 m/s, yet the increments between matching pressure crossings 68.35731 proper years apart are −3.3500e−15 and +3.3553e−15 m/s. The old transient gave approximately −6.7e−5 m/s for both phases. The oscillation is real in the calculation; the old common inward drift is not a universal prediction. Both the profile and the orbit preparation changed: this comparison does not isolate which change accounts for how much. Relative to the tiny net increments themselves the leading-order forecast misses its tolerance in 12/12 cases. Do not claim exact cancellation, stability over many cycles or a measured secular coefficient.

**Clock result.** For clocks held at the same areal radii the turning-phase supported real-minus-mean launch-referenced echo delay peaks are −15.81, −71.50 and −105.77 microseconds (inner/middle/outer). Subtract the corresponding isolated-wave oscillations, and the *mixed* peaks are −261.13, −359.27 and +371.71 femtoseconds. The kinetic-phase signs reverse. A signed peak of a difference need not occur at the signed peak of either input. All 72 frozen versus finite timing budgets (frequency and delay; isolated, supported, mixed) pass at 2e−5. The 36 receiver-calendar and 24 independent arrival-time derivative checks pass. These are the held-clock signals, not free Galactic clocks.

**Checks that distinguish physics from sampling.** Phase-averaged metric and field were independently solved in two domains with/without ordinary matter. The stationary mass- and wave-integral errors are below 1.0e−10 and the domain differences below 6.6e−11. Every measured velocity increment is resolved within the stated *oscillation-scale* budget. Four of 24 original motion/mass comparisons missed on relative mass at the innermost readout; the flux balances at those points still pass. On both resolutions and phases, the exact-history replays match every original linearly sampled mass bit for bit and then use fourth-order interpolation at the physical radius. All 24 revised spatial comparisons pass, with the earlier four misses preserved. The rest-count integral and angular-advection checks are in the raw histories. No change to grids, bank, particle mass, ordinary count or acceptance threshold.

**A result with observational leverage.** The isolated ground state permits two occupations at one solar density, but they do not predict the same outer gravity. Given the range of local densities inferred by [Feng et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.21780v1), the concentrated solutions give 531–539 km/s from the halo alone at 17.58 kpc, against the measured total 224.03±1.91 km/s. The diffuse solutions give 171–184 km/s and leave room for baryons. This separates alternatives without fitting the pole, if the remaining outer force is not outward. The original illustrative halo is too light; when one normalization is supplied externally the ground state and a same-input isothermal benchmark both give plausible local densities. **Different observable: free clocks on the J1713+0747 line of sight.** In the frozen illustrative Galactic assignment, the free timing calculation and the independent curvature integral agree to <1e−9 of the Earth amplitude. For the nominal 1.15 kpc timing and 1.05 kpc VLBI distances the raw free amplitudes are 4.10 and 3.17 microseconds; artificially holding these same endpoints still would instead give 7.45 and 5.85 milliseconds. For the timing distance, after projecting the saved calendar against timing nuisance terms, unknown phase gives 12.5–76.1 ns RMS at the nominal distance, or about 0.54–192 ns over its *sampled* distance interval. These are model and sampling projections, not detected signals, likelihoods or confidence limits. The two distances are literature examples, not an exhaustive modern distance analysis. No residuals or noise fit. The Earth density is only 0.084 GeV/cm³ here; the conditional mass-calibrated comparison was **not** substituted into the clocks. Distance sources: [Zhu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.00662) and [Chatterjee et al.](https://doi.org/10.1088/0004-637X/698/1/250). Clock and halo empirical appendices did not modify the frozen cloud experiment.

**Prospective next finite test.** At the intermediate scalar phase θ=π/4 the mean density/pressure match at zeroth launch order but scalar radial flux does not vanish. At 20.01 pc, a radial-rest launch predicts −68.386 km net displacement in one pressure cycle. A phase-matched radial launch instead needs +3.17012e−5 m/s and predicts zero displacement to first stress order. Both predictions were checked against a separate driven linear ODE and stored before any finite quadrature run; zero means “test on the oscillation scale,” not a certified exact zero. This is an immediate way to test whether apparent memory comes from state preparation.
