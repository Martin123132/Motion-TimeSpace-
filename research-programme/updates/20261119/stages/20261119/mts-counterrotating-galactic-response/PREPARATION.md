# Can ordinary gravity actually populate these modes?

At fixed total number stationarity of the two normalized field equations gives \(dE/(N\,df)=\epsilon_1-\epsilon_0\) along a self-consistent equilibrium branch (energies in physical units after multiplication by the saved squared speed unit). At fixed outer speed there is also \([(1-f)\epsilon_0+f\epsilon_1]\,dN/df\). The energy test independently differentiates the kinetic, external and *half-weighted* self-gravitational energy on the solved fields. A positive fixed-number cost distinguishes an energetically excited distribution from spontaneous selection of the minimum-energy state. It does not forbid a formation event supplying energy.

**An exact constraint.** If both the initial density/state and forcing are axisymmetric, the common Hamiltonian commutes with azimuthal rotations. A pure \(m=0\) state stays there. Even with a time-dependent twofold bar, half-turn symmetry preserves even \(m\) for an initially even pure wave: no repeated even azimuthal harmonics create \(|m|=1\). This follows as well for self-consistent Poisson evolution as long as the initial state, boundary and external source respect the symmetry and the deterministic evolution is unique. This does not exclude primordial odd occupation, finite asymmetries, noise seeds or other matter. Zero *net* spin alone is a much weaker condition.

**What survives free fall?** For a point source outside the body, a Taylor expansion in a frame freely falling with its origin removes the constant and uniform-acceleration terms. For an equatorial source along azimuth zero put \(s=\sin\theta\). The positive-\(m=1\) Fourier coefficient of \(P_\ell(s\cos\phi)\) is \(s/2,\ 0,\ 15s^3/16-3s/4\) for degrees 1, 2, 3. A tilted source has an \(m=1\) quadrupole, but that part is odd under \(z\mapsto-z\), so still cannot drive the two *mirror-even* states in this axisymmetric background at first order. Equivalently a quadratic tide has even inversion parity, while these two states have opposite inversion parity. The leading allowed distant tide here is an octupole. Even that has zero ground-to-pure-p-wave angular element on an exactly spherical background; actual disk-induced harmonic mixing is required. These statements do **not** remove a genuine nonuniform internal \(m=1\) mass asymmetry. Outside-source expansion is not valid for a satellite passing through the distribution.

The stored signed overlaps are for \(I_\ell=\langle v_+ | (r/L)^\ell P_\ell(s\cos\phi)|u\rangle/N\), with each of the two sector eigenvectors norm \(\sqrt N\). A distant equatorial body at distance \(D\) has a frozen-potential off-diagonal element \(-GM_{\rm p}L^\ell I_\ell/D^{\ell+1}\), in squared-speed units. The sign of an off-diagonal element depends on phase convention; its magnitude controls transitions. Independent uniform-azimuth integration checks the analytical angular coefficients. Report both signed and absolute radial/angular integrals and where their absolute weight lies. Do not take a halo orbit from a different branch to supply \(M_{\rm p},D(t)\).

A freely falling origin is a local convention, not necessarily the extended body's centre-of-mass trajectory in a nonuniform field. Translation of the whole foreground and wave must not be counted as internal excitation. These matrix elements and the bare one-particle phase period are not the frequency of a measured collective mode. A coherent perturbation also sources a first-order gravitational potential through its interference with the ground state; a power/occupation forecast needs that response and a preparation/dephasing model. In particular a rotating perturber need not populate the two opposite signs equally. The diagonal-mixed stress compared to Gaia is a test family, not a result of the static calculation.
