# What a finite rotating bar leaves behind

**A finite episode excites a localized collective mode of the self-gravitating MTS wave, even though the steady bar's leading frequency is above escape. We derived how much of that mode remains, predicted a different duration before evolving it, and verified the prediction. It is too small to explain the measured inner angular force on this prepared background.**

### Bound response: a prediction, not a fitted mode

Using the previous 294-file stage, evolve the perturbation and its gravitational potential together. The imposed bar builds and fades as sin⁴ over one stellar-pattern turn, 184.55 Myr at the [Clarke–Gerhard central speed](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.10875); follow the cloud another 184.55 Myr after switch-off. The duration is a response experiment, **not** a measured bar lifetime. The axisymmetric stellar mass is present throughout.

- The lowest live m=2 frequency is **1.60734** in parent units, safely below the open-channel threshold **2.31997**. The upper sideband specific energy is −17,005 (km/s)². Only 2.95×10⁻⁷ of the computed two-component squared shape lies outside 12 length units. Moving the boundary from 30 to 40 units presets no new occupation and reproduces its frequency and predicted action. Its period is **320.35 Myr**; a rigid twofold driver would resonate at **9.589 km/s/kpc**, not the measured 33.29. A finite pulse nevertheless has power away from its central frequency.
- A direct projection of the coupled equations gives the pulse-transform law in DERIVATION.md. For one turn it predicts retained dimensionless action equal to **6.51×10⁻¹⁰ of the saved ground number** (140.585 after multiplying by its mass unit). Independent full-field evolution recovers the complex co- and counter-rotating amplitudes at switch-off and one turn later. The final 512-step control agrees within 1.70×10⁻⁶ relative even on the smaller individual amplitude.
- **Independent duration challenge:** before running a two-turn pulse, predicted action × mass unit **0.150881657**; direct 1,024-step full first-order evolution at switch-off **0.150881699** (relative difference 2.76×10⁻⁷). Twice the duration at the same peak amplitude leaves about **932 times less** in this particular closed mode. The stored prediction hash is in the challenge receipt. This is an out-of-calculation test on the model, not an independent astrophysical observation. The collective action is not particle mass.
- For comparison, project onto the *one-body* localized orbital: about **125 solar masses** at switch-off and **144** one turn later. Switching off gravitational feedback would instead leave **78**. There is no inconsistency: a one-body orbital is not a normal mode of the coupled field. The other ≈18,800 solar masses of sideband norm are not all declared escaped; weakly bound, extended and open components are not separated by this one projection.

### What changes in the sky

The total m=2 sideband mass is about **18,981 solar masses** at the last reference epoch, balanced by the negative overlap of the original cloud with its second-order deformation. Integrating the signed mean density gives ≈46,663 solar masses each of local excess and deficit: this is redistribution, not net mass creation. Simply adding the positive intensity to a static halo gives the wrong answer.

At 6 kpc and |z|=1.5 kpc in the most favorable previously allowed stellar-addition case, the m=2,4 mean polar change is **+3.08×10⁻⁹** at switch-off and **−3.49×10⁻⁹** a turn later (all accelerations in units of 10⁻¹⁰ m/s²). At that later epoch the remaining coherent first-order wave contributes **+7.26×10⁻⁷** in the chosen direction. The resulting subtotal is **0.456380** versus a lower data edge of **0.573396**. The same baryons with a static spherical halo give **0.447524**. The tested source does not repair the inner residual; both models stay within the solar-radius polar band and outside its separate radial band. The local acceleration cuts are the inherited [Gaia DR3 comparison](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13358), not direct measurements of an azimuthal mean. After switch-off the direct rotating stellar harmonic is absent, so this subtotal is not the earlier *present-bar* prediction. It includes the linear wake and the second-order **axisymmetric** correction, not all nonlinear angular harmonics.

### A stronger limit for slower preparation

First derive a conservative action ceiling from the energy-weighted forcing. Then prove the sharper implication in CLOSED-MASS-BOUND.md: if the restoring operator with live gravity satisfies M≥ηH, the actual coupled *subthreshold sideband mass* after switch-off is at most 1/η times the uniform Fourier-strength bound. The discrete η=1/2 test passes without tuning for each of the 16 retained bar harmonics and both backgrounds on two radial meshes. For the reference and central speed the sum over harmonics is bounded by **4.63 solar masses after five turns**, **0.0144 after ten**. The one-turn ceiling is weak; the measured wave evolution, not the ceiling, gives the short-pulse result. For fixed detuning and this smooth envelope the ceiling decreases as T⁻⁸ at large T. Neither this bound nor the numerical positivity check is a continuum mass-gap proof or a bound on arbitrary assembly histories.
