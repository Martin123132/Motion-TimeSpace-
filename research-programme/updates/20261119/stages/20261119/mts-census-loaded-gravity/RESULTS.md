# A solar census repairs one force component, not the whole Galactic field

We let the retained heavy wave settle again with the counted stellar mass **inside** its gravitational potential. The independent measurement is local: 33.4 ± 3 solar masses/pc² in stars and remnants ([McKee et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.05334)). Extending that normalization across the whole Milky Way is a physical assumption under test. Both the wave and spherical comparison received the same stars, atomic and molecular gas, compact component, bulge and one outer rotation-speed calibration. No fit to the Gaia force map, no changed pole or coupling.

The quasar result is a distinct accelerometry test that does not infer a force from equilibrium of orbiting stars. [Gaia EDR3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.02036) measured 5.05 ± 0.35 µas/year. Catalogue MTS/NFW are +0.71/+0.79 quoted widths; column MTS/NFW are −1.85/−1.22. These use the same matched states with no dipole calibration. Do not combine this with the DR3 force map by assuming independent errors. A smooth Galactic model omits external and local structure.

## A constraint that does not depend on a chosen NFW scale

Hold the nominal catalogue stellar source fixed and allow **any** positive spherical dark density. Using the overlap of the north and south radial bands, the greatest allowed enclosed dark mass at spherical radius 6.185 kpc is 8.85 billion solar masses, while the least required at 8.434 kpc is 35.24 billion. The **mean density of the intervening shell would have to be at least 1.94 times the mean density inside the first radius**. A nonincreasing dark density cannot do that. At the low and high ends of the independent census sensitivity it is at least 1.29 and 4.16 times, respectively. This is an analytic mass-ratio constraint, not the result of an unsuccessful optimizer. Every spherical NFW scale is covered by the nonincreasing-density result. A positive spherical profile without monotonicity can satisfy the nominal catalogue bands and the outer calibration; that is an existence witness, not a proposed shell of dark matter.

We also tested a possible bookkeeping repair rather than just noting it. Removing the **entire** inherited 4.50-billion-solar-mass spherical bulge would not suffice: at nominal counts even a compact excess would need to be reduced by at least **8.33 billion**. At the lowest catalogue normalization that satisfies the polar bands within the census range, the corresponding minimum is **5.49 billion**. For the column geometry the polar bands themselves have no common spherical solution in the quoted count range: spherical bulge removal cannot change that component. These are conditional restrictions on the **source plus halo**, not a claim that the real Galaxy has a hollow halo, or that the nonspherical MTS wave satisfies a spherical bound. Non-spherical redistribution, source systematics, or deviations from the tracer assumptions are live possibilities.

## What was derived inside MTS

Metric variation of the existing minimally coupled real heavy field selects its spatial stress (DERIVATION.md). It has the same force divergence as the usual wave momentum flux, but differs locally by an explicit divergence-free tensor. No pressure law was fitted. The integrated trace equals twice the kinetic energy and is only **0.52–0.58 parts per million of the rest energy** for the two matched states. The integrated vertical and transverse tensor virials and the independent spectral gradient check are consistent at the unchanged 20-ppm target. The small number is **not** an estimate of the total gravitational effect of the wave: its rest density already gravitates at Newtonian order.

The force through the midplane from the direct metric stress balances the wave's integrated gravitational weight plus measured box-wall traction to **27.7 / 14.7 ppm** (catalogue / column, matched main box); the former misses the 20-ppm target. With the same physics on the second fine box this becomes **4.8 / 2.9 ppm**. Integrating the same field more finely without changing its representation barely moves the answer. A more derivative-sensitive plane identity differs by **0.90% / 0.64%** in the main box and **0.36% / 0.28%** in the other box. The exact split in the plane-defect CSVs assigns most of this to the residual in the retained angular modes; high modes sourced by gas and stars account for much of the remainder. The self-potential high-mode contribution here is negligible. Do not promote the derivative form to a locally converged pressure prediction merely because the Galerkin residual or global virial is small.

At fixed inherited occupation the catalogue outer speed moves from 224.03 to **242.25 km/s**; changing only the stellar readout gives **235.11**. The corresponding column values are **234.93** and **230.31**. Re-matching reduces the wave masses to 1.583/1.806 × 10¹¹ solar masses; these are calibrated norms, not a determination of state preparation. The force and matched occupation derivatives were tested with two central step sizes around each saved background before interpreting the finite change. The principal next empirical target is the **radial allocation of the counted stars**, not another scalar normalization, fitted pressure or arbitrary NFW scale. See NEXT.md and REPLAY.md.
