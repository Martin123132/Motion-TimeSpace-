# How much does the finite cloud shape matter?

The finite-strength wave/translated-cloud integrations use the original ordinary density, not a free size or fitted restoring spring. This note asks what that center approximation can miss. The following are three distinct levels of evidence.

1. **Conditional bound.** For any centered mass distribution with covariance Σ and support in a ball, Taylor's formula cancels the mean linear-gradient term. On that ball, |〈g〉−g(X)| ≤ sup‖D²g‖ tr(Σ)/2. Along the archived point trajectory compare a forced displacement to the local harmonic oscillator with h>0: for E=(|δv|²+h|δX|²)¹ᐟ² one obtains E′ ≤ ‖Dg+hI‖E/(2√h) + sup‖D²g‖ tr(Σ)/2. Integrate with a tube bootstrap. The bound uses the saved time interpolant of the wave and the analytic satellite, not a theorem that the cloud stays inside any particular radius. If it stays inside its original radius, even placing all its mass at that radius bounds the final *position* error from replacing it by a point in this field by 1.76 pc / 0.236 pc. If the original RMS radius is maintained these bounds tighten to 0.311 / 0.0417 pc. See `cloud-tube-angular16.csv` for the doubled-radius case and speeds. This does not bound a different wave solution sourced by the distorted material.

2. **Causal leading material response to the finite history.** Linearize only the internal displacements of the initially isotropic circular streams. Force them using the changing full tidal Hessian at the actual moving center, trace and traceless parts separately. With orbital frequency Ω, epicyclic frequency κ and quadrupolar potential V, solve the free radial and tangential epicycles in Laplace space at s and s±2iΩ. Project back to the radial density displacement A and the angular transport S. For a trial radial test potential F the density integral is ∫r²ρ(AF′+3SF/r)dr: the weak form retains transport without differentiating a discretized density. `continuum_cloud.py:gains` gives the four analytic transfer coefficients from V′ and V/r to A,S; `response_controls.py` checks them against the original 8-component orbital equations at independent complex frequencies. Poisson feeds the induced quadrupole back into these same orbits. In the monopole the Lagrangian enclosed ordinary mass is fixed; use the corresponding enclosed-shell cancellation. With zero initial displacement, solve the Volterra equation causally from free-orbit kernels, not by deleting poles or assuming final equilibrium. A manufactured growing mode is recovered as well as a decaying one. Evaluate the retarded center correction from −(∂ᵢ∂ⱼ∂ₖΦ)δΣⱼₖ/2 in the linearized center equation. This term is tiny on both histories. It is an estimate of one contribution, not an accuracy certificate at the nanoparsec scale.

3. **What happened to the apparent instability?** Replacing a continuous frequency distribution by a few gravitating rings gave a fast mode; retaining those packets rather than silently clipping the growth matters. Roots from the finite orbit matrix also annihilate its frequency-space determinant. Re-evaluate that *same complex frequency* with increasingly many integration nodes: the singularity does not persist. For example the diffuse 96-ring root has relative least singular value about 10⁻¹² at 96 nodes but 0.332 at 512 and 1024. This rules out identifying the particular discrete root as a converged physical one; it is not a proof that no continuum instability anywhere exists. The independently replayed trajectories use the retarded self-potential and exact free-orbit propagators at 32 test radii. Peak radial-strain envelopes on the sampled orbits are 0.0572% and 0.0277%. Tiny absolute covariance discrepancies can still be appreciable fractions of the induced shape. See `candidate-poles.csv`, `contour-refinements.csv`, `operator-controls.csv` and the two `shells-*-tail-n32.csv` tables.

Scope: angular-order-16 force history for the shape study; first-order shape on finite forcing, not an all-orders collisionless matter–wave solution or a long-time stability theorem. The computed shape is not used to adjust the wave occupation. No inference about the actual spatial distribution of Galactic stars follows from this compact-cloud test.
