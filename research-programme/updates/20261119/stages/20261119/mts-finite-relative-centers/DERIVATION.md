# A finite-strength, relative Newtonian response

This is the positive quadratic heavy-bath weak-field branch continued from the live dipole stage, not a new force law. The parent supplies a nonrelativistic Schrödinger field with fixed wave number integral, a common Poisson field and Newtonian motion for ordinary matter. Use the same two initialized ground-state occupations and extended satellite trajectory as the parent. The preparations differ; neither is a fit to the relative center. Use the freely falling point-origin frame throughout. The externally prescribed donor is not a live third member of a closed system.

## Two centers from one interaction

Write the translated unit-normalized ordinary density as B(x−X), the wave density as ρ, and the ordinary mass as M. The mutual energy is U=M∫B(x−X)Φ_w(x) d³x. Differentiation gives −∂_X U/M=−∫B∇Φ_w. Variation with respect to ρ gives the potential of the same translated material. Thus translating the ordinary source in the wave equation and moving its center in the mean wave acceleration are reciprocal, not separate fitted forces. In the auxiliary tracer calculation the material does not load or drive the wave. An additional point tracer follows the local point force; it is not the force-bearing source. The occupied wave reacts through its own nonlinear density. Spherical material deformation is examined separately below; do not replace the translated profile by a rigid wave.

For a regular Helmholtz mode u with wave number k, its angular mean at X+rn is j₀(kr)u(X). This follows by spherical averaging the Helmholtz equation with the regular central boundary condition, and commutes with spatial differentiation. Hence the material form factor is F(k)=∫4πr²B(r)j₀(kr)dr. Multiply the regular Poisson modes by F for the center force; the adjoint generates the moving potential from exactly the same coefficients. Evaluating solid harmonics with the even Bessel expansion about X yields the retained local polynomial. The complete wave is solved throughout the volume, not extrapolated there from a small polynomial. The disk-free control cloud has finite support; extrapolation to the Galactic disk is not justified. Independent Green integration, differentiation of the energy, and the opposite-force work test are in `direct-force.csv`.

The wave center is ∫xρ/∫ρ; its velocity differentiates numerator and denominator using the wave equation, not a difference of sampled positions. Our prediction is the ordinary center minus that wave center as a vector. For external strength ε the independent tangent and second variation are the coefficients of ε and ε² (one half the second derivative). In the wave density |ψ₀+εψ₁+ε²ψ₂|² the second coefficient is 2 Re(ψ₀*ψ₂)+|ψ₁|²; it also drives the force. Expand the moving material potential, the ordinary field sample at its displaced center, and the donor acceleration consistently. Signed runs at ±1/32 and ±1/64 separately recover odd and even coefficients; low angular order validates differentiation on that discretization, not its physical second-order coefficient. `second_response.py` propagates these terms rather than fitting a quadratic to the full-strength endpoint.

## Translational balance independent of the centroid readout

Equal and opposite internal forces drop out of dP/dt for wave plus material. In our accelerating coordinates the external differential force on each includes the subtracted origin acceleration. A spherical Dirichlet wave wall also contributes its normal stress −n|∂ᵣψ|²/2 in code units. Integrate F_ext+F_wall, once for momentum and twice for the total first mass moment (initial position and momentum zero). Subtract that moment from (M_w+M)X and divide by M_w to recover the relative position, and likewise for velocity. The force quadrature differentiates donor interaction in donor coordinates and splits the radial cell at the donor radius. No assumption that the material traces the wave, nor substitution of the primary wave centroid, is needed. Reporting both with and without the computational wall tests its numerical significance. This is a Newtonian common-metric consequence on these supplied histories.

## From an unobserved center to a differential field

On an ideal all-sky shell of radius R about the ordinary center, define the line-of-sight dipole vector D(R)=3〈n n·[g(X+Rn)−g_b]〉. The normalization makes a constant observer acceleration subtract exactly once; an exactly uniform field cancels, and a centered linear tidal field contributes no dipole. The central ordinary sphere's own radial acceleration on this shell has no dipole. In the wave potential, 〈n u(X+Rn)〉=j₁(kR)∇u(X)/k for every regular Helmholtz mode (differentiate a plane-wave angular integral, or expand in regular spherical modes). Differentiating in R shows that the observed radial projection filters the center acceleration mode by **3j₁′(kR)**, not the vector-mean factor j₀(kR). Subtract the actual reference force filtered by F. Evaluate the separate, extended donor in real sky directions before adding the signed vectors. Units in the tables are (km/s)²/kpc. This test uses the *full radial dependence* even at 17.58 kpc: the small-displacement expansion applies only to X, not R. A second calculation differences the potential moment on displaced physical skies with no radial-projection filter; both use the same underlying wave field.

The rigid comparison replaces only the distorted wave by the original spherical loaded density translated to the measured wave center. Its projected gradient uses the exact j₁(k|X−X_w|), not a linear expansion in the relative offset. Consequently the rigid-to-live difference at fixed centers isolates the effect of field shape in this observable. The ideal center observer is not the Solar System barycenter.
