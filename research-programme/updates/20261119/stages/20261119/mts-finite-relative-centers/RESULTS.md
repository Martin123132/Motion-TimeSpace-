# Finite relative motion survives; the acceleration field does not move as a rigid halo

On the same five-Gyr extended LMC orbit, the compact ordinary population and the wave remain displaced relative to each other when the source acts at its physical reference strength. The separation is about **20 pc** in the denser Cepheid-reference preparation and **247 pc** in the illustrative diffuse preparation. The ordinary population has the same 33.36-pc radius and 278,809-solar-mass preparation in both. These are two conditional predictions of the retained weak-field branch, not uncertainty bounds, observations of an offset or a choice of which occupation a galaxy forms.

| Final ordinary-minus-wave response | Denser | Diffuse |
|---|---:|---:|
| Previously sealed linear separation (pc) | 22.92 | 276.15 |
| Full-strength separation (pc) | 20.18 | 247.15 |
| Previously sealed linear speed (km/s) | 0.1960 | 2.6201 |
| Full-strength speed (km/s) | 0.1633 | 2.415 |

The nonlinear response reduces the magnitude of the separation by about 12% / 10.5%. On the matched angular-order-16 runs, including the derived quadratic term reduces the position-vector discrepancy from 13.64% / 15.51% to **0.32% / 0.63%**; the corresponding speed discrepancies fall to 0.67% / 1.17%. The small material exerts its own gravitational feedback: switching between matched live and isolated tracer runs changes the position vector by 0.00029 / 0.00375 pc at angular order five. That small effect is distinct from the much larger wave response to the satellite. `finite-outcomes.csv` lists the three signed axes, speeds and comparisons.

**What a tracer could measure.** From the same saved fields we derived an ideal full-sky line-of-sight acceleration dipole, measured relative to the moving ordinary center. For a shell at 8.1 kpc the compact and diffuse predictions are **2.40 and 2.13 (km/s)²/kpc**, respectively, including the direct satellite. The vectors differ in direction: (0.390, 0.215, −2.353) versus (0.059, 1.913, 0.940) in the inherited axes. The wave alone contributes norms of 3.28 and 0.779; the total is a signed vector sum, not a sum of magnitudes. A rigidly shifted spherical wave with the *same* center would give wave-only norms of **8.74 and 3.19**. This is why one cannot translate a halo centroid directly into a timing signal. At 17.58 kpc the total norms are 4.16 / 9.00; the radial dependence and the directions matter. These are predictions for the stated ideal observer, not measurements from Earth. `sky-components.csv` retains signed live, rigid, distortion-only and satellite vectors for all four radii and zero-source controls.

**Independent tests and actual precision.** The original signed small-source limit agrees with the earlier stage to better than 3.4 ppm. The same initially comoving wave and material remain comoving under uniform acceleration; no source leaves their centers at rest. An independent readout of the local gravitational gradient and its reciprocal energy derivative agrees to better than one part in a million. Integrating the external impulse and wall stress reproduces the final relative positions and velocities within **0.40 ppm denser / 0.87 ppm diffuse**. The denser coarse-cadence speed reconstruction originally missed at 25.8 ppm; increasing only the final force sampling fixes that discrepancy. Actual wave evolution did not change. A direct sky integration of the wave potential agrees with the analytic line-of-sight projection within 0.27 ppm across these eight cases.

This does **not** imply that the full predictions are converged to 20 ppm under every change of discretization. At fixed spatial representation, raising time steps from 1536 to 2304 changes the matched order-16 position and speed vectors by at most 1.36 ppm. At equal finer time step, changing the domain and radial basis shifts the position vectors by 0.0010 / 0.0029 pc and speeds by 0.000035 / 0.000055 km/s. The largest relative difference is 217 ppm, not 20 ppm. Both final angular refinements separately fall below 6 ppm. These comparisons support the rounded physical conclusions; neither two grids nor agreement between two readouts supplies a certified absolute error bound. Older misses are available in `grid-refinements.csv`, `time-and-space.csv`, and the coarse momentum tables.

**Material deformation.** The initially alarming fast growth occurs for discrete gravitating rings, but their candidate poles do not survive evaluation at resolved continuum frequencies. The causal leading deformation on the full-strength tide stays small in the sampled tests. Independent shell replays put the maximum sampled radial strain below 0.058% / 0.028%. Under the weaker assumption that material stays within its original support, separate conservative trajectory bounds are 1.76 / 0.236 pc relative to the point trajectory in the archived wave field. Do not confuse conditional support bounds or a leading deformation calculation with a general distribution-stability proof. Details: `CLOUD.md`.
