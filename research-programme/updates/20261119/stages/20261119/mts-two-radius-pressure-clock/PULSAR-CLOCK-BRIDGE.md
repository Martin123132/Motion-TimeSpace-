# Can we look for the frequency in actual sky data?

**Conditional frequency, not a fitted mass.** The retained example has m_h c²=9.585761787277403×10⁻²⁵ eV (MTS-HEAVY-BATH-COUNTERFLOW-AND-GALACTIC-STRESS.md, Stage50); the synthetic local branch uses its reduced Compton length, not a galaxy radius, as one unit. For a coherent nonrelativistic real field h≈A cos(ω_h t+φ), ω_h=m_h c²/ℏ and p≈−ρ c² cos(2ω_h t+2φ). The pressure frequency is f=ω_h/π=0.463565 nHz, period 68.3573 years. This doubles the field frequency, not the reduced-Compton time. The simulation's finite early peaks have small gradient/gravity shifts; the Galactic line frequency is the coherent, slowly varying limit. It is conditional on the earlier mass choice, not a new first-principles mass in eV. The time-dependent spherical ruler and the measured pulsar light-travel time are **not the same observable**.

**Map to the measured quantity.** Under the usual weak-field, nonrelativistic coherent-halo assumptions and metric coupling, the oscillating dimensionless potential at one endpoint is Ψ_c≈πGρ_mass/ω_h². The timing residual is the difference of two sinusoids at angular frequency 2ω_h, each of amplitude Ψ_c/(2ω_h), including the propagation delay and local phases. The Galactic density and coherence at Earth and the pulsar are *not* fixed by the synthetic cloud, the cosmological example fraction, or this schedule. Equal endpoints can enhance or cancel. This mapping is established scalar-field physics, not unique to MTS: [Khmelnitsky & Rubakov, especially eqs. 2.12 and 3.3–3.5](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.5888). A specified nonradial line of sight and its stress history are needed outside this halo approximation.

**Measured calendar, no residual fit.** Parsed the pinned [EPTA DR2full J1713+0747 directory](https://gitlab.in2p3.fr/epta/epta-dr2/-/tree/2911d0e52e0c8a4e528c4e3aa46b868ced1910e8/EPTA-DR2/DR2full/J1713%2B0747) through the project's public repository API. This is one schedule pilot selected before computing the transfer, not the whole array. Followed only active INCLUDE directives of the master TIM file. Retained integer MJD, instrument grouping and row counts only. No individual fractional arrival time, pulse residual, ephemeris solution or likelihood is retained or used for a signal fit. Source hash and byte ledger: epta-provenance.json; the [release](https://www.epta.eu.org/epta-dr2.html) and [versioned archive](https://zenodo.org/records/8300645) describe the data. Public collaboration summaries (including published ULDM constraints and aggregate pulsar results) have been seen. The raw TIM files were parsed for cadence; do not call this an untouched historical holdout.

**What was actually computed:** generate two unit sine/cosine waves at the retained frequency on the observing days; project out a quadratic in time (phase, spin, spin-down). Extra trials also remove annual/semiannual sinusoids and offsets per retained group. No pulses, timing residuals or measured dispersion parameters enter the projection. The square roots of the two generalized eigenvalues give the extreme post-/pre-fit RMS ratios over all phases. The ordinary eigenvalues instead give post-fit RMS per unit input sinusoid amplitude. Use this second normalization, not the first, to convert a conditional endpoint amplitude into ns. Two explicitly artificial weight choices: equal per included TOA and equal total per distinct day. The latter does not count channels on the same day as independent.

**Exposure and membership caveat.** There are 5,011 parsed active rows on 2,055 distinct days over 24.46 Julian years, just 0.358 pressure cycles. The [collaboration's summary](https://epta.pages.in2p3.fr/epta-dr2/J1713%2B0747.html) gives 5,003 TOAs and 37 backend labels; the pinned par file's NTOA line says 4,991. Our 25 groups use the TIM group flag, not the paper's backend taxonomy. These are not identical to a production timing-analysis mask. No points were deleted or added to force counts to match. If the production set differs *only* by deletion of up to 20 rows, equal-TOA post-fit RMS per unit amplitude cannot exceed the calculated maximum by more than √(5011/4991)−1=0.201%: the previous least-squares solution remains an available trial on the subset, so its sum of squared errors cannot grow before dividing by the new count. This is a one-sided nesting bound, not proof of the mask, a lower bound, or a result for other noise weights.

For this schedule, quadratic removal alone leaves **0.382–5.46%** of the pre-fit sinusoid RMS over phase (equal-TOA); adding the other listed nuisances leaves **0.325–3.93%**. Per illustrative 0.1 GeV/cm³ at one endpoint, the raw amplitude would be 6.05 μs; the last projection leaves 16.8–100 ns RMS for that one term. This is a conditional transfer coefficient, not a sensitivity limit or an expected signal in this pulsar. A statistically calibrated amplitude constraint would need the actual timing-design matrix, approved mask, correlated noise, delays, endpoint coherence model and null/injection recovery. No constraint has been inferred here.

**Existing external limit:** [Smarra et al. (EPTA DR2)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16228) report a density bound over an approximate mass interval whose lower edge is 10⁻²⁴ eV. Our illustrative mass is about 4.14% below that quoted edge. Neither extrapolate their headline bound to this specific point nor declare it unconstrained by all other data. A pulsar timing result could bound *occupation at a fixed mass* even if the parent cannot yet predict the local amplitude. It cannot by itself distinguish a minimally coupled MTS pole from ordinary scalar dark matter with identical stress.
