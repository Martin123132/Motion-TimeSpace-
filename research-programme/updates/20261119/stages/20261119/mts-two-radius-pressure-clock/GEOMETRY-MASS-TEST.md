# A metric-only mass-scale check on exterior patches

For ds²=g_ab dxᵃdxᵇ+R²dΩ², the radial two-dimensional trace of the common-metric Einstein equations gives e−p_r=2m/R³−□₂R/R in the retained normalization. A spherically symmetric canonical field contributes 2V to this expression; derivative terms, including those changed by a radial boost, cancel. Radial Maxwell charge contributes Q²/R⁴ and radial material contributes its rest density. Thus in a single massive-field exterior the subtracted invariant S is μ²h² for V=μ²h²/2. It vanishes for a sum of ordinary radial massless kinetic scalar fields. This uses the Einstein kinetic term and measured normalization; the subtraction is not valid for unconstrained extra matter. A conventional massive field with identical action is indistinguishable.

On a connected patch where h has one nonzero sign, U=√S=μ|h| solves the four-dimensional wave equation □₄U=μ²U. Equivalently 2S□₄S−(∇S)²=4μ²S²; this unsigned identity is degenerate at zeros. A more stable, nontrivial inverse test uses compact smooth φ: ∫U □₄φ √−g = μ² ∫U φ √−g. It does not require third derivatives of the measured metric or differentiating a noisy square root. Never continue |h| as a smooth massive wave across a nodal surface. In particular the mass-squared ratio is **undefined**, not zero, for electrovacuum.

Use the already sealed independent polar-areal strip. Read the spherical mass m and F−1 only for the metric calculation; α=F√f, f=1−2m/r. S_geo=2m_r/r²−f F_r/(Fr)−Q²/r⁴ (negligible exterior material is a controlled assumption). Then F r²□₄φ=−∂t[r²/(Ff)∂tφ]+∂r[Fr²f∂rφ]. The stored scalar value is a separately labelled check, not a substitute for negative or vanishing S. On the two original polar resolutions, test patches centred at areal 6 and 6.5 (halfwidth .30) and times [.25,.95], [2.55,3.65], [5.7,6.3] in the strip's polar time, not the falling clocks' proper time. Use φ=(1−s_t²)⁵(1−s_r²)⁵ on the rectangle and zero outside; orders 12,24,36 for quadrature. Require positive, resolved S on each massive patch, including a separately sampled edge/interior check, and separation from the ordinary false signal before computing a ratio. Report all six patches whether they meet 2e−5 or not, derivative/interpolation sensitivity and the ordinary floor. No fitting and no dimensional mass prediction: the test is whether the geometrical data return the input inverse-time scale of the retained field. Controls: analytic radial massive wave on flat space, constant U in de Sitter (zero quotient), and vanishing S in Reissner–Nordström. These mathematical controls are distinct from the physical wave-plus-gravity runs.
The quotient cancels a common multiplicative calibration of U. It does **not** cancel an unknown additive stress (e.g. a cosmological term), and it is not an absolute gravitational-coupling measurement. As a limited noise budget, keeping the operator and volume weights fixed, errors |δU|≤eU·U change the numerator by at most eU∫U|□φ|√−g. For |δS|≤eS·S with eS<1, take eU=1−√(1−eS). Report a conservative sign-only input allowance after reserving the observed numerical discrepancies. Real metric noise also perturbs the operator and differentiating mass/lapse amplifies it, so this allowance alone is not an instrument specification.
