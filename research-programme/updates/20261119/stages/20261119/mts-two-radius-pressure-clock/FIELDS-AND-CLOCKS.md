# Two independently propagated clocks and a matched radiation control

In polar-areal coordinates ds²=−F²f dt²+dr²/f+r²dΩ² and f=1−2m/r. For each real scalar species let h_i be the field, Π_i=h_{i,t}/(Ff) and μ_i its inverse reduced Compton length. It does not access either evolved detector history. With p the neutral probe's covariant radial momentum and γ=√(1+fp²), dr/dτ=fp, dt/dτ=γ/(F√f). It is a neutral geodesic even in the charged preparation. The radial stress in its frame is the sum of [(γπ_i+γv s_i)²+(γs_i+γvπ_i)²−μ_i²h_i²]/2 and the boosted dust pressure, where π_i=√fΠ_i, s_i=√f h_{i,r}. Coulomb pressure is treated separately. We compare identical proper times, not identical coordinate times.

In the conventional radiation control use two real massless fields, initially (h₁,π₁)=(h₀,0) and (h₂,π₂)=(0,μh₀). The μ on the right is the massive example setting the available launch energy, not the propagation mass of either comparator wave. Pressure need not: converting potential to kinetic stress changes its sign where gradients are small. The different lapse gradients then follow from the actual Einstein equations; neither source confines the other to its metric. This is a bounded conventional alternative, not an exhaustive catalogue of ordinary matter. This predicts common phases at different radii even without transport between them; gravity and gradients correct it. The reported light time is the crossing time on the *release* metric converted to the stationary outer clock at launch, not an independently ray-traced pulse front in the later evolving geometry.
