# The second clock was predicted; the two peaks are not a travelling front

Private controlled experiment, 19 November 2026. A frozen calculation from the retained charged matter and massive scalar launch predicted both free-fall clocks. No detector history at the new radius was used to select an amplitude, mass, peak time or tolerance. A second implementation in a different coordinate system then evolved the same original launch and read the freely falling geometry. This stage also makes an initial contact with actual pulsar observing dates, not with fitted sky residuals.

## Where and when the pressure appeared

At release radius 6.5 the four-clock fall statistic was predicted to be 2.8569171588×10⁻⁵. The independently sampled and time-differentiated radius gives 2.8569171579×10⁻⁵, relative error 3.32×10⁻¹⁰. At the original radius 6, the same comparison gives 2.2512322825×10⁻⁵ vs 2.2512322825×10⁻⁵ (using the stable displacement, not subtraction of nearly equal radii). The ordinary charged control remained at its numerical zero. The full outer pressure and flux predictions have peak absolute errors of 6.11×10⁻¹⁰ and 3.49×10⁻⁷ of their respective physical channel amplitudes, on the finer grid. 48/48 original physical forecast comparisons and 20/20 old-readout checks passed. Both radii belong to one simulation per grid and model; they are not independent universes.

The first positive pressure maximum was forecast at proper times 1.5702798361 and 1.5703190941. The measured outer-minus-inner lag was 0.00003925786, less than one ten-thousandth of the roughly 0.50017 release-metric light crossing. The field is present at *both locations initially*. A local oscillator using only the initial field already reproduces each full pressure history to 0.32% or better. The result is consistent with coherent local oscillation plus small gradient/gravity corrections. **It does not measure a superluminal wave speed.** The second peak's tiny lag is less precise: the numerical budget is 1.28×10⁻⁴ of that lag, outside the 2×10⁻⁵ relative target even though the individual peak times pass. At .02 and .04 source-time sampling the stress reconstruction reproduces the outer clock to 3.26×10⁻¹¹ or better on its own signal. Cubic at .2 and quintic at .4 miss; overall 32/40 reconstruction variants pass. See lag-budget.csv, radius-lags.csv and pressure-replay.csv.

## What kind of matter is identifiable?

A physically specified conventional pair of radial **massless waves** was given the same initial energy, net flux, ordinary material, charge and intrinsic geometry as the retained massive preparation; the matching error was 1.11×10⁻¹⁶ or less. Its initial non-Coulomb pressure at 6.5 is positive, not negative, and its signed clock statistic is −3.76×10⁻⁹ rather than +2.86×10⁻⁵. Replacing the potential by equal kinetic energy does not reproduce this signal. Replacing the MTS label by a conventional minimally coupled **massive** scalar with identical action *does*. Gravity is measuring a stress history, not a brand of field.

We found a way to read out the oscillation mass scale without inserting the scalar history into the estimator. Subtract electric and ordinary-material contributions from the radial trace of the metric equations. On this branch the result S is μ²h². On patches where the field does not cross zero, √S itself obeys the massive wave equation. Moving derivatives onto a compact test function gives a ratio of metric integrals that should return μ², not just a positive stress. Independent spherical clock geometry recovered the stress on both grids and all 24 differentiation checks. On a separate sealed polar-areal bank, all six patch estimates of μ² passed the unchanged target; the worst fine estimate differs from the input 1 by less than 9×10⁻¹⁰ and the worst error plus grid/interpolation/quadrature changes is 6.11×10⁻⁸. Electrovacuum correctly does not yield a mass estimate (zero signal). A de Sitter constant-field readout returned zero rather than one, and an analytic 0.8-mass wave returned 0.64. This inverse test assumes the stated matter subtraction, symmetry and non-nodal patches. It **recovers an input scale** from independent outputs; it does not derive the dimensional particle mass or establish that ordinary massive matter is absent. See GEOMETRY-MASS-TEST.md, geometric-stress.csv and weak-mass-robustness.csv.

## One step toward the sky

At the already retained illustrative mass, the coherent pressure period is **68.36 years**, or 0.4636 nHz. The established weak-field scalar pulsar-timing response is distinct from our synthetic falling ruler. A cadence-only calculation on EPTA's real J1713+0747 observing calendar spans 24.46 years. Across input phases, the spin quadratic leaves only **0.38%–5.46%** of a sinusoid's original RMS with equal weight per parsed arrival. Baseline and signal undergo the same projection; no observed timing residual was fitted. This tells us why a long period is difficult, not impossible, to test. No local wave occupation or halo density has been assumed for that dimensionless result. Group-mask count disagreement and conditional amplitude examples are documented in PULSAR-CLOCK-BRIDGE.md. A programmable offline replay uses the compact day/group cache, not the raw telescope archive.
