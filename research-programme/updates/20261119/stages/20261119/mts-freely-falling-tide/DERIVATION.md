# From common free fall to the first tidal response

This is the retained positive-quadratic, minimally metric-coupled heavy state in its isolated, spherical, nodeless Newtonian limit. Neither its particle mass nor the coupling of a test mass was adjusted. Its population/length normalization is supplied, not derived from galaxy formation. There are two distinct controls: the translating wave versus its untranslated ground, and true self-gravity versus evolution in its fixed ground-state potential. A no-wave baryonic model has zero **induced wave** moment by definition but the same direct satellite field; none of these controls is a fitted LCDM halo.

**Free fall.** In scaled units \(i\partial_t\psi=(-\nabla^2/2+\Phi+U)\psi,\ \nabla^2\Phi=|\psi|^2\), with \(M=\int d^3r\,|\psi|^2/(4\pi)\). For \(U=-{\bf g}(t)\cdot{\bf r}\) an isolated solution is translated exactly:
\[
 \psi({\bf r},t)=e^{i[\dot{\bf X}\cdot{\bf r}-\tfrac12\int^t|\dot{\bf X}|^2dt']}\psi_0({\bf r}-{\bf X},t),
 \quad \Phi({\bf r},t)=\Phi_0({\bf r}-{\bf X}),\quad \ddot{\bf X}={\bf g}.
\]
Substitution cancels both the gradient and acceleration terms. Only the center carries the new momentum and kinetic energy. Holding the self-potential fixed in space invalidates this proof. In a locally freely falling nonrotating frame the next term is \(U=\tfrac12 T_{ij}r^ir^j\). With the weak static metric \(g_{00}=-(1+2U/c^2)\), \(x^0=ct\), and geodesic deviation \(\ddot \xi^i=-c^2 R^i{}_{0j0}\xi^j\), \(T_{ij}=\partial_i\partial_jU=c^2 R_{0i0j}\) locally in physical units. Its trace drives the spherical sector; its trace-free part drives five quadrupoles. These statements use the previously retained common metric, not a derivation of Einstein's field equations from this wave equation.

**Linear system.** Write the real and imaginary parts of the perturbation of \(e^{-iEt}u(r)\) as \(a(r,t)P_2\) and \(b(r,t)P_2\). Use the radial inner product \(r^2dr\) (or reduced functions \(ra,rb\) in \(dr\)). Set \(H=-\Delta_2/2+\Phi_0-E\), \(Ka=u\Delta_2^{-1}(2ua)\). Then
\[
 \dot a=Hb,\qquad \dot b=-(H+K)a-uU_2 .
\]
Regularity at the origin and matching the self-potential to \(r^{-3}\) outside the box give \(V'+3V/r=0\), not a zero potential at the outer grid. The real symmetric stiffness \(H^{1/2}(H+K)H^{1/2}\) yields modes \(w_j\) with positive \(\omega_j^2\) in the computed quadrupole sector. Positivity of these discretized modes is not a global nonlinear stability proof. With \(a=H^{1/2}\sum_j w_j z_j\), each \(z_j''+\omega_j^2z_j=c_j\), \(c_j=-\langle w_j,H^{1/2}u U_2\rangle\). The state includes the homogeneous solution as well as the causal integral. A static Love coefficient cannot in general replace that integral.

**Normalization and finite extent.** \(Q_2=\int\delta\rho\,r^2P_2\,d^3r/(4\pi)=(2/5)\int r^4u a\,dr\). For an axis \({\bf n}\), \(Q_{ij}=Q_2(n_i n_j-\delta_{ij}/3)\); for general forcing use five orthonormal trace-free matrices, not five new parameters. In an imposed potential \(-{\cal E}r^2P_2\), define \(\alpha_2=Q_2/{\cal E}\). For a physical length unit \(\ell\), \(\lambda=\alpha_2\ell^5/(3G)\) in \(Q^{SI}_{ij}=-\lambda T_{ij}^{TF}\); \(G\lambda\) has units of length to the fifth power. Using the retained mass-length product, the invariant \(G\lambda(GM/c^2)^5/(\hbar/mc)^{10}=M_{\rm code}^5\alpha_2/3\). This is the quantity to compare to the Newtonian boson-star benchmark, not \(\alpha_2\) itself. A point perturber at distance \(D\) has exact radial driver \(-U_2=M_s\min(r,D)^2/\max(r,D)^3\). It is not proportional to \(r^2\) over the whole wave.

**Nonlinear pulse.** A shell at \(D=3\) with density proportional to \(1+\kappa h(t)P_2\), \(\kappa=\pm1/2,\ h=\sin^2(\pi t/T)\), has nonnegative mass everywhere. Rest mass \(10\epsilon D^3\) produces the signed quadrupolar driving amplitude \(\pm\epsilon h\). A mass-conserving surface flow is \(v_\theta=\kappa D\dot h\,\partial_\theta P_2/[6(1+\kappa hP_2)]\); use the spherical divergence and \(\Delta_\Omega P_2=-6P_2\). This is a prescribed, driven source, not a proposed material's equation of state. Each run begins at fixed wave mass in equilibrium with the matching monopole. Pair opposite signs to extract the odd moment, average energies for the even work, and extrapolate \(2D_{\epsilon/2}-D_\epsilon\) for the isolated tangent. This last step is necessary because the common positive shell mass itself scales with \(\epsilon\). Individual finite responses, extrapolations and fixed pre-run predictions are separately retained.

**Finite satellite and retarded tensor.** For a Hernquist potential \(-M_s/(p+b_s)\) with \(p=|{\bf r}-{\bf D}|\), the central trace-free amplitude is \(M_s(3D+b_s)/[3D(D+b_s)^3]\); the trace \(2M_s b_s/[D(D+b_s)^3]\) is not zero. Use the full \(l=2\) projection, not just the central Hessian. Integrating Rodrigues' formula twice gives \(-U_2/M_s=(5/16)(rD)^2\int_{-1}^1(1-\mu^2)^2(3p+b_s)/[p^3(p+b_s)^3]\,d\mu\). Integrate in separation \(p\) with split endpoints; it has no subtractive pole from the constant potential at small radius. An independent implementation subtracts the constant and linear Taylor terms of \(1/(p+b_s)\) exactly before a signed Legendre projection. It also recovers the point-source projection on both sides of \(r=D\).

Every source coefficient multiplies the tensor \(\hat D_i(t)\hat D_j(t)-\delta_{ij}/3\). The Green integration is checked by independently evolving real and imaginary wave coefficients for the six Cartesian tensor entries with a time-domain ODE, rather than evaluating the same sine integral twice. For each tensor component the induced potential uses both interior and exterior density:
\[
 \delta\Phi_2=-\tfrac25\left[r^{-3}\int_0^r s^4u a\,ds+r^2\int_r^\infty u a\,ds/s\right].
\]
Evaluating its derivative cancels the two boundary terms. The angle-dependent potential is \(3n_i\delta\Phi_{ij}n_j/2\) in the tensor expansion used by the code. The equal-radius maximum-to-minimum weak-field proper-clock-rate contrast is the corresponding potential span divided by \(c^2\) for clocks instantaneously at rest in the stated frame. This is the induced wave piece, not the full redshift of an emitter seen at Earth.
