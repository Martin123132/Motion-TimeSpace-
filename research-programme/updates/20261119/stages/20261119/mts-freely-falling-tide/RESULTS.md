# A uniform field transports the state; tides excite a calculable shape

This local stage concerns the retained isolated heavy-wave ground state. Source gravity and wave self-gravity both use the common Newtonian metric. No deformation, galaxy occupation, orientation, or satellite orbit was fitted to the outputs.

**Field checks first.** A uniform finite-duration pull reproduced the translated density, center, and velocity on the fine grid. Through release and two later samples the residual internal energy was at most 1.28e-12 of the acquired bulk energy; integrated absolute density error was at most 1.67e-7 of the actual density displacement. The coarse density reconstruction failed the unchanged 2e-5 criterion and is retained. This test distinguishes carrying the wave from heating it.

The quadrupole mode instead rings at dimensionless angular frequency **0.44959810**, independently obtained from a spectral calculation and coupled radial boundary-value equations. This matches the published 0.450 of [Chan, Sibiryakov & Xue, Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1007/JHEP08(2023)045). The static moment is **92.43133** per unit long tide; the normalized tidal deformability is **1149.195**, versus about 1149 in [Mendes & Yang, Eq. 31](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.03035). These are successful recoveries of known Schrödinger–Poisson physics, not new discoveries of a mode or Love number. Holding the self-potential fixed would give frequency 0.46551 and response 81.48864. Treating the tide as uniform in curvature across the wave would also be wrong for a nearby compact driver: at three scale lengths the exact static moment is 44.22690, only 47.85% of the long-tide value.

The positive shell's one-period pulse leaves positive work; 76.80% of the linear work goes to the lowest quadrupole mode. The full nonlinear wave, extrapolated to the isolated tangent using two positive donor masses and both tidal signs, recovers the pre-run energy forecast to **0.65 ppm** and every fine-grid moment/slope/work comparison within **16.1 ppm**. All 12 fine and all 12 intermediate comparisons pass at 20 ppm. Three coarse shell comparisons miss. The data distinguish the response of the finite loaded system from its isolated linear limit. Rotations of the saved finite-pulse densities check five independent tidal axes; this is not five nonlinear rotating-source experiments.

**A changing satellite gives a different answer from a static tide.** We drove all five linear components with the published five-Gyr orbit of the Large Magellanic Cloud (LMC) and its extended mass profile. The measured stream entered the *source authors'* reconstruction; we have not predicted that stream's observations. See [Erkal et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.08192) and [their released trajectory](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.2661108). For an initially unexcited wave on that orbit:

Here “shape amplitude” is the rotation-invariant quadrupole norm; the angle is unoriented (nearest end of the longest axis), not a uniquely defined temporal phase delay. The two rows represent **different supplied occupations of the same pole**, not a confidence interval. At the capped normalization the static extended-satellite response is only 47.05% of that for the same mass at the same distance concentrated at a point. The wave's own finite extent lowers it to 92.10% of the extended satellite's central-curvature estimate. Neither effect is a new coupling. Starting the full orbit in static deformation at rest instead of zero deformation gives 19.49° to the quoted precision; later-start comparisons and the unrestricted possibility of free oscillations are separate.

**An observational sensitivity check.** At the release's solar location (8.1 kpc) the present induced wave contributes 0.00803 microarcsec/year to the astrometric acceleration dipole for the capped state; 0.00925 for the illustrative one. Even holding all other accelerations fixed, these shifts are only **0.023 and 0.026 sigma** in the [Gaia EDR3 solar-acceleration covariance, Table 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.02036). The measured *total* amplitude is 5.05 ± 0.35 microarcsec/year; it must not be compared as though this single multipole were the entire Galaxy. This rules out that particular measurement as a useful discriminator of these small conditional signals at its quoted precision. It is not a detection, fit, or exclusion of MTS.

**Numerical and physical limits.** Direct integration of real/imaginary wave coefficients and six Cartesian components recovers the five-component Green calculation's entire present tensor to 0.073 ppm (illustrative) and 0.056 ppm (cap), on the same box at different radial resolution. All 26 independent rotation, projection, and time-evolution checks pass. Fourteen of 240 successive resolution comparisons miss the own-observable budget, all in the signed radial force along the satellite line; the total tensor is better conditioned. The last 72→96 miss is at 17.58 kpc in the capped, unexcited case. Extending to 120 then 144 scale lengths reduces the change to 12.9 then 2.58 ppm; the signed inward piece is +1.541e-13 m/s². This refines that comparison, not all Galactic accelerations.

The pulse tests the actual nonlinear field, but the orbital application is a **linear response on a spherical isolated state to a prescribed satellite**. Its present relative wave-perturbation norm is 1.45% (cap) or 7.33% (illustrative); neither number proves an error bound. The calculation does not evolve the Galactic disk, form the occupied state, or update the satellite orbit in MTS. The next test is whether finite driving and mixing of angular components change the predicted orientation or magnitude appreciably, before using streams as a discriminator.
