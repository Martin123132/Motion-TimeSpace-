## Full target in areal radius

Take ds²=−N²dt²+dr²/F+r²dΩ², F=1−2m/r, N=exp ν, with N(t,0)=1 as a time convention. B r²dr counts baryons; P is specific radial canonical momentum, J=ℓ² conserved along each particle, λ=J/r², γ=√(1+FP²+λ), v=NF P/γ its coordinate radial speed. The particle Hamiltonian Nγ gives
```
(∂t+v∂r)P = N[(λ/r−γ²ν'−F'P²/2)/γ],
(∂t+v∂r)J=0,         B_t=−(r²Bv)'/r².
ρ_b=B√F γ,            p_b=B√F FP²/γ,       j_b=BFP.
```
For normalized scalar H and W=Π/√F, Π=N⁻¹H_t, let z multiply the stress. Then
```
H_t=N√F W,      W_t=(r²N√F H')'/r²−NH/√F,
ρ_s=z[F(W²+H'²)+H²]/2,  p_s=z[F(W²+H'²)−H²]/2,  j_s=−z FWH'.
m'=r²ρ,        m_t=−r²N√F j,        ν'=(m/r²+r p)/F.
```
Sum matter and scalar in the last line. The target evolves mass from the momentum flux, checks the independent spatial constraint, and integrates the lapse constraint. Counter-orbiting particles have no net **angular** momentum; neither the radial flux nor the radial material response has been set to zero. The initial W is scaled by the actual loaded √F so different preparations prescribe the same local Π. At zero occupation the equilibrium satisfies λ_b=rν_b'(1+λ_b). Subtract that identity algebraically in the force, not an empirical drift or an artificial restoring potential. The unchanged coordinates do not pin matter shells in place.

## Independent driven-shell equation

Work to first order in scalar stress, finite in the ordinary source. Write ξ(t,r) for the perturbation of the shell initially at r, δB=−(r²B_b ξ)'/r² and δλ=−J_b' ξ/r². The static metric is F_b,N_b. Initial shell position and radial velocity changes vanish. The KG field is solved on this static background; at this order its evolution is independent of its own metric perturbation. The mass constraint rearranges to
```
δm = η − m_b' ξ,       η' + Dη = r²ρ_s,    D=r B_b γ_b/√F_b=[ln(N_b/√F_b)]'.
η = (√F_b/N_b) ∫₀ʳ (N_b/√F_b) s²ρ_s ds.
```
The cancellation of the terms proportional to ξ in the source uses both J_b=r²m_b/(r−3m_b) and ordinary equilibrium. η is positive for positive source, but δm at fixed areal radius also includes transported ordinary mass. The source on fixed clocks is not an arbitrary multiple of scalar density.

Now δν'=[δm/(F_b r²)+r p_s]/F_b. Linearizing the particle Hamiltonian and eliminating momentum gives
```
ξ̈ + κ²(r)ξ = −N_b² [ η/(F_b r²) + r p_s ],
κ² = N_b² m_b/(r³F_b) (1−6m_b/r).
```
Dots here are derivatives in the central time coordinate. In the coefficient of ξ the contribution from J_b' contains m_b', canceled by the −m_b'ξ part of δm. For example, with u=m_b/r the two pieces before subtraction are proportional to (m_b'+u−6u²) and m_b'. This is the self-consistent radial shell response, not a test particle moving through a permanently fixed density. For this source κ² is positive and its Newtonian limit is m_b/r³. This small radial-spherical test is not a proof of collective nonspherical stability. Forcing by η is inward; pressure may change sign. The time integral of the actual forcing, not the arithmetic mean of alternative launch phases, determines the velocity increment. The forecast solves this driven equation and the radial mass integral, *not* the conservative target's matter or mass evolution.

## Sending and receiving light

Use supported observers at fixed **areal** radii in all four states. Their proper clocks synchronize to zero on the common launch slice and read τ_i(t)=∫N(t,r_i)dt. They do not represent an orbiting material particle's wristwatch. A ray obeys dt/ds=n=(N√F)⁻¹ for s=|dr|. Its infinitesimal time-separation Jacobian is exp(∫∂_t n ds); received/emitted frequency is N_emit/(N_rec times that Jacobian), with both legs for an echo. Invert the emitter proper time before integrating, and convert back at reception. Clock readings at launch already involve finite flight times. Compare launch-referenced proper readings for scalar-only as well as the mixed matter/scalar difference, not subtracting large rounded endpoints. Propagate to common receiver proper dates for the calendar exercise. The old freely falling clocks follow different worldlines, so their sign is not an independent check on these supported clocks.

The energy test compares integrated outward flux to loss of mass inside a fixed areal sphere, including the small transport of ordinary matter. The first readout samples flux and then multiplies by r². A subsequent identical-history replay samples the **product** r²flux, matched to the sampled mass. Both ledgers are kept; a near cancellation should not be judged using incompatible spatial sampling. Separate flux components and the sum are integrated in time. Material label and specific angular momentum are advected; inversion of the label differs from minus its perturbation at fixed r only by the monitored displacement-gradient correction. Constraints are tested inside the domain of dependence; there is no extrapolation of the extended scalar to an ADM mass.
