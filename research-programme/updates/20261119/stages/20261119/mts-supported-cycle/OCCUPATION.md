# Can the clock source prepare the wave that the clock test assumes?

**Answer for the supplied 33.36 pc spherical source: not as a single stationary mode bound only by the ordinary cloud.** This is not a verdict on general unbound waves, larger environments or dynamical superpositions. It is possible to construct a *different* isolated state conditional on the chosen density. These are two distinct calculations.

## A local test without guessing an exterior wave boundary

For a probe mode H(r)cos(ωt) in the stationary ordinary metric, use the lapse N normalized to Schwarzschild time at infinity. Integrating the KG equation over any shell where H has one sign gives
```
ω²−1 = { ∫ r² H (N−1/N)/√F dr − [r² N√F H'] } / ∫ r² H/(N√F) dr .
```
The same ω must work on every shell. For an asymptotically flat, normalizable massive bound state it also must lie below the rest threshold. The six shell estimates for the saved envelope, out to r=6.5, give ω²−1=+0.002389 down to +0.000683, with changes vastly exceeding numerical quadrature errors. No constant frequency (even unbound) matches this exact profile in the tested ordinary metric. This does **not** invalidate evolving it as initial-value data. It invalidates calling it a stationary occupation selected by these baryons. No velocity or timing data entered this exclusion.

In the Newtonian weak limit use Compton lengths, c² units for Φ, and H as a real s-wave envelope. For any bound eigenvalue min Φ≤E<0, integrate (r²H')'=2r²(Φ−E)H from the regular center. Iterating the positive Volterra kernel bounding the absolute value gives |H(r)/H(0)−1|≤sinh(kr)/(kr)−1 with k²=2|Φ(0)| for spherical positive matter. The known baryons give a central escape speed of 15.03 km/s, hence a shape change of at most 1.05×10⁻⁸ to the source edge; the input changes by 0.006528. Inverting this necessary inequality would require ≥11,855 km/s of well depth for that amount of variation in a single bound mode, not a prediction of an actual halo escape speed. This second bound is approximate nonrelativistic physics; the first test used the exact ordinary metric.

## A population bound, even if orbital states are mixed

In the weak isolated spherical Schrödinger problem, if the **total** gravitating mass is M in geometric units, Φ(r)≥−M/r, independent of its detailed shape. A normalized negative-energy state then obeys ∥∇ψ∥²/2<M〈1/r〉≤M∥∇ψ∥; the second step follows from integrating div(r-hat)=2/r by parts. Also ∥ψ/r∥≤2∥∇ψ∥ (expand the nonnegative square of ∇ψ + r-hat ψ/(2r)). Consequently
```
P(r<R) ≤ R² ∥ψ/r∥² < 16 (MR)² .
```
In ordinary units replace MR by G M R/(ℏ/m_h)². It holds for each negative-energy orbital, hence for positive, incoherently weighted mixtures in the same static potential. It is not a bound on a driven wave or on interference between coherent different-energy states evaluated at a chosen phase. Finite total mass, spherical source potential, isolation and the nonrelativistic limit matter.

For the **test-field** interpretation using the baryons alone, the core fraction ceiling is 1.60×10⁻¹⁵. The imposed amplitude corresponds to ~413 solar masses of nonrelativistic field rest density inside the core. Producing it under that test-field assumption would require ≥2.58×10¹⁷ solar masses of total populated wave. That contradicts ignoring its gravity; it is a reductio, not a claim that such a halo exists. Alternatively for an isolated self-gravitating bound population, substitute M=M_b+M_s and solve the monotonic cubic inequality for M_s: at least 2.72×10⁹ solar masses for that specific core mass, as a necessary, not sufficient bound. Baryons alone have formal Bohr length 3.34 Gpc. The isolated Newtonian ground-energy bracket follows by comparing to the point mass (lower energy bound) and testing the hydrogenic 1s wave: the relative finite-core correction is ≤4(M_b R)². This is not a cosmological orbit prediction.

## Constructing an alternative, not merely rejecting the preparation

Solve the retained nonrelativistic Schrödinger–Poisson equations. Fix the *same illustrative rest-density normalization* 0.1 GeV/cm³ at r=6 and the retained pole, regular radial ground branch, no other environment. Do not impose a shape or tune a galaxy speed. Define the scale S=ρ_ref^(−1/4) for density in the 4πG convention, x=r/S, wave u with ρ_h=u²/S⁴ and self-potential v/S². The dimensionless boundary-value problem is
```
u''+2u'/x=2(v+S²Φ_b−e)u,     v''+2v'/x=u².
u'(0)=v'(0)=0,   u(6/S)=1,    u→0,    v→−mass/x .
```
Select a positive nodeless solution. Numerically solve on two outer domains with regular origin, vacuum-potential matching and vanishing outer wave. Enlarging the domain and refining the mesh changes the main quantities by <3×10⁻¹¹ relative; integrated density versus potential flux, energy versus eigenvalue and the virial relation including the external-force contribution are independent checks. The outer cutoff is numerical, not a claimed truncation radius. The ordinary profile here is Newtonian with the same baryon count; the circular speeds in this alternative are recalculated in the *combined mean potential*. They are not pre-existing particle actions in the dynamical clock experiment.

With baryons: M_s=1.48134×10¹¹ solar masses, half-mass radius 24.64 kpc. At 33.36 pc only 409.5 solar masses of scalar are enclosed and the envelope drops by 1.44×10⁻⁶. The halo is mostly well **outside** the clock box. Its scalar-only circular speed at half mass is 113.7 km/s, not a comparison to a particular galaxy. The isolated pure-wave result has G M_s R_half/(ℏ/m_h)²=3.92510; varying the local density changes M_s∝ρ_ref^(1/4), R_half∝ρ_ref^(−1/4) on the homologous isolated family, not their product. The parent’s November 7 finite-difference ground mode has the same mass-normalized shape up to ~3.3×10⁻⁵ raw resolution differences; retain those raw discrepancies as well as the two-grid extrapolation check. The previously tested family was not invented here. What is new is the transition from a tested *local density* to a global conditional equilibrium, a rigorous weak-field necessary mass bound, and the exclusion of the original envelope as a baryon-selected stationary state.

This solves a mean-field preparation under stipulated population, isolation and ground-state assumptions. It does not explain which galaxy selects that population. In particular do **not** attach the clock amplitudes of the old envelope and orbit set to this replacement state. Propagating the new state requires matching the time-averaged mean mass and particle actions first, avoiding double counting its scalar energy when the real oscillating field is restored.
