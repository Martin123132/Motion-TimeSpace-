# The supported cloud moves inward over a cycle; its assumed wave needs a larger source

**Time evolution.** At 20.01 pc the turning-phase scalar initially accelerates a supported particle outward, +6.22×10⁻¹⁴ m/s². Measured from one pressure zero to the next zero with the same slope (68.313 years on the local supported clock), the particle nevertheless gains **6.74×10⁻⁵ m/s inward**; the inferred displacement changes by −108.94 km. Starting instead at kinetic maximum gives **6.73×10⁻⁵ m/s inward** in 68.315 years (−108.81 km). Starting from launch and reading at the nominal period π gives −72.74 and −72.63 km respectively; those intervals start at another phase. The zero-scalar cloud remains stationary. These modest changes follow from the specified states with no radial kick, not a claim about repeated cycles indefinitely.

The source is the scalar's lapse-weighted enclosed energy *plus* its local radial pressure. The inertia/restoring coefficient is fixed by the ordinary enclosed mass; terms in the gradient of the ordinary mass cancel against ordinary matter transported across the observation radius. This predicts the matter motion without evolving the target's momentum equation. The full finite-stress target instead evolves the matter and scalar flux, mass, particle momentum, angular momentum and lapse. At 20.01 pc the mean coordinate accelerations over the *actual* pressure cycle are −3.126 and −3.120×10⁻¹⁴ m/s² respectively. See DERIVATION.md for the assumptions and equations. This is a prediction of the quadratic parent subfamily, not a unique distinction from GR with the same populated scalar.
