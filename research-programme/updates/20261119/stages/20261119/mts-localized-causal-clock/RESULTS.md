**Why did the clocks move?** We independently integrated the Coulomb-corrected inverse-square mass term, electric metric term and radial-pressure term through each receiver's history. At the outer massive clock at time 3.2 their respective signed displacements are −2.24240e−8, −2.88e−16 and **+7.89903e−8**, giving +5.65663e−8. The inverse-square contribution is present but predicts the wrong sign by itself. This concerns the *change* in fall under impulse reversal, not a reversal of the background gravitational pull. All four integrated clocks agree with the separately evolved polar clocks; the worst combined relative error budget is 4.13e−8. Omitting pressure misses by 111–118% of peak displacement. There was no fitted pressure coefficient.

**Resolution outcome.** The first additional polar refinement passed 83/84 checks; its one miss was the 1% outer massive pressure time. On 15,360 intervals all **84/84** refined histories, threshold times and mean null arrivals meet the unchanged 2e−5 target. The largest combined discrepancy is 1.51e−6. These checks ask whether two numerical methods resolve the same prediction, not whether the prediction fits the sky.

The null arrivals are independent predictions in two coordinate systems. They are not times at which a discontinuity was observed. On the finest independent receiver grid, pressure leakage up to 0.05 before the front is at most 1.63e−12 of the first-window amplitude for the massive pair (3.16e−12 for the massless control). The launch envelope vanishes to sixth order at its edge; the analytic flat field first rises as the seventh power of time after arrival. The flat massive radial Green function even reproduces the coupled **field** waveform to about 0.12–0.18% of peak, but is not the full gravity calculation. At 3.2 both massive field responses have changed sign; the equal-launch-energy massless responses remain positive.

**What distinguishes the two preparations using the metric alone?** Halving the impulse changed the signed responses by half to within 1.36e−7 of their half-strength peaks across both models, both sites and all four channels. The common even launch mass excess fell to one quarter, within 1.85e−8 relatively. Fixing one signed gain on the inner clock and predicting the outer clock at half strength gives relative RMS errors 1.94e−8 (massive→massive) and 1.05e−8 (massless→massless). The massless template predicting the massive clock misses by **62.86% RMS**; in reverse the error is **43.50%**. The gain, including its sign, was allowed to differ on each comparison; the second clock was not used to choose it. This is transfer across simulated receivers and drive strengths, not independent observed data. The massless control has a specified two-field occupation; the result does not rule out all radiation preparations. A conventional minimally coupled scalar with the same mass and state is exactly degenerate.
