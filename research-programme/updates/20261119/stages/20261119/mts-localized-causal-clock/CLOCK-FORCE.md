# Which part of the local force actually moved each clock?

Derivation before the force-channel replay. In the spherical synchronous equations write the areal radius as R=x b. Then R' (proper-time derivative) = −R k_θ/b². Combining (b²)'=−2 k_θ with k_θ'=b²(m/R³ + p_total − (k_θ/b²)²) cancels the squared expansion terms exactly:

    R'' = −m/R² − R p_total
        = −M/R² + Q²/R³ − R p_material+scalar,
    M = m + Q²/(2R).

Here m is the Misner–Sharp mass; the coefficients are in the saved rescaled field units, and p_total includes electric radial tension −Q²/(2R⁴). M is a convenient Coulomb-corrected mass, not necessarily a constant. This is a neutral free-fall clock, so Q²/R³ is a metric effect, not the Lorentz force on a charged probe. In a static electrovac patch, the neutral Reissner–Nordström force follows; setting charge and exterior pressure to zero gives the inverse-square result. We are testing use of the retained metric equations, not assuming the reduced scalar selects a galaxy's population.

For each sign keep the three terms separately. Take half the difference only after evaluating every term at that clock's actual radius. The initial difference in position and velocity is zero, so integrate each force with kernel (τ−u) from zero to τ, without fitting an integration constant, a mass or a time offset. Build flux/mass/pressure sources in independent synchronous evolutions at 0.0125 and 0.00625. Compare their net displacement history against the polar clock, and contrast deleting the pressure term and retaining only the Coulomb-corrected inverse-square part.
