# What a rotating measured mass distribution does to the retained wave

**Model and normalization.** This is the collisionless nonrelativistic, positive quadratic heavy-bath branch, on the same Newtonian metric potential that accelerates ordinary matter. Write iκ ∂t ψ = [−κ²∇²/2 + Φ]ψ, ∇²Φ = 4πG(ρ_b+ρ_h), with specific energies in (km/s)² and κ = ħ/m_h in kpc km/s. The saved single occupied mean state is a conditional preparation. No new coupling or occupation has been inferred here. Finite changes to baryons and normalization at the old outer anchor are inherited, not predictions of how the Galaxy formed. The static NFW comparison sees the same baryons and outer constraint. Its density is held spherical; this is not a calculation of a live collisionless NFW wake, which requires a phase-space distribution.

**Ordinary gravity before wave gravity.** Replace the old spherical central excess by the E3 density with that same mass. For P_l^m normalized over the angular average, write ρ = Σ_lm ρ_lm(r) Y_lm(μ) exp(imφ), adding conjugates. Each potential coefficient is

    Φ_lm = −4πG/(2l+1) [ r^(−l−1) ∫₀ʳ ρ_lm(s)s^(l+2) ds
                          + r^l ∫ᵣ^∞ ρ_lm(s)s^(1−l) ds ].

The force is minus the *three-dimensional* gradient, not the force of a spherical enclosed mass. There is one m=0 and a factor two for each complex positive-m coefficient in the real potential. The m=0 part changes the equilibrium; the instantaneous angular projection is a separate calculation at φ−φ_bar=π−29.4°. Reflection reverses the sine part of the induced force. A direct Newton integral in coordinates centred on the evaluation point checks potential and all force components independently of the spherical expansion.

**Selection rule and bound-state loading.** For H₀ψ₀=ε₀ψ₀, the co- and counter-rotating driving frequencies yield final energies ε₀±mκΩ. Φ(∞)=0 is the escape reference. The m=2 upper sideband is already above zero over the sampled speed range. For a *frozen single-particle* Hamiltonian, let S=||V₂ψ₀||²/||ψ₀||² for one complex harmonic. In the negative-energy spectrum ε₀≤E<0, Δ=E−ε₀ lies in [0,−ε₀). If the bar amplitude rises monotonically from zero to at most one at constant speed, integration by parts bounds each first-order transition amplitude by 2|matrix element|/|2κΩ∓Δ|. Integrating its square over the spectral measure gives

    p_co ≤ 4 S / (ε₀+2κΩ)²,     p_counter ≤ 4 S / (2κΩ)².

This also holds with Ω replaced by a positive lower speed for a monotonically *slowing* pattern and nondecreasing amplitude on the same frozen background: amplitude divided by either positive detuning is nondecreasing, so its endpoint plus total variation is at most twice the terminal ratio. No arbitrary switch-on time or random phase is needed. This extension is an inequality, not a reconstructed Galactic history. Nonmonotone frequency/amplitude, changes to the mean potential and collective feedback are not covered by this argument. The computed energy-weighted sum rule does not alone give a nontrivial continuum tail bound.

For any normalized state in the negative-energy spectral subspace, integration by parts with div[(x−y)/|x−y|]=2/|x−y| bounds the Newton kernel expectation by ||∇ψ||, uniformly in y. Nonpositive energy then gives ||∇ψ||≤2GM_tot/κ² for a nonnegative finite attracting mass distribution. Translated three-dimensional Hardy bounds the force modulus by 4GM_h||∇ψ||². Weighting by p_co+p_counter and including the ground mass removed gives the conservative bound in the drive tables. This is a dephased bound-sector estimate only, not a bound on first-order coherent interference, continuum or the self-consistently rearranged mean.

**Retarded collective response.** The coupled open (v₊), conjugate closed (v₋) and Poisson (w) equations and normalization are in OPEN-CHANNEL.md. This is a linear response in the nonaxisymmetric bar perturbation, not a test particle moving in the bar. The upper radial channel is outgoing in the Coulomb tail, the lower decays, the induced potential satisfies the vacuum multipole boundary; all radial amplitudes vanish at the regular centre. The open state is flux-normalized by its source, not a new square-normalizable equilibrium. Differential source work, the induced Newton potential, a free spherical-Bessel Green solution and an independent direct bar integral test different parts of the calculation.

**Which conserved quantity leaves?** With j(f)=Im(f†f′), the physical sideband number flux is J_N=j(v₊)−j(v₋): the physical lower-sideband coefficient is the conjugate of v₋. Angular momentum instead carries the sum. If U=V_m+δΦ_m, the second-order m=0 source in the Schrödinger equation is U*δψ₊+Uδψ₋* plus the real axisymmetric potential times ψ₀. Taking the imaginary part of its projection on the real ground state removes that real potential. In radial variables with F and B defined in OPEN-CHANNEL.md the local identities are

    J_N′ = 2 Im[(v₊−v₋)† (F+Bw)],
    [j(v₊)+j(v₋)+2j(w)]′ = 2 Im[(v₊+v₋)† F].

The factors of two multiplying j(w) and the source follow from the Schrödinger and Poisson coefficients. They are not adjustable. The first identity expresses loss from the mean projection; the second expresses external torque, after the internal gravitational stress is included. When self-gravity feedback is disabled, drop Bw and j(w) in those balances. Outside the source only the outgoing channel carries flux. The projected ground-number change is −J_out: verified separately by reconstructing both sidebands and the potential on the original saved radial/angular quadrature and projecting U*δψ₊+Uδψ₋* there. Local sideband flux is not by itself the net mass flow through every inner sphere: the second-order mean can also transport material.

Converting this number current to escaping mass per time gives dL/dt=mκ dM_out/dt and pattern power Ω dL/dt. Per unit escaping mass, mκΩ pays −ε₀ in unbinding and leaves ε₀+mκΩ at infinity. The latter relations are kinematics, not three independent numerical energy measurements. The comparison of integrated source torque with the outgoing flux is independent. This radiation is *matter wave flux*, not gravitational or electromagnetic radiation.

**Consequence.** An isolated, strictly periodic, finite-number branch differentiable about this ground state and with the tested first-order outgoing response cannot keep its bound occupation fixed at second order: its integrated continuity equation would require zero mean outward current, while the driven current is positive. At this order distinct outgoing azimuthal harmonics add nonnegative flux after angular averaging; they do not cancel one another. This statement is conditional on the assumed state, imposed pattern, open exterior and perturbation expansion. It does not forbid a transient, an incoming population, a nonlinear branch, or an explicitly evolving mean. A finite-time mean response needs its own retarded initial-value calculation.
