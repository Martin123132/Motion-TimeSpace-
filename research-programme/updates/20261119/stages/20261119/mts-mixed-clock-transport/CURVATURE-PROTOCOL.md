# Second source-mass coefficient without fitting a clock curve

At fixed occupied-minus-empty clock functional P[b,Q], let B denote the quiet ordinary number/charge profiles and d their finite one-percent increment. The event excess at common mass fraction f is P[f(B+d)]−P[fB]. Hence

    X(f)= f DP[0](d) + f² D²P[0](B+d/2,d) + O(f³).

The 1/2 comes from Taylor expansion, not a fitted coefficient. Includes the change of enclosed mass, charge energy and transport, induced scalar, changed ray path and reflection. Compute the Hessian contraction as a square-zero material variation along d on a complex background iε(B+d/2), retaining *finite* occupation as a separate axis. Its mixed clock component has imaginary part ε times the required second derivative (up to O(ε³)); the real part tends to the previous zero-mass first derivative. No negative-density physical star or adjustable mass coefficient. Use ε=1/64 and 1/128 on both 128/256 meshes. Richardson-extrapolate the second derivative, report separate step, mesh, ray and time-composition changes on the final predicted observable. Compare to the exposed 1e−3,1e−6 examples; do not call these fresh holdouts.

Then freeze predictions for the positive finite material event at f=0.01 before evolving that new real four-corner source. Include all three clocks and both frequency and timing readouts. Preserve the first-order failures and any miss of the second-order prediction under the original threshold. The likely large cloud mass is no basis for transplanting the timing to a galaxy.
