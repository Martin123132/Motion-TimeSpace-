# Finite-energy clock response, a source-energy ceiling, and an ordinary-matter drive

**Physical result.** Specifying the measured-on-a-slice positive scalar excitation energy does not by itself specify a clock waveform. The phase relative to the existing field matters; so does whether the source adds total mass or rearranges the field at fixed mass. More importantly, at low occupation not every proposed equal-mass scalar pulse can be prepared at all. Within the retained minimal Einstein–Maxwell–scalar subfamily we now have both a quantitative exclusion and a way to calculate a scalar response from a change in *ordinary matter*, without choosing its shape.

## Can the source actually make the proposed event?

On the saved dense quiet background, both signs of the new 1.70e−11 energy (mass units) exist at unchanged total mass. They require seeds +1.50642e−4 and −1.50324e−4 and slightly different compensating shells; equal energy is not identical absolute amplitude. The clock predictions were frozen before the finite targets. The same source norm is 4.2353e50 J at the retained 6.6713 pc length unit. Its mean excitation density inside the support is 2.678 GeV/cm³, with half the energy inside approximately 7.7 pc. This is a controlled excitation relative to an existing field, not a measured transient or energy arriving at Earth.

Now restore the earlier conditional scalar density of **0.1 GeV/cm³ at radius 6** with the same ordinary material geometry and scalar shape. The scalar amplitude is 0.0004639064 of the dense example. The regular positive-energy mass constraint itself excludes the proposed equal-mass event: its enclosed mass must **increase by more than 1.02e−11** at that energy. No choice of sign or compensating-shell amplitude in this family can fix that. With zero quiet scalar, the cross stress vanishes and the same argument excludes every nontrivial time-symmetric equal-mass pulse at unchanged ordinary sources.

For the dilute family and energies up to the original request, an independently evaluated nonlinear necessary bound is **E ≤ 2.88651e−14** for an equal-mass event. A direct full-constraint solution attains **2.88491e−14**. The interval between the two is about 0.055% of the lower value; the request is about 589 times too energetic. The upper result is an analytic inequality with numerical Gram integrals and explicit safety margins, not an exhaustive numerical sample or a formally certified real-arithmetic proof. The domain, integration-factor bounds and independently repeated quadratures are in SOURCE-ENERGY-LIMIT.md and NONLINEAR-CEILING.md. No upper bound on *all* MTS sources or on sources supplied with extra mass follows.

## What the clocks see at a feasible energy

We froze and tested **2.50e−16** (6.2284e45 J) on that dilute background, on the continuous through-zero branch. For the mass-matched positive event the far two-clock echo has fractional frequency response **−8.213e−18** at emission time 3.26 and largest proper delay in the sampled window **+1.66 ns**. At fixed number without mass compensation the positive-event echo delay reaches **+39.1 ns**, mostly a different source mass. Peaks need not be simultaneous or internal maxima of a longer event. These are predictions for clocks at radii 6 and 6.5, initially at rest, not a pulsar observation.

The mass-matched far link is silent ahead of the disturbance to the numerical mass-matching precision. A mass-only exterior geodesic calculation accounts for the early uncompensated signal but cannot account for the later matched one. Reversing the sign changes the waveform, but not by simple negation: on the dilute mass-matched echo frequency, the ratio of the peak of the sign-even part to the peak of the sign-odd part is **0.393** (dense example 0.0473). The even part comes from pulse self-stress and preparation as well as nonlinear geometry. For example, omitting all pulse self-stress misses 74% of the dense matched positive event's outer one-way frequency waveform in the leading calculation. This is a whole-wave peak-normalized difference, not 74% of gravity in a galaxy.

- At the dense energy, naive separate subtraction passed 90/96 independent combined waveform budgets at 128/256; the algebraically paired real evolution passed **94/96**. At the independently frozen dilute energy, leading stress on the ordinary metric passed **94/96** against nonlinear finite targets. All the actual positive and negative event waveforms passed in both paired comparisons. These are 48 signed-event plus 48 parity checks per environment, not 96 independent observations.
- The same two derived channels missed in each environment on those grids: the outer matched sign-even one-way frequency and delay. The largest budget terms were spatial refinement, not disagreement with the target at fixed resolution. At 256→384 cells per length, all four separately frozen follow-up comparisons passed. The relative budgets for frequency and delay were respectively 1.4391e−6 and 4.3876e−6 (dense), and 1.4111e−6 and 4.3710e−6 (dilute), below the unchanged 2e−5. The 384 runs check the previously failing channels only; they do not constitute a repeat of the entire 96-channel matrix at 384. See refined-even-budgets.csv; the original failures remain in the first ledgers.
- The real pairs evolve the midpoint and half-difference using **real** endpoint arithmetic (the difference unit squares to +1). This is distinct from the predictor's complex directional derivatives. The tiny flight-time difference is evaluated along the perturbed ray, including reflection. Two exact time-dependent metric controls at shifts +1e−10 and −1e−18 pass all 16 ray comparisons against 60-digit direct reference clocks. Previously exposed finite events gave 125/128 same-mesh model comparisons inside tolerance, not a new holdout. No observation was fitted or scored.
- The “dilute” density is one independently specified normalization, not a measured occupation or phase at a known source. A GR model furnished with the identical scalar stress would share the common-metric clock signal. The field's preparation and energy supply are therefore central to distinguishing a physical MTS proposal from an arbitrary prescribed stress.

## Can ordinary matter generate the field disturbance instead?

Yes, conditionally: gravitationally perturbing an **already occupied** massive scalar produces a calculable response even with zero initial scalar perturbation. Starting with the parent's compensated material-density seed, the central scalar response per unit seed starts as **−1.7986574e−6 τ⁴** after dividing the field by √n. The coefficient is derived from the constrained Klein–Gordon and Einstein equations, not a time-law fit. All **24** archived early-time checks pass (worst relative discrepancy 1.80e−6). The neutral and charged controls share this coefficient; their later histories need not be identical.

The sourced propagation test goes further. Given only the initial ordinary-matter change, the ordinary metric and its variation drive the scalar with zero inserted wave. On both charge backgrounds the aperture energy is independently accounted for by gravitational driving, volume work and flux. On the fine mesh the worst relative work residual through τ=0.8 is below 1e−7. After freezing predictions, full coupled evolutions passed all 12 field and all 12 aperture-energy comparisons (three material wavelengths, two charge choices, two grids). The worst peak-normalized discrepancy was 1.381e−7 for the field and 3.097e−7 for energy, against the unchanged 2e−5 threshold; see material-driven-full-160.csv and material-driven-full-320.csv. This is the energy norm of the induced perturbation in the stated window, not a net ADM energy or radiation at infinity.

The 1% normalization is a stated source choice in linear response, not a new fitted coupling or a finite-amplitude accuracy guarantee. The templates span only a small part of the induced state **for these inputs and this epoch**. It would be a mistake to assign one of those templates to an observed baryon disturbance and call the source derived. The next calculation should send the **actual driven field and its stress** to the clocks, with a matched ordinary-matter-only control that subtracts the direct gravitational signal of the material. Ultimately the ordinary motion and background occupation need independent observational inputs.
