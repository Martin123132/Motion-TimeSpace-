# Frequency transfer: what the two codes measured

In polar-areal coordinates let f=a⁻² and B=α/a. For an outward/inward geometric-optics ray σ=±1, dr/dt=σB, d ln|k_r|/dt=−σ B_r (canonical photon Hamiltonian σ B k_r). The frequency in a freely falling clock is proportional to |k_r| Cσ with Cσ=√f exp[−σ asinh(√f p_r)] from −k·u. Thus log transfer = log Cσ at reception minus emission minus the line integral of σB_r dt. The observer motion is included at both ends. Parameterising the ray by fractional progress between moving endpoints keeps its termination at the actual receiver rather than its launch coordinate. Both polar grids and all five preparations were finished and the full emitted-time predictions fingerprinted before opening the independent receiver stage.

In synchronous coordinates the same clocks remain at fixed x. If l is increasing coordinate distance along the link, dt/dl=a(t,x); log frequency evolves as d ln ν/dl=−a_t=Kxx/a. A separate pulse-separation solution gives J=∂t/∂τ_emit, J'=a_t J, J(0)=1, so ν_rec/ν_emit=1/J. Integrating a−1 directly yields small travel-time excesses without subtracting large nearly equal arrival timestamps. An immediately reflected phase has the product of the two frequency ratios and the sum of their travel times, with leg two evaluated at the actual reflection event. The outer oscillator need not be read or synchronised. Pulse phase need not represent any particular atomic species. Plasma, changing transmitter rest frequencies, finite-wavelength scattering, noise and actual sky initial data have not been inserted.

**Why the frequency changes.** Differentiating the line integral with respect to proper emission time gives d ln G/dτ_emit = −∫ a_tt J dl. In the reduced spherical Einstein equations the radial tidal component is E=−a_tt/a. Using the metric equations gives E=R_nn−2m/R³−2p_total. Substituting the scalar, electric and dust stresses cancels the explicit scalar potential and dust velocity factors:

    E = −2M/R³ + 3Q²/R⁴ + ρ_dust,rest + Σ_i [Π_i² − (∂proper h_i)²],
    M = m + Q²/(2R),         d ln G/dτ_emit = ∫ E a J dl.

For the echo, the second leg's drift carries the first leg's arrival Jacobian. Event-minus-quiet must be taken after integrating each actual ray. Removing the scalar term is a diagnostic, not another solution of the field equations. The mass still depends on all sources. The radial combination differs from the pressure measured previously via transverse free fall. Together they give two independent ways to test how the same scalar stress sources the metric without introducing a clock-specific force.

**Ordinary gravity check.** With only charged dust inside the link, f(R)=1−2M/R+Q²/R². For a geodesic launched at rest E_i=√f(R_i0), dR/dτ=v and dv/dτ=−M/R²+Q²/R³. Each radial photon has constant t−σr*, dr*/dr=1/f. Along a clock d(t−σr*)/dτ=1/(E_i+σv). Solve for emission and arrival at equal retarded/advanced time; the exact ratio is (E_emit+σv_emit)/(E_receive+σv_receive). This reference uses M and Q from the initial constraint, not either frequency waveform. Independent tests include flat space under a nontrivial time-coordinate change and the expanding a(t)=exp(Ht) metric, where the drift is genuinely nonzero in a known amount.

**Reading the CSVs.** Pulse time is in the retained code units. Packet fields log_ratio and echo_log_ratio contain natural logarithms. For the quantities carrying those names in budgets.csv, observables.csv and frequency-history.csv the reported numbers have been converted to frequency ratios minus unity, or the *difference of those ratios* across launches; they are not the difference divided by the already tiny background shift. Values of delay mean excess over the flat length 0.5 or 1, or the event-minus-quiet difference of those excesses. Tidal drifts remain derivatives of the logarithm. A dimensional conversion based on an illustrative scalar mass is not a source size/density measurement.
