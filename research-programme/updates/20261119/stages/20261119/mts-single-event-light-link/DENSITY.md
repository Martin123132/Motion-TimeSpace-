# A deliberate change of physical density, not a timing-data fit

A unit conversion exposed a problem with quoting the large experimental pulse shift on the sky. At the retained illustrative pole m_h c²=9.585761787277403e−25 eV the code length unit is 6.6713 pc. The launch energy density at radius 6 converts as e_code c⁴/(4πG L²) to about 464,664 GeV/cm³. The earlier pulsar example used 0.1 GeV/cm³. This is not a small calibration difference. Nor does that earlier number measure either clock's local occupation.

We therefore change the *field*, not the baryons or G. Write H for the rescaled scalar and z for the squared amplitude. Hold D, Q and the ordinary metric source prescription fixed. Use h=√z(H₀+c g) and P=√z εg for the single event. With ordinary charge-corrected mass U₀ and δ=(U−U₀)/z, f=f₀−2zδ/r, the properly divided launch constraint is regular even at z=0:

    δ' = −2r D δ/(√f+√f₀) + r² [ f (H'²+P₀²) + H² ]/2.

At each real density parameter impose equality of the event and quiet exterior masses, not equality of either clock record. Choose the branch connected to the earlier compact coefficient. Fix z* by the *quiet* launch scalar energy at radius 6 using the actual metric at that z. The event has the same exterior density. The leading field propagates on the charged ordinary background; its metric response is order z and is not the original nonlinear pulse divided by a density ratio.

To avoid losing the low-density signal in subtraction, use the analytic metric response to imaginary variations of z in the same nonlinear equations (choose the consistently continued scalar square root; the metric depends on z). Store the real baseline and Im(metric)/Im(z) before doing any clock subtraction. Two imaginary steps and two spatial grids check the derivative at zero. Also compute the derivative at z*/2 and z* with imaginary step z*, analytically following the mass-matched root; integrating from 0 to z* uses Simpson versus midpoint. The equality of event and quiet at z=0 supplies the integration constant. The ray's arrival tangent obeys D'=δa+a_t D; the transported log-frequency tangent obeys I'=δ(Kxx/a)+∂t(Kxx/a)D. An echo adds the second leg's time-shift term. Compare to the derivative of total arrival time. This is a numerical finite-density response via parameter integration, not an all-orders analytic enclosure. Direct real quarter- and original full-density waveforms are independent checks of how well scaling alone would work. Errors common to the numerical scheme are not ruled out by agreement.

Converting excess travel time to seconds is conditional on the chosen pole as well as the density and launch shape. An actual outward path here spans only about 3.34 pc between freely falling receivers and the simulated emission history spans decades. This is neither the line of sight to a particular pulsar nor an astronomical prediction of the source's abundance, orbit, phase or location. No pulsar timing residuals were opened or scored.
