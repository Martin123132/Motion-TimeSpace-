# From the retained action to stellar deflection

**Which branch.** Checkpoint 5211, “Selected-Trajectory Exact GR-Maxwell Consistent Truncation”, gives the restricted two-derivative action \(M_R^2(R-2\Lambda_{\rm cal})/2-F^2/4+L_{\rm visible}\), with matter and Maxwell seeing the same metric; its silent state is a consistent restriction, not an attractor theorem. Separately, MTS-HEAVY-BATH-COUNTERFLOW-AND-GALACTIC-STRESS.md retains minimally coupled, quadratic real scalars with Hilbert stress, giving \(\nabla^2\Phi=4\pi G_N(\rho_b+\rho_h)\) in the nonrelativistic branch. We do **not** declare that a populated wave is the exact zero-field solution of 5211. The smooth field contributes to the shared large-scale potential. At the scale of an isolated compact foreground star, subtract that smooth field and use the leading, weak, slowly moving, negligible-anisotropic-stress point source. This is an expansion in local gravitational potential, slow motion and external tides; it does not assert exact no-slip everywhere in MTS.

For \(ds^2=-(1+2\Phi/c^2)c^2dt^2+(1-2\Psi/c^2)d\mathbf{x}^2\), the Maxwell principal symbol gives the metric null cone. The optical path is \(\int [1-(\Phi+\Psi)/c^2]\,dl\). At linear order the Einstein scalar constraint gives Poisson for \(\Psi\); the trace-free spatial constraint sets the trace-free Hessian of \(\Phi-\Psi\) to zero for the isolated nonrelativistic point source. Matching to the decaying exterior removes the homogeneous difference. Thus its monopole has \(\Phi=\Psi=-G_N M/r\). A light ray with unperturbed impact b bends by magnitude \(\int |\nabla_\perp(\Phi+\Psi)|\,dl/c^2=4G_N M/(c^2 b)\); a slow tracer measures \(-\nabla\Phi\), not twice that. For a scale-independent exterior slip \(\Psi=\gamma\Phi\) this would multiply the deflection by \((1+\gamma)/2\), **not** introduce an adjustable MTS lensing amplitude here. Nonlinear/internal stellar binding renormalizes the exterior gravitational mass; the population construction uses stellar gravitational masses as its approximation. Broad halo shear, plasma, close binaries, finite-source size and multiple-deflector corrections are not implied by this point-lens calculation. A constant or uniformly accelerating foreground is not a newly counted stellar microlens.

Use the action and exact factors rather than the inconsistent linear summaries. Other operators and downstream bounds have not all been reassessed.

Independent calculation: in a local Ricci-flat frame the electric Weyl eigenvalues are proportional to (2,−1,−1), sign convention immaterial for magnitudes. For a tangential wave and radial screen vector, the time and space contractions give \(2\omega^2+p^2\) times \(G_N M/(c^2r^3)\); the orthogonal screen gives \(-\omega^2-2p^2\). This yields the two rational factors directly. Along a straight ray in the z direction the *on-shell* screen eigenvalues have magnitudes \(3(G_NM/c^2)b^2/(b^2+z^2)^{5/2}\) after division by \(\omega^2\). Each optical index shift has magnitude four times that quantity times \(|c_\gamma|\). Integrating the signed shift gives \(\pm16c_\gamma(G_NM/c^2)/b^2\) in optical length, and differentiating gives
\[
  |\delta\widehat\alpha|=32|c_\gamma|(G_NM/c^2)/b^3,\qquad
  |\delta\widehat\alpha/\widehat\alpha_{\rm GR}|=8|c_\gamma|/b^2.
\]
This is the mixed first order in curvature coupling and stellar gravity, for impact parameters exterior to the star; the unpolarized leading split has opposite signs. Direct integration of the two effective Schwarzschild ray equations at **fixed asymptotic impact parameter**, varying the turning radius, recovers 8.000053 at \(G_NM/(bc^2)=10^{-6}\). The weak-deflection literature independently treats this observable [Cao & Xie, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1140/epjc/s10052-018-5684-5); this is a consistency repair, not a new prediction of birefringence.
