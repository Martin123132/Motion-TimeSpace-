# A joint force and light response at fixed gravitational coupling

See LIGHT-COUPLING.md for the parent action, its assumptions and the correction to 4931. The equations below are local weak-field results; the adopted stellar source and populations are astronomical inputs, not all derived from the action.

## Optical-depth kernel and source-distance identifiability

In the Galactic thin-lens limit \(R_E^2=4G_N M D(1-D/s)/c^2\) for \(0<D<s\). Summing \(\pi R_E^2\) over stars per unit volume cancels the individual lens mass in favor of compact density. For a normalized *monitored source* distribution \(p(s)\) along a sightline, ideal point-source, point-lens depth is
\[
 \bar\tau=C\int_0^\infty D\,\rho_{\rm compact}(D)W(D)\,dD,\quad
 W(D)=\int_D^\infty p(s)(1-D/s)\,ds,\quad C=4\pi G_N/c^2.
\]
For a per-radius density multiplier, restrict the integrand to its cylinder along both near and far portions of the ray; the result is the derivative, with no fit. Angular averages are ratios of weighted sums. The fraction of total depth is a ratio of two averaged depths, **not** the average of the subfield fractions. The all-source angular count weights are measured; the all-source distances are not provided by those counts. The finite-duration target requires mass-resolved density and the appropriate transverse-velocity probability \(P(v_\perp>R_E/300{\rm\,d})\) for an upper timescale cut. The velocity factor in event rate cancels the duration in *unrestricted* optical depth, not this population selection. Published efficiency-corrected depth should not simply be multiplied by raw detection efficiency again.

For a compact foreground interval entirely in front of all sources, \(\tau_{\rm fg}(s)=C(I_1-I_2/s)\) with \(I_k=\int D^k\rho_{\rm fg}\,dD\). Arbitrarily many such background-source depths can determine at most **two** moments along that ray. As a constructive positive counterexample use 0.15-kpc-wide slabs centered at 0.70, 2.50 and 5.05 kpc along BLG505.01. A unit solar mass/pc² LOS column in the middle slab (wholly in the target annulus) gives exactly the same depth at every source past the third slab as positive columns 2.094396 and 0.204737 in the outer slabs (both outside the target annulus). They agree at all four far controls, disagree at the three foreground controls. The two models need not have the same total column; total column is not one of these two measured moments. This is a one-ray non-identifiability example, not a globally fitted disk. For *continuously varying* source distances inside the lens distribution, \([s\tau(s)]''=Cs\rho(s)\); differentiating noisy binned data twice is not an estimator used here. External source or lens distance information, angular structure and stellar dynamics can break this degeneracy.

## Actual geometry and red-clump distance controls

Include only the preselected [Mróz et al. fields](https://www.astrouw.edu.pl/ogle/ogle4/microlensing_maps/) and the positive-count CCDs in their tabulated footprints. Transform each LOS using \(x=R_0-D\cos b\cos l,\; y=D\cos b\sin l,\; z=z_\odot+D\sin b\). Test the CCD centre, not every pixel in it. Split the integral at both roots of each cylindrical stellar knot, each spherical central knot, the tangent point, midplane and discrete source plane. For the central bar alternative use the unshifted E3 profile from [Cao et al., Eqs. 3–5 and Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/1303.6430). In scaled principal axes the generalized radius is \(t=[(X^2+Y^2)^2+Z^4]^{1/4}\) and the shape is \(K_0(t)\). The volume inside t is \(V_1t^3,\;V_1=\pi abc\,B(1/4,3/2)/2\); the mass integral per unit density is \(3V_1\int t^2 K_0(t)\,dt=3\pi V_1/2\). Independently integrate in ordinary spherical polar coordinates in scaled axes to check. Normalize each component to the *removed* central mass. This redistributes rather than adds stars. It does not preserve the old projected central profile, nor is the red-clump shape a measured stellar-remnant distribution. The separate compact background in the gravity solver is not added as a second population of ordinary stellar lenses.

Match an OGLE-IV CCD centre to a unique reliable OGLE-III photometric rectangle from [Nataf et al. table 4](https://ftp.astrouw.edu.pl/ogle/ogle3/ext/blg/). The latter RA bounds are in **hours**, the former RA in degrees. Rows with no unique match or nonpositive published intrinsic variance are retained as missing for this test. A Gaussian in modulus implies \(\ln s\sim N(m,\sigma^2)\) for s in kpc; the window is \(\Phi((m-\ln D)/\sigma)-D e^{-m+\sigma^2/2}\Phi((m-\sigma^2-\ln D)/\sigma)\). Direct Gaussian quadrature controls use the defining distance average. A symmetric two-point distribution at the same modulus mean ±standard deviation is a shape sensitivity, not two observed populations. Neither is the all-magnitude OGLE-IV source selection. Field extinction is implicit in the corrected red-clump moments and observed angular counts, **not** a general dust-versus-distance selection for the faint sample.

## Bounding the lenses without inventing a distance distribution

For the annular living-disk response only, fix the reported red-clump mean \(m_\mu\) and variance \(v_\mu\). We allow **every** distribution on real distance modulus with those moments. If global quadratic polynomials \(q_-\le f(\mu)\le q_+\) bound the mono-distance depth, their expectations are \(q_0+v_\mu q_2\) in centered coordinates. This produces valid conservative limits, not posterior credible intervals or an assumption of lognormal distance.

Construct bounds on f by dividing the nonnegative annular lens mass kernel into short slabs and computing its zeroth/first distance moments under \(CD\rho\,dD\). At fixed s the function \((1-D/s)_+\) is convex in lens distance. Evaluating at each slab centroid therefore gives a lower bound, and the endpoint chord with the same centroid an upper bound. The lens moments use the retained quadrature, not exact real-number enclosures. Across each modulus interval f is nondecreasing: minorize its left lower bound and majorize its right upper bound. A quadratic's three Bernstein coefficients suffice to control it across that interval. Include outward slope and curvature and the zero/infinite-source limits beyond the numerical support grid, so the resulting polynomial bound is **not** restricted to sources on a modulus grid. The LP sees only reported distance moments and the already computed lens kernel. Feasible discrete source distributions give inner bounds on the possible extrema. An independent denser modulus and finer lens-slab calculation checks the continuous quadratic minima and both tails. The separate rectangular ±one printed-error experiment varies the mean and variance in the expectation of the already valid polynomials, using correct curvature signs. It is a sensitivity rectangle, not a joint confidence interval or a bound on photometric systematics/source-class mismatch.

## Response of slow matter to precisely the same annulus

Perturb only the existing **living disk** density in \(4\le R\le7\) by the fractional amplitude a; no new coupling. Direct ring integration includes the signed three-dimensional vertical force. The potential inserted into the stationary Schrödinger–Poisson equation is computed separately using a spherical multipole expansion with the angular integration split at the cylindrical edges and radial knots; its monopole equals the independently integrated annular mass. Around the saved matched MTS state u, at fixed wave norm solve the projected stationary Hessian equation \(L_+\delta u=-\Pi(u\,\delta\Phi_\star)\). Independently obtain \(u_N=\partial_{\ln N}u\). Preserving the measured outer speed gives
\[
 \partial_a\ln N=-\frac{\delta g_{\star,R}+\delta g_{h,R}[\delta u]}
                              {\delta g_{h,R}[u_N]}
\]
at the anchor; add \(u_N\partial_a\ln N\) to the wave tangent for the SEGUE forces. At fixed halo shape the NFW amplitude instead has \(\partial_a\ln A=-\delta g_{\star,R}/g_{{\rm NFW},R}\); fixed-amplitude controls have no NFW response. “Fixed” here holds the **already matched reference** mass/amplitude fixed, rather than swapping to a new baseline. The perturbation changes total ordinary mass; it is distinct from the previous mass-preserving height test. The associated change of ideal compact-lens depth is linear in a for both models; it depends on the same compact mass, not its induced smooth gravitational response at galactic distances. Compare the paired light/force tangent, not the ratio of two unrelated goodness-of-fit statistics. No nonlinear extrapolation to the observed acceleration deficit is licensed.
