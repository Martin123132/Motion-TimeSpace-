# From motion of ordinary mass to an internal wave response

Branch and normalization are inherited without refitting from ../mts-conserved-breathing-mode-20261119/DERIVATION.md. All radii s and times τ here use the same dimensionless Schrödinger–Poisson parameters, in which mass unit is KG/S, time unit SEC S², speed unit c/S and energy unit KG c²/S³. S labels the initial isolated normalization. The nonrelativistic number is held fixed during each evolution. These are response calculations about an *already occupied* spherical ground state.

## Forcing and a bound which does not need a prescribed time curve

Let (p,q) be a radial mode with J=∫s²pq ds and Ω its frequency. Let d(s)=2s⁻²∫₀ˢy²u(y)p(y)dy. For a time-dependent change δM_b(s,τ) of ordinary mass inside s, write the wave coordinate as C and its conjugate velocity as B=Ċ/Ω. Reciprocity in the Poisson action gives

    C̈ + Ω²C = Ω² C_eq(τ),   C_eq = (2ΩJ)⁻¹ ∫ d(s) δM_b(s,τ) ds.
    Z = B + i(C−C_eq),      Ż−iΩZ = −i Ċ_eq.

The source follows from the *enclosed mass change*, not a tunable strength. A uniform potential change has no effect; the surface term vanishes for this isolated number-preserving mode. For a spherical background the angular average of any external mass distribution gives its linear monopole response. Higher multipoles are not included in that statement.

Starting in equilibrium, |Z| ≤ [2Ω|J|]⁻¹ ∫ |d(s)| TVτ[δM_b(s,τ)] ds by temporal integration and the triangle inequality. For at most one outward passage of a total mass m, every radius has variation ≤m; for one passage and return it is ≤2m. Repeated passages, a pre-existing oscillation, changing cloud masses and unsurveyed material must be accounted for separately. At the centre d(s)=(2/3)u(0)p(0)s+O(s³), giving quadratic suppression in the size of a compact source. This is a bound on the *leading amplitude increment*, not an exclusion extrapolated to strongly nonlinear masses. one-excursion-bound.csv lists both the normalization and the radius interval.

## Realizing the frozen transport

The original two shells are disjoint. Their time-dependent enclosed mass is well defined, but their density alone vanishes where their connecting current is nonzero. Add the same positive carrier of total mass 4μ on [0.4,4.1] to both preparations. At each radius the total radial mass density is μ[(1−h)H′out+hH′in+4H′carrier] and the radial mass current times area is −μ ḣ(Hin−Hout). The carrier removes the empty-gap problem without changing δM_b. The density is nonnegative, the material has finite radial speed, and the continuity equation holds analytically; where the denominator vanishes, the current is zero. The instantaneous velocity is that of the combined fluid, not a claim that the background particles must stay still. At nominal normalization the maximum sampled speeds are 32.6, 16.3, 8.16 km/s for the three pulse durations. An external agent must supply the mechanical stress for this motion. We calculate gravitational work on the wave, not the total motor energy.

## Two full-field integrations and an independent spectral solution

For each positive μ, a nodeless initial equilibrium is solved at fixed wave number with the static donor and carrier in the potential. One evolution advances χ=sψ with a sine radial kinetic operator, isolated Poisson potential and fourth-order split steps. A matched static arm removes common phase/drift; coordinates are taken after projecting out the uniform phase. Endpoint and quarter-period-coast fields are retained. Three positive masses are used; with v(m)=coordinate(m)/m the zero-mass estimate [8v(m/4)−6v(m/2)+v(m)]/3 cancels the first two finite-mass corrections. Use a one-period nonzero amplitude scale for the two-period fundamental null.

A second nonlinear calculation solves the equilibrium of the *discrete* sine-Galerkin Hamiltonian, including its fixed-number constraint, then advances the wave difference ζ in the donor's rotating frame. The source density perturbation is δn=2χ₀ Reζ+|ζ|² in radial coordinates (ds measure). Its source potential δΦ is recomputed at every integrator stage. With a stationary control the endpoint grand-canonical energy change is exactly ⟨ζ,(H₀−E₀)ζ⟩+½∫δn δΦ ds in this discretization; ΔN is measured separately. For a return to the donor, W=∫dτ ḣ∫ds δn ΔU. The static source term integrates to zero, not discarded work. Independently in the direct solver this equals −μ∫dτ ḣ∫ds (Hin−Hout)δΦ′, apart from numerical integration errors, by source continuity and Poisson reciprocity. The second solver integrates W with its own time stages and advances the *nonlinear* field, not a prescribed response. Convergence of small net work is assessed on its own scale, not on the gross work that returned to the source.

In the third, independent (linear) solution, restrict the static radial operator H₀−E₀=L to the complement of its phase zero mode. If K is the induced Poisson operator and a the real radial perturbation, set y=L⁻¹ᐟ²a. The positive oscillators diagonalize D=L¹ᐟ²(L+K)L¹ᐟ². A normalized mode e_j is driven by g_j=⟨e_j,L¹ᐟ²s uΔU⟩, and the *whole* late wave energy in it is E_j=g_j² |∫₀ᵀ h(t) exp(−iΩ_j t)dt|² in our complex-wave normalization. Summing these nonnegative energies makes a stringent independent check on the net work obtained after a large cancellation in the time-domain calculation. This also distinguishes a quiet fundamental from no energy transfer at all. Varying the far boundary changes the discretized near-threshold/continuum partition. It is not an escaped-mass measurement.
