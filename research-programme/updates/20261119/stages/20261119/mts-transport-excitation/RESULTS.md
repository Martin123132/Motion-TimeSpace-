# Moving the same mass does not always excite the same motion

**The frozen prediction survived a full wave evolution.** No oscillation was inserted. Ordinary mass was moved inward and back, while an identical untransported donor was evolved as the control. At first order in moved mass the lowest spherical mode rings after a half-period pulse (field amplitude 0.00027505), rings in the other quadrature after a one-period pulse (0.00032404), and cancels after a two-period pulse. We measured both signed quadratures after release and again a quarter-period later. All twelve refined budgets (two modes × three histories × two times) pass the inherited 2×10⁻⁵ criterion. The first two-mass budget had four overtone misses; they are still recorded. The refined comparison uses three positive mass levels and an independent full-spectrum solution, not a relaxed threshold.

**A null in the fundamental is not a null in gravity.** The two-period pulse leaves about **82% of its linear wave energy in the second radial mode** (81.84% at the original outer boundary, 82.01% at the larger boundaries). The above-threshold box fraction varies from about 5.6% to 6.3%; it is not an escape prediction. In the actual finite runs the fundamental residual amplitudes were 5.606×10⁻⁷, 1.404×10⁻⁷, and 3.514×10⁻⁸ as the moved mass was successively halved. That is quadratic leakage, not an exact finite-amplitude zero. At nominal mass the projected fundamental accounts for about 1.2% of the small two-period net work, compared with about 99.6% of the one-period work.

**Energy ledger.** The second full nonlinear solver conserves the untransported reference by construction and calculates the work and field energy independently. Work delivered for half, one and two periods at the illustrative scale is 9.29×10⁴³, 1.06×10⁴⁴ and 2.72×10⁴⁰ joules respectively. The last case ends within 1.1 parts per million of the integrated work at full mass on the finest run; the three-mass tangent agrees with the independently summed spectrum to about 10 ppm at the original boundary. The smallest budget was **not** resolved by ordinary direct subtraction: simply refining the first solver left a 2.2×10⁻⁴ relative energy mismatch at nominal mass. Deriving the difference equation and checking the work at its own scale mattered. The energy transfer is specific to the wave, not the full cost of transporting the ordinary mass.

**How much mass must actually move?** We derived a timing-independent bound from the variation of the ordinary mass inside each radius. With the retained [Cepheid-normalized upper-envelope state](../mts-halo-outer-mass-20261119/RESULTS.md), attaining a leading field amplitude of 0.001 in a single out-and-back event confined to 2–20 kpc requires **at least 4.76×10⁷ solar masses** to move. That is a necessary, not sufficient, condition. Confining the excursion to the inner 1 kpc makes the formal linear requirement 5.73×10⁹ solar masses; the compact-source suppression, not an extrapolation into the strongly perturbed regime, is the useful point. This is a response about a pre-existing ground state; it cannot bound an initially populated mode or unlimited repeated forcing.

**Facing an actual source.** [105 modeled neutral-hydrogen clouds in the Galactic wind](https://vizier.cfa.harvard.edu/viz-bin/VizieR-3?-source=J%2FApJ%2F855%2F33) give a one-way timing-independent upper amplitude of 2.41×10⁻⁷ for their specified fixed masses and centroid paths. The age-dependent forced solution distinguishes the wave's present displacement from its lag behind a shifting equilibrium. One further detection lacks a modeled path. See CLOUDS.md for the source assumptions, cross-checks, missing material and catalogue discrepancies. This is not a measurement that the halo oscillates.

**Next: tidal rather than uniform forcing.** In free space a spatially constant potential changes phase only. An imposed potential g(t)·r+c(t) translates the self-gravitating system: take ψ=e^[i(Ẋ·r+β)]ψ₀(r−X,t), Ẍ=−g, β̇=−|Ẋ|²/2−c. Substitution removes the entire uniform force from the internal equations, provided the self-potential is translated too. A physical exterior perturber leaves a trace-free tidal Hessian, an ℓ=2 source, not a tunable monopole coupler. Derive the five quadrupole responses from the same Poisson action, with origin regularity and exterior condition V′+3V/R=0; use a freely falling uniform-field null. Compare the isolated frequency with [Chan, Sibiryakov & Xue, Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1007/JHEP08(2023)045) before applying any observed satellite trajectory. Use the finite-extent overlap, or explicitly exclude the wave tail outside the tide expansion. This is the route to separate measurable tides from apparent acceleration without changing the local force law.
