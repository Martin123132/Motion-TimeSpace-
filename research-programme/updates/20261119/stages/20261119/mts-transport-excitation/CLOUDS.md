# An observation-anchored illustration: the Milky Way nuclear H I clouds

**Inputs.** [Di Teodoro et al., 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.02152) report a 21-cm sample and a constant-velocity biconical wind model. We acquired [the authors' tables via VizieR J/ApJ/855/33](https://vizier.cfa.harvard.edu/viz-bin/VizieR-3?-source=J%2FApJ%2F855%2F33), with [CDS column definitions](https://cdsarc.cds.unistra.fr/viz-bin/ReadMe/J/ApJ/855/33?format=html&tex=true). The Harvard mirror served the 22,389-byte file after the Strasbourg file host timed out. Exact request and acquisition time are in the cached header; SHA-256 f734545b19facd2a7e0fb08d6f975f7ebf078cb63a5ff1e6338dc9205033d1f2. No cubes or rotation data were downloaded. Replay is offline.

Of 106 observed clouds, 105 have a wind-model radius R, heliocentric distance, travel time and corresponding H I mass. G3.5+8.6+366 has the catalogue's no-model flag and no trajectory; its unconstrained contribution is *not* zero. The modeled sample sums to 1.6012 million solar masses of H I. Use logMHI at the inferred distance, not logMHI8.2, which assumes the same 8.2-kpc distance for everyone. The reported R ranges from 0.57 to 2.69 kpc; t=r/v is a wind-model estimate, not a direct proper-motion age. Lifetime estimates, masses and distances are not independent. No inclination adjustment, helium multiplier, mass-completeness correction or unseen gas phase added.

**What we calculate.** Give each modeled cloud its listed, fixed mass, initially at a compact launch radius, then move its centroid monotonically out along the catalogue's constant-speed history until now. Linear monopole gravity only uses the enclosed ordinary mass, so the angles drop out for this channel even though the real wind is biconical. For a cloud of mass m, equilibrium shift now is −m[V_mode(s_now)−V_mode(s_launch)]/(2ΩJ) since V_mode′=d. The complex lag about instantaneous equilibrium is i m/(2ΩJ) times ∫d(s) exp[iΩ(time remaining from crossing s to now)]ds for that path. We add the *signed complex* lags, not their magnitudes. Integration of the forced ODE through all the asynchronous launches gives the same signed present C, B and equilibrium shift; all 12 independent comparisons pass, including the much smaller present C on its own scale.

At the retained central Cepheid upper-envelope normalization the mode period is 503.63 Myr versus the model's 1.69–7.98 Myr flights. Launching centrally, the equilibrium shift is −2.409×10⁻⁷, but the **present** coordinate is only −6.707×10⁻¹¹ and B=−4.453×10⁻⁹. If the mass distributions froze now the lag would ring with linear amplitude 2.408×10⁻⁷; we do not assert the clouds have stopped. The trajectory-independent single-passage bound for these same masses and endpoints is 2.409×10⁻⁷, about **0.0241% of the earlier 0.001 illustrative excitation**. At the earlier diffuse normalization the corresponding bound is 1.293×10⁻⁷. These are dimensionless field coordinates, not measured oscillations. The numbers are conditional on the wave normalization and on the cloud inventory and origin. Launch at 0.1 kpc is a separate finite-source check.

**Catalogue consistency.** Four modeled rows differ by ≥0.014 kpc in tabulated R versus the norm of tabulated Cartesian coordinates. For G6.8+5.6-149 the 8.2-kpc H I mass moved to the tabulated solar distance differs from its listed mass by 0.116 dex. No cause is assumed, and no catalogue value is overwritten. Rerunning *all* rows with Cartesian radii and consistently rescaled flight times, or separately with flux-rescaled masses, gives ≈2.414×10⁻⁷ or ≈2.405×10⁻⁷ for the same central-launch bound. Those variants are sensitivity checks, not a covariance model. An extended cloud is represented by its centroid; the bound is for that prescription. Unobserved hot and molecular gas, mass exchange, recurrent activity and other components are not bounded. In particular the calculation does not determine initial galactic wave occupation, detect the wave, or constitute an independent rotation-curve fit.
