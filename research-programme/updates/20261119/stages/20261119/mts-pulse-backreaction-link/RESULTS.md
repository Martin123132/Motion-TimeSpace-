## What was predicted, rather than reconstructed

Each link consists of two neutral freely falling clocks separated initially by 0.5 code length. We predict the full frequency-ratio and travel-time responses for 171 equally spaced proper emission times from 0 through 3.4; the fields evolve to 4.5. “Peak” below means maximum magnitude in this sampled window and preserves its sign. Several outer quantities are still evolving at the last emission. Reported values are derivatives per unit dimensionless scalar seed, not amplitudes of an observed event.

- The first forecast covered two charge backgrounds, two mass preparations and two links. Against fresh fully coupled evolutions **16/16 inner** waveform budgets passed and **0/16 outer** passed at the specified meshes. All failures remain in `leading-budget.csv`.
- The follow-up kept η=0.8, the fixed-mass preparation, the pulse, time window and tolerance fixed. Predictions for **all three** links were frozen before the refined tests. The middle link, 4.0–4.5, was new: its full waveform was not used to identify the discrepancy.

The worst second-order model-only residual is 4.84e−9; the larger combined budget includes both grid changes, both ray refinements and derivative extraction. These are numerical sensitivity checks, not probabilities or uncertainty on nature.

## Distinguishing mass from a travelling disturbance

For an unchanged material count and uncompensated scalar seed, a changed launch mass accounts for the early exterior signal. An independent Reissner–Nordström calculation, using neutral geodesic deviation and the derivative of the travel time rather than the simulated frequency integral, reproduces the *leading* prefront response to 4.55e−12 relative or better. The fully coupled massive-background response agrees within 1.21e−5 relative over the early window. In the fixed-mass case, both charged backgrounds are quiet at the distant clocks before the wave can arrive; for η=.8 the largest early fractional-frequency derivative in the full run is 1.7e−23, whereas the later echo reaches 2.78e−8 per unit seed. This contrast distinguishes transported scalar stress from simply putting a different static mass at the centre.

The distinction matters even for nominally small pulses. At η=0.8 the uncompensated mass variation at x=12 is −7.63369e−10 ε + 7.49772e−4 ε². The terms are equal in magnitude at **|ε|=1.018e−6**. Both signs of the larger finite test pulse add positive mass although the linear terms have opposite signs. This crossover is for the net exterior mass for this *particular preparation*, not a universal amplitude limit for MTS. The independently calculated quadratic compensation coefficient is −20.39174764, versus −20.39174706 from the real signed launches. With the mass actually matched, the early full finite clock difference is zero at stored precision. Without that compensation, the prefront finite signals are reproduced within 1.22e−5 relative. The full finite-amplitude controls are not universally converged under their stricter combined extrapolation budget: 9/16 pass in `real-controls.csv`. A first variation is not generally a waveform for a finite event.

**Constraints and control.** The full fixed-mass calculation independently checks Gauss, geometric mass and the radial Hamiltonian in three smooth radial windows at six epochs (two resolutions). At 256 the largest non-null normalized residual is 4.1e−12 (maximum integrated residual divided by maximum absolute integrand scale over epochs for each window/channel). In the exterior no perturbed electric charge is present: its isolated Gauss residual is below 4.1e−248, with no meaningful relative test there. A scalar perturbation about a completely empty scalar background has zero linear stress: the eight matter/geometry variables indeed acquire exactly zero imaginary/tangent response throughout the sampled evolution on that control.
