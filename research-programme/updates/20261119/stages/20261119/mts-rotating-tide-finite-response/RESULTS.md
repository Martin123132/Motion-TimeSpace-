# The five-Gyr misalignment survives full-strength driving

The same positive-mass Hernquist satellite follows [Erkal et al.'s released orbit](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.2661108) in the same nonrotating freely falling frame as the parent calculation. The two supplied occupations, mass pole, initial condition, satellite scale and orientation targets were not refitted. This time the wave and its self-gravity evolve nonlinearly, with all azimuthal components and the finite-extent dipole retained.

Angles are eigenvector angles of the recentered trace-free second moment, modulo its sign; amplitudes use the whole tensor. The orbit is the authors' best fit in their responding host, not an observed time series or an orbit re-solved with MTS. The rotation/occupation describes a specified spherical starting population driven by a satellite. It does not determine how much wave mass a galaxy formed. In fact the number-conserving equations leave an initially empty wave exactly empty. The fractions above are projections away from the original *comoving* state, not escaped mass or necessarily permanent excitations after the driver is removed.

**How the nonlinear check worked.** The second variation of the equations includes the squared first-order wave, interference with the second-order wave, and the first-order wave times the combined applied and induced potential. Removing these by hand is not a valid closure. For positive strengths 1/16, 1/8, 1/4, 1/2 and 1, the residual after the quadratic prediction scales approximately cubically at small strength. On the same degree-five grid, at full strength the quadratic term lowers the signed-tensor error from 1.73% to 0.239% (cap) and 3.06% to 0.165% (diffuse). These compare approximations to the *same* finite solver and are distinct from angular refinement. Suppressing just the residual dipole changes the quadratic tensor by 17.5% and 19.6% on that grid; using only a quadrupole driver changes it by 65% and 92%. Rotating an axisymmetric pulse after the fact would miss those terms.

**Connection to common gravity.** We derived the sign of the leading finite-size center acceleration without solving for the distorted wave. An extended spherical wave samples the satellite mass smoothed by the wave's own density. For a decreasing spherical satellite density, its mean pull towards the satellite cannot exceed the pull at its center. The independent orbit integrals recover the field's first-order displacement vectors within 0.3 ppm. A point satellite of the same mass would instead produce first-order offsets of only 0.14 and 81.0 pc. This is ordinary gravity on an extended body. The exact local-size expansion, positive remainder bound and compact-probe check are in LOCAL-LIMIT.md. For the deformed state, the trace and shape contracted with the local gradient of tidal curvature explain much, but not all, of the force correction; the remaining 11% and 27% **of the correction** are retained.

**Conservation and observability.** The signed second-order number identity and the common-metric torque balance both close independently. An arbitrary change of direction of a spatially uniform pull puts angular momentum in the center's motion but not in internal spin; the fine control reproduces this. The rotating finite tide, by contrast, deposits intrinsic spin almost parallel to the imposed orbital angular momentum. This is a torque on the wave in a prescribed environment, not a measured transfer out of the real LMC.

At the archive's 8.1 kpc solar position the full induced wave acceleration gives astrometric dipoles of about **0.0279 and 0.0144 microarcsec/year**. These include more than the quadrupole values of 0.00811 and 0.00912. Holding every other field fixed, even the full induced changes are only **0.076 and 0.040–0.041 covariance standard deviations** using [Gaia EDR3, Table 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.02036). EDR3 measured the total Solar System acceleration, not this response. This is a sensitivity calculation, not evidence that Gaia detected or excluded either state.

**Numerical limits kept visible.** Both weak tensors recover the sealed five-component targets within 5.5 ppm on the 44-function radial grid. Early radial and angular cutoffs miss the unchanged 20 ppm criterion. Extending the final angular cutoff from 13 to 16 (cap) and 19 to 22 (diffuse) changes the full tensor by 5.2 and 6.5 ppm; at degree 16 the independent larger radial box changes it by 1.5 and 3.1 ppm. Moving the angular switch earlier, or comparing to full-orbit degree-seven evolution, also passes. These are successive refinement checks, not rigorous infinite-domain error bars. An initially underresolved angular/radial quadrature preflight was rejected before it produced any data. Some original work integrations and one low-rank solar-force readout missed their budgets; better integration of the same power histories and expanded Poisson readout are both recorded along with the misses. The tabulated work check on the finest saved time quadrature passes for all 24 nonzero finite-packet cases. See REPLAY.md for provenance and independent checks.
