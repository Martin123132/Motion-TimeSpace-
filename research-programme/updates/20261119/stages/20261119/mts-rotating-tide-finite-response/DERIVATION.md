# Finite driving, conservation, and the galactic center of a wave

The parent common-metric weak-field limit, isolated nodeless occupation, units, orbital reconstruction and external Hernquist radius are unchanged. Write \(\psi=e^{-iEt}(u+v)\) and \(H=-\nabla^2/2+\Phi_0-E\), with \(Hu=0\), \(K=\Delta^{-1}\) (vacuum exterior). Let \(V({\bf r},t)=\Phi_s({\bf r},t)-\Phi_s(0,t)-{\bf r}\cdot\nabla\Phi_s(0,t)\). Only a constant phase and the acceleration at the reference origin have been removed. In particular the angular average need not vanish, nor need the whole dipole vanish. Multiplying \(V\) everywhere by \(\eta\) varies the total mass of the same extended source on the same orbit; its scale radius is not recalibrated at fractional masses.

**Field equations.** Write \(v=\eta f+\eta^2 k+O(\eta^3)\); \(k\) is a Taylor coefficient, not the unhalved second derivative. The two equations from the original Schrödinger–Poisson coupling are
\[
 i\dot f=H f+u(V+\phi_1),\qquad
 i\dot k=Hk+u\phi_2+(V+\phi_1)f,\qquad
 \phi_1=K[2u\,{\rm Re}f],\quad
 \phi_2=K[2u\,{\rm Re}k+|f|^2].
\]
Both variations vanish at the initial archive epoch; the satellite field there is not artificially set to zero. At unit mass the independent finite evolution instead uses \(i\dot v=Hv+(u+v)(\eta V+K[2u\,{\rm Re}v+|v|^2])\). No phase is reset later. With \(d\mu=d^3r/(4\pi)\), \(N_j=\int \rho_j d\mu=0\) for these zero-initial-data variations. In particular the second density's square term cancels the change in the interference term; it is not independent positive mass. The local currents are \(j_1=u\nabla{\rm Im}f-{\rm Im}f\nabla u\) and \(j_2=u\nabla{\rm Im}k-{\rm Im}k\nabla u+{\rm Im}(f^*\nabla f)\); using the two real potentials above gives \(\partial_t\rho_j+\nabla\cdot j_j=0\). Integrated boundary flux vanishes for the retained Dirichlet wave boundary.

**Internal rather than displaced shape.** Denote the dipole moment by \({\bf d}\) and the STF moment by \(Q_{ij}\). The recentered tensor is \(Q^{\rm int}_{ij}=Q_{ij}-[d_i d_j-\delta_{ij}|d|^2/3]/M\). At second order subtract the first-order dipole squared; at finite strength use the actual center and mass. The initial source's calibrated half-mass or central gradient is not a formation prescription for this wave. The longest eigenvector is compared to the frozen present direction; its angle from the satellite as seen at the translated wave center is reported separately. A single azimuthally symmetric wave rotated after evolving would miss the mixing in \((V+\phi_1)f\) and \(|f|^2\).

**A sign theorem for center motion.** For a spherical test wave with normalized radial density \(w=\rho_0/M\) and a spherical perturber of nonincreasing nonnegative density \(\rho_s\), the average gravitational field is that of the radial effective source \(w*\rho_s\), by translation invariance and Newton's kernel. By the shell theorem the first-order acceleration relative to the origin is
\[
 \ddot{\bf X}_1=\frac{G}{D^2}\big[M_{w*\rho_s}(<D)-M_s(<D)\big]\hat{\bf D}.
\]
The first enclosed mass cannot exceed the second: it averages the source mass in balls of radius \(D\) with displaced centers. For a radially decreasing density every such ball contains at most the mass of the concentric ball (apply the overlap inequality for balls to each density level set). Thus the finite-size part is always **away from the satellite** at that instant, or zero. The integrated displacement at the present epoch need not point away from its *current* position. For a point source this simplifies to \(-GM_s[1-M_w(<D)/M_w]/D^2\). A genuinely uniform field still translates every particle equally; this is the failure of the point-probe approximation in a gradient, not a different gravitational coupling. Equality for an external point if all the wave is inside \(D\) is a useful exact null.

Independently, in code units, integration by parts gives \(\ddot X_1=(3M)^{-1}\int r^2 \rho_0'(r)V_1(r,D)\,dr\), where \(V_1\) includes the inertial subtraction. We check this against the radial derivative in \(D\) of the spherical average of the Hernquist potential. Its signed antiderivative uses \(J=2\min(r,D)-b\log[(r+D+b)/(|r-D|+b)]\), \(\bar\Phi=-M_s J/(2rD)\). Integrate the independently computed acceleration twice along the supplied moving direction. No excited-wave output is needed to predict this leading center trajectory.

**Torque and work.** Rotational invariance gives \(d{\bf L}/dt=-\int \rho\,{\bf r}\times\nabla(\eta V)\,d\mu\) for a spherical domain, with the self-torque zero. Boundary pressure on a centered spherical wall also has zero torque about its center. An initially spherical real state has no angular momentum to linear order. Its leading angular momentum is exactly the bilinear \(\int f^*\hat{\bf L}f\,d\mu\); no unknown second variation enters. Its intrinsic part subtracts \({\bf X}_1\times{\bf P}_1\). Check the three signed components using the field-derivative side and the applied-torque side; with the orbit prescribed, this is a wave balance, not a claim to have observed an equal change in the satellite. Energies are relative to the stationary ground in the \(E\)-rotating phase: the tracked energy including source interaction with the density change has derivative \(\int(\rho-\rho_0)\eta\partial_t V\,d\mu\). This removes a reversible ground-source term from both sides.

**Local metric readout.** The induced acceleration at a fixed solar position uses the full difference \(-\nabla(\Phi_{\rm wave}-\Phi_0)\); a different question is the induced field around the translated wave center, or only its centered quadrupole. For the latter project \(\Phi_{\rm wave}({\bf X}+r{\bf n})\) onto \(n_i n_j-\delta_{ij}/3\) on a sphere before differentiating the angular and radial dependence. Do not substitute the exterior point-quadrupole formula at an interior location. In every case the projection on the astrometric glide is \(a/c\). The direct galaxy/satellite and baryonic contributions are not a fit or measurement made here.
