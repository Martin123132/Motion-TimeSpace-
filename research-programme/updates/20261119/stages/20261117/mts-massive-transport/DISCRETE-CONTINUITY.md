That first alternate implementation still used the Hamiltonian velocity for the shear. First use one CDM velocity consistently in the hierarchy and shear. Separately check a finite-quadrature identity; neither change fits a numerical or galactic target.

Let h=H/k, C the Hamiltonian CDM velocity and T the independent trace velocity. Energy conservation plus η_x=J/2 propagates the constraint. For massive particles let E=1+ΣW(1/v−1) denote the retained background energy per massless density, where W is the background Fermi weight. Its enthalpy from scale differentiation is B=4E/3−E_,ln(a)/3. The separate brightness quadrature supplies M=(4/3)Σw/v as the coefficient of the CDM metric source. Integrating by parts gives M=B for continuous Fermi–Dirac momentum, not generally for two finite grids. The density continuity defect is e=(3 fν/g²)(M−B)c_x. In the trace-driven equations (h(T−C))_x=e/2; in the constraint-driven equations (T−C)_x=e/(2h) for the passive trace state as implemented. Test the sign, magnitude, and residual, including numerical time-step drift. Pressure and density here include the native a² normalization.
