## A pressure discrepancy with a specific origin

For a canonical slow scalar in the synchronous CDM frame, the *homogeneous velocity enters twice*: it drives the perturbation throughout its history, and multiplies the perturbation velocity in the pressure readout. With u=δφ the source equation is u_xx+2(H/k)u_x+[1+(am/k)²]u=δ_c,x φ′/k. The prime denotes conformal time. The stored pressure source P=φ′ k u_x−(am)²φu is the native a²-scaled pressure in its own field normalization, not pressure in pascals.

On the same solved geometry, we propagated a passive second perturbation using the archived velocity while leaving the physical fields and bath untouched. Both started with identical perturbations. Independent reconstruction of the archived background from its momentum, density and pressure agrees within 2.3×10⁻¹⁶ at the nine clocks. We compare against the old numerical implementation to identify its difference, **not** to replace the independently evolved velocity by a less accurate one.

## What the remaining pressure error can tell us

Starting once at the material event with its actual field state and directly captured CDM derivative predicts the following eight native pressures to 1.5×10⁻¹⁴ (baseline) and 2.5×10⁻⁹ (MTS) relative. Those percentages apply to the smaller *remaining* gap at that endpoint, not the original 57.7%.

Going all the way back on the sparse transfer output, we tested the metric forcing two ways: differentiating the CDM density difference, or using the independently saved shear and metric curvature. Neither cleared the two-route resolution gate. The widest original gap in x=kτ is 4.637. More importantly, the CDM contrast is stored in binary32. An adjoint of the actual pressure equation measures how its cubic-interpolated history can change within those rounding intervals. Two synthetic histories, verified to round to the **same** stored CDM values as their branch, can shift the pressure by 0.595 (baseline) or 0.967 (MTS) times the native answer; separate forward integrations confirm the adjoint estimates within 1.4×10⁻⁸ relative. These are worst-direction witnesses for the *density-only reconstruction*, not physical cosmologies or typical statistical errors. Additional digits printed from that transfer series cannot settle the small residual. Use an independently constrained metric source.

## The independent Einstein check and its repair

The initial trace alternative evolved the CDM with the trace velocity but inadvertently evaluated the metric shear with the constraint velocity. Its 4.386% change in the **paired angular contrast** was real as a software result, not evidence of extra angular energy. The independent trace version now uses its own evolved velocity in both places. A separate matter discretization issue also had a predictable sign: the homogeneous Fermi energy and perturbation monopole had been integrated with different finite momentum weights. The continuum identity (4/3)∫w/v=E+P gives a unique quadrature correction to the monopole metric source. Neither fix uses the native pressure or radiation targets.

- The raw 24-node metric-source normalization error was +4.52×10⁻⁹ at the start; independent 32-node integration gives −6.78×10⁻¹¹. Its integrated source predicts about 99.9% of the raw material trace-constraint invariant h(T−C) on both branches. The sign reversal under quadrature refinement is also predicted. In the finest balanced trace run the material relative CDM velocity discrepancy is 6.81×10⁻¹⁰ (baseline) and 1.43×10⁻⁹ (MTS). MOMENT-LAW.md derives the energy and momentum contractions; both agree with the actual changes of the corresponding massive-particle moments in all eight intervals of both finest trace runs. This is local, branch-specific conservation evidence.
- At the finest common step the independent trace and Hamiltonian calculations differ by only **2.84×10⁻¹¹** (drag) and **3.17×10⁻⁸** (angular), relative to the paired contrast. Including time, momentum, angular and thermal refinements, a second early restart, interpolation, the quadrature correction and the trace test gives sensitivity budgets **1.91×10⁻⁷** and **2.53×10⁻⁶** against 2×10⁻⁵. Both internal numerical checks pass. These are sums of observed sensitivities, not rigorous bounds on all approximations. They do not remove the reported differences from the older numerical archive.

## Next discriminating test

The archived implementation **was actually using** a three-moment fluid approximation for massless neutrinos at all 2,262 captured points on both branches. The massive species used five momentum nodes, multipoles through 16, and was not in its final truncated-fluid mode. We checked the native derivative readouts, not just the switch names: all 2,262 satisfy π_x=−3π/x−D_x at the unchanged tolerance.
