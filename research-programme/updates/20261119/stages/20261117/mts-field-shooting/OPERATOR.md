# Finite perturbation of the native operator on the common geometry

The baseline's early smooth radiation transfer remains sensitive to arithmetic even with a resolved background. Its physical scalar displacement is small and its contribution to the expansion is much smaller. A finite-difference probe limited to a fraction of the scalar amplitude can therefore leave the native expansion/metric evaluation at nearly identical rounded numbers. Test this explanation without changing any physical displacement, field, bank, clock, perturbation, advertised tolerance or empirical parameter.

For frozen time and unchanged original perturbations let F(b) be the native derivative with geometric primitives b=(a,phi_i,v_i). The exact forcing on the difference hierarchy is F(b1)-F(b0). At bmid define C(A)=[F(bmid+A d)-F(bmid-A d)]/(2A), d=b1-b0. The desired finite source is C(1/2), not a derivative attained by neglecting the physical separation. C is even in A. Interpolate three symmetric secants as a quadratic polynomial in A² to A²=1/4, cancelling the leading cubic and fifth-order response of F. This is a numerical operator evaluation, not a modified constitutive law or an exact polynomial claim.

Choose the power-of-two probe by the scale perturbation and canonical field energy norm, max(|da/a|,sqrt(sum[(dv)²+(am dphi)²])/(sqrt(6) H)). Cap that displacement at 0.0025 and test half that cap independently. The scalar can change sign in a *probe*, not along the physical solution. Positive scale, positive total energy and finite derivatives are required. Leave the cancellation-safe coupled Einstein constraint, material densities, baryon/photon momenta and radiation quadrature untouched. Retain all original material variables; no deletion of weak channels. Controls: a zero-direction pair, resolved synthetic nonzero scalar/scale perturbations against the literal native operator at the initial state, and unchanged physical source, signed/absolute quadrature and per-case step/amplitude checks. Compare both globally prepared models; do not interpret failure as a physical null.
