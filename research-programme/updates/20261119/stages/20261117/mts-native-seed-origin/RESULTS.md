# From the original adiabatic preparation to signed field pressure and photon scattering

**Decision.** Retain the physical wave and photon collision evolution from the original single-mode start. It predicts the signed pressure of every active scalar at all 408 saved early epochs on the baseline and MTS branches. It also resolves two distinct radiation-source contrasts without choosing a switch-on time or fitting any amplitude. These are conditional calculations for one normalized Fourier mode, not observations, a total sky spectrum, or a replacement for the late material restart.

## Why matching the archive was not the final test

The integrating factor J=(1+R)²(v_b−v_γ)/L turns the retained first-order slip derivative into a regular current equation (SLIP.md). Its predicted slip lies in the archive's baryon-velocity rounding interval at all 384 tested times on each branch. The raw archived difference itself, however, usually cannot resolve a 2e-5 fractional test: one stored velocity is binary32. This is compatibility, not 384 precise measurements of slip.

The native intermediate dipole-slope convention reproduces the archived photon quadrupole at all 57 points in a new interval on both branches. But when we instead evolve intensity, linear polarization, slip and the fields **together**, the second-order relaxation expression using the physical photon acceleration passes 80/81 post-switch comparisons with the evolved quadrupole in each branch. The native convention at the same states passes only 6/81 baseline and 7/81 MTS. This identifies a closure/readout difference, not a failure of the bulk matter equations. Here the saved tight-coupling polarization column is exactly a quarter of the saved quadrupole; it is not an independent measurement of polarization. The 32/45 and 11/6 arise from the retained polarized Thomson collision matrix; neither is an MTS coupling.
