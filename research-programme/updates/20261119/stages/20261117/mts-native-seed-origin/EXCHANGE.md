# Integrated relative-motion work on the same physical interval

For the coupled kinetic trajectories use x lower = later of the two **second** saved times (one sample after collision switch-on), and x upper = earlier of the two final saved times. This compares the identical conformal interval at the same k, rather than subtracting mismatched archive epochs. Do this before inspecting the integrated heating. Reuse the baseline, MTS and alternate startup restarts; do not normalize any state to observational values. The original photon energy normalization must match between branches.

Normalize energy to the comoving photon enthalpy, constant in the background used here. Put μ=R/(1+R), S=v_b−v_γ, K=μS²/2 and F=δp_b/ρ_b−h v_b−δ_γ/4+π_γ/2. The original collision equations give S_x=F−S/(μL), μ_x=hμ/(1+R) and hence

    dK/dx = W−Q,    W=μ S F+μ_x S²/2,    Q=S²/L ≥ 0.

This is the signed relative **bulk-motion** balance and dipole drag only. It is not total energy dissipated by all photon multipoles or a sky prediction. Q has no free amplitude once the saved unit adiabatic mode is specified; real spatial/spectral averaging would require a separate normalization and power spectrum. W includes differential pressure, gravity through the evolving metric/flows, and the time-dependent baryon loading. Directly integrate Q, W and the smaller storage derivative from the kinetic state; independently subtract the two endpoint K values. Test both the total flux scale and the storage's own scale, and compare the small baseline–MTS difference with conservative errors from each run without assuming error cancellation. Do not promote a difference whose own budget fails.
