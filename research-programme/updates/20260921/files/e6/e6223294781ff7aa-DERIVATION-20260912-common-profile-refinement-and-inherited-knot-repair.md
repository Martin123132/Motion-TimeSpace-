## 3. The integration failure and its diagnosis

The original mass faces are generated by accumulated SBP norm weights. Near each boundary these are not simple midpoint faces, and the fine mass faces do **not** contain all coarse mass faces.

The inherited K_seed has derivative changes at the original mass faces. We had included these inherited knots in the refined bulk integration and coordinate-bubble partition. The old time-link quadrature still split only at the new mesh's own nodes and faces. Therefore it integrated some piecewise functions across an unmarked change of formula.

Six coarse breakpoints were missing on both refined link grids:

    5.880533854166667, 5.899739583333333, 5.913736979166667,
    6.086263020833333, 6.100260416666667, 6.119466145833333.

This matters especially for the newly constructed trace-dual momentum tests. The trace map itself still passed; the force load supplied to it came from a different numerical integration of the same parent covector.

We decomposed the observed endpoint defect without a fit:

    endpoint_defect = parent_family_projection_defect
                    + E V_new M_new^(-1) (G_P,bulk - G_P,link).

The decomposition error is below 3.9e-19. The family-projection term is negligible. At N32 the inconsistent load reaches 2.84453e-9 and accounts for endpoint errors (-8.01364e-9,-1.71901e-8). This identifies a concrete pipeline failure, not a rejection of the continuum equations.

The repair is simply

    link_partition = union(fine_scalar_nodes, fine_mass_faces,
                           inherited_physical_profile_knots).

Factor coefficients, anchors, targets, orientation, quadrature order, action, time-link formula and boundary histories are unchanged. The N16 quadrature arrays are unchanged as well.

## 6. A derived conditional endpoint bound

There is also an analytic explanation for a shrinking boundary correction. For an endpoint factor, write B_f as a weighted combination of third forward differences. At most the first five mesh spacings enter the inner endpoint; reflect the argument at the outer endpoint.

Assume:

- chi is C3 on that endpoint neighbourhood, with |chi'''| <= M3;
- C_i=R_i^2 N_i sqrt(F_i) is bounded by Cmax and the metric/lapse are positive;
- |g|=|kappa sqrt(F) Pdot/N| <= gmax along those links.

The exact integral identity for a third forward difference gives |Delta_h^3 chi| <= h^3 M3. Also

    D_f / J_f,endpoint
      = sum_i S_fi C_i exp(integral from endpoint to R_i of g),

whose absolute value is at most Cmax exp(5h gmax), since S_fi are nonnegative and sum to one. The endpoint scalar covector is -sum_f B_f,endpoint A_f D_f/(h J_f,endpoint). Bounding its three contributing factor types yields

    |G_chi,endpoint| <= c_end Cmax exp(5h gmax) h^2 M3,

    c_end = 59097/573104 + 253/50568 + 1/392
          = 190279/1719312 = 0.110671594219083.

The three terms come from the diagonal, adjacent and exceptional endpoint Gram weights, including their signs through absolute-value bounds. Their factor construction is checked independently at 17, 33, 65 and 129 nodes; no 129-node dynamics is claimed.

Multiplying by kappa sqrt(F)|q|/N bounds the corresponding parent mass-flux correction. Under uniform versions of these assumptions it is O(h^2). This is a **conditional bound**, not a claim that uniform regularity of the entire nonlinear solution family has been proved.

For our fixed piecewise-cubic source, a nested N128 grid is the first of 16/32/64/128 whose full five-spacing endpoint stencil stays inside the original first/last cubic cell. Thus the bound also identifies a useful next test. Here h is numerical mesh spacing; it must not be relabelled as the theory's physical coarse-graining length.
