## 2. Exact kinetic-complement identity

Let B be the field basis, chi=-k H the physical field displacement generated by source motion, and use the positive inner product <u,v>_A=integral A u v dR. The field/source velocity Hessian is

    [ M        c ]
    [ c^T  mu_s+<chi,chi>_A ],
    M_ij=<B_i,B_j>_A, c_i=<B_i,chi>_A,
    mu_s=S N^2/(U^2 ell^3), ell=sqrt(N^2-V^2/U^2).

Writing P_h for the A-orthogonal projection onto the field space, exact block elimination gives

    mu_eff = mu_s + m_add,
    m_add  = <chi,chi>_A-c^T M^-1 c
           = ||chi-P_h chi||_A^2 >= 0,
    bddot  = (g_b-c^T M^-1 g_q)/(mu_s+m_add).

The g terms are the complete Euler-Lagrange right-hand sides after differentiating the velocity-dependent momenta; no global field momentum derivative is dropped. In this flat control the mechanical force is mu_s*bddot. Both identities pass 56 checks over two degrees, seven resolutions and both branches.

This is a **finite representation's kinetic complement**, not a derived new physical particle mass or a new coupling. If a nonzero source gradient makes chi have nonzero one-sided boundary trace, while every admissible field variation has zero source trace, chi cannot lie exactly in this finite continuous polynomial space. Positive integration then makes m_add strictly positive. An initial Ritz projection does not remove that structural mismatch while keeping the original source gradient. The complement may tend to zero on refinement; the statement does not assert a nonzero continuum obstruction.

For the locally affine initial profile, the flat continuum wave equation gives W_t=2 H0/b at the source side, while the finite field variations obey the eliminated boundary value. A leading local residual model therefore yields

    F_initial approximately -(2/b) m_add mu_s/(mu_s+m_add).

The exact elimination identity above is distinguished from this local approximation. Far-profile, variable-weight and finite-grid contributions are retained in the measured force. The approximation is poor on some coarse grids and is not asserted as an all-time theorem.

## 3. Derive the boundary capacity without fitting it

For a quadratic interval of length h, the unweighted consistent mass matrix is

    h/30 * [[4,2,-1],[2,16,2],[-1,2,4]].

Minimizing its norm over the midpoint value gives the endpoint matrix

    h/24 * [[3,-1],[-1,3]].

This condensation is used only to analyze the projection norm. Midpoint degrees of freedom are NOT removed from the time evolution. On a uniform semi-infinite mesh the minimum squared norm with unit boundary trace is kappa*h, where

    kappa = sqrt(2)/12,
    residual_vertex_ratio = 3-2 sqrt(2).

Indeed kappa=1/8-(1/24)^2/(1/8+kappa), and the decaying vertex recurrence is r^2-6r+1=0. For a first cell of dimensionless length theta and a remaining tail capacity d, the exact frozen-weight update is

    T_theta(d) = theta/8-(theta/24)^2/(theta/8+d).

Start with d=kappa, and apply this update backwards through the source-adjacent subcells. Add the left and right capacities. The resulting local model is

    m_add approximately b^2 H0^2 h [beta_left+beta_right].

No force result is fitted to obtain kappa or the recurrence. Variable radial weights and nonaffine distant fields mean this is a leading local model, not a certified remainder bound for the actual trajectory. For the old quadratic1025 case it predicts the initial reference force within 0.009%; for2049 within 0.002%. Coarse disagreements remain in the table rather than being excluded.

## 6. A distinct trace-sensitive coupling mechanism

The original Gram weights are fixed during local subdivision. Write y=G phi evaluated at the original vertices, a=G rho for the reference hinge, j for the actual reference-coordinate source derivative jump, and W for the positive sampled coefficient matrix including 1/h. Then

    V_Gram = (y-a j)^T W (y-a j)/2,
    D = a^T W a > 0,
    j_star = (a^T W y)/D,
    V_Gram = [y^T W y-(a^T W y)^2/D]/2 + D(j-j_star)^2/2,
    partial_j V_Gram = D(j-j_star).

This exact square completion and its derivative are verified symbolically and in four old/new states (11 checks). The static minimizer j_star is not automatically the GR trace, and the evolving field is not asserted to attain that minimum. All jump comparisons here use the reference-coordinate jump J_right H_right-J_left H_left, not a mixture of physical and reference gradients.

At base257 and t=.4, refinement moves j from -0.00198102 to -0.00199538. Its distance to its own instantaneous static minimizer decreases from 1.08675e-5 to 2.60881e-6, while its distance to the GR reference jump INCREASES from 1.61181e-5 to 3.04761e-5. The force discrepancy also increases. This is a measured association, not a proof that this single effect causes the entire force error. At base513 and t=.05 the trace comparison instead improves; that contrasting case is retained.

There is also a precise topology issue. A compact source-adjacent perturbation epsilon*eta((r-b_star)/epsilon), with eta(s)=s(1-s)^2, can change j by one while vanishing at every original sampled vertex. Its bulk squared H1 derivative cost is 2*epsilon/15, tending to zero, but y stays fixed and the Gram change generally does not vanish. Therefore this point-derivative Gram extension is not continuous in the bulk H1 topology alone. For a fixed y, the displayed square completion gives its static trace-relaxed energy; arbitrary narrow layers explain why that relaxation cannot be inferred from bulk-waveform convergence alone.

This does NOT prove that the complete energy space, an augmented trace domain, or a suitably stronger parent action is ill posed. Nor does it authorize replacing V_Gram with the relaxed expression, deleting its rows, or forcing j=j_star during evolution. A correct trace-domain/trace-response completion must be derived and its dynamical/current terms retained. The new result isolates a concrete part of the coupling to investigate; it is not a claim that the fundamental theory has been ruled out or completed.
