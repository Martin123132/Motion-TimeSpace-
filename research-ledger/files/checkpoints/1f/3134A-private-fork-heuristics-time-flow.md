# 3134A - Private Fork Heuristics: Time, Flow, and GR-Limit Caution

Private working note. This is not a theorem, not evidence, and not public-facing prose. It records search heuristics to keep in mind when a derivation forks.

## Core Heuristic

Do not reject a branch solely because an intermediate variable appears to make "time dilation work the opposite way to GR".

First check whether the branch has confused one of the following:

- coordinate time;
- proper time along a material worldline;
- observed clock rate after readout;
- null affine parameter;
- motion-through-background or flow parameter;
- local gravitational redshift;
- fitted empirical time variable.

If the observable clock prediction still reduces to tested SR/GR limits after the readout map is applied, the branch remains worth investigating. If it cannot reduce to those limits, the branch fails or becomes a bounded deformation, not a replacement claim.

## Martin-Style Search Bias

When a route "nearly works" but seems to invert a standard intuition, try the following before demoting it:

1. Identify which time variable is actually being varied.
2. Derive the clock observable rather than reading it off the raw internal parameter.
3. Check whether massless/null behaviour can arise from a structural reason other than "local time stops".
4. Keep photons, massive high-speed probes, gravitational clocks, and cosmological time as separate readout arenas.
5. Ask whether the contradiction is physical, or only a mismatch between internal flow time and measured proper time.

## Guardrail

This heuristic cannot excuse a wrong limit. The theory still has to recover:

- SR time dilation for local inertial clock comparisons;
- GR gravitational redshift and geodesic weak-field limits;
- Newtonian mechanics in the appropriate slow-motion weak-field limit;
- Maxwell/EM stress-energy conservation where the EM sector is claimed;
- PPN, clock, orbital, and laboratory bounds when extra coupling survives.

## Practical Use

At future forks, do not write "wrong sign, dead branch" until the readout chain has been separated:

```text
internal flow variable -> quotient/readout map -> clock observable -> tested limit.
```

If the sign inversion survives all the way to the tested observable, it is a real problem. If it disappears at the readout map, it may be useful structure.
