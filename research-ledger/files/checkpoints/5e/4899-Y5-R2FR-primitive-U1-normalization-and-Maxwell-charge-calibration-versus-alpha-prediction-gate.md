## 2. Exact normalization-orbit theorem

Canonical normalization is

\[
A_c=\sqrt{Z_A}A,
\qquad
e_R=\frac{g_J}{\sqrt{Z_A}}.
\]

In Heaviside-Lorentz natural units,

\[
\boxed{
\alpha=\frac{e_R^2}{4\pi}
=\frac{g_J^2}{4\pi Z_A}.
}
\]

Under the legal field-coordinate change `A'=sA`,

\[
Z_A'=\frac{Z_A}{s^2},
\qquad
g_J'=\frac{g_J}{s},
\]

so `g_J^2/Z_A` is invariant. Equivalently,

\[
\ln\alpha=2\ln g_J-\ln Z_A-\ln(4\pi),
\]

with Jacobian

\[
D\ln\alpha=(2,-1).
\]

The rescaling direction `(-1,-2)` is exactly null:

\[
(2,-1)\cdot(-1,-2)=0.
\]

Therefore the two coefficients have rank one and normalization-orbit nullity
one. Unlike the Newton case, this nullity is a field convention rather than
several physically distinct threshold terms. After choosing `Z_A=1`, one
physical low-energy coupling `e_R` remains. The parent still needs either to
predict that invariant before using data or calibrate it once.
