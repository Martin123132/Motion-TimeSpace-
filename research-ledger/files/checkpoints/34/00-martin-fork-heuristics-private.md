# Martin Fork Heuristics - Private Notes

**Status:** not mathematics, not evidence, not a claim. These are fork heuristics: when the formal MTS work hits a branch point, use these to remember the founding intuition before choosing whether to derive, bound, or demote a route.

## Core Instinct

Space and time should not be treated as two separable substances. You cannot traverse space without some form of time/traversal process. If a route says an object is moving through space while having no meaningful time/process at all, pause and check whether the language has collapsed distinct notions of time into one word.

## Local Time Versus Traversal

The standard GR/SR language says local proper time slows with speed and tends to zero on a null path. That is accepted as a successful limit, but the MTS fork instinct is:

```text
Do not automatically identify "local clock/proper time tends to zero" with "there is no traversal/process/field-time at all."
```

When a branch uses time, separate at least:

```text
proper time / local clock time
coordinate time / public readout time
field phase or propagation parameter
cosmic/background traversal time
source or memory time
```

This is especially important for photons, massless fields, and high-speed massive limits.

## Photon Heuristic

Photons may not experience local proper time in the GR/SR sense, but MTS should not assume too quickly that this exhausts the physical story. At forks involving EM waves, null propagation, Poynting flux, or constitutive background fields, ask:

```text
Is the photon "timeless" because proper time along a null curve is zero,
or because it is a special excitation of the background/flow/Hodge structure?
```

Do not turn this into a claim. Use it as a search direction for EM/Hodge/flow derivations.

## Gravity As Traversal Gear

Think of gravity/local geometry as climbing gear for traversing spacetime. The local gravitational state changes how clocks, rods, paths, and source motion move through the public spacetime. Fork question:

```text
Is this variable changing the path,
the clock/readout,
the energy-flow/source,
or the background traversal rule?
```

Do not let these four roles blur silently. If they are distinct, put them in distinct variables or residuals.

## High-Speed Massive Limit

If a branch suggests that massive matter at high speed "goes through spacetime/time faster" in one sense while local proper time slows in another, do not reject it immediately as wordplay. First check whether two different time notions are being conflated.

Allowed fork test:

```text
Can MTS preserve the tested SR/GR proper-time result while assigning a different traversal/flow/phase role to motion through the background?
```

If yes, write the split cleanly. If no, reject the branch.

## Time-Dilation Sign Warning

If a derivation appears to produce a time-dilation behaviour with the opposite sign to standard SR/GR language, do not instantly mark the whole route dead. First classify exactly which time variable the sign belongs to:

```text
measured clock/proper-time rate,
public coordinate readout,
field phase/oscillation rate,
energy-flow or Poynting transport parameter,
background traversal/process time,
source-memory time.
```

The hard rule stays strict:

```text
the observable clock prediction must reduce to SR/GR where SR/GR is tested.
```

But a reversed sign in a non-clock traversal, phase, or source-memory variable may be a useful clue rather than an immediate contradiction. Try one derivation pass that asks whether the apparent sign flip is a variable-identification error. If the sign flip survives into observable clock physics, reject or bound the branch.

## Compatibility Rule

MTS must reduce to Newton and GR in their tested domains. But the aim is not merely to copy their language. A fork is worth exploring if it:

```text
keeps Newton/GR predictions where tested,
explains why their limiting language looks broken or incomplete,
does not introduce an observable contradiction,
and makes a new variable/residual testable.
```

This is the "work with GR, but explain the sore spots" rule.

## Do Not Do

- Do not force every fork into standard GR wording before checking whether MTS has a cleaner time/flow distinction.
- Do not use intuition as proof.
- Do not hide a failed derivation behind poetic language.
- Do not claim photons, mass, or time behave differently unless the equations preserve known tests.
- Do not erase the difference between local clock time, public readout time, and background traversal/process.

## Use At Future Forks

Before demoting a local-GR, EM, time, or source-coupling branch, ask:

```text
Would Martin first try a split between local clock time and traversal/process time?
Would he check whether EM waves/Poynting are revealing the background Hodge/flow rule?
Would he ask whether gravity is changing the traversal gear rather than merely adding a force?
Can the branch preserve GR/Newton while explaining why the standard language feels incomplete?
```

If the answer is yes, attempt that derivation once before demoting the branch.

## Spacetime-Ontology Compass

The preferred end state is a simple mechanism that answers three questions
together rather than assigning each one an unrelated module:

```text
What is spacetime: substance, relation network, emergent state, or fundamental field?
Why is traversal of spatial separation inseparable from traversal/process in time?
Why does matter change geometry, and why must that same response change clock rates?
```

Treat the first sentence as Martin's interpretive prompt, not as a verified
quotation from Einstein unless a primary source is supplied. The physical
question is independent of attribution.

At a future ontology fork, prefer a candidate in which one mathematical object
or one tightly constrained constitutive relation owns all of:

```text
spatial adjacency and distance,
temporal interval and local clock rate,
causal propagation,
matter/energy response,
and the exchange or conservation law.
```

This does not authorize discarding the integrated metric-density `H`, the
motion sector, Diff/BRST, or the derived GR/Newton/Maxwell work. Instead ask
whether `H` can be understood as the collective relational or constitutive
state through which motion and matter determine both rods and clocks. A new
route is valuable only if it explains an existing parent ingredient or
derives a coupling; merely renaming the metric as a substance does not help.

## Simple-Mechanism Selection Test

Before enlarging the ontology, test whether a candidate can satisfy this
contract:

1. one Lorentzian interval governs spatial and temporal measurements;
2. matter enters through an action-owned universal source, not an arena switch;
3. the Bianchi/Ward identity explains the allowed matter-geometry exchange;
4. the local proper-time prediction reduces to tested SR/GR;
5. Newton, Maxwell and causal propagation follow as limits of the same object;
6. at least one non-GR MTS consequence survives and is independently testable.

The simplicity target is explanatory economy, not the smallest possible field
count. A ten-component relational/coframe/metric variable with a derived
constraint can be simpler physically than one scalar forced to impersonate
ten independent geometric directions.

## Preserve the Question, Not Every Early Formula

Do not freeze an early equation merely because it expressed the founding
intuition first. Preserve the deeper question:

```text
Can motion, spatial relation, temporal rate, and matter response be shown to
belong to one dynamical structure whose controlled limits are GR and Newton?
```

If a revised formulation answers that question more directly while retaining
the tested limits and producing a new observable, it remains in the spirit of
MTS. If it only appends a motion label to GR without deriving any additional
structure, it does not.

## Collider Missing-Momentum Test Held In Reserve

Future MTS time/traversal dynamics should be confronted with collider
missing-energy data once the parent mechanism is derived. The motivating
example is deliberately noncommittal: a massive excitation at high boost
might exchange energy-momentum with the same structure that defines its
traversal or clock rate. Do not assume that law, sign, or scaling in advance.

Translate any eventual intuition such as "the particle traverses more time
and loses energy" into one covariant possibility:

```text
an action-owned exchange current Q^nu with equal and opposite MTS flux;
a modified but unitary dispersion/propagation law;
or production of a real invisible MTS excitation carrying four-momentum.
```

Literal disappearance of energy is not allowed. The complete parent must
obey total four-momentum conservation and the relevant Ward identities.

Before comparing with data, derive without using the target sample:

1. dependence on boost, invariant mass, species and scattering angle;
2. whether the signature is missing transverse momentum, missing invariant
   mass, altered timing, displaced propagation, or a correlated combination;
3. the event-by-event four-vector distribution rather than only a total
   missing-energy number;
4. a zero/decoupling limit reproducing Standard-Model collider predictions;
5. one coefficient already fixed by the parent or by a disjoint calibration.

The comparator must include Standard-Model neutrinos, detector acceptance and
resolution, initial/final-state radiation, pileup, reconstruction cuts and
background-control regions. A residual that appears only after ignoring these
effects is not MTS evidence.

Useful falsification pattern when ready:

```text
derive MTS event weight before opening the signal region;
validate the Standard-Model pipeline in control samples;
test several energies and particle species with one frozen coefficient;
require the predicted boost/topology scaling, not merely an excess;
compare against a no-MTS baseline using identical cuts and nuisance treatment.
```

This is a reserved empirical arena. It does not currently imply that collider
missing momentum is anomalous or that MTS explains any observed event class.
