# Motion-TimeSpace: what changed since November 7

This release answers two different questions. **Can a prepared MTS field remain close to ordinary gravity?** In a specified local experiment, quantities calculated from the initial state now bound the later departure from GR. **Does the same gravitational coupling automatically supply the missing force in a galaxy?** No: the Milky Way tests in this release identify a force that the calculated response cannot supply. Neither result was obtained by tuning a new coupling to the outcome.

| Question | What was calculated | Where the result stops |

| Local GR and Newton | For an isolated, initially resting spherical dust cloud and a particular nonzero scalar packet, the initial profiles suffice to bound the difference from the GR comparison at two freely falling rulers. These are synthetic rulers, not two measured objects or a theorem for arbitrary matter. |

| A galaxy that pushes back | We propagated all six of [Vasiliev's published LMC histories](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.8015660) on two retained Galactic backgrounds. The direct satellite pull is shared by the MTS and static NFW comparisons. The MTS wave also responds through the independently derived coupling. [Bar control](../../research-programme/updates/20261119/stages/20261119/mts-finite-bar-mean-response/) · [LMC calculation and units](../../research-programme/updates/20261119/stages/20261119/mts-lmc-tidal-history/) | On the more favorable stellar background, the inner north polar force moves from 0.4564 to 0.4528–0.4538; the comparison band begins at 0.5734 (all in units of 10⁻¹⁰ m/s²). No amplitude of this one mode satisfies both radial and polar bands. The static NFW controls also miss the inner polar band. These are alternatives for the same satellite, not six independent galaxies. |

**What this narrows down.** The Galactic discrepancy is not just “too little amplitude”: at the inner site, repairing the polar and radial force with the retained mode requires opposite signs. Nor can a spatially constant extra tidal tensor satisfy the even polar requirements at both radii on these foregrounds. A useful next calculation is the spatially varying, axisymmetric response of the existing wave density at fixed occupation. It has not been substituted for the missing force. A formation-derived occupation and environment remain separate physical inputs.
