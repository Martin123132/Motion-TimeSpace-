# MTS: verified-work status, November 7, 2026

## What this update adds

This release extends the September 21 public record. It preserves the older
parent framework and adds completed derivations, controlled calculations,
negative results and numerical repairs. It is not a replacement theory or
a new observational discovery. The complete inclusion/exclusion inventory is
in the [dated evidence package](../../research-programme/updates/20261107/README.md).

### 1. The retained action now has more explicit dynamical tests

The annular-action work continued beyond the September energy/current
calculation into coupled support, source and interface refinements. Its
October 3 P7-bisection seal records completed calculations but a failed
spatial-jump convergence gate. Completion of that audit is not convergence
of that route. The record is retained rather than edited into a success.

The isolated condensate investigation started from a rollback checkpoint,
not by discarding the previous action. Extending the original real-scalar
potential to a charged complex condensate introduced a new stability issue;
the added repulsive term gives a conditional stable branch but is an added
operator, not a parameter derived from the original theory. Phonon geometry
alone did not provide a dynamical spin-2 graviton.

Finite-body response, moving-surface storage and source/radiation energy
budgets were subsequently derived and tested within the chosen action.
Stage44's interior-action storage coefficient, 120.202427736 in its saved
normalization, agrees with the response coefficient 120.202434643 to about
6e-8 relative. A positive tested storage term is not an all-mode stability
theorem.

### 2. Cosmological evidence remains connected to real observations

Stage48 reran the same Pantheon+ and DESI comparison for both the retained
parent-scalar branch and the condensate alternatives. On its 1,624 SN rows
and 13 BAO elements, the saved best-fit chi-squared values are:

| Model | chi-squared |
| --- | ---: |
| Flat LambdaCDM | 1470.56 |
| Constant-w wCDM | 1465.42 |
| CPL | 1465.09 |
| Retained parent-scalar, Lambda = 0 | 1465.13 |

These are shared-data fit results, not a fresh held-out confirmation or
an automatic model-preference claim. Mass/state selection still carries
empirical inputs. The tested homogeneous condensate with Lambda preferred
zero condensate fraction; its no-Lambda version gave approximately 3575.60.
That negative result kept the older parent-scalar cosmology in play rather
than silently replacing it with the newer speculative state.

### 3. Source physics is connected to radiation, gas and metric response

The October work developed the two-field radiation hierarchy, kinetic
source terms, low-frequency response, gas feedback and source-to-gravity
coupling. It includes explicit baseline/cold and MTS/heavy comparisons,
precision repairs, fixed-tolerance convergence checks and saved failures.

These are controlled physical-model calculations, not new sky measurements.
The final-spectrum uncertainty work separated temperature, mu, y and
remaining spectral shape, while explicitly retaining incomplete integration
and continuation qualifications. Error estimates from finite refinements
are not rigorous continuum error certificates.

The November scattering receipt verifies individual directions 0 through
38: **39 of a 128-direction grid**. Rays 0-2 use their independently verified
stable reconciliation. This is individual-direction coverage, not a qualified
full angular integral. The next directions and unfinished drafts are not
published as completed results.

### 4. The current GR-to-Newton programme has quantitative comparisons

The November 7 series connects source and constraint terms to the scalar
action, follows the coupled metric/kinetic feedback, and compares independently
evolved GR, Poisson-spatial and dust-force systems on frozen backgrounds.
This tests reductions inside the adopted gravitational theory; it does not
derive the Einstein term or the numerical value of Newton's constant.

For the saved MTS/heavy cases, the final cold-matter contrast differences
between Poisson-spatial and GR, normalized by the maximum GR contrast, are
approximately -0.175439%, -0.0479051% and -0.0254285% at k = 0.324977,
0.649953 and 1.299907 Mpc^-1. Each lies inside its selected saved error bound.
These are three controlled wavelength comparisons, not a cosmological fit
or a claim that MTS differs from GR by those percentages observationally.

The subsequent cold-adapted norm avoids an artificial minimum-neutrino-speed
restriction. The latest completed stage extends the same-action background
to a chosen interval a = 0.36273335 to 0.46575884 (z approximately 1.757 to
1.147), where all five represented neutrino momentum nodes have speeds at
most 0.1c. It carries an analytic heavy-phase averaging uncertainty estimate
through that background: the maximum relative H bound is 7.25994e-7, or
0.0000726%, within the unchanged numerical gate. Five represented nodes do
not bound an unrepresented distribution tail, and the background estimate
does not by itself qualify the next perturbation calculation.

## Next scientific steps

Carry that qualified cold background into the relative-phase perturbation
equations, then test the resulting response and reduction bounds. Complete
the remaining real-scattering directions before promoting the full angular
source. Use qualified source histories for further observable predictions,
with matched baseline tests and a documented data-exposure audit.

The coframe/visible-matter origin, scale/state selection and fully unified
empirical account remain distinct research tasks. The concrete advance here
is more of the dynamics being derived and independently checked, with the
remaining assumptions and failed alternatives kept visible.
